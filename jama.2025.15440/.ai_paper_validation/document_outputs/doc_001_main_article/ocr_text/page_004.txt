Complete primary care follow-up information was avail-
able in 2408 participants (95.6%) in the patch group and 2410
(95.6%) in the control group; the main reason for incomplete
primary care follow-up was the participant moving to a non-
participating GP practice during the trial (Figure 1). Complete
secondary care data were available for 2514 participants
(99.8%) in the patch group and 2512 (99.7%) in the control
group.

At randomization, AMALFI participants had a mean (SD)
age of 78 (6) years, 2360 (47%) were female, 4528 (90%) had
hypertension, 963 (19%) had a prior stroke or transient ische-
mic attack, and the median (IQR) CHA,DS.VASc score was 4
(3-5) (Table). A small number of individuals (n = 68 [1.3%]) had
arecorded CHA,DS.VASc score lower than 3, likely due to meth-
odological changes to data coding in primary care after the eli-
gibility check. A small proportion (7%) were taking oral anti-
coagulants prior to randomization, most likely for treatment

tions, 1.26 [95% CI, 1.02-1.57]; P = .03) (Figure 2). The results
