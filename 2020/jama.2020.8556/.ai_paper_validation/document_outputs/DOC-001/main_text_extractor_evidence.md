## Trial, groups, and stated outcome hierarchy

- **Location:** PDF p. 1 (printed p. 47), Abstract: Design/Setting/Participants; Interventions; Main Outcomes and Measures.  
  **Excerpt:** “Placebo-controlled randomized clinical trial of **310** care home residents…,” randomized to probiotic **n = 155** or placebo **n = 155**, “for up to 1 year.” The primary outcome was “cumulative antibiotic administration days for all-cause infections measured from randomization for up to 1 year.”
- **Location:** PDF p. 3 (printed p. 49), Methods: Outcomes.  
  **Primary outcome definition:** cumulative systemic antibiotic-administration days for all-cause infections, calculated from recorded systemic-antibiotic days; denominator = observed study days.  
  **Secondary outcomes stated:** infection-category antibiotic days (UTI, gastrointestinal, upper/lower respiratory [split post hoc], skin/soft-tissue, unexplained fever, other); infection number/site/duration; diarrhea outcomes; *C difficile* incidence; stool/oral microbiology; EQ-5D-5L and ICECAP-O; hospital stays/days; deaths.
- **Location:** PDF p. 3 (printed p. 49), Methods: Statistical Analyses.  
  **Excerpt:** Primary and secondary comparative analyses included randomized participants providing outcome data, in randomized group, without imputation for loss of observation time. Secondary-endpoint findings were stated to be exploratory because of multiple comparisons.

## Subgroups, adverse events, and authors’ results statements

- **Location:** PDF p. 8 (printed p. 54), Results: Subgroup Effects.  
  **Excerpt:** “There were no statistically significant different intervention effects for any of the prespecified subgroups.” Details are referenced to eTable 6 in Supplement 2.
- **Location:** PDF p. 8 (printed p. 54), Results: Adverse Events.  
  **Reported counts:** **120** residents experienced **283** adverse events: **150** in probiotic and **133** in placebo. Hospitalizations: **94** events vs **78**; deaths: **33** vs **32**. Three trial-related adverse events, all placebo: product stopped for choking risk (1), reported worsening diarrhea (1), and reported bloating (1).
- **Location:** PDF p. 8 (printed p. 54), Discussion, first and final results paragraphs; PDF p. 9 (printed p. 55), Conclusions.  
  **Main-result wording:** daily probiotic combination “did not result in significantly fewer cumulative systemic antibiotic administration days for all-cause acute infections.” The discussion states no beneficial effect relative to placebo on overall antibiotic use; main infection categories; infection duration; health utility/well-being; hospitalizations; death; antibiotic-associated diarrhea; or carriage of antibiotic-resistant stool organisms. It also states that lower-respiratory antibiotic days and lower self-reported generic well-being/capability at 3 months were statistically significant, and that “a prespecified sensitivity analysis found a significant increase in cumulative systemic antibiotic days”; “These findings should be interpreted with caution, given multiple testing.”
- **Location:** PDF p. 9 (printed p. 55), Conclusions.  
  **Excerpt:** “did not significantly reduce antibiotic administration for all-cause infections. The findings do not support the use of probiotics in this setting.”

## Abstract results (top-line duplicate report)

- **Location:** PDF p. 1 (printed p. 47), Abstract: Results and Conclusions.  
  **Excerpt/value set:** Among 310 randomized residents (mean age **85.3**, **66.8%** women), **195 (62.9%)** were alive and completed trial; diary data available **98.7%** probiotic and **97.4%** placebo. Primary outcome values: **12.9** vs **12.0** days; absolute difference **0.9 [95% CI, −3.25 to 5.05]**; adjusted IRR **1.13 [0.79–1.63]; P=.50**. Adverse events: **283** among **120** residents (**150/133**); hospitalizations **94/78** events; deaths **33/32** events. Conclusion: no significant reduction in antibiotic administration.
