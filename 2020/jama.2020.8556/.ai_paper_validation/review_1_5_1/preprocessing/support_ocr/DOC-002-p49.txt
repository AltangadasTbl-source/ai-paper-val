1. INTRODUCTION

This ‘statistical analysis plan provides guidelines for the final presentation and analysis for the ,
PRINCESS trial. This plan, along with all other documents relating to the analysis of this trial, will be
stored in the Statistical Analysis Master File electronically and/or in hard signed copy formats.

2. BACKGROUND

2.1 RATIONALE AND RESEARCH QUESTION .

Care home residents (CHR) are prescribed far more antibiotics than the general population(1). In our
previous research in care homes, we found that CHR took antibiotics for an average of 17.4 days per
year(2). High antibiotic use increases the risk of reservoirs of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in care
homes that can spread within care homes and to hospitals and the community(1). Reviews of studies
found that probiotic reduced antibiotic use and risk of respiratory tract infections in adults(3) and
reduced antibiotic-associated diarrhoea(4). Probiotics also reduced carriage of AMR bacteria in a
review of critically ill patients, and have reduced duration of common infectious diseases(5) and
enhanced immune response, including to flu vaccination(6). However, research in CHR is currently
lacking.

2.2 OBJECTIVES

2.2.1 RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS —

The null hypothesis for our primary analysis is that there is no difference between care home residents
(CHR) given a daily probiotic for up to 12 months and those given a daily placebo for up to 12 months
in their. cumulative antibiotic administration days (CAAD) for infections. The alternative hypothesis is
that there is a difference between the two arms.
