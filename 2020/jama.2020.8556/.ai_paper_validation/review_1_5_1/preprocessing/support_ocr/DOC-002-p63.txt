Death

The proportion of participants who die will be compared between arms using a two-level logistic
regression model. The adjusted OR will be provided alongside 95% Ct and p-values. A two-level frailty
model will also be fitted to compare the time from randomisation to death between arms.

6.2.2 COVARIATE ADJUSTMENT

All analyses will adjust for gender of the participant.

6.2.3 ASSUMPTION CHECKING

Appropriate checking of the data and models used in the primary and secondary analyses will be
carried out.

For count outcomes that will be modelled using Poisson regression, negative binomial regression will
be used in the presence of over-dispersed count data.

6.2.4 ALTERNATIVE METHODS IF DISTRIBTIONAL ASSUMPTIONS NOT MET

Negative binomial regression will be used in the presence of over-dispersed count data.

Appropriate transformations will be used to assumptions of linear regression are fulfilled for the EQ-
5D-5L, EQ-VAS, and ICECAP-O mixed models. If transformation is not applicable (e.g. due to excess of
particular values, such as values of 1 for the EQ-5D-5L utility score), a two-part Hurdle model will be
fitted.

6.2.5 SENSITIVITY ANALYSES

Sensitivity analysis will be carried out by counting the number of hospital stays using a Poisson
regression model. Primary analysis will be rerun by including antibiotics that prescribed as prophylaxis
antibiotic in the CAAD outcome. Other sensitivity analyses were described within the analysis and
missing data.sections (see sections 6.2/1 and 6.3).

The impact on the primary analysis of ignoring periods of hospitalisation for which sufficient data are
not available to calculate CAAD during these periods (i.e. assumption 1 in Appendix III) will be
explored.
