## C001 — Person-day totals do not reconcile with stated mean follow-up days

- **Cited location found:** Yes. [jama_butler_2020_oi_200054.pdf — PDF p. 4](<../../../jama_butler_2020_oi_200054.pdf#page=4>), narrative below the participant-flow figure, left column. The same physical page also shows 155 randomized per arm and 152 probiotic versus 153 placebo participants included in the primary analysis.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. The probiotic arm is printed as 39,798 person-days and a mean (SD) number of days per probiotic participant of 252.4 (110.51). The placebo arm is printed as 37,974 person-days and mean days of 242.9 (115.24).
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** Yes. The page prints 155 randomized per arm and 152 probiotic versus 153 placebo participants included in the primary analysis. Both displayed denominator sets were checked.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes, conditional only on the printed person-day total and mean number of days per participant describing the same day definition and participant population. Under that condition, total person-days equal the arithmetic mean multiplied by the number of participants.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Using 155 per arm gives 39,798/155 = 256.7613 days and 37,974/155 = 244.9935 days, rather than 252.4 and 242.9. Using the printed primary-analysis counts gives 39,798/152 = 261.8289 and 37,974/153 = 248.1961. Conversely, 39,798/252.4 implies 157.6783 participants and 37,974/242.9 implies 156.3359 participants, both exceeding the 155 randomized in the respective arm. Rounding the printed one-decimal means cannot bridge these gaps.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The two totals, two displayed means, randomized counts, and primary-analysis counts are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The page does not state the exact participant set, day inclusion rule, treatment of unobserved days, or weighting rule used for each displayed mean; unrounded means are also not supplied.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The paragraph separately discusses observed and unobserved days, including truncation due to death and other missing-day causes. The totals and means may therefore use different day inclusion or weighting rules, although the page does not label such a distinction.
- **Direct observation:** The two totals, two means, and both denominator sets are printed on PDF page 4, and none of the displayed denominator calculations reproduces the printed means.
- **Inferred explanation:** A differing unreported day definition, participant set, weighting rule, or transcription step could produce the combination. The package does not identify which explanation applies.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which participants, day records, missing-day rules, and weighting convention define each person-day total and each mean, and which displayed quantity should be used when the total and mean are expected to describe the same arm population?

## C008 — CACE coefficient, confidence interval, and P value need reconciliation

- **Cited location found:** Yes. [joi200054supp2_prod.pdf — PDF p. 3](<../../../joi200054supp2_prod.pdf#page=3>), CACE method paragraph; [joi200054supp2_prod.pdf — PDF p. 5](<../../../joi200054supp2_prod.pdf#page=5>), eTable 2; and [joi200054supp1_prod.pdf — PDF p. 52](<../../../joi200054supp1_prod.pdf#page=52>), SAP section 4.1 on physical PDF page 52, internally labeled page 8 of 31. The SAP page is image-based, so its statement was checked visually against the PDF page.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. Supplement 2 page 3 states that a two-stage least-squares instrumental-variable regression was fitted with randomization as instrument, percentage of full or partial study product taken as exposure, sex adjustment, and cluster-robust standard errors, and that the coefficient and associated 95% CI were multiplied by 100 for presentation.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** Yes. Supplement 2 eTable 2 prints N = 305, adjusted coefficient 0.01, 95% CI -0.20 to 0.41, and P = .52. SAP page 52 states that all presented confidence intervals will be 95% and two-sided and all applicable tests will be two-sided at a 5% significance level.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** The direct midpoint and endpoint-distance checks are applicable as descriptions of the printed interval. Compatibility with the P value is mechanically testable only if the interval and P value use a common symmetric Wald statistic on the displayed linear coefficient scale. The package establishes linear multiplication by 100 and two-sided 95% inference, but it does not explicitly state the CACE CI construction, test statistic, degrees of freedom, or finite-sample correction.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The displayed interval midpoint is (-0.20 + 0.41)/2 = 0.105, not 0.01. The endpoint distances from 0.01 are 0.21 and 0.40. Under a symmetric normal-Wald diagnostic, the half-width is (0.41 - (-0.20))/2 = 0.305, implying SE approximately 0.305/1.96 = 0.1556. Centering that interval gives a diagnostic z of 0.105/0.1556 = 0.6748, while using the printed 0.01 gives z = 0.0643; these cannot be the same symmetric Wald coefficient/interval calculation. Multiplication of a coefficient and both endpoints by the positive constant 100 preserves symmetry and therefore does not itself explain the displaced midpoint. These are conditional diagnostics, not a reconstruction of an unreported analysis.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The displayed coefficient, endpoints, P value, N, two-sided 95% convention, model family, instrument, exposure definition, sex adjustment, cluster-robust standard-error statement, and presentation multiplier are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The exact unrounded coefficient and endpoints, coefficient standard error, cluster count used in this fit, degrees of freedom, small-sample or finite-cluster correction, CI construction, P-value test statistic, and whether the CI and P value came from the same model output are not supplied.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** A non-Wald or finite-sample interval, a separately constructed test, distinct unrounded outputs, or a transcription difference could yield an asymmetric printed combination. The use of cluster-robust standard errors makes a finite-sample implementation possible, but the package does not name one.
- **Direct observation:** The methods page prints the 2SLS model and ×100 presentation rule; eTable 2 prints 0.01 (-0.20 to 0.41), P = .52; and the SAP prints the two-sided 95% convention.
- **Inferred explanation:** The mismatch under a symmetric Wald reconstruction may reflect an unreported inferential method or a transcription issue. The supplied evidence does not permit choosing between those explanations or reconstructing the exact P value.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What are the unrounded coefficient, standard error, degrees of freedom, cluster correction, confidence-interval construction, and P-value test for this CACE row, and did the point estimate, interval, and P value all come from the same scaled model output?
