## Design, analysis population, and outcome definitions

- **Population/randomization:** 2331 eligible participants were randomized (painTRAINER 776, health coach 778, usual care plus 777); mean age 58.8 (SD 14.3) years, 1712 (74%) women, and 1030 (44%) rural/medically underserved. **Location:** PDF p1 / JAMA p592, Abstract—Results; PDF p4 / p595, Results—Baseline characteristics; Table 1 (PDF pp6-7 / pp597-598).
- **Primary outcome:** attaining/exceeding a 30% decrease from baseline in BPI-SF 11-item pain-severity score at 3 months; 6 and 12 months were secondary time points. **Location:** PDF p3 / p594, Main Outcomes and Measures—Primary Outcome.
- **Key secondary outcomes:** 30% improvement in BPI-SF pain intensity and pain-related interference; continuous change in pain severity, intensity, interference, PROMIS social-role and physical functioning, and patient global impression of change (PGIC), assessed at 3, 6, and 12 months. **Location:** PDF pp3-4 / pp594-595, Main Outcomes and Measures—Secondary Outcomes.

## Reported synthesis, safety, and subgroup claims

- **Authors' reported synthesis:** both intervention groups had significantly better secondary pain and functioning outcomes than usual care at 3 months, persisting over 12 months with modest attenuation; 3-month pain-severity standardized mean differences were -0.26 for painTRAINER and -0.36 for health coach vs usual care. **Location:** Results—Secondary Outcomes, PDF p7 / JAMA p598.
- **Health coach vs painTRAINER:** the article reports that the post-treatment difference was no longer significant at 6 and 12 months for the primary outcome. Table 2 RRs: 1.13 (0.98-1.30) and 1.07 (0.96-1.19), respectively. **Location:** Results—Primary Outcomes, PDF p7 / p598; Table 2, PDF p9 / p600.
- **Safety:** 183 participants (7.9%) had 247 hospitalizations and 17 (0.7%) died. Hospitalization: UC 63 participants/82 events (8.1%), painTRAINER 55/81 (7.1%), health coach 65/84 (8.4%). Death: UC 7 (0.9%), painTRAINER 3 (0.4%), health coach 7 (0.9%). The article states that no hospitalization/death was related or possibly related to study participation; participant-reported adverse events were health coach 16 (2.0%), painTRAINER 1 (0.1%), UC 0. **Location:** Results—Adverse Events, PDF p8 / JAMA p599.
- **Subgroup claims:** No subgroup-specific treatment-effect estimates, interaction tests, or subgroup efficacy claims were located in the main-article Results, Figures 1-2, or Tables 1-3 (PDF pp4-11 / JAMA pp595-602). The paper reports baseline rural/medically underserved residence (1030/2331 [44%]) and stratification by sex, baseline pain severity, site, and rural/medically underserved residence, but does not present main-article subgroup-effect results. **Location:** PDF pp3-4 / pp594-595; Table 1, PDF pp6-7 / pp597-598.
