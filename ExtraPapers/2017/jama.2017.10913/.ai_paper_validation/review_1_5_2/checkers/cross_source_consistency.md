# Cross-Source and Cross-Location Quantitative Consistency Review

## Scope and method

Complete review of the fresh DOC-001, DOC-002, and DOC-003 extraction maps and numeric/statistical relationship inventories. A value was compared only after matching the study identity, document role, population/analysis set, outcome or process measure, time anchor, contrast, model/adjustment, effect-measure scale, and displayed precision. Protocol plans, contextual literature values, and preliminary original-SSSP values were not treated as final SSSP-2 results. `PASS` means that the matched printed displays agree or that the apparent difference is explained by a printed role, population, time, model, or measure distinction; it is not a scientific adjudication.

## Provisional qualifying candidates

### CROSS-001 — Preliminary original-SSSP baseline-table arm headers do not reconcile with the stated total

**Category:** Denominator, proportion, or total inconsistency.

**Exact locations:** DOC-002, [PDF p. 9](../../../joi170091supp1_prod.pdf#page=9), narrative paragraph headed `SSSP study` and Table 2, `Baseline characteristics in SSSP participants` (N208-N210, N209, S214).

**Direct observation:** The narrative says the original SSSP had 89 enrolled participants and that primary-outcome data were available for 74. The immediately following Table 2 prints `Total n=76`, `SSSP n=36`, and `Control n=44`. The two arm headers sum to 80, not the printed total 76. Several printed percentages are additionally consistent with a control denominator of 40 rather than 44 (for example, 31 HIV-positive participants shown as 78%, and 27 confused participants shown as 68%).

**Comparison rule and calculation:** For a two-arm baseline table whose columns are labelled as a total and the two constituent arms, the stated total should equal the sum of its stated arm denominators: `36 + 44 = 80`, whereas the printed total is `76`. The percentage check is diagnostic only: `31/40 = 77.5%` and `27/40 = 67.5%`, which round to 78% and 68%; `31/44` and `27/44` do not.

**Inference, separated from observation:** At least one printed Table 2 denominator/header is inconsistent with the other printed headers. The supplied protocol does not state whether the table is a defined 76-person baseline subset, nor does it explain why the arm columns would then total 80.

**Source-grounded alternatives:** The table may contain a typographical control-arm denominator, a typographical total denominator, or a baseline subset definition missing from the table/narrative. The narrative's 89 enrolled and 74 primary-outcome values could describe different original-study stages and are not, by themselves, treated as contradictory to Table 2.

**Human verification question:** What were the intended original-SSSP Table 2 total and arm denominators, and was a 76-person baseline subset defined? Verify the source dataset or the original table proof, then confirm the correct displayed header(s) and any affected percentages.

### CROSS-002 — eTable 4 quartile-adjusted Cox model prints a P value that is not concordant with its displayed 95% CI under the table's apparent Wald-style presentation

**Category:** Statistical reporting inconsistency.

**Exact locations:** DOC-003, [PDF p. 9](../../../joi170091supp2_prod.pdf#page=9), eTable 4 final row, `Adjusted Cox proportional hazards model` with SAPS-3 categorized by quartile (N642/N644, S407); comparison context DOC-001 [Fig. 2, PDF p. 6](../../../jama_andrews_2017_oi_170091.pdf#page=6), S006, and DOC-002 [p. 15](../../../joi170091supp1_prod.pdf#page=15), S204.

**Direct observation:** The eTable prints HR 1.69, 95% CI 1.14-2.51, and P=.001 for the n=209 quartile-adjusted Cox model. The table labels the interval as 95% CI and labels the same row as a Cox proportional-hazards model; its footnote defines HR direction. DOC-001's Figure 2 supplies a distinct unadjusted log-rank P=.02 and does not duplicate the quartile-adjusted model.

**Comparison rule and diagnostic calculation:** When a two-sided model P value and a 95% Wald-type CI arise from the same coefficient and standard error, the CI implies an approximate standard error on the log-HR scale of `(ln(2.51)-ln(1.14))/(2*1.96) = 0.201`; `ln(1.69)/0.201` is approximately 2.61, corresponding to a two-sided P near .009. Conversely, P=.001 would ordinarily imply a CI materially farther from 1 than 1.14-2.51 for HR 1.69. This is a diagnostic consistency check, not a replacement analysis.

**Inference, separated from observation:** The printed P=.001 appears inconsistent with the printed HR and 95% CI if the row uses a common two-sided Wald-style coefficient/SE basis. Because the source does not provide the test type, sidedness, coefficient, or standard error, the mechanism cannot be determined from supplied materials.

**Source-grounded alternatives:** The P value may derive from a different test (for example, a model-level or likelihood-ratio test), the interval and P may have been generated under different parameterizations or precision, or one printed value may contain a transcription/formatting error. The unadjusted log-rank P=.02 is not an alternative value for this adjusted Cox model.

**Human verification question:** For the quartile-adjusted Cox row, do the printed HR, 95% CI, and P=.001 arise from the same coefficient/test and model parameterization? Retrieve the model output or analysis code and confirm the intended P value, interval, confidence level, and test.

## Limitations

This review used only supplied PDFs and their fresh local extraction assets. No raw data, statistical code, original original-SSSP dataset, Cox-model coefficients/standard errors, or test-specific P-value definitions were supplied. Consequently, CROSS-002 remains a provisional printed-result consistency observation, and no causal explanation or correction is asserted. No `P = 0` display occurred in the reviewed scope.
