## C001 — Talc-arm ECOG unknown percentage does not match the count and denominator

- **Cited location found:** Yes. [jama_thomas_2017_oi_170130.pdf](../../../jama_thomas_2017_oi_170130.pdf#page=4), PDF page 4, Table 1, `ECOG score, No. (%)`, talc pleurodesis column.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. The column header gives `n = 72`. The three talc ECOG cells are `53 (74)`, `14 (19)`, and `5 (17)` for scores 0-2, 3-4, and Unknown, respectively.
- **Comparator matched:** Yes. The talc-arm ECOG counts sum to the header denominator: `53 + 14 + 5 = 72`. The other talc-arm percentages reproduce whole-percent rounding: `100 x 53/72 = 73.611...`, displayed as 74, and `100 x 14/72 = 19.444...`, displayed as 19. The IPC companion cells also follow the same convention: `53/74`, `19/74`, and `2/74` display as 72%, 26%, and 3%.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. A cell under a `No. (%)` heading should pair its count with the percentage obtained from the applicable column denominator, subject to the table's demonstrated whole-percent rounding convention.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `100 x 5/72 = 6.944...%`, which rounds to 7%, whereas the source prints 17%. A percentage of 17% with a count of 5 would require an implied denominator near 29.4, and no such denominator is printed for this cell.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The printed count, column denominator, companion category counts, and table rounding convention are available. The participant-level ECOG data and table-production record are not supplied, so the intended count, percentage, and denominator cannot be distinguished mechanically.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The count `5` is supported by the within-column count total of 72, while an unprinted denominator or a different intended count remains possible. The supplied page does not select among these possibilities.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Direct observations are the header `n = 72`, the three printed counts and percentages, and the companion cells. The percentage calculation is a reproduced arithmetic comparison. Any claim about an extra digit, transcription, or a different underlying count is an inferred explanation, not stated by the source.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What count, denominator, and percentage were intended for the talc-arm ECOG Unknown cell in the table-production record?

## C002 — Final-protocol sample-size addition does not equal the printed total

- **Cited location found:** Yes. [joi170130supp1_prod.pdf](../../../joi170130supp1_prod.pdf#page=37), PDF page 37, AMPLE protocol Version 4 dated 05/05/2014, section 5, `SAMPLE SIZE CALCULATIONS`.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. The section states 62 patients in each group, a 20% lost-to-follow-up rate, an additional 24 patients, and a total recruitment target of 146.
- **Comparator matched:** Yes. The comparator is the total target printed in the same paragraph.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. When a whole-person total is described as two equal base groups plus an additional whole-person allowance, the printed components should add to the printed total.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The base is `62 x 2 = 124`; adding the stated 24 gives `124 + 24 = 148`, not 146. Conversely, target 146 minus base 124 leaves 22, not 24. Also, `20% x 124 = 24.8`; neither rounding 24.8 to 25 nor using the printed addition of 24 yields 146. If a 20% loss is instead treated as a fraction of the recruited total, `124/0.80 = 155`, also not 146.
- **Necessary inputs available:** All inputs needed to test the printed addition are available. The power-analysis output, intended attrition-inflation convention, rounding decision, and table or document production record are not supplied.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** One of the displayed base size, added-person allowance, attrition percentage, or target may reflect a different calculation stage. The page itself does not identify such stages or reconcile the components.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Direct observations are the four printed quantities and the protocol version/date. The additions, subtraction, and percentage calculations are reproduced arithmetic. A typographical or version-control explanation would be inferred because the source does not state one.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What complete sample-size calculation, including the attrition convention and rounding rule, produced the intended final-protocol recruitment target?

## C003 — Final protocol and SAP/main article give different sample-size inputs for the same target

- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. Protocol page 37 prints 62 per group, 20% lost to follow-up, an additional 24, and target 146. SAP page 62 prints 65 per group, 12% lost to follow-up, 73 per group, and target 146. Main-article page 3 prints 65 per group, 12% lost to follow-up, and target 146.
- **Comparator matched:** Yes. The SAP and main article agree with each other on 65 base participants per group, 12% attrition, and target 146; those inputs differ from the packaged protocol's 62 per group and 20%/24 allowance while retaining the same target.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. Numeric planning inputs presented as the basis for the same trial recruitment target should either agree or be linked by supplied version/amendment information that identifies which calculation superseded the other.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Protocol basis: `62 x 2 = 124`, with the stated addition yielding 148 rather than 146. SAP/main basis: target 146 equals `73 x 2`; increasing 65 to 73 adds 8 per arm, or 12.3077% of 65. The two source sets therefore use different base sizes (`62` versus `65`) and attrition descriptions (`20%`/24 total versus `12%`/8 per arm) for the same target 146.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The compared printed parameters, target, document titles, and dates/versions are available. The protocol change summary says that a sample-size-calculation section was added in Version 4 ([joi170130supp1_prod.pdf](../../../joi170130supp1_prod.pdf#page=51), PDF page 51), and the SAP cover identifies Version 1 dated 11/01/2015 ([page 52](../../../joi170130supp1_prod.pdf#page=52)). Missing are an operative amendment or approval record that explicitly replaces the Version 4 numbers, a dated calculation record, and documentation of which parameter set governed recruitment.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The later-dated SAP and the main article may present a deliberate revised planning basis while the packaged protocol preserves an earlier basis. The chronology supports that possibility, but the supplied change summary only describes adding the protocol section and does not explicitly connect the two numeric parameter sets.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Direct observations are the printed parameters, shared target, document versions/dates, and change-summary language. The arithmetic comparison is reproduced. Supersession or use of one calculation in actual recruitment is inferred unless supported by an unprovided amendment or approval record.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which approved parameter set governed recruitment, and where is the supplied-source link or amendment that explains the change from 62 per group with a 20%/24 allowance to 65 per group with a 12% allowance?

## C004 — SAP ITT definition conflicts with the reported 144-patient ITT denominator

- **Cited locations found:** Yes. The SAP definition begins in [joi170130supp1_prod.pdf](../../../joi170130supp1_prod.pdf#page=62), PDF page 62, section 4.2, and continues on [page 63](../../../joi170130supp1_prod.pdf#page=63). - **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. SAP page 62 defines ITT as all randomized subjects, including those lost to follow-up or with missing data, and states that a patient who did not receive the assigned post-randomization intervention remains in ITT according to randomized group. Main page 3 prints 146 randomized (`74 + 72`), one pre-treatment withdrawal in each arm, and primary-analysis counts of 73 and 71. Main page 4 states that the two pre-intervention withdrawals were excluded from all analyses and calls the remaining 144 patients the ITT analyses. Table 2 labels `n = 73` and `n = 71` as ITT.
- **Comparator matched:** Yes. The two excluded participants shown in the flow diagram are the one-per-arm pre-intervention withdrawals described in the Results text, and `73 + 71 = 144`, two fewer than the 146 randomized participants covered by the SAP's printed ITT definition.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. Applying the supplied SAP's explicit population definition to the supplied randomization counts places all 146 randomized participants in ITT, including randomized participants who did not receive the assigned intervention.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Randomized total: `74 + 72 = 146`. Reported ITT total: `73 + 71 = 144`. Difference: `146 - 144 = 2`, matching the one excluded pre-intervention withdrawal in each arm. The printed SAP definition does not state an exception for these two participants.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The SAP definition, treatment-receipt clause, randomized counts, exclusion reason, Results population label, and Table 2 denominators are available. Missing are any later SAP amendment, prespecified modified-ITT definition, consent/data-use instruction applicable to the withdrawals, participant-level availability record, and analysis-program population flag.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The reported analysis could reflect an unstated modified-ITT convention, a later amendment, or inability/authorization not to use data after withdrawal. None of those explanations is named in the supplied pages.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Direct observations are the SAP text, flow counts, exclusion text, ITT label, and Table 2 denominators. The population-size comparison is a reproduced logical and arithmetic application of the SAP definition. A modified convention, later amendment, or withdrawal-related data restriction is an inferred explanation.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Was there an operative prespecified amendment or participant-withdrawal rule authorizing exclusion of these two randomized participants from every ITT analysis, and, if so, where is its exact definition and effective date?

## C005 — Estimated-difference contrast direction is unlabeled and reverses between the main and MI tables

- **Cited locations found:** Yes. The main outcome table is in [jama_thomas_2017_oi_170130.pdf](../../../jama_thomas_2017_oi_170130.pdf#page=6), PDF page 6, Table 2. The multiple-imputation eTable is in [joi170130supp2_prod.pdf](../../../joi170130supp2_prod.pdf#page=2), PDF page 2.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. Both tables order IPC before talc and head the signed column `Estimated Difference (95% CI)` without naming the subtraction order. Main Table 2 prints dyspnea baseline `50.0` versus `52.2` with `2.27`, and day 1 `64.5` versus `69.7` with `5.25`. The MI eTable prints baseline `49.8` versus `51.9` with `-2.06`, and day 1 `65.5` versus `71.7` with `-6.19`.
- **Comparator matched:** Yes. The main table footnote describes differences in estimated means but does not name a reference group or subtraction order. The MI footnote describes estimates from 20 multiply imputed data sets but likewise does not name the direction.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. A signed between-group difference requires an identified reference/contrast direction when the sign cannot otherwise be interpreted unambiguously across related tables.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Main baseline: talc minus IPC is `52.2 - 50.0 = +2.2`, consistent in sign and rounding proximity with `+2.27`; main day 1 is `69.7 - 64.5 = +5.2`, consistent with `+5.25`. MI baseline: IPC minus talc is `49.8 - 51.9 = -2.1`, consistent with `-2.06`; MI day 1 is `65.5 - 71.7 = -6.2`, consistent with `-6.19`. Thus the displayed main rows imply talc minus IPC, while the displayed MI rows imply IPC minus talc.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The group order, displayed estimates, signed differences, confidence intervals, and footnotes are available and are sufficient to infer the sign direction from multiple rows. Exact model coefficients, unrounded estimated means, contrast coding/reference levels, analysis code, and table-production specifications are not supplied; therefore exact equality between rounded group means and model-based contrasts is not required or testable.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** Opposite parameterizations may have been intentionally used for the primary and MI models, and MI can change the displayed group estimates. Those facts can explain magnitude differences, but neither footnote labels the opposite contrast directions reconstructed from the signs.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Direct observations are the table order, values, signed estimates, and footnote wording. The subtractions are reproduced arithmetic showing the implied direction. Intentional model parameterization or a production-stage sign choice is inferred because neither source states it.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What reference group and subtraction order were specified for each table, and were the opposite signed contrast directions intentional?
