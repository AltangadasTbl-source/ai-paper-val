# Numeric Consistency Review

## Scope and method

This checker independently reviewed every canonical numeric/reporting relationship `N001` through `N071` in `relationships/numeric_relationship_inventory.md`. It used the mapper evidence only as a locator and checked the printed inputs against the supplied main article and supplement PDFs. Checks applied where relevant were arithmetic, totals, numerator/denominator/percentage reconciliation, missingness and population identity, rounding, subgroup/category sums, unit and scale labels, rate-versus-count distinctions, repeated values, and matched main/supplement values. Participant-flow observations were retained only where they could produce a concrete numeric contradiction.

For percentages printed to one decimal place, the reconciliation tolerance is plus or minus 0.05 percentage points; for means printed to one decimal place, it is plus or minus 0.05 unit before multiplication by a stated integer denominator. Values presented as model-based adjusted estimates, rates, or explicitly labelled absolute differences were not forced to equal differences of rounded descriptive values. Different signs in main-text ARDs and supplement-table differences were treated as potentially different stated comparison conventions unless the printed values themselves failed to reconcile.

No stable candidate IDs, severity, validity, or disposition is assigned here. The provisional keys below are checker-local proposal keys only.

## Provisional candidate proposals

### NC-N010-01 — Reported person-day totals do not reconcile with the stated mean days per participant

- **Category:** Denominator, proportion, or total inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** [jama_butler_2020_oi_200054.pdf — PDF p. 4](<../../../jama_butler_2020_oi_200054.pdf#page=4>), person-time paragraph and enrollment figure; relationship `N010`.
- **Printed inputs:** The paragraph reports 39,798 probiotic person-days and a mean of 252.4 days per probiotic participant; it reports 37,974 placebo person-days and a mean of 242.9 days per placebo participant. The figure on the same page reports 155 randomized participants in each arm; Table 2 reports 152 and 153 participants with primary-outcome data.
- **Rule and calculation:** A reported total person-days and stated mean per participant must reconcile when multiplied/divided by the named participant denominator. Using the explicitly randomized denominators: 39,798 / 155 = 256.761, which rounds to 256.8 rather than 252.4; 37,974 / 155 = 244.994, which rounds to 245.0 rather than 242.9. Using the available primary-analysis denominators also does not reconcile: 39,798 / 152 = 261.829 and 37,974 / 153 = 248.196.
- **Tolerance:** A one-decimal mean permits plus or minus 0.05 day before multiplication: at n=155, plus or minus 7.75 total days. The total discrepancies are 676.0 and 324.5 days, respectively, far outside that tolerance.
- **Direct observation versus inference:** Direct observation is the four printed totals/means and the printed arm denominators. The nonreconciliation is derived arithmetic; no unprinted denominator is assumed.
- **Alternative source-grounded interpretations:** The prose may use an unstated person-level denominator or a differently filtered set of days for its mean, while the total includes a broader set. Neither p. 4 nor the Table 2 data line names such a denominator.
- **Quality-control relevance:** A reader may use the reported person-time and per-participant follow-up to assess exposure, missingness, or rates. A discordance can propagate to extracted person-time denominators.
- **Exact human question:** Which participants and which day records were used for each stated mean, and should either the person-day totals, the means, or their population label be corrected?

### NC-N011-01 — Dose-method percentage does not match its printed numerator and denominator

- **Category:** Denominator, proportion, or total inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** [jama_butler_2020_oi_200054.pdf — PDF p. 4](<../../../jama_butler_2020_oi_200054.pdf#page=4>), “Intervention Fidelity” paragraph; relationship `N011`.
- **Printed inputs:** “89.4% (68 356/73 302)” doses were swallowed as capsules or sprinkled on food. The two remaining stated methods are 4.4% (3258/73,302) and 2.3% (1688/73,302).
- **Rule and calculation:** A percentage attached to an explicit fraction must equal that fraction to displayed precision. 68,356 / 73,302 × 100 = 93.2526%, which rounds to 93.3%, not 89.4%. The three counts sum to 73,302, and the other two stated percentages reconcile after rounding.
- **Tolerance:** One decimal percentage: plus or minus 0.05 percentage point. The discrepancy is 3.8539 percentage points.
- **Direct observation versus inference:** The fraction and percentage are direct observations; the division is a reproducible arithmetic check.
- **Alternative source-grounded interpretations:** The percentage may have been calculated with a different denominator, but the parenthetical fraction explicitly supplies 73,302 and all three method counts partition that total.
- **Quality-control relevance:** Fidelity and route-of-administration data may be abstracted as adherence or intervention-delivery measures.
- **Exact human question:** Is 89.4% a transcription error, or is 68,356/73,302 not the intended fraction for the reported administration-method percentage?

### NC-N011-02 — Main-text adherence median is below its printed lower IQR bound and conflicts with the supplement restatement

- **Category:** Numeric or arithmetic inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** [jama_butler_2020_oi_200054.pdf — PDF p. 4](<../../../jama_butler_2020_oi_200054.pdf#page=4>), “Intervention Fidelity” paragraph; [joi200054supp2_prod.pdf — PDF p. 5](<../../../joi200054supp2_prod.pdf#page=5>), eTable 2 introductory text; relationship `N011`.
- **Printed inputs:** The main text reports a median of 93.3% with IQR 93.56% to 99.45%. Supplement eTable 2 reports median adherence 97.8% with the same IQR, 93.56% to 99.45%.
- **Rule and calculation:** For a conventional IQR, the lower quartile cannot exceed the median. Even allowing display precision, main-text median 93.3% has interval [93.25, 93.35), whereas lower IQR bound 93.56% has interval [93.555, 93.565); the intervals do not overlap. The supplement’s 97.8% is within the same reported IQR and differs from the main-text median by 4.5 percentage points.
- **Tolerance:** Plus or minus 0.05 percentage point for the one-decimal median and plus or minus 0.005 percentage point for the two-decimal IQR bound; no rounding-compatible ordering exists.
- **Direct observation versus inference:** The two printed medians and shared IQR are direct observations. IQR ordering and comparison are derived diagnostic checks.
- **Alternative source-grounded interpretations:** The main-text 93.3% could be a typographical transposition, or the two locations could refer to different adherence summaries; both locations describe full or partial doses among the high-adherence analysis population and print the identical IQR.
- **Quality-control relevance:** The adherence summary supports the CACE interpretation and may be copied into intervention-fidelity evidence extraction.
- **Exact human question:** Which median adherence percentage is correct, and do both locations describe the same dose-summary population and calculation?

### NC-N014-01 — Nonprophylactic-antibiotic percentages do not match the printed arm fractions or overall total

- **Category:** Denominator, proportion, or total inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** [jama_butler_2020_oi_200054.pdf — PDF p. 5](<../../../jama_butler_2020_oi_200054.pdf#page=5>), paragraph immediately before “Primary Outcome”; relationship `N014`.
- **Printed inputs:** The article reports 202 (66.2%) residents prescribed at least one nonprophylactic antibiotic, comprising 63.4% (97/155) in probiotic and 69.1% (105/155) in placebo.
- **Rule and calculation:** Percentages attached to explicit fractions and the stated total must reconcile. 97/155 = 62.5806% (62.6% to one decimal), not 63.4%; 105/155 = 67.7419% (67.7%), not 69.1%; 202/310 = 65.1613% (65.2%), not 66.2%. The counts nevertheless sum, 97 + 105 = 202.
- **Tolerance:** Plus or minus 0.05 percentage point at one decimal. The arm discrepancies are 0.8194 and 1.3581 percentage points; the overall discrepancy is 1.0387 percentage points.
- **Direct observation versus inference:** The counts, denominators, and percentages are direct observations; the divisions and count sum are arithmetic checks.
- **Alternative source-grounded interpretations:** The percentages approximately match alternate denominators of 153 (97/153 = 63.4%) and 152 (105/152 = 69.1%), suggesting that percentages may have used outcome-data populations while the printed fractions retained randomized denominators. That possibility does not reconcile the stated 202 (66.2%) total without another unstated denominator.
- **Quality-control relevance:** This is a descriptive antibiotic-exposure result; mismatched fractions can affect baseline event-frequency or safety/outcome data extraction.
- **Exact human question:** Were the percentages calculated using 153 and 152 outcome-data participants rather than the printed 155/155 denominators, and what denominator supports the reported overall 66.2%?

### NC-N041-01 — Three-month oral-candidiasis ARD does not reconcile with the printed arm proportions or the matched supplement difference

- **Category:** Cross-document numeric inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** [jama_butler_2020_oi_200054.pdf — PDF p. 7](<../../../jama_butler_2020_oi_200054.pdf#page=7>), oral-candidiasis narrative; [joi200054supp2_prod.pdf — PDF p. 8](<../../../joi200054supp2_prod.pdf#page=8>), eTable 5 “Presence of oral Candidiasis,” three-month row; relationship `N041`.
- **Printed inputs:** Main text: 88/113 (77.9%) probiotic versus 80/105 (76.2%) placebo, “ARD, −0.2% (95% CI, −11.3% to 10.9%).” Supplement table: the same 88/113 (77.9%) versus 80/105 (76.2%) and absolute difference 0.02 (−0.10 to 0.13).
- **Rule and calculation:** The printed proportions give probiotic minus placebo = 77.8761% − 76.1905% = +1.6856 percentage points, which rounds to +1.7 percentage points (or +0.02 as a proportion). Reversing comparison order gives −1.6856 percentage points. Neither comparison direction rounds to the main text’s −0.2%. The supplement’s 0.02 is compatible with the printed fractions at its stated precision.
- **Tolerance:** The main ARD is printed to one decimal percentage point, permitting plus or minus 0.05 percentage point. The closest raw comparison-direction value is −1.6856%, 1.4856 percentage points away.
- **Direct observation versus inference:** Both locations’ counts, percentages, and differences are direct observations. Fraction subtraction and comparison-direction reversal are derived checks.
- **Alternative source-grounded interpretations:** The main-text ARD might be a separately adjusted risk-difference estimator rather than the displayed crude difference, but neither cited location labels it adjusted; the supplement explicitly presents the matched fractions and difference. A transcription/decimal error is another possibility.
- **Quality-control relevance:** The numerical effect direction and magnitude for a microbiology outcome could be copied into evidence tables or systematic-review extraction.
- **Exact human question:** Does the main-text −0.2% ARD represent a separately adjusted estimator with an unstated method, or should it be replaced by a value consistent with the printed three-month arm proportions and the supplement table?

## Summary and limitations

All 71 assigned N relationships received explicit coverage. Five distinct provisional candidate proposals were emitted: one in `N010`, two in `N011`, one in `N014`, and one in `N041`. This checker did not assess inferential-statistical compatibility beyond the numeric/reporting checks within scope and did not treat coherent finite-precision P-value displays as candidates. Some differences use model-based estimates or unstated comparison conventions; those were not called candidates unless direct printed fractions or values failed to reconcile under every stated comparison direction.
