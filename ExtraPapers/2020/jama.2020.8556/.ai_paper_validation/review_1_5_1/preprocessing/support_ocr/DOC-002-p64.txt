. 6.3 MISSING DATA
By including the time at risk as an offset variable in our primary analysis, this will automatically account
for missing data.
It is likely that the majority of missing data will be due to drop-out, with reasons for dropout falling
into two broad categories, withdrawal from the trial and death. An examination of the distribution
and amount of missing between trial arms, by care home and reason will be tabulated.
A comparison will be made using baseline variables between those for which primary outcome and
selected secondary outcome (depending on the volume of missing) data is and is not availabie.
Appropriate modelling techniques will be used (e.g. likelinood-based methods or multiple
imputation). Sensitivity analysis will be conducted using joint modelling approaches (e.g. selection
and/or pattern mixture models) to explore departures from the missing at random assumption.
For additional sensitivity analyses, a comparison between participant death during an infection and
death from other cause will be explored. An approach which is valid under a missing at random
assumption will be applied to the deaths from any other cause and take a more extreme approach
with those that died during an infection (e.g. assume that they would have remained on antibiotics
. for the remainder of the follow-up period).

Similarly, we will explore stratifying drop-out due to residents moving to another care facility into two
groups: those who move for health-related reasons (e.g. from a residential home to a care home
providing nursing care) and those who move for other reasons. A missing at random-valid modelling
approach will be applied to the latter strata, with a more extreme approach taken for those dropping
out due to moving from the care home for health-related reasons as this represents a decline in health,
which may also be subject to unmeasured selection bias.
