6.4 ADDITIONAL ANALYSES
6.4.1 ADHERENCE-ADJUSTED ANALYSIS
Structural mean models will be fitted in order to obtain adherence-adjusted estimates of the primary
and secondary analyses. Depending on the type of outcome, these models will be fitted using the
generalised methods of moments framework (for generalised linear models) or two-stage least
squares procedure (for linear models).
Two definitions of study product adherence will be used for this analysis:
e Initiation — as described in 4.2.2
e Percentage of days that participants took their study product as prescribed (with those not initiating
set to zero)
The model will include gender as a stratification variable and use robust standard errors to account for
clustering effects by care homes.
6.4.2 DAILY STUDY PRODUCT IMPLEMENTATION OVER TIME .
A three-level logistic regression model (responses nested within participants within care homes) will
be fitted to daily study product use (study product taken / study product not taken). Time will be
included as a covariate, with non-linear patterns of adherence over time accounted for using splines,
if necessary.
6.4.3 ASSESSMENT OF BLIND BEING BROKEN
At the final follow-up, care home staff will be asked what trial arm they think the participant is in
(active, placebo, or no opinion). The agreement between response from care home staff and actual
allocation will be assessed using James’ blinding index. (13) Summary statistics will be described with
respect to overall, trial arm and care home.
6.4.4 METHOD OF INGESTION
The method of ingestion (swallowed with water, sprinkled on food and sprinkled on drink) will be
collected over the 12 months study period. Frequency of each method will be reported by overall and
split by care home.
