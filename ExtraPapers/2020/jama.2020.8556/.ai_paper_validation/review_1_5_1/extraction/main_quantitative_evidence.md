## Page mapping

### PDF p. 1 — Abstract

The randomized population was 310 residents from 23 UK care homes, recruited December 2016-May 2018; last follow-up was October 31, 2018. Arms were probiotic 155 and placebo 155, up to one year; probiotic capsule total cell count was 1.3 x 10^10 to 1.6 x 10^10. Mean age was 85.3 years; 66.8% were women; 195 (62.9%) were alive and completed trial. Diaries were available for 98.7% and 97.4% of randomized probiotic and placebo residents. Primary means were 12.9 (95% CI 0 to 18.05) and 12.0 (0 to 16.95) days; absolute difference 0.9 days (−3.25 to 5.05), adjusted IRR 1.13 (0.79 to 1.63), P=.50. A total of 120 residents had 283 adverse events (150 probiotic; 133 placebo), including hospitalizations 94 and 78 and deaths 33 and 32, respectively. This supplies narrative matches for local N001-N004 and S001.

### PDF p. 2 — Design and procedures

The study was an individually randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind multicenter trial in UK care homes (December 2016-May 2018). Eligibility age was at least 65 years. Allocation was online 1:1 minimization by care home and sex with an 80% random component. EQ-5D and ICECAP-O collection was baseline, three months, and 12 months/as near as possible; the defined scales are recorded on p. 3 and Table 3. No additional result result-values are reported on this page.

### PDF p. 3 — Outcome definitions, analysis, and participant narrative

Primary outcome: total systemic-antibiotic administration days divided by total observed participant days. Secondary outcome families include infection-specific antibiotic days, infection count/site/duration, diarrhea, microbiology, EQ-5D-5L (index −0.594 to 1; health status 0-100), ICECAP-O (0-1), hospital stays/days, and deaths. Planned sample: 330 from 20 homes, 90% power at 5% to detect a 10% reduction from 17.4 to 15.6 antibiotic days/resident-year; 30% with no outcome data was allowed, target later adjusted after 33 participants to at least 258; assumed outcome availability about 250 days and at least 82% power. Primary comparison: 2-level negative-binomial regression, with residents nested in homes and accounting for observed time and sex. Most rate secondary outcomes used 2-level Poisson or negative-binomial models; all analyses used two-sided 95% CIs and P values, P<.05 significance threshold.

Results narrative: 310 (155/arm) residents from 23 homes; 106 had truncated follow-up (147-362 days). Of 199 eligible for second follow-up, 195 (98.0%; 98/97) responded. This supplies N005-N009 and the model/context for all S relationships.

### PDF p. 8 — C difficile, adverse events, and results claims in discussion

At three months, 7/107 samples (6.5%) were C difficile positive: 6/55 (10.9%) vs 1/52 (1.9%), ARD −9.0% (−18.4% to 0.4%), AOR 6.51 (0.75-56.57), P=.09. At second follow-up, 2/64 (3.1%) were positive, both probiotic. No subgroup effect was statistically significant (details in supplement). Adverse events: 120 residents, 283 events, 150 vs 133; hospitalizations 94 vs 78; deaths 33 vs 32. Three trial-related events, all placebo. Discussion makes matching narrative claims: no significant primary reduction; 1.3% eligible study days missing; more probiotic organisms and higher counts in probiotic arm; significantly more lower-respiratory antibiotics, lower self-reported capability at three months, and a significant sensitivity-analysis increase, all cautioned for multiple testing.
