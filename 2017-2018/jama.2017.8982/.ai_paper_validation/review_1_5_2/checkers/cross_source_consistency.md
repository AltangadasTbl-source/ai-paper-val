# Cross-Source Consistency Review

## Scope and method

Fresh-source cross-document review of all result-relevant comparable material in DOC-001 (main article, PDF pp. 1--9), DOC-002 (trial protocol, PDF pp. 1--21), and DOC-003 (results supplement, PDF pp. 1--12). I used the fresh source-matched extraction maps and the main/support numeric and statistical relationship registers, then checked the supplied PDFs at the cited locations where an exact comparator was needed. A result was compared only after matching its population, time window, contrast, model/analysis set, measure, scale, unit, reference group, direction, and displayed precision.

`MATCH` means the printed values/definitions agree to displayed precision. `NO DIRECT MATCH` means the material is planned, a different analysis population, a different contrast, or lacks a same-result comparator; it is not a finding. `NOT APPLICABLE` means the unit has no trial result or definition that can be matched.

## Provisional cross-source candidates

### CROSS-CAND-001 — Control room-sharing percentage differs for the same printed count and denominator

- **Category:** Cross-document numeric inconsistency.
- **Exact linked locations:** [DOC-001, Table 3, p7](../../../jama_moon_2017_oi_170077.pdf#page=7); [DOC-003, eTable 5, p9](../../../joi170077supp2_prod.pdf#page=9).
- **Direct observation:** In the BF NQI/BF mHealth control cell for usual room sharing without bed sharing, DOC-001 Table 3 prints **205/291 (70.4%)**. DOC-003 eTable 5 prints the same all-race control cell as **N=291, 205 (70.5%)**.
- **Match rule:** Both locations identify the same raw control group, outcome, all-race population, infant age threshold (>=60 days in Table 3/eTable 5), numerator 205, denominator 291, and one-decimal percentage display. Thus no population, time, contrast, model, measure, or precision distinction accounts for the 0.1-percentage-point difference.
- **Diagnostic calculation:** 205 / 291 x 100 = 70.4467...%; conventional nearest-one-decimal rounding yields 70.4%, but the candidate is the supplied-source display conflict, not a prescribed rounding convention.
- **Supported alternative explanation:** One table may have used a different rounding/export convention or contains a transcription/formatting discrepancy; neither source states a distinct denominator or analysis set.
- **Human verification question:** Which percentage was intended for the all-race BF/BF control room-sharing row, and were the two tables generated from the identical analysis extract and rounding rule?

### CROSS-CAND-002 — Aggregate 8--11-week response-age percentage differs despite identical counts

- **Category:** Cross-document numeric inconsistency.
- **Exact linked locations:** [DOC-001, Table 1, p5](../../../jama_moon_2017_oi_170077.pdf#page=5); [DOC-001, discussion, p8](../../../jama_moon_2017_oi_170077.pdf#page=8); [DOC-003, eTable 2, pp. 3--4](../../../joi170077supp2_prod.pdf#page=3).
- **Direct observation:** DOC-001 Table 1 provides four treatment-group 8--11-week counts of **205, 214, 262, and 236**, totaling **917** among **1,263** respondents. DOC-001 p8 states that **72.6%** of responses occurred at age 8--12 weeks. DOC-003 eTable 2 prints the same 8--11-week count and respondent denominator as **917 (72.7%)**.
- **Match rule:** The article discussion and eTable 2 refer to the aggregate respondent follow-up-age distribution. The Table 1 counts independently identify the same numerator/denominator. The labels “8--12 weeks” and the 8--11-week category are not proven strictly identical at their endpoint, so the candidate is limited to the directly shared 917/1,263 aggregate percentage between Table 1/eTable 2 and the article's reported aggregate proportion; it does not assume a different age population.
- **Diagnostic calculation:** 917 / 1,263 x 100 = 72.6049...%. Both 72.6% and 72.7% are printed at one decimal in supplied displays; the issue is the inconsistent displayed rounding for the same counted distribution.
- **Supported alternative explanation:** Different rounding/export settings or an unreported denominator/age-bin convention could explain the difference. The supplied sources do not state such a convention.
- **Human verification question:** Was the aggregate 8--11-week/8--12-week proportion computed from exactly 917 of 1,263 respondents, and which one-decimal value and age-bin label should appear consistently?

### CROSS-CAND-003 — Supplement figure uses a strict age threshold where its linked table uses an inclusive threshold

- **Category:** Measure, label, or scale inconsistency.
- **Exact linked locations:** [DOC-003, eTable 5, pp. 9--10](../../../joi170077supp2_prod.pdf#page=9); [DOC-003, eFigure, p11](../../../joi170077supp2_prod.pdf#page=11).
- **Direct observation:** eTable 5 title states the race/ethnicity outcomes are reported “when infant was **>=60 days** of age.” The eFigure title, which displays the same control/combined-intervention race-stratified outcomes and directs the reader to eTable 5 for sample sizes, instead says “when infant was **>60 days** of age.”
- **Match rule:** The eFigure and eTable 5 identify the same outcome set, intervention groups, race/ethnicity presentation, and post-hoc display. `>=60 days` includes infants exactly 60 days old; `>60 days` excludes them. This is a concrete population/time-boundary label conflict even though no participant count at exactly 60 days is printed.
- **Supported alternative explanation:** The figure title may use “>60” as informal shorthand, or no analysed infant was exactly 60 days old. Neither possibility resolves the displayed inclusive-versus-strict difference from supplied evidence.
- **Human verification question:** What was the actual age eligibility rule for the eFigure data, were any records at exactly 60 days included, and should both titles use the same threshold notation?

## Coverage conclusion and limitations

- **Comparable match keys/scopes checked:** 16 coverage rows above, encompassing all 43 main numeric records (N001--N043), all 21 support numeric records (N1001--N1021), all 17 main statistical records (S001--S017), and all 12 support statistical records (S1001--S1012), with source-unit no-match/not-applicable coverage recorded where no same-result comparator exists.
- **Provisional candidate count:** 3 (`CROSS-CAND-001` through `CROSS-CAND-003`). These are provisional quality-control observations only and require human adjudication.
- **Limitations:** The protocol is a prespecified planning document, so planned values/procedures were not treated as contradictory to final observed results without a matched final claim. Complete-case and imputation analyses were not compared as identical populations. The available sources do not give the number of infants exactly 60 days old or the unrounded aggregate age percentage, so those facts remain human-verification questions rather than inferred corrections.
