63   A2. Social marketing techniques and culturally competent approaches in health
64   promotion.
65       Social marketing is the use of commercial marketing concepts to design and implement
66   programs to effect social change and is guided by the following principles: 1) the ultimate
67   objective is to influence action; 2) action occurs when the target population perceives the
68   benefits to outweigh the costs of the action; 3) programs are more effective when based on an
69   understanding of the target population’s perceptions; 4) target populations are rarely uniform in
70   perceptions and/or responses to social marketing efforts; 5) there are always behaviors that
71   compete with recommended behaviors, and these must be understood and addressed; and 6)
72   because of constant changes in the “marketplace,” there must be frequent monitoring in order to
73   rapidly alter strategies as needed.32 Many health campaigns, however, have a one-size-fits-all
74   approach, and thus are not necessarily effective in reaching all targeted audiences. Our goal is
75   to develop educational messages for nursery staff and mothers that maximize social marketing
76   techniques.
77       Furthermore, culturally and linguistically competent health promotion approaches respect
78   cultural values, beliefs and practices of the target population; therefore, health promotion
79   messages should ideally reflect these health beliefs and practices. This is often a challenge, as
80   some of those beliefs and practices may be inconsistent with emerging knowledge of behaviors
81   that support healthy outcomes. Culturally competent health promotion supports and honors
82   those practices and beliefs that are protective or benign, and respectfully helps to identify and
83   change those beliefs and practices that have a negative health impact.33 Our interventions will
84   incorporate techniques to favorably influence infant care practices, while respecting parental
85   beliefs.
86   A3. Rationale for interventions during the postpartum hospital stay.
87       Studies, including our own, have shown that the postpartum hospital stay provides a unique
88   venue for intervention for several reasons.34, 35 First, the birth of a child results in a change in the
89   family dynamic that makes parents and family members more receptive to new information.
90   Second, the postpartum hospital stay is a time when information can be delivered over several
91   days and through multiple sources. Third, role modeling is a powerful tool and mothers often
92   model what they learn in the nurseries.36, 37 Fourth, since mothers often receive visitors,
93   education can be directed to family and friends. Our research shows that family and friends can
94   play a key role in influencing infant care practices at home27, 28 Finally, since virtually all
95   newborns are born in the hospital, the newborn nursery provides access to mothers who might
96   otherwise be inaccessible for intervention.
