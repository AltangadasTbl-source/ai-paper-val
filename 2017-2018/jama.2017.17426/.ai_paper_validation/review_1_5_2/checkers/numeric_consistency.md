## Candidate proposition: incorrect talc-arm ECOG unknown percentage

**Exact source location:** `jama_thomas_2017_oi_170130.pdf#page=4`, Table 1, row “ECOG score, No. (%)”, “Unknown,” talc pleurodesis column (`n = 72`). The rendered fresh page `preprocessing/rendered_pages/DOC-001-p4.png` visually confirms the printed entry `5 (17)`.

**Printed inputs:** Table 1 identifies the talc randomized denominator as `n = 72`, and its ECOG rows as 0-2 `53 (74)`, 3-4 `14 (19)`, and Unknown `5 (17)`. The counts total `53 + 14 + 5 = 72`.

**Reproducible rule and calculation:** for a `No. (%)` row, percentage = `100 × count / stated column denominator`, with ordinary nearest-whole-percent rounding tolerance ±0.5 percentage point. Here, `100 × 5 / 72 = 6.944...%`, which rounds to **7%**, not 17%. The printed 17% is 10.06 percentage points from the exact value, outside tolerance. The companion talc rows validate the denominator/rounding convention: `53/72=73.61% → 74%` and `14/72=19.44% → 19%`.

**Observation/inference and alternatives:** Direct observation is the printed denominator, counts, and `5 (17)` entry. The contradiction is an arithmetic inference from those printed values. It could be a typographic digit transposition (`7` rendered/printed as `17`) or one of the count/denominator/percentage fields could be intended differently; the table supplies no alternate denominator that makes 5 equal 17% (a denominator about 29.4 would be needed).

**Quality-control relevance:** The table presents baseline ECOG distributions as `No. (%)`; an incorrect percentage can distort a reader’s numeric assessment of baseline performance-status completeness and balance, and may be reused in evidence extraction.

**Exact human question:** “Should the talc-pleurodesis ECOG ‘Unknown’ cell in Table 1 be corrected from `5 (17)` to `5 (7)`, or is a different count/denominator intended?”

## Limitations

This was a consistency review of supplied, reported numbers only. It did not reconstruct patient-level data, recompute model-based estimates from raw data, or treat historical protocol versions, blank SAP shells, different analysis populations, or finite-precision P-value displays as discrepancies. In particular, no display-zero P value was registered as a candidate.

## Superseding N052 addendum (coverage repair)

**N052 — COMPLETE — one additional candidate proposition recorded.** Fresh review of the final-protocol sample-size section at `joi170130supp1_prod.pdf#page=37` identifies a concrete arithmetic inconsistency that is distinct from the SAP/main-paper calculation already reviewed above.

**Exact source location and printed inputs:** DOC-002 p. 37 states: “62 patients in each group” are needed (therefore `62 × 2 = 124`), then “To allow for a 20% lost-to-follow-up rate, an additional 24 patients will be needed,” followed by “a total recruitment target of 146.” The same page gives 80% power, 2-sided 5% level, a 0.54-standard-deviation difference, and about 5 days from a preliminary 18-day estimate. By comparison, the SAP's later calculation at `joi170130supp1_prod.pdf#page=62` and DOC-001 p. 3 state 65/group and 12% loss, yielding 73/group/146, which is arithmetically coherent after rounding (`130 × 1.12 = 145.6`).

**Reproducible rule and calculation:** An explicitly stated base recruitment total plus explicitly stated additional participants must equal the stated target, with zero tolerance because all three are whole-person counts. `62 + 62 + 24 = 148`, not 146; difference = 2 participants. Further, if “20% lost-to-follow-up” means inflation of a 124-person analysable target to retain 124 under 20% loss, the conventional calculation is `124/(1−0.20)=155`, illustrating that neither 146 nor 148 has that usual retention interpretation. This latter point is an inference about a possible interpretation, not needed for the direct count contradiction.

**Observation/inference and alternatives:** Direct observations are the three printed whole-person quantities (62 per group, additional 24, target 146). The `124+24=148` mismatch is a direct arithmetic inference. Plausible alternatives are that “additional 24” should have been 22, that the intended base was 61 per group, or that the final target was 148; the supplied page does not resolve which field is intended. The different later SAP calculation is a changed planning derivation and does not itself repair the final-protocol page.

**Quality-control relevance:** Sample-size rationale and recruitment target are reported numerical planning facts. The mismatch can misstate the intended allowance/target to readers and downstream evidence extractors.

**Exact human question:** “In the final protocol's sample-size section, should the base count, the stated additional 24 participants, or the printed recruitment target of 146 be corrected so that the recruitment arithmetic is internally consistent?”
