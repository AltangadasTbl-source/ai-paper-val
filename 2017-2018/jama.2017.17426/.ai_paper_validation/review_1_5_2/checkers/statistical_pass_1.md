## Candidate propositions for later registration

### P1-01 — ITT analysis-population definition versus reported ITT denominator

- **Relationships:** `S044` (with the reported ITT results in `S002`, `S005`-`S025`, and `S027`-`S032` as affected results).
- **Proposed category:** Analysis-unit or population inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** DOC-002, `joi170130supp1_prod.pdf#page=62` (SAP PDF p. 11); DOC-001, `jama_thomas_2017_oi_170130.pdf#page=4` (main PDF p. 4); DOC-001, `jama_thomas_2017_oi_170130.pdf#page=6` (Table 2, main PDF p. 6).
- **Direct observation:** The SAP defines ITT as “all randomized subjects,” including those who did not receive their assigned intervention.  The main article reports 146 randomized, then says one participant in each group withdrew before presenting for randomized intervention and “they were excluded from all analyses”; it subsequently calls 73 IPC plus 71 talc patients (144 total) the ITT analyses.  Table 2 labels those same 73 and 71 denominators as ITT.
- **Reproducible comparison:** `74 + 72 = 146` randomized, whereas `73 + 71 = 144` reported ITT.  The stated SAP ITT rule includes randomized subjects who did not receive assigned treatment; the main text instead excludes two randomized pre-intervention withdrawals from every analysis.
- **Human question:** Was a protocol amendment, prespecified exception, or a differently dated SAP definition in force that authorizes excluding these two randomized participants from the reported ITT set?  If not, do the ITT label and all affected denominators need clarification?
- **Alternative supplied-source interpretation:** The main article may use “intention-to-treat” under a modified analysis-set convention after pre-intervention withdrawal; no supplied source explicitly names that convention or its authorization.

### P1-02 — Unstated and apparently reversed estimated-difference contrast in the main table and MI eTable

- **Relationships:** `S010`-`S024`, `S033`, and `S051`.
- **Proposed category:** Measure, label, or scale inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** DOC-001, `jama_thomas_2017_oi_170130.pdf#page=6` (Table 2); DOC-003, `joi170130supp2_prod.pdf#page=2` (eTable).
- **Direct observation:** Both tables place IPC before talc and use the label “Estimated Difference (95% CI)” without naming the reference group or subtraction order.  In main Table 2, the baseline dyspnea estimated means are 50.0 (IPC) and 52.2 (talc), with a positive difference of 2.27; in the eTable they are 49.8 (IPC) and 51.9 (talc), with a negative difference of -2.06.  The same pattern is visible at other visits: main day-1 dyspnea is 64.5 versus 69.7 with +5.25, while the MI eTable is 65.5 versus 71.7 with -6.19.
- **Reproducible comparison:** The printed group values make the main-table signs consistent with talc minus IPC and the eTable signs consistent with IPC minus talc.  The imputation status explains changed magnitudes but does not itself identify a contrast direction; neither table labels the contrast.
- **Human question:** Were the two tables intentionally parameterized with opposite reference groups?  If so, should each label state its contrast (for example, talc minus IPC or IPC minus talc) so readers do not read numerical sign changes as contradictory clinical directions?
- **Alternative supplied-source interpretation:** The displayed group values permit reconstruction of each implied direction, and the result estimates arise from different analyses (observed-data mixed model versus 20-imputation sensitivity analysis).  Thus this is a label/contrast-clarity proposition, not a claim that the estimates themselves must match.

The supplied source package does not state whether the 62/group/20% relation was formally superseded, or which of the differing planning bases governed the final sample size.  Different protocol versions alone are not treated as a contradiction.
