The unique features on the medication bottles will be the dosing instructions which vary by
drug. Patient ID numbers will provided to each site on separate label sheets. After
enrollment and randomization, the patient ID labels will be removed and placed on the
medication bottles by the study coordinator. Separate labels will also be created with dose
reduction instructions (at the two reduction levels) for each drug. If a dose reduction is used,
the study coordinator will place the reduction labels on the medication bottles. At no time will
the study doctor handle labeling or distribution of the study medication.
To maintain masking throughout the trial, steps will be taken to avoid the study doctors
discovering patient assignment. Not only will the study doctors have no part in handling the
medication bottles, all patients will be given dark bags to place and keep their bottles in
throughout the trial. They will be instructed to bring their medication to their visits enclosed
in the bags, and an extra bag will be kept at the front desk of the clinics in case they forget.
This will minimize the chances of the study doctor seeing the dosing instructions on the
bottles. Additionally, patients will meet with the study coordinator first, before seeing their
study doctor. The study coordinator will review the patients’ medication diaries with them
and keep the study bottles in his/her office for the entire patient visit. Before bringing
patients to the study doctor, the study coordinator will remind them to not discuss their
dosing and medication name with their study doctor.
Clinical examiners, refractionists, OCT operators, fundus photographers and fundus graders
will not be given any information on medication assignment. If at any time masking of these
personnel is broken, a protocol deviation form must be completed, scanned and emailed to
both the Medical Monitor and Principal Investigator
. Note that these patients should still continue on in the trial.
2.5. Definitions for primary endpoint
2.5.1. Treatment success
Treatment success is defined as controlled ocular inflammation (see Table 2) in both eyes*
with < 7.5 mg/day of prednisone, and < 2 drops/day of topical 1% prednisolone acetate at
the 6 month visit, without tolerability or safety issues warranting discontinuation.

2.5.2. Treatment failure
Discontinuation of study medication at any time due to efficacy, tolerability, or
safety may result in a declaration of treatment failure.
