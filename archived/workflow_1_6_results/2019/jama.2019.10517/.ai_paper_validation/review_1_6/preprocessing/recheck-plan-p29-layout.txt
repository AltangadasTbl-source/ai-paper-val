1432   5.2 CULTURAL COMPETENCE OF STUDY PERSONNEL
1433   Investigators, research coordinators, and research associates will participate in training in cultural competence,
1434   as part of the study’s personnel training. These personnel will be required to complete the five core modules of
1435   the Curriculum in Ethnogeriatrics, an internet-based resource for education and training in cultural competence
1436   [http://www.stanford.edu/group/ethnoger] supported by the Bureau of Health Professions, Health Resources
1437   and Services Administration, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. The five modules cover the
1438   following topics: i) an introduction to, and overview of, culturally-competent care of elders, ii) patterns of health
1439   risk among people from diverse ethnic backgrounds, iii) culturally-based health beliefs, iv) knowledge and skills
1440   needed to provide a culturally competent assessment, and v) cultural issues in the delivery of health care after
1441   assessments are completed, including health promotion, informed consent, treatment, and working with
1442   families. The Curriculum in Ethnogeriatrics also has 12 ethnic-specific modules that can be used as an
1443   additional resource when needed. Although this educational tool has a focus on the older population, which is
1444   very pertinent to the proposed study, its principles and themes are also applicable to younger and middle-aged
1445   adults.
1446
1447   Data from STOP-PD are consistent with our research group having culturally competent attitudes and skills. In
1448   STOP-PD, 84.2% of participants were white,11.2% were African American or black, and 4.6% were Asian;
1449   11.6% of participants described themselves as Hispanic or Latino. Based on census data, these percentages
1450   were comparable to the racial and ethnic make-up of the U.S. population at the time that the study was
1451   conducted. Rates of completion at Weeks 6 and 12 of the 12-week study were highly comparable between
1452   racial and ethnic groups (see Table 5). Thus, these data show that we were successful in both recruiting and
1453   retaining minorities in STOP-PD.
