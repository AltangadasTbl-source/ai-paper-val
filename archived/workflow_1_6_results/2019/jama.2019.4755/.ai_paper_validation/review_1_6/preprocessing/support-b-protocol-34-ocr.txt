8.8 Results of the stool tests
As colonoscopy is the gold standard and all participants have already decided to undergo
colonoscopy before the study, there is no need to inform participants about the results of their
iFOBTS. The colonoscopy is not part of the study and communication of the results of the
colonoscopy to the patients is done by the gastroenterologists according to the usual
procedure in their practice. However, if a participant partly completes the study, but does not
undergo colonoscopy for any reason, the result of the iFOBT may be of importance to
him/her. The study center has to inform the coordinating center per fax or email, that the
participant did not undergo the scheduled colonoscopy in a period of 3 month after
randomization. If the participant agrees (as indicated on the informed consent), a positive
stool test result will be sent by the coordinating center in writing to the participant’s
gastroenterologist if the participant does not undergo colonoscopy in the 3 months following
randomization. The gastroenterologist will then be responsible for further steps. If these
patients come back after more than 3 months and undergo colonoscopy, they can be
reincluded into the trial.
8.9 Results of optional biomarker studies
‘The blood samples will only be used for research related to colorectal neoplasia. Biomarker
analyses may include several markers including genetic or epigenetic markers. Participants
will be informed about this and will have the option of participating in the main study without
providing a blood sample. In addition, participants will be informed that they can request
destroying their blood samples at any time. Participants will not be informed about the results
of such analyses, as they have no clinical relevance to them and it is unclear which analyses
will be performed at which point in time. Only the staff of the coordinating center will have
access to the samples.
In the future, it might be more cost effective to perform whole-exome or even whole-genome
analyses instead of analyzing several genetic markers separately. In that case, only markers
potentially related to colorectal neoplasia will be extracted from the dataset and analyzed.
