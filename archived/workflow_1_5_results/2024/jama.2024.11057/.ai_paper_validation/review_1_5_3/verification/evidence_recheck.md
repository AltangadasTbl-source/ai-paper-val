## C001 — Quit-date pre-message duration conflicts between the main article and both support documents

- **Cited location found:** Yes. The relevant statements are present in the [main article, PDF p. 3](../../../jama_graham_2024_oi_240078_1739900423.19074.pdf#page=3), the [study protocol, PDF p. 2](../../../joi240078supp1_prod_1739900423.22574.pdf#page=2), and the [results supplement, PDF p. 2](../../../joi240078supp2_prod_1739900423.24574.pdf#page=2).
- **Source printed value or text matched:** Yes. The main article states, “Those who set a quit date receive messages **6 weeks before and 8 weeks after** their quit date.”
- **Comparator printed value or text matched:** Yes. The study protocol states that those who set a quit date receive messages “for **a week before and 8 weeks afterward**.” The results supplement states, “Users receive messages for **1 week preceding their quit date and 8 weeks afterward**.”
- **Comparator matching basis:** All three passages name the This is Quitting program, distinguish users who set a quit date, use that quit date as the temporal anchor, and describe the pre-quit and post-quit message periods. The three passages agree on four weeks for users without a quit date and on eight weeks after a set quit date, strengthening the match of the intervention component and subgroup.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. Matched descriptions of the duration of one intervention component require the same pre-quit duration unless the source supplies a version, effective-date, subgroup, or other scope qualifier that explains the difference.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Yes. The main article gives 6 pre-quit weeks, whereas both support documents give 1 pre-quit week. Therefore, `6 weeks - 1 week = 5 weeks`, and `6 weeks != 1 week`. The agreed eight-week post-quit duration does not remove the five-week difference in the pre-quit duration.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The supplied PDFs provide the intervention name, quit-date subgroup, temporal anchor, pre-quit duration, and post-quit duration needed to reproduce the printed comparison.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not provide the trial-period message-delivery specification, participant-level delivery logs, an intervention release identifier for each description, a version history linking the one-week and six-week schedules to effective dates, or an editorial source file identifying a transcription change. The three cited passages do not attach a version or effective-date qualifier to the duration.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The protocol page describes program enrollment “as of July 27, 2021,” while the main-article page describes enrollment “as of May 12, 2024.” Those dated contextual statements permit the possibility that the pages describe different program states. They do not establish that the pre-quit schedule changed or identify which schedule trial participants received. A production or transcription difference is also possible but is not identified by the supplied sources.
- **Direct observation:** The main article prints six weeks before the quit date; both support documents print one week before or preceding the quit date; all three print eight weeks after or afterward.
- **Inferred explanation:** A program-version change, outdated support description, or production error could explain the mismatch, but none is demonstrated in the package.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What pre-quit message duration was actually delivered to trial participants, and were the one-week and six-week descriptions intended to refer to different program versions or effective periods?

## C002 — eTable 4 labels motivation and confidence as median (IQR) but prints single parenthetical dispersion values

- **Cited location found:** Yes. The affected cells and same-table comparators are present in [results-supplement eTable 4, PDF p. 12](../../../joi240078supp2_prod_1739900423.24574.pdf#page=12). The table's scale note appears on the [eTable 4 continuation, PDF p. 13](../../../joi240078supp2_prod_1739900423.24574.pdf#page=13). The matched measure-format comparator is present in [main-article Table 1, PDF p. 4](../../../jama_graham_2024_oi_240078_1739900423.19074.pdf#page=4).
- **Source printed value or text matched:** Yes. eTable 4 labels “Motivation to quit vaping” as `median (IQR)` and prints `4.1 (0.8)` for both nonresponders and responders. It labels “Confidence to quit vaping” as `median (IQR)` and prints `3.2 (1.1)` and `3.5 (1.1)`.
- **Comparator printed value or text matched:** Yes. On the same eTable 4 page, “Days per month vaping, median (IQR)” is printed as `30.0 (27.0-30.0)` and `29.0 (26.0-30.0)`, while “Concern about health consequences of vaping, median (IQR)” is printed as `4.0 (3.0-5.0)` and `3.0 (3.0-4.0)`. Main-article Table 1 prints the same motivation and confidence measures under `median (IQR)` as `4.0 (4.0-5.0)` for motivation and `3.0 (3.0-4.0)` for confidence in both trial groups.
- **Comparator matching basis:** The strongest internal comparators are rows in the same eTable with the identical `median (IQR)` label. Main-article Table 1 is an additional comparator because it uses the same two named measures, scale context, and label, although its columns summarize randomized groups rather than seven-month response groups.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. Within eTable 4, rows labeled `median (IQR)` ordinarily present the parenthetical as two hyphen-separated values. The affected motivation and confidence rows instead present one parenthetical value per group without a note defining a different display convention for those rows.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Yes. Each same-table endpoint-form comparator contains two displayed values inside parentheses, `Q1-Q3`. Each of the four affected motivation or confidence cells contains one displayed value inside parentheses. Thus, the displayed arity is two endpoints for the comparator convention versus one value for every affected cell. Numeric rounding does not by itself identify two missing endpoints or determine the intended summary type.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The supplied PDFs provide the affected labels and values, multiple same-table format comparators, main-table comparators for the same measures, and the footnote that defines the scales as ranging from 1 to 5. These inputs reproduce the label-versus-display comparison.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not provide a note stating that `IQR` is reported as a scalar width for the affected rows, the underlying first and third quartiles, participant-level values, the analysis output used to construct eTable 4, or a table-production specification identifying whether the entries are medians with IQR widths or means with standard deviations.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** Because an interquartile range can be expressed as the scalar width `Q3 - Q1`, the single parenthetical numbers could be IQR widths under an unstated convention. Alternatively, the decimal centers and single parenthetical dispersions could be means and standard deviations with the row labels left unchanged. The PDF's explicit `median (IQR)` labels and its endpoint-form comparator rows do not choose between those explanations.
- **Direct observation:** The affected eTable 4 labels say `median (IQR)`; their four cells contain single parenthetical values; other eTable 4 rows with the same label contain hyphen-separated endpoint pairs; and Table 1 displays endpoint pairs for the same motivation and confidence measures.
- **Inferred explanation:** Interpreting the single parenthetical numbers as standard deviations, scalar IQR widths, or production artifacts is inferential because the supplied package does not define those values.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Were the eTable 4 motivation-to-quit and confidence-to-quit entries intended to be means with standard deviations, medians with scalar IQR widths, or medians with omitted first- and third-quartile endpoints, and what values and labels are present in the authoritative analysis output?

## C003 — eAppendix C prints IPWR once for a result otherwise defined and labelled as IPRW

- **Cited location found:** Yes. [D003 results supplement, PDF p. 5](../../../joi240078supp2_prod_1739900423.24574.pdf#page=5) contains eAppendix C and the repeated-PPA paragraph; [D003 results supplement, PDF p. 14](../../../joi240078supp2_prod_1739900423.24574.pdf#page=14) contains eTable 5; [D001 main article, PDF p. 4](../../../jama_graham_2024_oi_240078_1739900423.19074.pdf#page=4) contains Statistical Methods.
- **Source printed value or text matched:** Yes. D003 p. 5 defines `Inverse Probability of Retention Weighting (IPRW)` and repeatedly uses `IPRW`, but the repeated-PPA sentence prints `IPWR` beside RR 2.21, 95% CI 1.67-2.93, and P<.001.
- **Comparator printed value or text matched:** Yes. D003 eTable 5 labels the matched repeated-PPA row `IPRW` and prints RR 2.21 (1.67, 2.93), P<.001; its footnote expands `IPRW`. D001 p. 4 also defines and uses `IPRW` and does not introduce `IPWR`.
- **Comparator matching basis:** Outcome, analysis context, point estimate, confidence limits, and P-value display match between the D003 narrative and eTable 5; only the abbreviation differs.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. A method abbreviation attached to a result should agree with the explicit expansion and the method label attached elsewhere to the same matched result.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Yes. `IPWR != IPRW`; the last two letters are transposed. At the same time, `RR 2.21 = RR 2.21` and interval endpoints `1.67-2.93 = 1.67-2.93`, confirming the result match.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The candidate occurrence, explicit IPRW expansion, repeated IPRW uses, and matched eTable result are supplied.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not define `IPWR` and lacks the analysis-output label, production source, and editorial change history needed to establish the intended label or production mechanism.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** `IPWR` could be an unstated second abbreviation, but no source expands it and the identical result is assigned to `IPRW` in eTable 5.
- **Direct observation:** The source prints both `IPWR` and `IPRW`, explicitly expands `IPRW`, and attaches matching numeric results to the two strings.
- **Inferred explanation:** A typographical transposition and an intended reading of `IPWR` as `IPRW` are plausible but not established by the supplied package.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Does `IPWR` on D003 p. 5 denote the same inverse probability of retention weighting analysis labelled `IPRW` elsewhere; if it denotes a different analysis, what is its source-defined expansion and documentation?
