## C001 — ITT Figure 2 mRS distributions conflict with ITT eTable 2 threshold counts

- **Cited locations found:** Yes. The title, legend, four bars, and bar denominators of Figure 2 were found at [main article, PDF p. 7](<../../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=7>). The intention-to-treat heading, arm denominators, and threshold rows of eTable 2 were found at [Supplement 5, PDF p. 38](<../../../joi240006supp5_prod_1708623115.01733.pdf#page=38>). The main Results text at [main article, PDF p. 4](<../../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=4>) reports 336 included participants, with 168 randomized to EVT and the remaining randomized to medical management. The flow diagram at [Supplement 5, PDF p. 8](<../../../joi240006supp5_prod_1708623115.01733.pdf#page=8>) directly shows the 336-person study cohort, 168 randomized to EVT, 168 randomized to MM, and the distinct 170/166 as-treated population.
- **Source printed value or text matched:** Yes. Figure 2 is titled “Distribution of Modified Rankin Scale Score at 90-Day Follow-Up in the Study Population (Intention to Treat).” In mRS legend order 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, its printed whole percentages are EVT core less than 100 mL, n=117: 2, 6, 18, 22, 15, 8, 30; EVT core at least 100 mL, n=51: 2, 6, 10, 20, 0, 10, 53; MM core less than 100 mL, n=119: 0, 2, 8, 14, 23, 20, 34; and MM core at least 100 mL, n=49: 2, 0, 4, 16, 0, 16, 61. The zero values are zero-width, unlabeled segments whose positions are determined by matching each visible segment color to the fixed legend: mRS 4 is absent from both core-at-least-100-mL bars, mRS 0 is absent from the MM core-less-than-100-mL bar, and mRS 1 is absent from the MM core-at-least-100-mL bar. The labels must not be shifted across these zero-width categories.
- **Comparator printed value or text matched:** Yes. eTable 2 is headed “Clinical and imaging outcomes in intention-to-treat population, stratified by treatment” and prints N=168 for each arm. It reports, in EVT and MM order, mRS 0-2 as 34/168 (20.2%) and 12/168 (7.1%), mRS 0-3 as 65/168 (38.7%) and 31/168 (18.5%), and mRS 5-6 as 76/168 (45.2%) and 102/168 (60.7%). It separately reports all-cause mortality as 62/168 (36.9%) and 70/168 (41.7%).
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. For a printed whole percentage `p`, denominator `n`, and integer category count `k`, ordinary nearest-whole-percentage compatibility requires `p - 0.5 <= 100k/n < p + 0.5`; no relevant value falls exactly on a half-percentage boundary. Because each Figure 2 bar displays the mutually exclusive mRS categories 0 through 6 for its stated stratum, the seven feasible category counts must also sum to that bar's denominator. Threshold counts are the sums of their component category counts across the two core strata within the same randomized arm.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Yes. Applying that rule to each segment and enforcing each bar total gives the following unique feasible integer vectors, in mRS 0-through-6 order:

| Figure 2 arm and core stratum | Printed percentages in mRS 0-6 order | Unique feasible counts in mRS 0-6 order |
|---|---|---|
| EVT, core less than 100 mL, n=117 | 2, 6, 18, 22, 15, 8, 30 | 2, 7, 21, 26, 17, 9, 35 |
| EVT, core at least 100 mL, n=51 | 2, 6, 10, 20, 0, 10, 53 | 1, 3, 5, 10, 0, 5, 27 |
| MM, core less than 100 mL, n=119 | 0, 2, 8, 14, 23, 20, 34 | 0, 2, 9, 17, 27, 24, 40 |
| MM, core at least 100 mL, n=49 | 2, 0, 4, 16, 0, 16, 61 | 1, 0, 2, 8, 0, 8, 30 |

  The count reconstruction is unique even where a percentage permits two counts before the bar-total constraint. For EVT n=117, the printed 15% permits 17 or 18 participants by itself, but only 17 makes the seven categories total 117. For MM n=119, the printed 8% permits 9 or 10 and the printed 34% permits 40 or 41, but only 9 and 40 make the bar total 119.

| Endpoint | Figure-derived EVT count | eTable 2 EVT count | Figure-derived MM count | eTable 2 MM count |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| mRS 0-2 | (2+7+21)+(1+3+5) = **39** | **34** | (0+2+9)+(1+0+2) = **14** | **12** |
| mRS 0-3 | 39+26+10 = **75** | **65** | 14+17+8 = **39** | **31** |
| mRS 5-6 | (9+35)+(5+27) = **76** | **76** | (24+40)+(8+30) = **102** | **102** |

  Thus the Figure-derived minus eTable-count differences are +5, +10, and 0 for EVT and +2, +8, and 0 for MM. Figure-derived mRS 5-6 reconciles exactly in both arms. As an additional cross-check on category placement, Figure-derived mRS-6 counts are 35+27=62 for EVT and 40+30=70 for MM, exactly matching eTable 2's separate mortality counts. The exhaustive partition mRS 0-3, mRS 4, and mRS 5-6 also makes the remaining difference explicit: eTable 2 implies mRS-4 counts of 168-65-76=27 for EVT and 168-31-102=35 for MM, while Figure 2 yields 17+0=17 and 27+0=27. The Figure therefore has 10 and 8 more participants, respectively, at mRS 0-3 and 10 and 8 fewer at mRS 4 than the eTable-derived partition.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The direct sources provide the figure title, mRS legend and category order, four stratum denominators, all nonzero whole percentages, the zero-width category positions recoverable from the fixed legend order, the ITT arm totals, and the exact eTable threshold counts. Those inputs are sufficient to reproduce the stated nearest-whole-percentage feasibility comparison and show that the table counts are outside the unique feasible Figure-derived counts under that rule.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not provide the unrounded Figure 2 percentages; the underlying integer mRS 0-through-6 counts by treatment and core stratum; the figure-generation dataset or program output; an explicit rule stating whether the displayed whole percentages were nearest-rounded, truncated, normalized, weighted, or otherwise transformed; or a note identifying a different mRS derivation, outcome dataset, missing-outcome rule, or data version for Figure 2 versus eTable 2.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretations:** The identical ITT labels and 168-per-arm totals support a matched comparison, but the package leaves several alternatives open. Figure 2 may have been generated from a different outcome derivation or data version without a distinguishing note. Its percentages, legend-category assignments, or one or more labels may contain a production error. eTable 2 may use an unstated outcome set. A non-nearest rounding, normalization, or weighting method could invalidate the integer reconstruction, although no such method is stated on the figure or in its caption. The exact reconciliation of both mRS 5-6 totals and both reconstructed mRS-6 counts, alongside the mRS 0-2, mRS 0-3, and implied mRS-4 differences, permits a category-boundary or category-specific production discrepancy rather than a whole-population denominator discrepancy.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Direct observations are the source headings, population labels, denominators, Figure percentages and legend order, eTable counts and percentages, and the separate mortality counts. The arithmetic feasibility sets and threshold sums are derived diagnostic calculations from those observations. Any assertion that a particular display, category label, dataset, analysis rule, or production step caused the differences is an inference; the supplied package does not identify the cause.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Were Figure 2 and eTable 2 generated from the same 168-per-arm ITT 90-day mRS records and the same category definitions, missing-outcome handling, and data version; if so, what are the authoritative mRS 0-through-6 counts by treatment and core stratum and which printed figure percentages or table threshold counts should correspond to them, and if not, what exact source note and percentage-calculation rule distinguish the two displays?
- **Workflow status:** Pending Human Adjudication.

## C002 — `aRR` is labeled as an absolute risk reduction but displayed and interpreted as a ratio

- **Cited locations found:** Yes. The narrative use of “absolute risk reduction [aRR]” was found at [main article, PDF p. 6](<../../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=6>). Table 2's abbreviation expansion, estimates, and greater-than-1 interpretation were found at [main article, PDF p. 7](<../../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=7>). Table 3's aRR and aRD values and abbreviation key were found at [main article, PDF p. 9](<../../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=9>). The trial SAP's relative-risk analysis statement was found at [trial SAP, PDF p. 17](<../../../joi240006supp3_prod_1708623114.99733.pdf#page=17>). The secondary-analysis SAP's modified-Poisson statements were found at [secondary-analysis SAP, PDF p. 4](<../../../joi240006supp4_prod_1708623115.00733.pdf#page=4>) and [PDF p. 5](<../../../joi240006supp4_prod_1708623115.00733.pdf#page=5>). The side-by-side `aRR` and `aRD` effect rows were found in eTable 7 at [Supplement 5, PDF p. 43](<../../../joi240006supp5_prod_1708623115.01733.pdf#page=43>).
- **Source printed value or text matched:** Yes. The main narrative states “Functional independence (absolute risk reduction [aRR], 0.89 [95% CI, 0.84-0.95] per 10-mL increase)” and then reports aRR 0.91 and 1.05 for the other binary outcomes. Table 2 expands `aRR` as “absolute risk reduction,” prints estimates including 0.89 (0.84-0.95), 0.91 (0.87-0.95), and 1.05 (1.02-1.08), and states that “aRR greater than 1 indicates higher rate ratio” for mRS 0-2, 0-3, and 5-6. Table 3 again expands `aRR` as “absolute risk reduction,” separately expands `aRD` as “absolute risk difference,” and prints paired values including aRR 1.33 (0.52 to 3.44) and aRD 0.178 (-0.109 to 0.465). Table 3 repeats the greater-than-1 “higher rate ratio” interpretation.
- **Comparator printed value or text matched:** Yes. Trial SAP PDF p. 17 states that the treatment effect for mRS 0-2 and mRS 0-3 will include “the relative risk with its 95% confidence interval and p-value, calculated using modified Poisson regression.” Secondary-analysis SAP PDF p. 4 states that mRS 0-2 is evaluated using a modified Poisson regression model with robust standard errors and gives the same model for mRS 0-3; PDF p. 5 gives that model for mRS 5-6. Supplement 5 eTable 7 prints `aRR` and the separately named `aRD` in the same outcome rows, for example aRR 1.31 (0.80-2.15) and aRD 0.127 (-0.039 to 0.293).
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. A risk reduction or risk difference is an additive contrast between risks, has null value 0, and is expressed in absolute risk units. A risk ratio or rate ratio is a multiplicative quotient, is dimensionless, and has null value 1. A source label that calls the same reported quantity both an absolute reduction and a ratio-scale measure does not preserve the measure class, scale, or null value.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Yes. No model reconstruction is needed. The direct source itself interprets `aRR` relative to 1 and calls it a “rate ratio.” Values such as 1.33 and 1.05, with multiplicative confidence intervals, are compatible with a ratio-scale interpretation. Table 3 simultaneously displays a separate additive `aRD` value of 0.178 with an interval crossing 0. The trial SAP expressly calls the modified-Poisson treatment effect a relative risk. These printed facts do not logically reconcile with the repeated expansion `aRR = absolute risk reduction`.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The package supplies the conflicting abbreviation expansion, the greater-than-1 interpretation, representative estimates and confidence intervals, a separately labeled adjusted risk-difference quantity, and direct SAP statements identifying relative-risk or modified-Poisson analyses. These inputs are sufficient for the scale-and-label comparison without an unreported P value, standard error, covariance, or model coefficient.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not provide an authoritative abbreviation dictionary or analysis-program variable label stating what the initial `a` in `aRR` was intended to mean; it does not resolve whether the intended term is adjusted risk ratio, adjusted rate ratio, or another relative measure; and it does not explain any nonstandard definition under which “absolute risk reduction” could have a null value of 1 while `aRD` separately carries the additive risk-difference result.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretations:** The SAP's “relative risk” wording, the modified-Poisson model, the estimates centered on 1, and the separately printed aRD support the possibility that `aRR` was intended to denote an adjusted risk ratio, while “absolute risk reduction” was a repeated expansion error. The tables' own “rate ratio” sentence permits adjusted rate ratio as another intended expansion. Alternatively, “higher rate ratio” could be the unintended wording, or the authors could have used a nonstandard internal term, but the supplied package gives no mathematical definition that makes the absolute-reduction expansion compatible with the displayed ratio scale.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Direct observations are the narrative and table expansions, the printed aRR and aRD estimates and intervals, the greater-than-1 rate-ratio sentence, and the SAP model and relative-risk statements. The classification of additive versus multiplicative scales is the applicable logical comparison. Any claim that the abbreviation should be expanded in a particular way, or that a specific wording was a production error, is an inferred explanation and is not assigned here as a correction.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What exact effect measure and expansion was intended for `aRR` in the narrative, Tables 2 and 3, and related supplement results—including whether `a` means adjusted and whether `RR` means risk ratio or rate ratio—and how should that intended ratio-scale label be distinguished from the separately reported additive `aRD` measure across all source locations?
- **Workflow status:** Pending Human Adjudication.
