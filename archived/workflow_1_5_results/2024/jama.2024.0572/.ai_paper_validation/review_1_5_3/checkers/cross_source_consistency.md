# Cross-Source Consistency Review

## Scope and method

This independent review covered every result-bearing repeated relationship in the assigned maps across the main article (abstract, narrative, tables, figures, captions, and footnotes), results supplement, protocol, trial SAP, and secondary-analysis SAP. Administrative Documents 002 and 007 contain no applicable repeated quantitative result. The review used direct PDFs as the authority; reusable maps and text were locators only.

Before a comparison was made, the population, analysis set, time point, contrast, model, outcome definition, scale, units, reference group, and displayed precision were matched. Planned protocol/SAP quantities were not compared as though they were post-analysis results. The secondary-analysis SAP explicitly describes the exploratory analysis as as-treated with no imputation; its definitions and model statements were compared only with reporting that uses the same analysis context.

**Matched-result groups checked:** 19.  
**Qualifying provisional candidates:** 2.  
**Non-candidate matched groups:** 17.  
**Displayed-zero P values:** none were treated as candidates.

## Provisional candidate 1 — ITT Figure 2 mRS distributions do not reconcile with the ITT eTable 2 threshold counts

**Category:** Cross-document numeric inconsistency; denominator/proportion or total inconsistency.

**Exact direct-source locations:**

- [Main article, Figure 2, PDF p. 7](<../../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=7>). The title says “Distribution of Modified Rankin Scale Score at 90-Day Follow-Up in the Study Population (Intention to Treat).” It gives CTP/MRI-core stratum denominators EVT 117 and 51, and MM 119 and 49.
- [Supplement 5, eTable 2, PDF p. 38](<../../../joi240006supp5_prod_1708623115.01733.pdf#page=38>). The heading says “Clinical and imaging outcomes in intention-to-treat population, stratified by treatment,” with EVT N=168 and MM N=168.
- [Main article, Results, PDF p. 4](<../../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=4>) and [Supplement 5, eFigure 3, PDF p. 8](<../../../joi240006supp5_prod_1708623115.01733.pdf#page=8>) identify the same 168/168 randomized allocation and 336-person included analysis.

**Printed values compared:**

- Figure 2 EVT distributions for CTP/MRI core <100 mL (n=117) are 2%, 6%, 18%, 22%, 15%, 8%, and 30% for mRS 0 through 6; for >=100 mL (n=51) they are 2%, 6%, 10%, 20%, 10%, 0%, and 53%.
- eTable 2 prints, for ITT EVT N=168: mRS 0-2, **34/168 (20.2%)**; mRS 0-3, **65/168 (38.7%)**; and mRS 5-6, **76/168 (45.2%)**.

**Comparison logic and calculation:**

The two Figure 2 EVT denominators sum exactly to its stated ITT EVT total (117+51=168). Summing the Figure 2 mRS categories at each eTable 2 threshold yields approximate Figure-derived counts that are not compatible with the exact eTable counts even allowing ordinary whole-percentage display rounding:

| Endpoint | Figure 2 calculation from displayed percentage and denominator | Figure-derived count | eTable 2 printed count |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| EVT mRS 0-2 | <100: (2%+6%+18%) x 117; >=100: (2%+6%+10%) x 51 | 30 + 9 = **39** | **34/168** |
| EVT mRS 0-3 | <100: (2%+6%+18%+22%) x 117; >=100: (2%+6%+10%+20%) x 51 | 56 + 19 = **75** | **65/168** |
| EVT mRS 5-6 | <100: (8%+30%) x 117; >=100: (0%+53%) x 51 | 44 + 27 = **71** | **76/168** |
| MM mRS 0-2 | <100: (0%+2%+8%) x 119; >=100: (0%+2%+4%) x 49 | 12 + 3 = **15** | **12/168** |
| MM mRS 0-3 | <100: (0%+2%+8%+14%) x 119; >=100: (0%+2%+4%+16%) x 49 | 29 + 11 = **40** | **31/168** |
| MM mRS 5-6 | <100: (20%+34%) x 119; >=100: (0%+61%) x 49 | 64 + 30 = **94** | **102/168** |

For example, ordinary nearest-whole-percent rounding permits only 2, 7, and 21 EVT participants for the Figure 2 <100-mL mRS 0, 1, and 2 segments, respectively, and 1, 3, and 5 in the >=100-mL stratum: 39 overall. It cannot yield the eTable 2 value 34. The same incompatibility occurs in the MM arm. The displayed one-percentage-point summation differences within bars do not account for the observed threshold-count differences.

**Supported alternatives:**

- The two displays may have used different outcome datasets, a different treatment-analysis derivation, or a version-specific figure/table extract despite both displays stating ITT and the same arm totals.
- Figure 2 could contain one or more mislabeled percentages, denominators, or mRS segments.
- eTable 2 could be referring to a differently derived 90-day mRS dataset. The supplied headings do not state such a distinction.

**Human verification steps:**

1. Inspect the production data or figure-generation dataset for Figure 2 and recover the seven category counts by treatment and CTP/MRI core stratum.
2. Confirm whether Figure 2 and eTable 2 used the same ITT set, outcome ascertainment set, and handling of the four missing 90-day outcomes.
3. Recalculate mRS 0-2, 0-3, and 5-6 from the verified Figure 2 category counts and reconcile each with eTable 2.
4. If the datasets differ, revise the title, denominator, or analytic-set note so that the distinction is explicit; otherwise correct the discrepant display.

## Provisional candidate 2 — `aRR` is expanded as “absolute risk reduction” while the supplied analysis and displayed values identify a ratio measure

**Category:** Measure, label, or scale inconsistency.

**Exact direct-source locations:**

- [Main article, narrative, PDF p. 6](<../../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=6>) calls the functional-independence estimate “absolute risk reduction [aRR], 0.89 (95% CI, 0.84-0.95) per 10-mL increase.”
- [Main article, Table 2 and footnote, PDF p. 7](<../../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=7>) expands `aRR` as “absolute risk reduction,” but says “aRR greater than 1 indicates higher rate ratio” for mRS 0-2, 0-3, and 5-6.
- [Main article, Table 3 footnote, PDF p. 9](<../../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=9>) again expands `aRR` as “absolute risk reduction,” prints estimates such as 1.33 (0.52 to 3.44), and separately expands `aRD` as “absolute risk difference.”
- [Secondary-analysis SAP, PDF pp. 4-5](<../../../joi240006supp4_prod_1708623115.00733.pdf#page=4>) specifies modified Poisson regression with robust standard errors for mRS 0-2, 0-3, and 5-6. [Trial SAP, PDF pp. 17-20](<../../../joi240006supp3_prod_1708623114.99733.pdf#page=17>) likewise specifies binary-outcome RR estimates from modified Poisson regression.
- [Supplement 5, eTable 7, PDF pp. 43-45](<../../../joi240006supp5_prod_1708623115.01733.pdf#page=43>) displays `aRR` and a separately named `aRD` in the same effect-size rows.

**Printed values compared:**

- Main Table 2 displays `aRR` 0.89 (0.84-0.95), 0.91 (0.87-0.95), and 1.05 (1.02-1.08) for per-10-mL associations, and explains values greater than 1 as a higher “rate ratio.”
- Main Table 3 displays `aRR` 1.33 (0.52 to 3.44) and, in separate rows/cells, `aRD` 0.178 (-0.109 to 0.465).
- The main article's abbreviation definitions call `aRR` “absolute risk reduction,” whereas the SAP model description provides RR/rate-ratio context and the paper separately uses `aRD` for absolute risk difference.

**Comparison logic:**

An absolute risk reduction/difference is an additive quantity on the risk-difference scale; the displayed `aRR` estimates are ratio-scale values and the main footnote explicitly calls a value above 1 a higher rate ratio. The main table's own expansion therefore conflicts with its interpretive sentence, with the binary modified-Poisson RR analysis specified in the supplied SAPs, and with the separate `aRD` absolute-risk-difference label. This is a label/measure issue; no new effect estimate has been inferred.

**Supported alternatives:**

- `aRR` may have been intended to mean adjusted risk ratio (or adjusted rate ratio), and “absolute risk reduction” is a repeated abbreviation-expansion error.
- The authors may use a nonstandard internal expansion for `aRR`; if so, the table's “higher rate ratio” interpretation and the relationship of `aRR` to the separately displayed `aRD` need an explicit source definition.

**Human verification steps:**

1. Verify the analysis-program output and table shell for the expansion intended for `aRR`.
2. Confirm whether the modified Poisson output was reported as an adjusted risk ratio or adjusted rate ratio, and standardize the term across narrative, Tables 2-3, and the supplement.
3. Confirm that `aRD` remains the additive adjusted risk-difference quantity and that no ratio value is presented as a risk reduction.
4. Check all occurrences of the abbreviation in the main article and supplement after resolving the intended expansion.

## Limitations

- Figure 2 supplies rounded percentages rather than category counts. The candidate is based on the stated ITT denominators and the fact that ordinary percentage rounding cannot bridge the observed threshold-count gaps; source data are required to determine which display is in error.
- Protocol and SAP documents chiefly provide prospective definitions and planned analyses. They were used to match model/measure meaning, not to allege disagreement between planned and observed numerical results.
- No supplied structured workbook, CSV, DOC, or DOCX source was present for an additional cross-format comparison.
