## C003 — eTable 10 `Difference` header and stated odds-ratio estimand

- **Location found:** Yes. eTable 10 begins in [DOC-003, PDF p. 13](../../../joi240111supp2_prod_1733431204.76024.pdf#page=13) and continues through [PDF p. 15](../../../joi240111supp2_prod_1733431204.76024.pdf#page=15). - **Source printed value or text matched:** On PDF p. 13, eTable 10 prints `Difference (95% CI)` as the modeled-effect header under each of the three cohort groups: `All Participants`, `SSPedi Score ≥ 1`, and `SSPedi Score ≥ 3`. That header governs the table's `Symptom Documentation`, `Any Intervention`, and continued `Intervention Clearly for Symptom (Symptom-Specific Intervention)` blocks on pp. 13-15. Examples in that column include 0.53 (0.28, 1.01) on p. 13 and 5.30 (2.50, 11.24) and 17.96 (1.03, 313.1) on p. 14.
- **Comparator printed value or text matched:** DOC-003 p. 22 states that each documentation, any-intervention, and symptom-specific-intervention analysis fit a mixed-effects logistic regression model `to estimate the odds ratio` comparing intervention and control groups, with a fixed-effects logistic fallback when the mixed model could not be fit. The eTable 10 footnote on p. 15 also states that P values were calculated using a mixed- or fixed-effects logistic regression model depending on the number of events.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** The rule is an effect-measure identity check. The source explicitly identifies the logistic-regression estimand as an odds ratio, a multiplicative measure with null value 1. An unqualified `Difference` ordinarily identifies an additive contrast and does not name the supplied odds-ratio estimand. The rule applies across the three outcome blocks because eMethods names all three and the table uses one shared modeled-effect header.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** This is a label-to-estimand comparison, not a reconstructed test. The table's 0.53 (0.28, 1.01), P=.05 example is organized around the multiplicative null of 1, as are 5.30 (2.50, 11.24), P<.001 and 17.96 (1.03, 313.1), P=.003. The direct eMethods statement identifies these modeled effects as odds ratios, while the table header identifies them only as `Difference`. No P value, standard error, or model coefficient was reverse-engineered.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The table title, cohort headers, modeled-effect header, three outcome-block labels, representative values and intervals, logistic-model footnote, and eMethods estimand definition are all available in DOC-003. They are sufficient for the label-versus-measure comparison.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The source does not define `Difference` as a generic synonym for a between-group modeled effect, does not state that the header intentionally encompasses odds ratios, and does not identify any table block as reporting an additive difference instead. The per-cell mixed-versus-fixed fallback, CI construction, test statistic, standard error, and variance estimator are also absent, but those details are not necessary for this effect-label comparison.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** `Difference` may have been intended as a generic umbrella heading for the modeled comparison, while all cells remain odds ratios as specified in eMethods. Under that interpretation, the numerical estimand remains multiplicative and the issue is limited to how the header identifies it. The package supplies no definition confirming that use of the word `Difference`.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Direct observations are the `Difference (95% CI)` header, the three cohort and outcome blocks, the example values and intervals, the logistic-regression footnote, and the eMethods phrase `estimate the odds ratio`. Applying the method's named estimand to the table column is the logical comparison. A generic-heading intention, a production-template cause, or the possibility that any block uses a different estimand is inferred rather than stated.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Is every modeled-effect cell in eTable 10 intended to report an odds ratio as stated in eMethods; if so, how should the shared `Difference (95% CI)` header identify that estimand, and if not, which exact blocks or cells use a different effect measure?
