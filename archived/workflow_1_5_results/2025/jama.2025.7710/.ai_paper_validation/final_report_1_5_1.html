C001 — Conflicting economic price-year labels within the protocol

Pending Human Adjudication.

Candidate statement: Two linked protocol descriptions of the economic-analysis framework state different monetary price years.

Category: Measure, label, or scale inconsistency

Exact source locations: joi250034supp1_prod_1750956984.09018.pdf — PDF p. 16, section 7.2.3; joi250034supp1_prod_1750956984.09018.pdf — PDF p. 17, section 8.2.

Source evidence: Section 7.2.3 states that all costs will be presented in 2024/25 dollars, with no discounting, for a payer-perspective one-year cost-utility analysis and five-year budget-impact analysis. Section 8.2 describes the same perspective, horizons, and no-discounting statement but says all costs will be presented in 2023/24 dollars.

Reported-versus-comparator: Reported: 2024/25 dollars (p. 16). Comparator: 2023/24 dollars (p. 17).

Reasoning procedure: Compare the monetary base-year label after matching the described economic-analysis perspective, time horizons, and discounting convention.

Calculation: 2024/25 is not equal to 2023/24; no rounding or transformation is involved.

Alternative source-grounded interpretations: One section may retain wording from an intended price-year update, or the sections may have meant different explicitly converted analyses. The supplied protocol neither identifies a superseded section nor distinguishes separate price-year conventions.

Mechanical evidence recheck: Direct-page recheck found both cited locations and matched the printed labels and surrounding framework. Missing inputs are an authoritative amendment/version note, executed economic specification, and stated conversion rule.

Quality-control relevance: An unambiguous monetary base-year label is needed to describe a planned economic analysis consistently.

Potential downstream evidence impact: If confirmed, an economic-data extractor or evidence synthesis could copy an ambiguous price-year label; this report does not assert that any extraction or economic result was affected.

Human verification steps: Review approved protocol/amendment history and the economic-analysis plan; identify the prespecified and executed base year; determine whether either statement requires correction or qualification.

C002 — Primary-composite counting rationale does not reconcile with the displayed endpoint list

Pending Human Adjudication.

Candidate statement: The protocol displays ten separate endpoint bullets but explains a total of ten by saying two listed outcomes each contain two individual components.

Category: Measure, label, or scale inconsistency

Exact source locations: joi250034supp1_prod_1750956984.09018.pdf — PDF p. 15, section 7.1; joi250034supp1_prod_1750956984.09018.pdf — PDF p. 16, section 7.2.1.

Source evidence: Page 15 displays ten separate primary-composite endpoint bullets. Page 16 states, “There are ten items because outcomes (i) and (viii) each contain two individual components.” Section 7.1.1 groups intrapartum stillbirth with 28-day neonatal death, while 7.1.2 separately defines 28-day neonatal mortality; the eighth bullet names Special Care or Intensive Care within one neonatal-unit-admission endpoint.

Reported-versus-comparator: Reported list: ten displayed bullets. Comparator rationale: a total of ten attributed to expanding outcomes (i) and (viii).

Reasoning procedure: Reconcile the displayed enumeration with the stated expansion rule, retaining possible grouped endpoint structures only where the source provides them.

Calculation: Displayed count = 10. Under the conditional reading that two already displayed items are each expanded from one to two components, 10 + 1 + 1 = 12. An 8 + 1 + 1 = 10 reconciliation would require an authoritative eight-group starting list, which is not displayed at the cited locations.

Alternative source-grounded interpretations: The 7.1.1 heading and Special Care/Intensive Care wording could reflect an older or unstated eight-group structure. That structure is not the displayed ten-bullet list, and the supplied materials do not provide a definitive numbered taxonomy.

Mechanical evidence recheck: Direct-page recheck matched the ten bullets, the explanatory sentence, headings, and care-setting wording. It identified the missing authoritative component taxonomy, explicit eight-group list, and component definitions for the care settings.

Quality-control relevance: The count and definition of composite components should be consistently specified for reporting and extraction.

Potential downstream evidence impact: If confirmed, a data extractor or evidence synthesis could code the component definition ambiguously; this report does not state that the implemented composite or an observed estimate is wrong.

Human verification steps: Obtain the authoritative numbered list of ten components; establish whether stillbirth/neonatal death and Special Care/Intensive Care are separate components or grouped alternatives; align the display and rationale.

C004 — Reported no-adjustment statement conflicts with the supplied multiplicity plan

Pending Human Adjudication.

Candidate statement: The article reports no adjustment for multiple testing, while the supplied protocol and draft SAP plan Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted P values for the ten component comparisons.

Category: Statistical reporting inconsistency

Exact source locations: jama_kumar_2025_oi_250034_1750956984.08518.pdf — PDF p. 4, Statistical Analysis; jama_kumar_2025_oi_250034_1750956984.08518.pdf — PDF p. 7, Table 3; joi250034supp1_prod_1750956984.09018.pdf — PDF p. 20, section 13.2; joi250034supp2_prod_1750956984.11521.pdf — PDF p. 12, section 3.2; joi250034supp2_prod_1750956984.11521.pdf — PDF p. 25, proposed Table 11.

Source evidence: The article states, “There was no adjustment for multiple testing,” and its Table 3 displays ten component rows with one P-value column. The protocol and draft SAP state that adjusted P values for ten component comparisons will be derived using the Benjamin-Hochberg procedure; the SAP shell includes separate P-value and adjusted-P-value columns. The SAP identifies itself as Draft Version 1.2 on p. 1.

Reported-versus-comparator: Reported multiplicity treatment: no adjustment. Comparator planned treatment: adjusted P values for ten component comparisons.

Reasoning procedure: Match the same ten component comparisons across the article, protocol, and draft SAP; distinguish a planned-versus-reported treatment difference from arithmetic evaluation of raw P values; condition the comparison on document governance.

Calculation: Article: adjustment applied = no. Protocol/draft SAP: adjusted P values for 10 component comparisons = planned; the SAP Table 11 shell allocates an adjusted-P-value column. These descriptions cannot both be the implemented treatment for the same comparisons without a superseding plan decision or a narrower scope for the article statement.

Alternative source-grounded interpretations: A final approved SAP, amendment, or documented pre-unblinding decision may have superseded the supplied draft plan. The article notes an interpretive approach to interrelated secondary outcomes, but does not state that the planned adjustment was superseded. Raw P values are not thereby arithmetically invalid.

Mechanical evidence recheck: Direct-page recheck matched the article statement, Table 3 component rows, protocol plan, draft-SAP plan, and Table 11 shell. Missing inputs are a final approved SAP, amendment/departure record, analysis output containing adjusted values, and an explicit scope limitation for the article statement.

Quality-control relevance: Reporting should make the multiplicity treatment for the component comparisons traceable to the governing analysis plan or a documented change.

Potential downstream evidence impact: If confirmed, an extractor or evidence synthesis could treat the displayed component P values as the prespecified multiplicity presentation; this report does not claim that those values are arithmetically invalid, that conclusions changed, or that propagation occurred.

Human verification steps: Obtain the final approved SAP, amendment log, and analysis-program specification; determine whether the adjustment was abandoned, performed but omitted, or replaced; verify adjusted values and the reporting rationale if applicable.
