# Numeric Consistency Check — N001–N129

## Scope and method

This checker reviewed all 129 canonical numeric/reporting relationships against the direct-source-backed maps named in the canonical inventory. Checks applied where applicable: displayed arithmetic, totals and subgroup sums, numerator/denominator/percentage reconciliation (one-decimal percentage tolerance of 0.05 percentage points unless the table prints a different precision), population and missingness identities, quantity type, units, labels/scales, reference groups, and matched repeated values. A `PASS` means no source-grounded inconsistency was observed under the stated relationship and available definition; it is not an adjudication. `NOT_APPLICABLE` means that the relationship is a planning, operational-definition, or administrative value with no same-identity numeric comparator in the supplied package.

Direct observations below are transcribed from the cited direct-source records; calculations and identity tests are diagnostic inferences. Counts in Figure 1 ineligibility footnote are explicitly allowed to have multiple reasons and therefore were not falsely tested as mutually exclusive.

## Candidate observations for human adjudication

### NUM-CAND-001 — RR abbreviation is expanded as “risk difference” while the table identifies relative risk

- **Category:** Measure, label, or scale inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** DOC-005, Supplement 4, PDF p.7 eTable 4 column header; PDF p.8 abbreviation line and model footnote.
- **Direct observation:** The p.7 header is “Relative Risk (RR) or Mean Difference (MD)” and the table reports values such as `RR: 1.09 (0.76, 1.57)`. On p.8 the abbreviation line prints `RR = risk difference`; the p.8 footnote instead says binary outcomes report “relative risks,” estimated by robust Poisson regression.
- **Rule and calculation:** An abbreviation must expand consistently with its header/model and displayed quantity. The ratio-form values (for example 1.09) are relative risks, whereas a risk difference is expressed on an absolute-risk scale. No tolerance applies to a categorical label conflict.
- **Diagnostic inference (not direct observation):** The isolated `risk difference` expansion is likely a label error, but the package does not identify its production cause.
- **Source-grounded alternatives:** A revised abbreviation line may have been intended; no supplied evidence supports interpreting all `RR` table values as risk differences.
- **Quality-control relevance:** A data extractor could misclassify the effect measure and its scale.
- **Human question:** Which expansion of `RR` is intended for eTable 4, and should the p.8 abbreviation line be corrected to “relative risk”?

### NUM-CAND-002 — Eligibility gestational-age upper bound differs across supplied trial documents

- **Category:** Denominator, proportion, or total inconsistency; Measure, label, or scale inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** DOC-002 protocol PDF p.4 (`22 0/7` to `27 6/7` weeks); DOC-003 manual PDF p.7 (`22 0/7` to `28 6/7`); DOC-004 SAP PDF pp.8 and 15 (`22 0/7` to `28 6/7`); DOC-001 PDF p.2 (`22w0d-28w6d`).
- **Direct observation:** These supplied documents print different upper gestational-age eligibility bounds: `27 6/7` versus `28 6/7` weeks.
- **Rule and calculation:** A gestational-age eligibility interval must use one upper boundary for the same trial version/population. This is a one-week (7-day) boundary difference; zero tolerance applies to an eligibility cutoff.
- **Diagnostic inference (not direct observation):** It may reflect a protocol amendment or version difference rather than an error in the final article.
- **Source-grounded alternatives:** The protocol may be an earlier version, while the manual, SAP, and article may reflect a later operative criterion.
- **Quality-control relevance:** Eligibility labels affect interpretation of the analysis population and reproducible extraction of the trial criterion.
- **Human question:** Which document version governed enrollment, and should the protocol record be version-labelled or reconciled with the 28 6/7-week criterion?

### NUM-CAND-003 — First-dose surfactant volume differs between the protocol synopsis and later trial documents

- **Category:** Numeric or arithmetic inconsistency; Measure, label, or scale inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** DOC-002 protocol PDF p.4 (`1.25 mL/kg` poractant alfa in each first-dose arm); DOC-003 manual PDF p.7 and p.12 (`2.5 mL/kg` first dose; `1.25 mL/kg` second dose); DOC-004 SAP PDF p.8; DOC-001 PDF p.2.
- **Direct observation:** The protocol synopsis prints first-dose poractant alfa `1.25 mL/kg`; the manual, SAP, and article print `2.5 mL/kg` first dose and identify `1.25 mL/kg` as the second dose.
- **Rule and calculation:** The same intervention’s first-dose volume and dose-order label must agree. The printed difference is 1.25 mL/kg, or a twofold difference, with zero tolerance for a dose specification.
- **Diagnostic inference (not direct observation):** This could be an obsolete synopsis version rather than a misstatement in the final article.
- **Source-grounded alternatives:** A protocol amendment may have changed the first dose; the supplied sources do not provide amendment/version control that resolves it.
- **Quality-control relevance:** Dose volume can be copied into trial-characteristic extraction or intervention replication.
- **Human question:** What first-dose poractant volume governed the trial, and should the protocol synopsis be corrected or version-labelled?

### NUM-CAND-006 — First interim nominal alpha differs by one decimal place between protocol and SAP

- **Category:** Numeric or arithmetic inconsistency; Statistical reporting inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** DOC-002 protocol PDF p.29 (`0.00015` at first interim); DOC-004 SAP PDF pp.26-28 (`.000015` at first interim); both list `.0030`, `.0183`, and `.0440` for later looks/final analysis.
- **Direct observation:** The first interim nominal alpha is printed as `0.00015` in the protocol and `.000015` in the SAP; the remaining listed alpha values match.
- **Rule and calculation:** A matched Lan-DeMets/O'Brien-Fleming interim plan should state one first-look alpha. The values differ by factor 10 (0.00015 / 0.000015 = 10); zero tolerance applies to a nominal threshold.
- **Diagnostic inference (not direct observation):** This may reflect a version amendment or a decimal transcription difference. The final paper reports termination for futility, not efficacy, and does not identify which first-look boundary was operative.
- **Source-grounded alternatives:** One document may be superseded; the passages lack an amendment history that resolves the conflict.
- **Quality-control relevance:** An interim boundary may be extracted as a design/statistical-plan characteristic.
- **Human question:** Which first interim alpha was operative, and should the differing protocol/SAP value be reconciled or version-labelled?
