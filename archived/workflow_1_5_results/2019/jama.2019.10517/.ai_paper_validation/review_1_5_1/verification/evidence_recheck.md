## C001 — Table 5 absolute difference does not reproduce from the displayed counts

- **Status:** Pending Human Adjudication
- **Cited location found:** Yes. [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 9](../../../jama_flint_2019_oi_190079.pdf#page=9), Table 5, contains the total-cholesterol and LDL rows cited in the ledger.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. Each of the two rows prints `9 (14.1)` under sertraline plus olanzapine, whose column header is `n = 64`, and `6 (9.7)` under sertraline plus placebo, whose column header is `n = 62`.
- **Comparator matched:** Yes. Each row prints an `Absolute Unadjusted Difference Between Groups, % (95% CI)` of `4.3 (-8 to 17.2)`. The same count, percentage, point difference, and interval are printed in both rows. Footnote `a` defines an incident high-fasting value; footnote `b`, which states `Exact confidence interval`, is marked only on the triglyceride and glucose rows and does not supply a different point-estimate rule for the two cited rows.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes, conditionally on the table headers supplying the event denominators for the displayed unadjusted proportions. Under the ordinary raw risk-difference identity, the first group proportion minus the second group proportion is the absolute unadjusted difference. The PDF does not state a different estimator or a rounding convention for this column.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Yes. `9 / 64 = 0.140625`, or `14.0625%`, which displays as `14.1%` to one decimal. `6 / 62 = 0.09677419`, or approximately `9.6774%`, which displays as `9.7%`. The unrounded raw difference is `(9 / 64 - 6 / 62) x 100 = 4.38508065` percentage points, which is `4.4` to one decimal under nearest rounding. Subtracting the two printed percentages also gives `14.1 - 9.7 = 4.4` percentage points. The table prints `4.3`.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The two event counts, the two group sizes in the column headers, the displayed percentages, and the printed point difference are available. These inputs are sufficient to reproduce the ordinary raw difference.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The source does not state whether the point difference was truncated rather than rounded to nearest, whether a point estimator other than the raw difference was used, whether row-specific denominators different from the column-header group sizes were used, or whether unprinted precision or another analysis population entered the point estimate.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** A truncation convention would display `4.385...` as `4.3`, but the table does not declare truncation. Alternatively, the point estimate could use undocumented row-specific denominators or another estimator; no such denominator or estimator is identified on the cited page. The repeated `4.3 (-8 to 17.2)` in two rows with identical displayed counts is also compatible with intentional reuse of the same result, but it does not identify how `4.3` was obtained.
- **Direct observation:** The PDF directly prints the two `9/64` versus `6/62` displays and the repeated `4.3` point difference. Direct arithmetic from those printed values gives approximately `4.3851` percentage points.
- **Inferred explanation:** Truncation, hidden denominators, an undocumented estimator, or a production-entry mechanism are possible explanations; none is established by the supplied page.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Was `4.3%` calculated by truncating the raw difference, by using row-specific denominators or another point estimator not shown in Table 5, or is the intended one-decimal absolute unadjusted difference `4.4%` under nearest rounding?

## C002 — HbA1c interaction is labelled with a concentration unit while matched displays use percent

- **Status:** Pending Human Adjudication
- **Cited location found:** Yes. [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 1](../../../jama_flint_2019_oi_190079.pdf#page=1), abstract Results; [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 7](../../../jama_flint_2019_oi_190079.pdf#page=7), Secondary Outcomes; [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 8](../../../jama_flint_2019_oi_190079.pdf#page=8), Table 4; and [DOC-003 eFigures — PDF p. 9](../../../joi190079supp2_prod.pdf#page=9), eFigure 8, contain the cited HbA1c displays.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. Main-paper pages 1 and 7 both print the HbA1c daily treatment-by-linear-time result as `-0.0002 mg/dL` with `95% CI, -0.0021 to 0.0016`. Page 7 additionally prints `adjusted P = .99` and states that the result concerns the daily rate of anthropometric and metabolic measures.
- **Comparator matched:** Yes. Main-paper Table 4 on page 8 labels the HbA1c row `HbA1c, %`. eFigure 8 on DOC-003 page 9 is titled as mixed-model estimated least-square means of HbA1c in the randomized groups, uses `Day` on the horizontal axis, and labels the vertical axis `HbA1c (%)`.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. Repeated displays of the same named HbA1c outcome and its longitudinal model should use compatible unit/scale labels unless the source defines a separate transformation or concentration-scale outcome. The supplied cited pages define no transformation connecting `mg/dL` and `%` for HbA1c.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The logical label comparison is reproduced: `mg/dL` is the unit printed with the treatment-by-time coefficient in two main-paper locations, while `%` is the unit printed for HbA1c in Table 4 and on the matched longitudinal model figure. No numeric conversion is reproduced because the package supplies no conversion rule or transformed-variable definition. The coefficient and confidence interval match exactly between main-paper pages 1 and 7, so the comparison concerns their repeated unit label rather than a discrepancy between their numeric estimates.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The repeated coefficient and confidence interval, the narrative description as a daily treatment-by-linear-time interaction, the Table 4 HbA1c unit, the eFigure 8 outcome unit, and the figure time axis are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The model coefficient's intended dimensional unit, any outcome transformation, any conversion rule, and any definition of a distinct concentration-scale HbA1c variable are absent. The cited text also does not explicitly print a per-day unit suffix beside the coefficient even though it describes a daily rate.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The model may have used a transformed HbA1c variable whose coefficient was intended to have a different unit, but neither Table 4, eFigure 8, nor the cited narrative defines such a variable. The repeated `mg/dL` could instead be a shared production label carried over from the surrounding lipid and glucose results. The supplied sources do not distinguish these possibilities or establish the intended replacement label.
- **Direct observation:** The two narrative locations directly print `mg/dL`; the table and longitudinal figure directly print `%` for HbA1c. No cited source location declares a transformation between them.
- **Inferred explanation:** A production-label carryover or an unreported transformed analysis could explain the conflicting labels, but either explanation is inferential.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Did the HbA1c treatment-by-linear-time model use the percent-scale HbA1c outcome displayed in Table 4 and eFigure 8, making `mg/dL` an unintended label, or did it use a distinct transformed outcome whose unit and transformation need to be specified?

## C003 — Protocol recruitment target conflicts between 82 and 98 participants per site

- **Status:** Pending Human Adjudication
- **Cited location found:** Yes. [DOC-002 protocol/SAP — PDF p. 7](../../../joi190079supp1_prod.pdf#page=7), Figure 1 and Section 3.9.1; [DOC-002 protocol/SAP — PDF p. 17](../../../joi190079supp1_prod.pdf#page=17), Table 4; and [DOC-002 protocol/SAP — PDF p. 21](../../../joi190079supp1_prod.pdf#page=21), Section 4.2.1, contain the cited recruitment statements.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. Page 7 Figure 1 prints `Acute Phase (N=392)`, and the adjacent General Overview states that `392 patients` will be enrolled `across 4 sites`. Page 17 Table 4 states that all four sites perform subject recruitment and that `Each site will recruit 98 acutely ill participants and randomize 44 remitted participants`.
- **Comparator matched:** Yes. Page 21 Section 4.2.1 states that the proposed multicentered study will involve `four research sites`, with `each site recruiting 82 patients` aged 18 years or older with the stated psychotic-depression population.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes, if the page 21 recruitment statement concerns the same acute-study recruitment total as pages 7 and 17. For four equally contributing sites, the per-site recruitment number multiplied by four should reproduce the stated total unless a different population, phase, target, or document version is identified.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Yes. `98 x 4 = 392`, exactly reproducing the page 7 acute-phase total. `82 x 4 = 328`, which is `64` fewer than `392`. The two per-site statements differ by `98 - 82 = 16` participants per site. The page 17 randomization target separately gives `44 x 4 = 176`, matching the page 7 Discontinuation RCT total of `N=176`; it does not account for the 82-versus-98 recruitment difference.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The number of sites, acute total, per-site acute recruitment target of 98, per-site recruitment statement of 82, and per-site randomization target of 44 are all printed in the same supplied protocol/SAP PDF.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The source does not identify a different recruitment phase or subset for the 82/site statement, an amendment date linking the two targets, a version history, or a denominator definition that maps 82/site to the 392 acute total. It also does not state that the page 21 human-subjects count excludes any named group included in the page 7 or page 17 target.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The 82/site statement may refer to an earlier or amended target, a human-subjects subset, or a different recruitment denominator, while 98/site may refer to all acutely ill participants. Those interpretations are compatible with the document containing planning material in different sections, but no cited passage labels a distinct version, subset, or phase for 82/site.
- **Direct observation:** One supplied PDF directly prints four sites and 392 acute participants on page 7, 98 recruited acute participants per site on page 17, and 82 recruited patients per site on page 21. The first two quantities reconcile arithmetically; the third does not reconcile with them under a common four-site target.
- **Inferred explanation:** An earlier target, amendment, or unlabelled population distinction could explain the difference, but the supplied locations do not establish one.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Do `82 patients` per site and `98 acutely ill participants` per site refer to different protocol versions, recruitment phases, or defined populations; if so, what is that distinction, and if not, which per-site number defines the intended four-site acute recruitment total of 392?
