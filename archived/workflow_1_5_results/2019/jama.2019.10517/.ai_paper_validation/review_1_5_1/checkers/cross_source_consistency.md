# Cross-Source Consistency Review

## Scope, matching rule, and outcome

This review covers the complete current relationship inventory: 60 numeric/reporting relationships (`N001`-`N060`) and 48 inferential/statistical relationships (`S001`-`S048`). I compared only occurrences that could describe the same quantity after matching the population, phase/time, contrast, model, measure, scale/unit, reference group, analysis set, and displayed precision. Protocol/SAP values were treated as planned or historical unless the text presents the same result construct as the published trial; a planned value and an observed trial value are not a conflict merely because they differ. Figure traces without printed point estimates or a defined uncertainty interval were not converted into exact comparisons.

Direct-source authority was the four supplied PDFs: [main article](../../../jama_flint_2019_oi_190079.pdf#page=1) (PDF pp. 1-10), [protocol/SAP](../../../joi190079supp1_prod.pdf#page=1) (PDF pp. 1-42), [eFigures](../../../joi190079supp2_prod.pdf#page=1) (PDF pp. 1-10), and [data-sharing supplement](../../../joi190079supp3_prod.pdf#page=1) (PDF p. 1). The current mapper artifacts were used as locators and relationship definitions, not as source authority. Direct PDF page text/visual inspection was used to confirm the two leads below.

Results: 108/108 assigned relationships received an explicit cross-source check. Two distinct candidate leads meet the source-location and concrete-quantitative-inconsistency threshold for downstream registration and human verification. No stable candidate ID, AI judgment, severity, validity, or disposition is assigned here.

## Qualifying candidate leads for registration

### Lead A — HbA1c daily interaction is labeled in a concentration unit although the matched outcome is percentage

- **Category:** Measure, label, or scale inconsistency; cross-document numeric inconsistency.
- **Exact linked locations:** The main-article abstract prints “HbA1c, −0.0002 mg/dL (95% CI, −0.0021 to 0.0016)” as a daily treatment-by-time effect ([main PDF p. 1](../../../jama_flint_2019_oi_190079.pdf#page=1)). The results narrative repeats “HbA1c levels (−0.0002 mg/dL [95% CI, −0.0021 to 0.0016])” in the series of daily interaction effects ([main PDF p. 7](../../../jama_flint_2019_oi_190079.pdf#page=7)). In the same main article, Table 4 labels the measure “HbA1c, %” and reports the baseline-to-termination values in that scale ([main PDF p. 8](../../../jama_flint_2019_oi_190079.pdf#page=8)); Table 2 identifies HbA1c among the metabolic measures in the same trial ([main PDF p. 6](../../../jama_flint_2019_oi_190079.pdf#page=6)). The matched randomized-group eFigure is explicitly titled “Mixed Model Estimated Least Square Means of HbA1c” and labels its y axis “HbA1c (%)” ([eFigures PDF p. 9](../../../joi190079supp2_prod.pdf#page=9)). The protocol calls HbA1c a secondary glycemic measure ([protocol/SAP PDF p. 11](../../../joi190079supp1_prod.pdf#page=11)).
- **Printed values compared:** The point estimate and interval are identically printed as `−0.0002` and `−0.0021 to 0.0016` at main PDF pp. 1 and 7, but their printed unit is `mg/dL`. The directly matched Table 4 and eFigure outcome use `%`.
- **Comparison logic:** Population is the randomized trial; the abstract and results occurrence are the same treatment-by-time model result (same estimate and interval, same displayed precision). Table 4 is not used as a numerical comparator because it is an unadjusted within-arm baseline-to-termination table with different analysis details. It is valid scale evidence because it labels the same named outcome in the trial. The eFigure is a mixed-model randomized-group display and independently labels the same outcome `%`. HbA1c is therefore presented as a percent-scale outcome in the matched source displays, while the main paper labels its matched interaction estimate with a concentration unit.
- **Supported alternatives:** The values and interval may be correctly computed on the HbA1c percentage-point scale and only the `mg/dL` unit may be a copy/editing label error. The supplied sources do not give a conversion or an alternative HbA1c concentration scale that would make the printed unit directly verifiable. The protocol’s statement that HbA1c is secondary establishes outcome identity, not the unit of the published interaction.
- **Human verification steps:** Inspect the final statistical output/data dictionary for the HbA1c analysis variable and its unit; confirm whether `−0.0002` is percentage points per day; check the production source for whether `mg/dL` was carried from adjacent glucose/lipid rows; preserve the point estimate, interval, model, and population while correcting only a confirmed unit label.

### Lead B — protocol/SAP gives incompatible per-site acute recruitment totals without a stated population or time distinction

- **Category:** Cross-document numeric inconsistency; denominator, proportion, or total inconsistency.
- **Exact linked locations:** Protocol/SAP Figure 1 prints `Acute Phase (N=392)`, with 196 participants in each age group ([protocol/SAP PDF p. 7](../../../joi190079supp1_prod.pdf#page=7)). The recruitment narrative states that the study plans to recruit `392 patients` to the acute phase and identifies a 38-month recruitment period ([protocol/SAP PDF p. 8](../../../joi190079supp1_prod.pdf#page=8)). Table 4 says each of the four sites “will recruit 98 acutely ill participants” and randomize 44 remitted participants ([protocol/SAP PDF p. 17](../../../joi190079supp1_prod.pdf#page=17)). In the human-subjects section, the same protocol says the study involves four research sites, “with each site recruiting 82 patients 18 years or older with non-bipolar major depression with psychotic features” ([protocol/SAP PDF p. 21](../../../joi190079supp1_prod.pdf#page=21)).
- **Printed values compared:** `392` acute participants / `98` acute participants per site across four sites versus `82` patients per site across four sites. Arithmetic gives `4 × 98 = 392` and `4 × 82 = 328`.
- **Comparison logic:** The p. 7 figure, p. 8 recruitment narrative, and p. 17 table explicitly identify the planned acute/recruitment population and reconcile internally. The p. 21 statement identifies the same protocol, four research sites, recruitment action, adult age threshold, and psychotic-depression population, but gives 82 per site. No text at p. 21 labels 82 as a different phase, subset, site count, time period, or an older proposal version. Thus the direct printed planned recruitment denominators cannot all describe one common four-site acute recruitment target.
- **Supported alternatives:** The p. 21 number may be an obsolete human-subjects/template value; it may refer to a narrower eligibility subset; or an omitted amendment may define a distinct time frame. Those explanations are not printed in the supplied pages and cannot be selected here. The published main article’s observed 350 assessed/269 acute entrants/126 randomized ([main PDF p. 3](../../../jama_flint_2019_oi_190079.pdf#page=3)) is a later actual flow, not a comparator that resolves or creates this within-protocol planned-total conflict.
- **Human verification steps:** Check the approved protocol version history, IRB/human-subjects amendment, and grant/budget table for the denominator intended on p. 21; determine whether 82 means per site, per period, or a subset; then make all recruitment-plan displays state the same target or label their distinct populations/time frames.

## Noncandidate explanations and limitations

- The abstract completion fraction `114/126` and Figure 1 terminal-status counts are not matched without a printed completion definition; this is documented as an unresolved definition, not a quantitative inconsistency.
- Planned protocol recruitment, sample-size, attrition, dose, and power values can differ from later actual trial values because population/time/implementation are different. They were not called differences unless the protocol itself gave incompatible values for the same stated target (Lead B).
- Table 4’s unadjusted changes and the main interaction coefficients have distinct models/analysis sets; the table footnote expressly warns that displayed raw baseline/termination values do not necessarily reproduce its reported changes.
- The eFigures supply graphical least-square means but not point tables, sample sizes at each time, interval type, confidence level, model terms, or exact coefficient precision. Their visual positions therefore support outcome/scale matching only.
- No supplied structured dataset, workbook, or CSV exists. The data-sharing supplement contains availability conditions, not trial results.
