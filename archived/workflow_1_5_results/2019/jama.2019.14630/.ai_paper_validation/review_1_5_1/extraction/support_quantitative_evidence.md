### SUP-N002 — dietary-score labels, ranges and point rules, DOC-004 PDF p. 2

er-MedDiet is 0-17 points; MDS (Trichopoulou) 0-9; MEDAS 0-14; PDQS 0-42. MDS positive components are scored one point at or above the sex-specific median and negative components one point at or below it. PDQS positive and negative components each use 0 points at the adverse/extreme frequency, 1 at 2-3 servings/week, and 2 at the favourable/extreme frequency; exact direction and thresholds are retained in the page-matched layout transcription. The score definitions establish that higher plotted scores are higher dietary quality (also stated in eFigure 1).

## Statistical definition map

### SUP-S001 — trial design/randomization, DOC-003 PDF p. 6

Six-year parallel multicentre RCT, 6,874 participants from 23 centres. Primary CVD composite: non-fatal MI, non-fatal stroke, cardiovascular death. Randomization stratified by centre, sex, age <65/65-70/>70 in blocks of six; same-household members together, later household member assigned (not randomized).

### SUP-S002 — CVD sample-size assumptions, DOC-003 PDF pp. 6-7

Two-tailed alpha .05; anticipated >=10% control cumulative incidence after six years; anticipated HR .702; 20% dropout; 2,400/group required. Conservative plan 6,000 (3,000/group), 23 centres at approximately 300 each (150/group).

### SUP-S003 — weight sample-size assumptions, DOC-003 PDF p. 7

Control minimum change, intervention loss 3-4.5 kg, SD 8 kg; assumed control +1 kg and intervention +3 kg, net 2 kg; .80 power needs 337/group.

### SUP-S004 — interim testing/timing, DOC-003 PDF p. 8

O'Brien-Fleming preserves overall alpha .05. Planned stopping P thresholds: 3-year median follow-up .0005; 5-year .014; final 8-year .045. Active intervention is six years, follow-up two further years; diabetes assessed at seven years (anticipated 620 incident cases); Parkinson disease/cancer at 10 years.

### SUP-S005 — outcome/time-point/clinical-meaning definitions, DOC-003 PDF pp. 9-12

Table 1 gives primary/secondary/intermediate outcome times. Continuous SBP additionally evaluated as 5-mm-Hg reduction; triglycerides 10% reduction; HDL 5% increase; LDL 5% reduction. Weight/waist duplicate yearly measurement; blood at odd-year visits and eight years.

### SUP-S006 — analysis populations and adherence censoring, DOC-003 PDF p. 13

ITT retains randomized allocation with complete-case and multiple-imputation analyses. Intervention per-protocol censoring: er-MedDiet <12 at two consecutive annual assessments or persistent PA/fitness decrease; for non-weight outcomes also persistent baseline-weight gain. Control censored for 14-item ad-libitum MedDiet score <10 at two annual assessments. As-treated classification uses diet adherence, weight loss and PA; mediation approach targets direct/indirect CVD effects.

### SUP-S007 — planned baseline and endpoint summaries, DOC-003 pp. 14-17

Categorical variables as frequency/percentage; quantitative traits as mean/SD. Outcome definitions supplied for MI and all named secondary diagnoses. These are definitions, not inferential results.

### SUP-S008 — CVD and continuous-outcome model, DOC-003 pp. 25-26

Cox models: follow-up from randomization until first endpoint, last visit/contact, withdrawal, censoring date, or death. Weight/BMI/waist: repeated-measure multilevel mixed-effects linear regression with random intercept, household intra-cluster correlation and centre random factor; assess within- and between-group changes. Clinically meaningful: 5% weight loss (baseline 86.5 kg -> 4.3 kg), BMI 5% (32.5 -> 1.6 kg/m2), waist 5% (108 -> 5.4 cm).

### SUP-S009 — adjustment/assumption rules, DOC-003 pp. 25-26

Primary ITT crude with robust household-cluster variance and centre-stratified Cox. Combined outcome additional covariates: centre, sex, age, education, smoking, baseline hypertension/dyslipidemia/diabetes, family history CHD, BMI, waist-height ratio, PA MET-min/day. Schoenfeld residual and time-varying-covariate tests assess proportional hazards; strata then follow-up-period effects if non-proportional.

### SUP-S010 — planned sensitivity/subgroups/missingness, DOC-003 p. 26

Completers-only ITT is main; MI ITT sensitivity; primary-outcome per-protocol. Subgroups: sex, median-age cut-point, education two categories, diabetes, MetS 3 versus >=4, smoking, obese/non-obese BMI, baseline er-MedDiet score. Chained-equation MI creates 20 imputations for measurements missing among those lost >=2 years; predictors are listed in the source transcription.

### SUP-S011 — per-protocol notation/censoring weights, DOC-003 pp. 26-27

Aj=1 adherent and 0 otherwise; artificial censoring at interval end when Aj=0. Cj indicates incomplete-follow-up censoring; Nj attendance (1 yes/0 no); among Nj=1, Rj missing adherence information (1 yes/0 no). Inverse-probability weights address selection bias assuming mechanisms are randomized conditional on measured pre/post-randomization prognostic factors.

### SUP-S012 — harms reporting, DOC-003 p. 27

Headache, fatigue, constipation, increased bowel rhythm collected at six months/yearly and planned as intervention-group percentages.

### SUP-S013 — protocol historical model and cluster rule, DOC-002 pp. 52-53

As SUP-S004/SUP-S008, with historical protocol language specifying Cox for CVD, mixed-model ANOVA/GEE for weight/waist; test PH/linearity/outliers; centre stratification/frailty and robust household variance. This is a matching source for SAP comparison, not a current study result.
