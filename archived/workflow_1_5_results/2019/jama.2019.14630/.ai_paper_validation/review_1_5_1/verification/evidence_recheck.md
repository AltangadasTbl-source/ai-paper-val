- **Consistency rule applicable:** `At least x` denotes an inclusive boundary (`>= x`), whereas `>x` denotes a strict boundary. For diastolic blood pressure, the Methods translate to `>= 2.5 mm Hg`, while the figure supplies the magnitude `2.5 mm Hg` without an operator. Thus the printed boundary expressions are not logically identical at equality.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The Methods threshold magnitudes and inclusive wording, the Figure 4 threshold magnitudes and displayed operators, the outcome identities, and the reported classification percentages are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The classification code, the operational comparison operators, the precision at which each participant's change was compared with the threshold, counts of participants exactly on each boundary, and an author statement identifying the intended wording are not supplied.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** Figure 4 may use `>` as compact typography for the Methods' inclusive phrase, and the diastolic operator may have been omitted typographically. If no analyzed observation was exactly on a boundary, the two operators could yield identical displayed percentages, but boundary-case counts are absent.
- **Direct observation:** The Methods use the inclusive phrase `at least`; Figure 4 prints strict greater-than signs for ten classifications and no operator for the diastolic classification.
- **Inferred explanation:** Whether the analysis implemented inclusive or strict comparisons, and whether any participant lay exactly on a threshold, cannot be inferred from the displayed percentages alone.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which comparison operator and measurement precision were implemented for each Figure 4 classification, how many analyzed participants were exactly at each threshold, and which Methods or figure labels should state the implemented rule?
