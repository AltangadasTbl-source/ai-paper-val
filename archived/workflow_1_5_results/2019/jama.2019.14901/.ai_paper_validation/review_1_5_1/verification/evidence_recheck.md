## C001 — Day-7 respiratory-failure absolute difference differs across matched article locations

- **Cited location found:** The abstract result is present in [the main article, PDF #page=1](<../../../jama_thille_2019_oi_190108.pdf#page=1>); the Secondary Outcomes narrative is present in [the main article, PDF #page=6](<../../../jama_thille_2019_oi_190108.pdf#page=6>); and the matched Table 2 row is present in [the main article, PDF #page=8](<../../../jama_thille_2019_oi_190108.pdf#page=8>).
- **Source printed value/text matched:** PDF pp. 1 and 6 print postextubation respiratory failure at day 7 as 21% versus 29%, with a difference of -8.7% (95% CI, -15.2% to -1.8%; P = .01). The narrative order is high-flow nasal oxygen with NIV versus high-flow nasal oxygen alone.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** Table 2 on PDF p. 8 prints 88 (29) for high-flow nasal oxygen alone (n = 302), 70 (21) for high-flow nasal oxygen with NIV (n = 339), and an absolute difference of -8.5% (95% CI, -15.2% to -1.8%; P = .01).
- **Consistency rule applicable:** For the same population, endpoint, day, treatment contrast, interval, and P value, the absolute-difference point estimate should agree across article locations after allowing only the displayed precision. The treatment-minus-control difference can also be calculated from the printed counts and denominators.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `70 / 339 = 0.206489676` and `88 / 302 = 0.291390728`; therefore, `(70 / 339 - 88 / 302) x 100 = -8.490105296` percentage points, which rounds to -8.5 percentage points at one decimal. The printed -8.7 and -8.5 values differ by 0.2 percentage points. Subtracting the integer percentages alone gives `21 - 29 = -8`, so the narrative's -8.7 is not recoverable from those rounded percentages either.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The package supplies the two group counts, group denominators, group labels, endpoint, time point, contrast direction, absolute-difference values, confidence interval, and P value. These inputs are sufficient to reproduce the count-derived point difference and the cross-location comparison.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not supply the unrounded analysis output behind -8.7, a separate analysis-population or model label for that value, or a calculation rule that distinguishes the narrative result from Table 2. The method used to obtain -8.7 is therefore not available.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The narrative could contain a value retained from a different analysis output or editing stage, while Table 2 could report the direct difference from the displayed counts. No distinct population, endpoint, contrast, or model is labelled on the cited pages, so this explanation remains an inference.
- **Direct observation:** The source directly shows -8.7 on pp. 1 and 6 and -8.5 on p. 8 for otherwise matching result descriptors; it directly shows identical confidence limits and P values at those locations.
- **Inferred explanation:** A retained earlier estimate, undocumented alternate computation, or transcription/editing difference could have produced the two point estimates. The supplied pages do not identify which mechanism occurred.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which absolute difference was intended for this matched result, and is there an analysis output or documented calculation distinction that supports -8.7 rather than the count-derived and Table 2 value of -8.5?

## C002 — Reintubation respiratory-acidosis cutoff differs between article and protocol

- **Source printed value/text matched:** The main article states that patients were immediately reintubated if one listed criterion was fulfilled. One criterion is severe respiratory failure, defined by at least two listed findings, including respiratory acidosis defined as a pH level below 7.25 units and PaCO2 greater than 45 mm Hg.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** Protocol version 4, dated October 17, 2017, states in section 5.4 that reintubation will be performed according to one listed criterion. Its severe-respiratory-failure criterion requires at least two acute-respiratory-failure criteria and prints respiratory acidosis as `pH < 7.35 units and PaCO2 > 45 mm Hg` across the p. 30-to-p. 31 continuation.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** A threshold used for the same physiological component of the same reintubation decision rule should agree across the article and supplied protocol unless a dated amendment or a separately labelled operational definition distinguishes them.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The criterion role, requirement for at least two respiratory-failure findings, PaCO2 threshold, PaCO2 unit, and reintubation decision context match. The pH cutoffs differ: `7.35 - 7.25 = 0.10` pH units.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The source supplies both complete threshold pairs, their units, their placement within the severe-respiratory-failure criteria, the reintubation context, and the date/version printed on the protocol.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not supply an amendment history, a later approved protocol version, the operative case-report-form definition, or an event-adjudication instruction that identifies which cutoff governed ascertainment. It also does not state that the article intentionally changed the supplied protocol threshold.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** Within the article itself, pH below 7.35 with PaCO2 above 45 mm Hg defines postextubation respiratory failure, whereas the more severe reintubation criterion uses pH below 7.25. The article may therefore intentionally distinguish respiratory failure from severe respiratory failure. The supplied protocol, however, prints pH below 7.35 in both its acute-respiratory-failure definition and its severe-respiratory-failure reintubation criterion; a later refinement or amendment could explain the article-protocol difference, but none is supplied.
- **Direct observation:** The source directly prints `<7.25` in the article and `<7.35` in protocol section 5.4, while printing the same `PaCO2 >45 mm Hg` condition and the same reintubation-rule role.
- **Inferred explanation:** A protocol amendment, an implementation-specific refinement, or a protocol transcription issue could account for the changed pH threshold. The package does not identify which, if any, occurred.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which pH cutoff governed reintubation event ascertainment, and is there a dated approved amendment or operational definition documenting a change from `<7.35` to `<7.25`?

- **Consistency rule applicable:** In a cell labelled `No./total No. (%)`, the percentage should equal the printed numerator divided by the printed denominator, subject to whole-percent rounding. The mutually partitioned hypercapnic and nonhypercapnic fractions should also sum to the matching whole-group fraction.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `14 / 45 x 100 = 31.1111%`, which rounds to 31%, not 69%; `16 / 59 x 100 = 27.1186%`, which rounds to 27%, not 73%. The P value cannot distinguish a category from its complement because the two-category comparison is unchanged by reversing both groups.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The percentages 69% and 73% exactly describe the complementary counts, 31/45 and 43/59, which could represent effective cough. Under that interpretation, the percentages could be numerically intended but would not match the printed ineffective-cough fractions and row label.
- **Direct observation:** The source directly prints fractions of 14/45 and 16/59 beside 69% and 73% under the ineffective-cough label. It directly prints stratum fractions that add to the main-article totals.
- **Inferred explanation:** Complementary coding or a transcription mismatch among the numerator, percentage, and row label could produce the cells. The source does not identify which component, if any, carries the intended meaning.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What do 69% and 73% denote in the hypercapnic ineffective-cough cells, and should the cells instead show 31% and 27%, different complementary numerators, or a different row label?

## C005 — Matched nonhypercapnic day-7 reintubation P values differ across article and supplement

- **Source printed value/text matched:** The article states that among 530 patients with PaCO2 of 45 mm Hg or less, day-7 reintubation was 13% with NIV versus 18% with high-flow nasal oxygen alone, with a difference of -5.0% (95% CI, -11.2% to 1.1%; P = .10).
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** eTable 4 is headed `Nonhypercapnic Patients (PaCO2 <=45 mm Hg)` and prints high-flow nasal oxygen alone (n = 254) followed by high-flow nasal oxygen with NIV (n = 276). Its day-7 reintubation row prints 45 (18), 35 (13), an absolute difference of -5.0 (95% CI, -11.2 to 1.1), and P = .1057.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** When population, endpoint, time point, group sizes, counts, contrast direction, effect estimate, and confidence interval match, the attached P value should also agree after applying a stated test and display rule. Under ordinary decimal rounding, .1057 rounds to .11 at two decimal places, not .10.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `45 / 254 x 100 = 17.7165%` and `35 / 276 x 100 = 12.6812%`; therefore, the NIV-minus-alone difference is `-5.0354` percentage points, reproducing the printed -5.0 after one-decimal rounding. A diagnostic two-sided uncorrected Pearson comparison of the two printed proportions gives pooled proportion `80/530 = 0.150943`, standard error `0.0311273`, `z = 1.61767`, chi-square `2.61686`, and approximately `P = .1057`, reproducing the supplement value. This diagnostic does not establish that every cited location used that exact test setting.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The package supplies the population definition, group sizes, event counts, proportions, effect estimate, confidence interval, two printed P values, and a general article-methods statement that proportions at predefined times were compared by a chi-square test and that tests were two-tailed.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The cited result is not tied to a complete analysis output specifying Pearson versus another chi-square form, continuity correction, exact-test use, handling of the subgroup analysis, or an editorial truncation/rounding rule. The source therefore lacks the exact test and display metadata needed to explain P = .10 if .1057 is the underlying value.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The narrative and eTable could reflect different test variants or separately formatted analysis outputs even though their displayed counts, effect, interval, and result descriptors match. The article could also have applied a nonstandard truncation or editorial display convention, but no such rule is stated in the supplied package.
- **Direct observation:** The source directly prints P = .10 in the article and P = .1057 in eTable 4 for result descriptions with matching population, counts-derived proportions, difference, and confidence limits.
- **Inferred explanation:** A different test variant, output stream, or display convention could produce the discrepancy. The supplied pages do not identify which mechanism occurred.

## C006 — Protocol total-duration breakdown does not arithmetically reach the printed total

- **Cited location found:** The study-duration synopsis is present in [the protocol, PDF #page=11](<../../../joi190108supp1_prod.pdf#page=11>), and section 5.6, `Duration of the Study`, is present in [the protocol, PDF #page=32](<../../../joi190108supp1_prod.pdf#page=32>).
- **Source printed value/text matched:** The synopsis on PDF p. 11 prints an inclusion period of 36 months, participation of 3 months for each patient, and a total study duration of 51 months comprising 39 months for the study and 12 months for analysis.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** Section 5.6 on PDF p. 32 prints three consecutive lines: participation of each patient for 3 months; anticipated recruitment for 36 months; and total study duration of 51 months, with 36 months for the study and 12 months for analysis.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Components explicitly presented as the breakdown of a total duration should sum to that total. Adjacent duration components may supply a source-grounded interpretation only if their sequential or overlapping relationship is stated or otherwise made clear.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The p. 11 durations reconcile as `36 months of inclusion + 3 months of final-patient participation = 39 months for the study`, followed by `39 + 12 months for analysis = 51 months`. On p. 32, the two components named within the total-duration sentence give `36 + 12 = 48 months`, which is 3 months below the printed 51. If the adjacent 3-month participation line is added as a sequential final-participant period, then `36 + 3 + 12 = 51 months`, reproducing both the printed total and the p. 11 decomposition.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The source supplies the 36-month inclusion or recruitment duration, the 3-month participation duration, the 39-month study duration on p. 11, the 12-month analysis duration, and the 51-month total at both locations. These values are sufficient to reproduce the two displayed decompositions.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** Page 32 does not state within its total-duration sentence that the separately listed 3-month participation period follows recruitment or is included in the 51-month total. It also does not define whether analysis begins only after the final participant completes follow-up or overlaps any study period. These timing relationships are needed to interpret the p. 32 component wording literally.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The adjacent p. 32 line giving 3 months of participation for each patient may be intended as the otherwise unnamed period after the 36 months of recruitment, so that the total is `36 + 3 + 12 = 51`. Page 11 supports this interpretation by explicitly naming 39 months for the study and 12 months for analysis. Under that reading, the p. 32 phrase `36 months for the study` would function as shorthand for the recruitment component rather than the complete pre-analysis study period.
- **Direct observation:** Page 11 directly prints 36 months of inclusion, 3 months of participation, 39 months for the study, 12 months for analysis, and 51 months total. Page 32 directly prints the adjacent 3-month participation line and 36-month recruitment line, but its total-duration sentence directly names only 36 months for the study plus 12 months for analysis while printing 51 months total.
- **Inferred explanation:** It is an inference that p. 32 intended the separately printed 3-month participation period to follow recruitment and complete the total without repeating it in the breakdown sentence. The source does not explicitly state that relationship on p. 32.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Should p. 32 state 39 months for the study plus 12 months for analysis, or should it explicitly state that the adjacent 3-month final-participant follow-up is added to 36 months of recruitment to produce the printed 51-month total?
