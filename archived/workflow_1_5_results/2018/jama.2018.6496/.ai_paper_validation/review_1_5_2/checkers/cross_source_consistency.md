# Cross-source consistency check

## Scope, method, and outcome

This check covers all 47 numeric/reporting relationships (`N001`-`N047`) and all 37 inferential-statistical relationships (`S001`-`S037`) in the current canonical inventories. It used only the fresh evidence maps and fresh native/layout assets, with the supplied PDFs consulted at the cited exact pages. It did not use any preserved audit derivative, candidate set, report, web source, or external literature.

Each comparison was matched before evaluation on population, time, intervention contrast, analysis set, model/adjustment, effect measure, scale, unit, reference group, and displayed precision. A difference between an unclustered main-table result and a physician-clustered supplementary result is therefore recorded as model-distinct rather than as a conflict. Percentages are treated as printed rounded displays unless a displayed count and denominator contradict them beyond that precision.

**Matched-comparison total:** 84 relationship records (47 `N`, 37 `S`), with 118 direct occurrence-to-occurrence comparisons because several records have more than two matching locations.

**Provisional candidate-record total:** 4. These are discovery records only; they have no stable candidate ID, severity, validity decision, or disposition.

## Provisional candidate record 1 — reversed displayed confidence-interval endpoints for all-patient duration

**Potential category:** Statistical reporting inconsistency.

**Exact linked location:** [DOC-001 Table 3, PDF p. 7](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=7), row “First-attempt duration, median (IQR), s,” all patients.

**Printed value:** Bougie median 38 (IQR 29 to 51) seconds; ETT+stylet median 36 (IQR 25 to 54) seconds; Difference column prints `1 (4 to −1)`; P=.24.

**Direct observation:** The table footnote defines that column as the median difference and its 95% CI. Its printed lower endpoint is 4 and its printed upper endpoint is -1.

**Comparison logic/rule:** A displayed confidence interval must list its lower endpoint before its upper endpoint. Here, 4 is greater than -1, so `4 to −1` is reverse-ordered. This issue does not depend on reconstructing the analysis or treating the median difference as ordinary subtraction.

**Inference:** The surrounding value `1` lies between -1 and 4, so a transposed endpoint order (`-1 to 4`) is a source-grounded possible explanation, not an asserted correction. The physician-clustered eTable prints the corresponding clustered result as `1 s (-1 s to 4 s)` ([DOC-003 p. 2](../../../joi180054supp2_prod.pdf#page=2)), but that is a different adjusted analysis and cannot itself establish what the unclustered interval should have been.

**Supported alternatives:** PDF table composition/order error; an unusual but unstated interval-display convention; or a source-specific transcription error. The source does not identify the production mechanism.

**Human verification question:** Does the unclustered Table 3 analysis output give the interval endpoints as -1 and 4 seconds, and was the printed order reversed during table production?

## Provisional candidate record 2 — mismatched ETT+stylet denominator for linked hypoxemia outcomes

**Potential category:** Denominator, proportion, or total inconsistency.

**Exact linked locations:** Main Table 3, all-patient “First-attempt intubation success without hypoxemia,” [DOC-001 PDF p. 7](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=7); main Table 5, “Hypoxemia, No. of patients/total patients with data (%),” [DOC-001 PDF p. 9](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=9).

**Printed values:** Table 3 prints ETT+stylet `282/366 (77%)` for success without hypoxemia. Table 5 prints ETT+stylet `50/364 (14%)` for hypoxemia. In the Bougie column, both linked outcomes use the same denominator, 371 (`317/371` and `47/371`).

**Direct observation:** Both table footnotes use the same hypoxemia threshold and state that valid pulse-oximetry waveform during intubation was not available for all patients. The two ETT+stylet denominators nevertheless differ by two patients (366 versus 364).

**Comparison logic/rule:** “Success without hypoxemia” and “hypoxemia” are complementary classifications with respect to the same hypoxemia ascertainment window only after first-attempt success is separately accounted for; the denominator for the valid hypoxemia data should be traceable across the linked outcomes. The printed source does not define a separate ETT+stylet availability population for Table 5.

**Inference:** A distinct Table 5 completeness rule, an unreported exclusion of two ETT+stylet patients, or a denominator transcription/table-production difference could explain the display. This record does not infer a corrected rate or assume that the two outcomes are simple complements.

**Supported alternatives:** Outcome-specific denominator rules not stated in the footnotes; post-table data cleaning affecting one display; or a printed denominator discrepancy.

**Human verification question:** What exact ETT+stylet patient set supplied the Table 3 `282/366` outcome and the Table 5 `50/364` hypoxemia outcome, and is the two-patient difference intentional and documented?

## Provisional candidate record 3 — protocol versus published hypoxemia observation-window endpoint

**Potential category:** Measure, label, or scale inconsistency.

**Exact linked locations:** Protocol secondary-outcome definition, [DOC-002 p. 9](../../../joi180054supp1_prod.pdf#page=9); main Table 3/Table 5 hypoxemia footnote, [DOC-001 pp. 7 and 9](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=9); eTable 1 footnote, [DOC-003 p. 3](../../../joi180054supp2_prod.pdf#page=3).

**Printed values/statements:** The protocol says hypoxemia is recorded from first-attempt start through “one minute after inflation of the ETT cuff.” The main article and eTable say hypoxemia is assessed “during or within 1 min[ute] after completion of the intubation attempt”; their published duration definition ends the attempt when the blade is removed from the mouth.

**Direct observation:** The saturation threshold and baseline-low rule agree across all locations. The stated end event does not: cuff inflation in the protocol versus attempt completion/blade removal in the published sources.

**Comparison logic/rule:** These are non-identical endpoints for the outcome-observation window. Because cuff inflation and blade removal can occur at different times, the printed definitions do not define the same interval solely by wording or precision.

**Inference:** This may be a documented protocol-to-publication operational change, a shorthand description of an intended equivalent window, or a reporting inconsistency. The package does not state whether the two endpoint events were synchronized or whether a formal definition amendment occurred.

**Supported alternatives:** Planned-versus-executed measurement change; abbreviated published wording; or a definition-label mismatch. No rate/count arithmetic is inferred.

**Human verification question:** Which event was used to end hypoxemia surveillance in the analyzed data, and was the published blade-removal/attempt-completion wording an approved change from the protocol's cuff-inflation endpoint?

## Provisional candidate record 4 — protocol versus published first-attempt duration endpoint

**Potential category:** Measure, label, or scale inconsistency.

**Exact linked locations:** Protocol secondary-outcome definition, [DOC-002 p. 9](../../../joi180054supp1_prod.pdf#page=9); article outcome methods, [DOC-001 p. 3](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=3); main Table 3 duration footnote, [DOC-001 p. 7](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=7); eTable 1 duration footnote, [DOC-003 p. 3](../../../joi180054supp2_prod.pdf#page=3).

**Printed values/statements:** The protocol defines “Time to intubation (first attempt only)” as attempt beginning to “inflation of the ETT cuff when the tube is in the trachea.” The article and eTable define duration as time from laryngoscope blade entering the mouth to blade removal from the mouth. The final results reported under the latter label include all-patient medians 38 versus 36 seconds.

**Direct observation:** The starting event is compatible, but the printed ending event is cuff inflation in the protocol and blade removal in the article/supplement.

**Comparison logic/rule:** These descriptions identify different measurement endpoints and hence potentially different time scales. The contrast, unit (seconds), and result population can be matched, but the endpoint cannot be treated as identical without an explanation.

**Inference:** The article/eTable may report an implemented deviation from the protocol, or the protocol wording may be an earlier plan. The supplied package does not state an amendment, equivalence rule, or which time stamp generated the final Table 3 values.

**Supported alternatives:** Deliberate protocol-to-analysis change; divergent labels for an otherwise harmonized timing procedure; or a reporting-definition inconsistency. This record does not assert that any median, difference, CI, or P value is numerically incorrect.

**Human verification question:** Which timestamp generated the published first-attempt duration analyses, and is there a protocol amendment or prespecified rationale documenting the change from cuff inflation to blade removal?

## Limitations and non-applicable comparisons

The protocol supplies planned definitions and analytic rules, not a final result table; it cannot be used as a same-model numeric comparator for final estimates without preserving that distinction. The supplementary eTable supplies physician-clustered inferential outputs; it is not a contradiction of the main unclustered outputs. Figure curves were checked through their printed risk sets, captions, HR/CI, population labels, and reference group; no curve-coordinate digitization was performed. No supplied structured dataset exists for an additional record-level comparison.

All assigned `N001`-`N047` and `S001`-`S037` scopes were completed. No candidate was created for any display-zero P value because none was present, and no candidate was created for a numerical difference that was explained by rounding, a distinct population, an outcome-specific denominator, a planned-versus-observed scope, or physician-cluster adjustment.
