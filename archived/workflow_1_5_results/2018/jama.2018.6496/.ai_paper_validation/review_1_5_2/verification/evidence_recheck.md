DOC-001 Table 3 prints all-patient medians of `38 (29 to 51)` seconds for Bougie and `36 (25 to 54)` seconds for endotracheal tube plus stylet, followed by the difference `1 (4 to −1)` and `P=.24`. Its note states that group columns were unchanged while the difference, P value, and interaction P value were recalculated to account for physician clustering. This is a distinct inferential calculation and is not substituted for the main-table result.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. For an interval displayed as `L to U`, the lower endpoint must not exceed the upper endpoint. Other intervals in the same main table follow lower-to-upper order.

The diagnostic reordering `−1 to 4` matches the numerical order printed in the clustered supplement, but the supplied sources do not establish that the unclustered and clustered interval limits must be identical.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The two group summaries, printed difference, printed interval endpoints, P value, lower-to-upper usage elsewhere in the table, stated Hodges-Lehmann method, and separately recalculated clustered comparator are available. These are sufficient to reproduce the endpoint-order check.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** Individual first-attempt durations, the analysis-specific Hodges-Lehmann calculation output, its exact unclustered interval limits before table production, and any table-production record defining a special endpoint-order convention are not supplied. Consequently, the recheck cannot determine whether the two numbers were transposed or whether one or both limits differ from the intended unclustered result.
- **Alternative source-grounded interpretation:** The main cell may contain the intended two numerical limits in reverse display order. The supplement's `−1 s to 4 s` provides source support for that possibility, but its explicit physician-clustered recalculation prevents treating it as the authoritative unclustered interval.
- **Direct observation:** The main source directly prints `1 (4 to −1)`, and the first endpoint exceeds the second. The supplement directly prints the same point difference with `−1 s to 4 s` under a recalculated clustered analysis.
- **Inferred explanation:** A transposition or table-production mechanism is plausible but is not documented in the supplied package.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What are the analysis-specific unclustered Hodges-Lehmann 95% confidence limits for this all-patient comparison, and were the two endpoints in DOC-001 Table 3 displayed in reverse order?

## C002 — Two-patient ETT+stylet denominator difference across linked hypoxemia outcomes

- **Status:** Pending Human Adjudication.
- **Cited location found:** Yes. The composite outcome appears in [DOC-001 Table 3 — PDF p. 7](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=7), and hypoxemia alone appears in [DOC-001 Table 5 — PDF p. 9](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=9). The planned composite definition is in [DOC-002 secondary outcomes — PDF p. 9](../../../joi180054supp1_prod.pdf#page=9), and the protocol's outcome-specific missing-data procedure continues on [DOC-002 statistical/analytic issues — PDF p. 21](../../../joi180054supp1_prod.pdf#page=21).
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. Table 3 prints ETT+stylet first-attempt success without hypoxemia as `282/366 (77%)`. Its footnote defines the hypoxemia threshold/window and states that valid pulse-oximetry waveform during intubation was not available for all patients.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** Yes. Table 5 prints ETT+stylet hypoxemia as `50/364 (14%)`, using the same threshold/window wording and waveform-availability statement. The Bougie denominators are `371` in both linked rows. The ETT+stylet randomized total printed in the tables is `376`.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Conditionally. If both rows require an observed hypoxemia classification for every person counted in the denominator, their arm-specific valid-waveform denominators should match. If the composite can be classified as not achieved from first-attempt failure alone even when hypoxemia is unavailable, denominator identity is not required. The sources do not print the implemented denominator-construction rule at these table locations.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `366 − 364 = 2`. Relative to the randomized ETT+stylet total, `376 − 366 = 10` and `376 − 364 = 12`. Thus, two more ETT+stylet patients are counted in the composite denominator than in the hypoxemia-only denominator. A patient-level reconciliation cannot be calculated from aggregate counts.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The arm total, both numerators and denominators, both outcome labels, matching hypoxemia threshold/window language, the shared waveform limitation, and the protocol statement that the composite requires first-attempt success and no hypoxemia are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The joint patient-level cross-tabulation of first-attempt success, hypoxemia, and waveform availability; the identities and outcome states of the two additional patients; the actual denominator-construction code or rule; and any outcome-specific data-recovery record are not supplied.
- **Alternative source-grounded interpretation:** The protocol defines success without hypoxemia as the conjunction of first-attempt success and no hypoxemia. Therefore, patients known to have failed the first attempt could be classified as not achieving the composite without a usable hypoxemia waveform, while those same patients could not be classified for hypoxemia alone. The protocol also anticipates outcome-specific handling when secondary-outcome data remain missing. Either mechanism could produce a composite denominator two larger than the hypoxemia-only denominator, but the aggregate tables do not show whether it did so here.
- **Direct observation:** The printed ETT+stylet denominators are `366` and `364`; the corresponding Bougie denominators are both `371`; and both table footnotes use matching hypoxemia language and note unavailable waveform data.
- **Inferred explanation:** An outcome-specific missing-data rule or deterministic classification of known first-attempt failures could explain the two-patient difference. The package does not identify the two patients or state which rule generated their classifications.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Were the two additional ETT+stylet patients in the `282/366` composite denominator known first-attempt failures who lacked sufficient waveform data for the `50/364` hypoxemia analysis, or did another documented denominator rule create the difference?

## C003 — Protocol and published hypoxemia observation windows use different endpoint events

- **Status:** Pending Human Adjudication.
- **Cited location found:** Yes. The protocol definition is in [DOC-002 secondary outcomes — PDF p. 9](../../../joi180054supp1_prod.pdf#page=9) and its measurement procedure is in [DOC-002 measurement of secondary outcomes — PDF p. 10](../../../joi180054supp1_prod.pdf#page=10). Published definitions appear in [DOC-001 Table 3 — PDF p. 7](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=7), [DOC-001 Table 5 — PDF p. 9](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=9), and [DOC-003 eTable 1 notes — PDF p. 3](../../../joi180054supp2_prod.pdf#page=3). - **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. DOC-002 states that hypoxemia observation begins when the first attempt begins and ends one minute after inflation of the ETT cuff. The next page instructs recording at 20-second intervals until one minute after cuff inflation and also recording the lowest SpO2.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** Yes. DOC-001 Tables 3 and 5 and DOC-003 eTable 1 define hypoxemia as occurring during or within one minute after completion of the intubation attempt. DOC-001 defines duration of an attempt from laryngoscope-blade insertion through blade removal; DOC-003's postintubation form expressly states that attempt 1 ends when the blade is removed from the patient's mouth.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. Definitions attached to a matched outcome should use the same observation-window endpoint, or the supplied record should state the implemented change. `ETT cuff inflation` and `completion of the intubation attempt`, with attempt end defined as blade removal, are different named procedural events.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** No numeric arithmetic is needed. The logical comparison is `one minute after ETT cuff inflation` versus `one minute after attempt completion/blade removal`; the printed endpoint events are not textually identical, and the sources provide no identity rule equating them.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The common hypoxemia threshold, common start at the first attempt, protocol end event, protocol measurement schedule, published end-event wording, and supplied attempt-end definition are available. These are sufficient to reproduce the definition comparison.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The implemented case-report timestamp used to stop surveillance, any protocol amendment or analysis-plan revision, the temporal relationship between cuff inflation and blade removal for each attempt, and any operational instruction equating these events are not supplied.
- **Alternative source-grounded interpretation:** `Completion of the intubation attempt` could have been used as shorthand for cuff inflation, or cuff inflation and blade removal could have occurred close together in practice. However, the supplied data form explicitly defines attempt end as blade removal, so operational equivalence cannot be established from the package.
- **Direct observation:** The protocol directly names cuff inflation; the article tables and clustered supplement directly name attempt completion; and the article/data form directly associate attempt completion or end with blade removal.
- **Inferred explanation:** A protocol amendment, editorial shorthand, or operational synchronization is possible but not documented in the supplied sources.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which event actually ended hypoxemia surveillance in the analyzed data—ETT cuff inflation or attempt completion/blade removal—and where is any implemented change or equivalence documented?

## C004 — Protocol and published first-attempt-duration measures use different endpoint events

- **Status:** Pending Human Adjudication.
- **Cited location found:** Yes. The planned measure is in [DOC-002 secondary outcomes — PDF p. 9](../../../joi180054supp1_prod.pdf#page=9). The published measure is defined in [DOC-001 outcome methods — PDF p. 3](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=3) and [DOC-001 Table 3 — PDF p. 7](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=7). The clustered table values and definition are in [DOC-003 eTable 1 — PDF p. 2](../../../joi180054supp2_prod.pdf#page=2) and [DOC-003 eTable 1 notes — PDF p. 3](../../../joi180054supp2_prod.pdf#page=3); the supplied data form repeats the blade-based attempt definition in [DOC-003 postintubation form — PDF p. 10](../../../joi180054supp2_prod.pdf#page=10).
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. DOC-002 defines `Time to intubation (first attempt only)` as elapsed time from the beginning of the intubation attempt to inflation of the ETT cuff when the tube is in the trachea. On the same page, attempt start is defined as laryngoscope entry into the mouth.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** Yes. DOC-001 defines first-attempt duration as elapsed time between laryngoscope-blade insertion and removal. Table 3 prints all-patient medians of `38 (29 to 51)` and `36 (25 to 54)` seconds under that definition. DOC-003 eTable 1 retains those medians, and its footnote and data form also use blade entry through blade removal.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. A duration measure matched across a protocol and results report should retain the same start and end events or document an implemented change. The starts are compatible, but `ETT cuff inflation with the tube in the trachea` and `laryngoscope-blade removal` are different end events and can generate different elapsed times.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Let the common start be blade insertion. The planned elapsed time ends at cuff inflation; the published elapsed time ends at blade removal. Their patient-level difference would equal the time of blade removal minus the time of cuff inflation. No numerical difference can be computed because neither event timestamp is supplied.
- **Necessary inputs available:** Both printed duration definitions, their common start event, their different end events, the reported all-patient summaries, the clustered display, and the data-form attempt definition are available. These are sufficient to reproduce the measure-definition comparison.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** Patient-level cuff-inflation and blade-removal timestamps, the exact stopwatch stop instruction used during trial data collection, any protocol amendment or revised analysis plan, and the rule for a failed first attempt that never reaches cuff inflation are not supplied. The main table places one duration row under all patients and separately reports duration among successful first attempts, while the planned cuff-inflation endpoint is naturally defined only when the tube reaches the trachea; the package does not reconcile that handling.
- **Alternative source-grounded interpretation:** The published blade-removal endpoint may represent a deliberate operational measure that assigns a duration to failed attempts, whereas the planned cuff-inflation measure describes time to successful tracheal placement. The repeated blade-entry/blade-removal definition in the article, eTable note, and supplied data form supports deliberate use of that operational measure, but no supplied record documents its relationship to the protocol endpoint.
- **Direct observation:** The protocol directly prints a cuff-inflation endpoint. The article, clustered supplement, and data form directly print a blade-removal endpoint. Table 3 directly reports summaries under the published definition.
- **Inferred explanation:** An intended measurement change, harmonized recording procedure, or attempt to accommodate failed first attempts is plausible but not documented in the supplied package.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which stop timestamp generated each published duration analysis, how were failed first attempts handled, and where is the change from cuff inflation to blade removal documented and justified?
