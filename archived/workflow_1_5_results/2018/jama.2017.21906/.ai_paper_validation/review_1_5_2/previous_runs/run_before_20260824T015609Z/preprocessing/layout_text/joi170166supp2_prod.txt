1035   1. Introduction
1036       This statistical analysis plan (SAP) describes the planned analysis and reporting for
1037   Northwestern University Protocol (Version 22 September 2014) “Acute Coronary Syndrome
1038   Quality Improvement in Kerala (ACS QUIK)”, a cluster-randomized, stepped wedge clinical trial
1039   that aims to implement and evaluate the effect of a locally-developed quality improvement
1040   toolkit on 30-day major adverse cardiovascular event rates in patients admitted with acute
1041   coronary syndrome in Kerala.
1042       The structure and content of this SAP provide sufficient detail to meet the requirements
1043   identified by the Food and Drug Administration and International Conference on Harmonisation
1044   of Technical Requirements for Registration of Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH): Guidance
1045   on Statistical Principles in Clinical Trials. All work planned and reported for this SAP will follow
1046   internationally accepted guidelines, including those published by the American Statistical
1047   Association and the Royal Statistical Society, for statistical practice.
1048       The following documents were reviewed in preparation of this SAP:
1049        ACS QUIK Protocol Version 22 September 2014.
1050        In-Hospital (Version 18 September 2014) and 30-day Follow Up (Version 18 September
1051           2014) Case Report Forms (CRFs).
1052        ICH Guidance on Statistical Principles for Clinical Trials.
1053
1054       The reader of this SAP is encouraged to also read the study protocol for details on the
1055   conduct of this study and the Data Management Plan for details regarding data review,
1056   cleaning, and management. The analysis plan also outlines the proposed layout of tables and
1057   figures that will be presented.


1058   2. Study objectives

1059      2.1. Efficacy

1060          2.1.1. Primary objective
1061               Compared with usual care, to evaluate the effect of a locally-developed, evidence-
1062          based health care quality improvement toolkit on 30-day major adverse cardiovascular
1063          events (MACE) in patients admitted with acute coronary syndrome.

1064          2.1.2. Secondary objective
1065              To evaluate 30-day health related quality of life in patients with acute
1066                 coronary syndrome using a translated and validated version of the Seattle
1067                 Angina Questionnaire (SAQ).
1068              To evaluate individual- and household-level impoverishing effects of an ACS
1069                 event in the context of the recent implementation of a national government
1070                 insurance program for families below the poverty line.
1071              Compared with usual care, to evaluate the effect of a locally-developed,
1072                 evidence-based health care quality improvement toolkit on in-hospital and

1078      2.2. Safety
1079             The intervention does not include implementation of any new drug nor invasive
1080         procedure. Rather, the intervention will occur at the health system level in an effort to
1081         increase the use of evidence-based therapies for the management of patients admitted
1082         with acute coronary syndromes. Because patients hospitalized with acute coronary
1083         syndromes are expected to have adverse events and serious adverse events, we will
1084         capture in-hospital and 30-day major adverse cardiovascular events. We will collect
1085         information regarding unexpected adverse events, which may be related to
1086         implementation of the intervention.


1087   3. Study design
1088       This study is a cluster randomized, stepped wedge clinical trial assessing implementation
1089   and effect of a locally-developed quality improvement toolkit for patients admitted with acute
1090   coronary syndrome (ACS) in Kerala, India. Hospitals will be randomized after stratification for
1091   size to one of five steps. After a four-month baseline period, the quality improvement toolkit
1092   will be implemented in a random subset of hospitals in step 1 (cohort 1). Through a one-way
1093   crossover design, these hospitals will continue to use the quality improvement toolkit through
1094   the end of the trial for all acute coronary syndrome (ACS) patients. Cohorts 2 through 5 will
1095   implement the quality improvement toolkit every 4 months at months 8, 12, 16 and 20,
1096   continuing the use of the toolkits from that time forward to the end of the study. The primary
1097   outcome is 30-day major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) rates. Rates will be
1098   continuously collected and compared at one interim time point for safety and efficacy. Final
1099   analysis compares MACE rates before and after implementation of the quality improvement
1100   toolkits, accounting for cluster effects of hospital, cohort, and time.

1107   4. Sample size
1108          Power and sample size are calculated based on a cluster randomized stepped wedge
1109      design (STATA v.12). The average anticipated cluster size is based on previous data
1110      from the Kerala ACS Registry. Intra-class correlation (ICC) is set to 0.05 based on the
1111      comparable BRIDGE ACS study (Berwanger et al. JAMA 2012; 307:2041) and ICC from
1112      the Kerala ACS Registry in-hospital MACE rate.
1113      At an alpha of 0.05, the anticipated sample size of 15,000 patients will result in 80%
1114      power to detect a 2.4% difference from a baseline 10.4% 30-day MACE rate. This
1115      sample size is increased to 15,750 to account for up to 5% drop out between discharge
1116      and 30-day follow-up.
1117
1118   5. Randomization
1119          The biostatistician at the central coordinating center (Centre for Chronic Disease
1120      Control) will centrally randomize the clusters using a computer generated randomization
1121      sequence and maintain privacy of randomization records prior to the beginning of the data
1122      collection on a password protected secure server. Stratified (based on hospital size)
1123      randomization will be used to randomize one cohort of hospitals to receive the intervention
1124      in each of the four time periods. These strategies will be used to minimize selection bias
1125      through sequence generation and allocation concealment. The group of hospitals that are
1126      randomized to the intervention in the final step will know that they will be randomized
1127      ahead of time, but we know of no other strategy to minimize this bias. No other group of
1128      hospitals will be aware of where they fall on the allocation schedule. Local investigators will
1129      be notified three weeks in advance of the start date for the quality improvement project at
1130      their hospital.


1131   6. Study endpoints

1132      6.1. Efficacy

1133          6.1.1. Primary endpoint
1134                     Composite of 30-day major adverse event rate, defined as all-cause death,
1135                 myocardial infarction, stroke, and major bleeding defined by GUSTO criteria
1136                 (intracerebral hemorrhage or bleeding resulting in substantial hemodynamic
1137                 compromise requiring treatment (GUSTO Investigators. N Engl J Med 1993;
1138                 329:673-682)). Because these data will be collected at the site-level, the
1139                 outcome assessors will not be blinded. However, there does not appear to be
1140                 evidence of outcome assessment bias in the case of objective outcomes (OR
1141                 [95% CI] 1.01 [0.92, 1.10], Wood L, et al. BMJ, 2008; 336:601).

1142          6.1.2. Secondary endpoints
1143                  30-day all-cause mortality rate
1144                  30-day cardiovascular disease mortality rate
1145                  In-hospital all-cause mortality rate
1146                  30-day myocardial infarction (re-infarction) rate
1147                  30-day stroke rate

1149                 Optimal in-hospital medication use (defined as aspirin, ADP receptor

1156      6.1.3. Implementation of intervention
1157                 We will also evaluate the degree to which the intervention is implemented to
1158             evaluate for potential type III error through the number and frequency of
1159             downloads of quality improvement materials (standard order sets, audit and
1160             feedback reports) and number and frequency of quality improvement team
1161             meetings by self-report. These quantitative data can be used in sub-group
1162             analyses to evaluate if the effect of the intervention is modified by the use of the
1163             quality improvement materials. We will complement this quantitative
1164             evaluation with a mid-trial qualitative evaluation of sites through interviews and
1165             focus group discussions, as recommended by the UK Medical Research Council
1166             guidelines on complex interventions (Craig P, et al. MRC, 2008).

1167      6.1.4. ACS QUIK sub-studies
1168                We also aim to evaluate the effect of the intervention on two sub-studies on
1169             sub-sample of 2200 subjects:

1170                 Health related quality of life at 30 days, using a translated and
1171                  linguistically validated version of the Seattle Angina Questionnaire (SAQ).
1172                 Individual and household level impoverishing effects of an ACS event and
1173                  compare against historical data (Huffman MD, et al. PLoS ONE, 20116:
1174                  e20281) to evaluate the effect of a national government insurance
1175                  program for families below the poverty line.

1176              We will also evaluate the association between concordance of in-hospital and
1177              discharge medical therapy (>80% of recommended drugs [in-hospital and
1178              discharge]) and 30-day MACE rates.

1179   6.2. Safety
1180       At the recommendation of the Data & Safety Monitoring Board, we will collect and
1181   report data on serious adverse events and unanticipated adverse events. All patients will
1182   be under the care of a hospital-based medical team for their usual care, including the
1183   diagnosis and treatment of any serious or unexpected adverse events.

1184   7. Statistical Analysis

1185      7.1. General Principles
1186           Data analyses will be performed at Central Coordinating Centre (CCC) at the Centre for
1187      Chronic Disease Control in New Delhi, India. Only de-identified data will be analyzed by the
1188      team at Northwestern University. This SAP will be finalized prior to the database lock. The
1189      statistical analysis will be performed using STATA Version 12.0, SAS, and R-program. The
1190      analysis will be performed on the principle of ‘intention to treat’ unless otherwise specified
1191      (i.e., all patients recorded in the data base during the 24 month period will be included, and
1192      considered exposed to the intervention according to randomization regardless of when the
1193      intervention was actually implemented). The intervention start for each cluster will be fixed
1194      regardless of whether implementation proceeds on time. The stepped wedge design is in
1195      effect a matched design with before and after comparisons for each cluster randomised.
1196      Random effect models will be used to account for the within cluster correlation (Hussey and
1197      Hughes, 2007). Methods for handling missing data for the primary and secondary endpoints
1198      are described below.
1199           Individual-level data will be summarized using descriptive statistics (i.e., number of
1200      subjects [n], mean, standard deviation [SD], median, minimum, and maximum) for
1201      continuous variables, and using frequency and percentage (i.e., number and proportion of
1202      subjects [n, %]) for discrete/categorical variables, unless specified otherwise. We will also
1203      report data on number and sizes of clusters. Participant-level data from the electronic data
1204      capture (EDC) system and any derived variables will be reported.

1205      7.2. Missing data handling
1206          If the data are “not obtained” on the 30-day follow-up assessment form following the
1207      outpatient department follow-up visit or after three telephone call attempts by the site
1208      coordinator, then data will be considered as missing. We will perform sensitivity analyses
1209      using complete case analysis and multiple imputation to evaluate the potential effect of
1210      missing outcomes.

1211      7.3. CONSORT statements for reporting trials of non-pharmacologic interventions and
1212           cluster-randomized trials
1213          All participants who were screened and invited to participate in this trial will be
1214      accounted for, in accordance with the CONSORT statements for reporting trials of non-
1215      pharmacologic interventions (Boutron I, et al. Ann Int Med 2008; 148:295) and cluster-
1216      randomized trials (Campbell MK, et al. BMJ 2012; 345:e5661). Reasons for early withdrawal
1217      will be listed for all participants that prematurely discontinued the study. The number of
1218      participants that were registered but not randomized will be presented and the reasons for
1219      non-participation.

1220   7.4. Patient disposition
1221       Number of patients who were registered, fulfilled eligibility criterion, together with the
1222   reasons for exclusion, and the number randomized by study center will be summarized.
1223   Baseline demographic variables such as age, gender, and relevant clinical variables (e.g.
1224   smoking status, medical history, blood pressure and cholesterol) will be summarized for
1225   each group. Summaries of continuous baseline variables will be presented as means and
1226   standard deviations together with medians and minimum and maximum values. Categorical
1227   variables will be described as frequencies and percentage (appendix for a list of tables that
1228   will be used for presenting baseline characteristics).

1229   7.5. Primary endpoint analyses
1230       The primary outcome will be 30-day major adverse cardiovascular event (MACE) rate.
1231   Overall differences in 30-day MACE rates between control and intervention periods will be
1232   reported. In the primary analysis, 30-day MACE will be modelled using mixed effects logistic
1233   regression with random cluster (hospital) effects and including a fixed time effect within
1234   every 4 months periods. Baseline data collected from the first time period will be tabulated
1235   by order of implementation, 12 clusters in 5 groups. The adequacy of randomization will be
1236   examined and any hospital level variable unbalanced at baseline will be included in the
1237   model as sensitivity analysis. The patient level covariates included will be finalized prior to
1238   unblinded statistical analysis.

1239      7.5.1. Adjusted Analyses
1240         In case of some general individual information will be not balanced between two
1241      groups, a sensitivity analysis will be performed, by adjusting for those factors in the
1242      format of a GRACE adjusted risk score.

1243      7.5.2. Subgroup Analyses
1244         The following a priori sub-group analyses will be carried out to evaluate potential
1245      heterogeneity of effect.

1246         Site level characteristics
1247              o Hospital size
1248              o Use of quality improvement toolkit components
1249
1250         Participant level characteristics
1251             o Age (<65 years and >65 years)
1252             o Sex
1253             o ST segment elevation myocardial infarction vs. non-ST segment elevation
1254                 myocardial infarction
1255

1258   30-day myocardial infarction (re-infarction), 30-day stroke, 30-day major GUSTO
1259   bleeding will be analyzed using mixed effects logistic regression. We will also evaluate
1260   the proportion of patients receiving optimal in-hospital medication use, optimal

1263   7.7. Sub-studies
1264       We will evaluate the effect of the intervention of health-related quality of life and

1267   7.8. Safety analysis
1268       All serious and unanticipated adverse events data will be reported. Serious adverse
1269   events (SAEs) are defined as any adverse event that results in any of the following
1270   outcomes: death, a life threatening adverse reaction, inpatient hospitalization or
1271   prolongation of existing hospitalization, a persistent or significant incapacity or substantial
1272   disruption of the ability to conduct normal life functions, or a congenital anamoly/birth
1273   defect. SAEs will be routinely captured through the electronic data capture form and
1274   reported semi-annually and at the interim safety analysis. By definition, unanticipated
1275   adverse events are not previously anticipated by the investigators but are defined as:
1276          Unexpected (in terms of nature, severity, or frequency) given (a) the research
1277             procedures that are described in the protocol-related documents, such as the
1278             IRB-approved research protocol and informed consent document; and (b) the
1279             characteristics of the subject population being studied;
1280
1281           Related or possibly related to participation in the research (in this guidance
1282            document, possibly related means there is a reasonable possibility that the
1283            incident, experience, or outcome may have been caused by the procedures
1284            involved in the research); and
1285
1286           Suggests that the research places subjects or others at a greater risk of harm
1287            (including physical, psychological, economic, or social harm) than was
1288            previously known or recognized (HHS, 45 CFR, part 46).

1289   7.9. Interim analysis
1290       Interim analysis for efficacy will be performed after one year. The analysis will assess
1291   MACE rates at hospitals operating per usual care and compare with MACE rates at hospitals
1292   in steps 1 and 2 after implementation of the quality improvement threshold. To adjust for
1293   one interim analysis, the O’Brien Fleming stopping boundary for the interim analysis will be
1294   set at z=2.797, p-value < 0.005 and for the final analysis to z=1.977, p-value < 0.048. In
1295   addition, consideration would be given to the consistency between effects seen on the
1296   primary endpoint and those seen in the secondary endpoints. Interim analysis for adverse
1297   events will be performed on a semi-annual basis. The DSMB will review the reports and
1298   make recommendation about continue or stop the study based on the interim analysis
1299   results.
