C001 — Missed-dose summaries and stated Welch comparison do not reconcile

Candidate statement: The main article describes a two-sided Welch t test for missed-dose percentages, but reports mean (SD) 4.6 (1.0)% versus 4.3 (0.5)% with P=.87 in both table and prose. These summaries and P value do not reconcile for any valid Welch sample sizes, including all allowed display intervals.

Review domain: statistical_results Relationship type: S_W All relationships: S_W Compared document roles: REPORT main p.4 statistical methods vs REPORT main p.6 adherence table and prose Evidence state: reproduced Inclusion audit: CONFIRMED Finding IDs: F-ST1-001 Classification rationale: Both the stated comparison method and its numerical summaries are printed within the main REPORT. No PLAN or second REPORT is a conflicting comparator. The issue concerns estimate/test and method logic. Classification ambiguity: None Status: Pending Human Adjudication Relationship inventory references: S017.

Exact source locations: Main PDF p.4 — Statistical Analyses; main PDF p.6 — Table 2 missed-dose row and matching paragraph. All table and prose occurrences of the pattern are consolidated.

Source evidence: Methods identify a Welch t test for missed-dose comparison and two-sided testing. Table 2 labels the summaries “Missed doses, mean (SD), %” and gives 4.6 (1.0), 4.3 (0.5), P=.87; prose repeats mean (SD) and the same values. The footnote defines each patient's missed doses relative to expected doses throughout enrollment. The current checker and independent screener directly read and visually inspected p.6.

Reported-versus-comparator: Reported P=.87, whose two-decimal rounding interval is [.865,.875], versus an upper bound .8508783587 on P under the explicitly stated Welch comparison using all permissible rounded mean/SD inputs and any valid group sizes.

Reasoning procedure: Permit the full half-unit interval around each one-decimal mean and SD. A valid sample SD/Welch test requires each group to have at least two observations. Bound the smallest mean difference and largest possible standard error without assuming actual adherence sample sizes. Welch degrees of freedom are at least one. The df=1 Student t tail is a conservative upper bound on the two-sided P for the resulting positive statistic.

Calculation: Means are within [4.55,4.65] and [4.25,4.35]; SDs within [.95,1.05] and [.45,.55] percentage points. Absolute difference >=.20; SE <=sqrt((1.05^2+.55^2)/2)=.8381527307; |t| >=.2386199945; df>=1. Therefore P<=1−(2/pi)atan(.2386199945)=.8508783587, below the printed P interval. Endpoint inclusion makes the bound conservative. Percent-to-proportion conversion cancels in the test statistic. This is an analytic compatibility bound under the source-stated method, not an assumed-denominator reconstruction.

Affected result or extracted value: The adherence comparison P=.87 and the mean/SD summaries 4.6 (1.0)% versus 4.3 (0.5)%. Why context does not resolve it: Table and prose repeat the values and SD label, and the methods expressly identify Welch. Neither rounding, missing sample sizes nor units resolves the discrepancy, and the package does not identify which reported summary or test value should be interpreted differently. Alternative source-grounded interpretations: A different dispersion quantity, adherence variable or comparison method may underlie the printed P. None is established by the supplied materials. No replacement result is inferred. Mechanical evidence recheck: Fresh direct-source recheck found all cited locations and reproduced the stated comparison; no transcription or classification correction. Complete separate facts are recorded in the mechanical evidence recheck. Potential downstream evidence impact: If confirmed, an extractor could copy an adherence P value or dispersion statistic that does not match the stated comparison. No primary-outcome conclusion change is asserted. Human verification steps: Check the printed method and both p.6 occurrences; establish the actual adherence summaries, dispersion definition and comparison result from the corresponding analysis output.
