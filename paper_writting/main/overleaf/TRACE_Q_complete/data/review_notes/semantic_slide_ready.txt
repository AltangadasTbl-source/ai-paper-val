LLM-assisted semantic profile of final human-confirmed statistical-results candidates

METHOD AND PROVENANCE
Scope: final human-reviewed statistical_results domain only; 139 confirmed candidate records, including 37 labeled substantive. Other domains are not included in this recategorization.
The source Notes were authored by earlier Claude Opus 5.5 high AI review runs. Subsequent human-reviewed copied workbook decisions determine confirmation and substantive status; no Note is represented as human-written.
LLM full-note semantic coding uses seven mutually exclusive primary categories plus separate overlapping secondary tags. Candidate titles provide context only; no judgments.json rationale is imported.
Every full Note was read by the second agent and checked against v1; all 139 have exact evidence excerpts and rationales. Four category-boundary differences were discussed and three provisional primary labels changed.
Three bundled candidates retain explicit ambiguous-primary flags. Their confirmation and substantive status are unchanged. One ambiguous candidate was also revised, so final audit statuses are 134 agree, 2 revise, 3 ambiguous while total primary revisions equal 3.
This was an LLM coding audit with v1 available during reconciliation, not a blinded interrater reliability study. The new taxonomy is not human validated; no kappa or human-coder reliability statistic is claimed.
Candidate records are the counting unit. Multiple linked candidates can share an underlying defect. Counts therefore do not estimate unique defects or trial prevalence.

THREE CAREFUL CONCLUSIONS
Counts/percentages/population accounting form the largest overall category: 39/139 (28.1%), but only 4/37 (10.8%) of substantive candidates. Effect/estimand identity is the largest substantive category: 8/37 (21.6%), followed by intervals/dispersion 7/37 (18.9%) and attribution 6/37 (16.2%). Categories and substantive status measure different features.
The sampled later-period profile contains more analysis/measure-definition candidates per paper (0.16 to 0.68) and more count/denominator candidates (0.68 to 1.04), while interval/dispersion candidates decline (0.74 to 0.40). These are descriptive differences in this corpus, not evidence of a temporal or causal trend.
Across all categories, counts rise from 56 to 83 confirmed candidates and from 16 to 21 substantive candidates as the paper denominator grows from 19 to 25. Zero-inclusive means are 2.95 to 3.32 for all confirmed candidates and 0.842 to 0.840 for substantive candidates. The substantive mean is essentially unchanged despite the larger later count.
