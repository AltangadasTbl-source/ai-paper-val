- **Source printed value/text matched:** Under the placebo-group “No. of Participants” column, the calcium row prints `1994`; the same row prints placebo mean `512 (489 to 536)` mg/d and between-group difference `−12.0 (−46.0 to 22.0)`.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** The paper prints `1147` randomized to placebo on PDF pp. 1 and 4. The adjacent placebo outside-study vitamin-D row on PDF p. 5 prints `1094` under the same participant-count header.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** For a participant-level group summary, the number of unique placebo participants contributing cannot exceed the total number randomized to placebo unless the cell uses a different unit that the source defines. The table explicitly labels this column “No. of Participants,” so the participant-count rule applies to the printed label.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `1994 − 1147 = 847`, so the printed cell exceeds the entire randomized placebo cohort by 847. It differs from adjacent placebo `N=1094` by 900. Separately, `500 − 512 = −12`, which reproduces the printed mean difference and does not resolve the count mismatch.
- **Necessary inputs available; exact missing inputs or definitions:** The cell value, column label, randomized placebo denominator, adjacent cell, and means are available. Missing are the production/source-data value for this cell and any definition stating that it counts observations or another unit rather than unique participants.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The printed `1994` may be a transcription of another participant count (the adjacent row supplies `1094` as one numerical possibility), or the cell may intentionally count a different unit despite the participant header. The supplied PDFs do not establish either explanation.
- **Direct observation:** Table 2 prints participant `N=1994` for the placebo calcium row, while the same article prints only 1147 participants randomized to placebo.
- **Inferred explanation:** A digit transposition is plausible because `1094` is adjacent, but that mechanism is inferred and is not demonstrated by the supplied sources.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What source-data participant count generated the placebo calcium row, and was this cell intended to count unique participants or another explicitly defined unit?

## C002 — Protocol’s ≥70-year vitamin-D “limit” has the opposite inequality direction

- **Cited location found:** DOC-002, [joi170019supp1_prod.pdf — PDF p. 7](../../../joi170019supp1_prod.pdf#page=7), section 5, “Intervention,” contains the complete age-stratified outside-vitamin-D instruction.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** The sentence says participants may take their own vitamin D, “but we will ask them to limit that to no more than 400 IU/day if they are < 70 years of age and to more than 600 IU/day if they are ≥ 70.”
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** The same sentence supplies the paired clauses `no more than 400 IU/day` for age `<70` and `more than 600 IU/day` for age `≥70`; it follows both from the governing phrase “limit that.”
- **Consistency rule applicable:** “No more than” defines an upper bound (`≤`), while “more than” defines a lower bound (`>`). When two coordinated quantities are both governed by an instruction to limit outside intake, their inequality directions cannot represent parallel upper limits unless a distinct purpose is explicitly supplied for the older group.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The younger clause is `outside vitamin D ≤400 IU/day`; the older clause is `outside vitamin D >600 IU/day`. The second inequality points in the opposite direction from an upper-limit instruction. This is a direct logical comparison; no rounding or statistical calculation is involved.
- **Necessary inputs available; exact missing inputs or definitions:** The age thresholds, quantities, units, governing verb, and inequality phrases are available. Missing is the intended allowable upper bound—or an explicit statement of a different lower-bound purpose—for participants aged 70 years or older.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** Read literally, the protocol may have intended a maximum for the younger group but a recommended minimum for the older group, because the sentence ends by calling the amounts “currently recommended intake levels.” That would make the two clauses serve different purposes, but the protocol does not state such a distinction and still governs both with “limit.”
- **Direct observation:** The protocol prints `no more than 400 IU/day` for participants younger than 70 and `more than 600 IU/day` for participants aged 70 or older in the same limiting instruction.
- **Inferred explanation:** An omitted “no” before “more than 600 IU/day” is a plausible production explanation, but the direct source does not establish that wording.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What inequality and maximum outside-vitamin-D intake were intended for participants aged 70 years or older: `≤600 IU/day`, another upper bound, or an explicitly different lower-bound instruction?

- **Consistency rule applicable:** Quantities stated as the same included supplementation level must agree after unit conversion. `1000 mg = 1 g`.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `600 mg/caplet × 2 caplets/day = 1200 mg/day = 1.2 g/day`. In contrast, `1200 g/day = 1,200,000 mg/day`, which is 1000 times the stated 1200 mg/day regimen.
- **Necessary inputs available; exact missing inputs or definitions:** The dose per caplet, caplets per day, total regimen, compared recommended-intake quantity, and units are available. Missing are the intended unit in the `1,200 g/day` sentence and the production/source text from which that sentence was typeset.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The latter quantity may have intended `1,200 mg/day` or equivalently `1.2 g/day`, both of which agree with the regimen printed immediately before it. The supplied PDFs do not identify the intended rendering.
- **Direct observation:** The same paragraph prints a 1200 mg/day calcium regimen and calls 1200 g/day the included supplementation level.
- **Inferred explanation:** A unit transcription from `mg` to `g` is numerically plausible, but the source does not document the production mechanism.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What unit and value were intended in the recommended-intake sentence, and does the underlying protocol source specify `1,200 mg/day` or `1.2 g/day`?

## C004 — Death-difference confidence interval is discordant with the printed flow counts under a labelled diagnostic calculation

- **Cited location found:** DOC-001, [jama_lappe_2017_oi_170019.pdf — PDF p. 4](../../../jama_lappe_2017_oi_170019.pdf#page=4), Figure 1 and the adjacent Results narrative, contains the death counts, randomized denominators, point difference, and confidence interval.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Figure 1 prints 7 treatment deaths and 9 placebo deaths, with 1156 and 1147 participants randomized, respectively.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** The adjacent narrative prints 16 total deaths and “difference in proportion, `0.002` [95% CI, `−0.006 to 0.037`]” for dying while participating.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** The point difference must be reproducible from the printed event counts and denominators after contrast orientation is identified. A confidence interval can be compared diagnostically with a standard count-based interval, but exact inferential compatibility requires the source’s CI method, variance rule, and orientation; those definitions are not supplied.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Placebo minus treatment is `9/1147 − 7/1156 = 0.001791`, which rounds to the printed `0.002`. As an explicitly diagnostic calculation, the ordinary unpooled-binomial Wald standard error is `sqrt[(7/1156)(1−7/1156)/1156 + (9/1147)(1−9/1147)/1147] = 0.003463`; the nominal 95% interval is approximately `0.001791 ± 1.96×0.003463 = −0.0050 to 0.0086`. The printed lower endpoint is near this diagnostic, whereas the printed upper endpoint `0.037` is not.
- **Necessary inputs available; exact missing inputs or definitions:** Counts, randomized denominators, point estimate, confidence level, and printed endpoints are available. Missing are the analysis population for this interval, contrast orientation as implemented, CI construction, variance method, and any continuity, clustering, or other adjustment.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The upper endpoint may reflect a transcription/typesetting issue, or an analysis method not described in the supplied PDFs may have generated a wider or asymmetric interval. The package does not distinguish these possibilities.
- **Direct observation:** The article prints 7/1156 versus 9/1147 deaths, difference `0.002`, and 95% CI `−0.006 to 0.037` together on PDF p. 4.
- **Inferred explanation:** Discordance of `0.037` with the ordinary unpooled-Wald diagnostic is derived; it does not identify the article’s actual method or establish a replacement endpoint.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Using the article’s intended death-analysis population, contrast, and CI method, what interval is produced from the analysis data, and does the production source support the printed upper endpoint `0.037`?

## C005 — Outside-study vitamin-D difference CI includes zero while printed `P=.002`

- **Cited location found:** DOC-001, [jama_lappe_2017_oi_170019.pdf — PDF p. 5](../../../jama_lappe_2017_oi_170019.pdf#page=5), Table 2, “Outside of Study Supplement Intake (Visit 2 to Visit 9),” vitamin-D3 row, contains all compared values in one row.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** The row prints treatment `N=1099`, mean `740 (691 to 789)` IU/d, and placebo `N=1094`, mean `869 (803 to 934)` IU/d.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** The same row prints between-group difference `−128.1`, 95% CI `−209.5 to 46.6`, and `P=.002`; visual inspection of the direct PDF confirms no printed minus sign before `46.6`.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** For a corresponding same-contrast two-sided 95% confidence interval and two-sided null test, inclusion of zero and `P<.05` are incompatible by test/interval inversion. Applying that exact rule here requires confirmation that Table 2 used corresponding two-sided procedures with the same model and variance estimate; the source does not provide those definitions.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `740 − 869 = −129`, consistent at displayed precision with `−128.1`. The ordered interval `−209.5 to 46.6` contains both `−128.1` and zero, while the row prints `P=.002`. As a diagnostic alternative only, an upper endpoint of `−46.6` would give half-widths of `81.4` and `81.5` around `−128.1`; `SE≈81.4/1.96=41.53` and `|z|≈128.1/41.53=3.08`, which is numerically compatible with a two-sided normal-tail P near `.002`.
- **Necessary inputs available; exact missing inputs or definitions:** Group means, participant counts, point difference, confidence level, interval endpoints, and P value are available. Missing are the exact CI and hypothesis-test methods, sidedness, common or distinct variance/model specifications, and analysis output or production source for the upper endpoint.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** A lost minus sign before `46.6` is a numerically coherent possibility because it nearly symmetrizes the interval and aligns with the P value. Alternatively, the CI and P value may come from noncorresponding procedures not described in the table. The direct sources do not choose between them.
- **Direct observation:** One Table 2 row prints a 95% interval crossing zero and `P=.002` for the displayed between-group difference.
- **Inferred explanation:** The missing-minus-sign account and the normal diagnostic are derived explanations; neither is established by the supplied sources.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Do the analysis output and production source show the upper endpoint as `−46.6` or `46.6`, and were the reported 95% CI and P value generated by corresponding same-contrast two-sided procedures?

## C006 — Figure 1 discontinuation counts conflict with the p. 7 vitamin-D/placebo discontinuation total and percentages

- **Cited location found:** DOC-001 Figure 1 on [jama_lappe_2017_oi_170019.pdf — PDF p. 4](../../../jama_lappe_2017_oi_170019.pdf#page=4) contains the arm-level “Discontinued intervention” counts. The Results narrative on [PDF p. 7](../../../jama_lappe_2017_oi_170019.pdf#page=7) contains the total and arm percentages for stopping the vitamin-D or placebo supplement.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Figure 1 prints `238 Discontinued intervention` in the vitamin-D-plus-calcium arm, decomposed as `11 + 93 + 134`, and `246 Discontinued intervention` in placebo, decomposed as `16 + 76 + 154`.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** PDF p. 7 prints “304 participants (13.2%; 12.4% of the vitamin D3 + calcium group and 14.0% of the placebo group) stopped taking the vitamin D or placebo supplement.” The article prints randomized denominators 1156 and 1147 on PDF pp. 1 and 4.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Counts for the same participant-level discontinuation construct, trial arms, and follow-up period must agree across figure and narrative, subject only to percentage rounding. Whether that identity rule is fully applicable depends on the unstated meaning of Figure 1’s broader label “Discontinued intervention.”
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Figure components reconcile internally: `11+93+134=238` and `16+76+154=246`; the figure total is `238+246=484`. The narrative total is 304, a difference of 180. Under ordinary one-decimal percentage rounding, `143/1156=12.370%` prints as 12.4% and `161/1147=14.037%` prints as 14.0%; `143+161=304`, so the narrative percentages reconcile exactly with its total. Rounding cannot reconcile 304 with 484.
- **Necessary inputs available; exact missing inputs or definitions:** Figure counts and component sums, narrative total and percentages, randomized denominators, and broad follow-up context are available. Missing are Figure 1’s exact discontinuation event definition, component or supplement scope, time window, participant-level counting rule, and relationship to the narrative endpoint of stopping vitamin D or placebo.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** Figure 1 may use a broader intervention-discontinuation construct, such as stopping either study component or meeting another protocol-status rule, while the narrative counts only vitamin-D/placebo stopping. It may also use a different unstated time or event rule. If so, both displays could describe distinct quantities, but the direct PDFs do not provide the needed distinction.
- **Direct observation:** The article prints 238 and 246 under “Discontinued intervention” in Figure 1, and elsewhere prints 304 participants with arm percentages for stopping vitamin D or placebo.
- **Inferred explanation:** Treating both displays as the same endpoint produces the 484-versus-304 mismatch. The broader-composite explanation is plausible from the differing labels but remains inferred because Figure 1 does not define its construct.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What exact supplement component(s), event rule, time window, and participant-counting rule generated the Figure 1 counts 238 and 246, and is that construct distinct from the p. 7 endpoint of 304 participants stopping vitamin D or placebo?
