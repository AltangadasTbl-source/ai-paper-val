## Findings

### F-XS-001

**Title:** Reported per-protocol primary sensitivity uses a different adherence threshold from the final SAP population definition.

**Compared locations and document roles:** PLAN [final SAP §4.1, PDF p.71](../../../joi190092supp3_prod.pdf#page=71), with major deviations in [§4.2, p.72](../../../joi190092supp3_prod.pdf#page=72); REPORT [main narrative, PDF p.6](../../../jama_rathinam_2019_oi_190092.pdf#page=6) and [Figure 2 model 4 and footnote e, p.7](../../../jama_rathinam_2019_oi_190092.pdf#page=7). Original SAP [§4.1, p.31](../../../joi190092supp3_prod.pdf#page=31) has the same planned definition.

**Printed statements/values:** The final SAP defines the per-protocol efficacy population as the intent-to-treat population excluding major deviations or noncompliance, defined as “less than 50% of the study drug received by self report or pill counts at study visits.” Main p.6 says the sensitivity model included only patients who missed less than 20% of their doses. Figure 2 labels this model “Per protocol”; footnote e repeats “patients who missed less than 20% of study medication (n = 184).” Printed results are 61/90 versus 54/94, OR 1.56 (95% CI 0.84–2.96), P=.17.

**Comparison logic:** Receipt below 50% is the planned noncompliance exclusion. Missed doses below 20% requires receipt above 80% on the reported missed/expected-dose definition. These thresholds select distinct adherence populations (for example, 70% receipt is above the SAP adherence minimum but below the reported sensitivity requirement). This is a comparison of explicitly defined analysis-selection rules, not a claim that a particular participant had 70% receipt. The exact actual membership difference cannot be reconstructed without participant data. Source-wide original/final SAP comparison shows no supplied amendment changing the threshold to 80% receipt. Major-deviation exclusions are additional planned conditions; no finding is inferred merely from their abbreviated description in the report.

**Focus area:** D, planned versus reported analysis population. **Proposed review domain:** protocol_report. **Proposed relationship:** P_R. **All relationships:** P_R.

**Affected result or extracted value:** The primary outcome's per-protocol sensitivity estimate, OR 1.56 (0.84–2.96), P=.17, n=184, and its interpretation as analysis of the prespecified per-protocol population.

**Why context does not resolve it:** The report clearly defines its actual displayed cutoff, but does not explain why a model labeled per protocol uses a substantially different adherence rule from the final SAP's named population. Neither common terminology nor rounding makes 50% receipt and more than 80% receipt equivalent. The intended status of the displayed estimate as the planned per-protocol estimate versus an additional stricter-adherence sensitivity remains unresolved.

**Supported alternatives:** The reported result may be an additional stricter-adherence sensitivity with a shorthand population label; an amendment absent from the supplied version might have changed the rule; or the planned population and displayed population may have been operationalized differently. The package does not establish which applies.

**Human verification steps:** Compare the final SAP population definition with Figure 2 footnote e; establish the operative adherence rule and deviation exclusions for the n=184 model; clarify whether the planned per-protocol analysis was separately conducted and which estimate corresponds to it. No preferred analysis or final correction is proposed.

### F-XS-002

**Title:** Reported rescue comparison does not reconcile with the specified primary rescue model's first-drug failure-reason adjustment.

**Compared locations and document roles:** PLAN [final SAP §3.2.3 primary analysis, PDF p.61](../../../joi190092supp3_prod.pdf#page=61) continuing at [p.62](../../../joi190092supp3_prod.pdf#page=62); REPORT [main statistical analysis, p.4](../../../jama_rathinam_2019_oi_190092.pdf#page=4), [Table 2 switched-antimetabolite row and model footnote b, p.6](../../../jama_rathinam_2019_oi_190092.pdf#page=6), and [prespecified 12-month rescue narrative, p.7](../../../jama_rathinam_2019_oi_190092.pdf#page=7). Original SAP [§3.2.3, p.21](../../../joi190092supp3_prod.pdf#page=21) states the same primary rescue covariates.

**Printed statements/values:** The final SAP's primary Aim 2 model predicts success/failure with “treatment group and reason for failure of the first drug (lack of efficacy vs any other reason).” Anatomy and country are supplementary additions. SAP p.62 separately describes site adjustment as a secondary analysis. Main p.4 states that the same primary logistic model was applied at 12 months; that model has treatment fixed effect and site random effect. Table 2 footnote b specifies that model for its OR estimates. The switched-drug comparison prints 20/29 (69%) versus 7/20 (35%), RD 34.2% (95% CI 6.6–52.6), OR 4.17 (1.32–13.16), P=.02. The p.7 narrative gives the same comparison, rounded OR 4.2 (1.3–13.2), under “Prespecified 12-Month Outcomes.”

**Comparison logic:** Match the PLAN comparison MMF rescue after MTX failure versus MTX rescue after MMF failure to the REPORT's explicitly described switched-drug groups. Contrast direction reversal is merely a reference-group choice and reconciles. The primary rescue model explicitly includes first-drug failure reason; the report explicitly describes the common primary treatment/site model for its 12-month ORs, without that covariate. These are different analysis specifications. This is grounded in an affirmative report model statement, rather than inference from an absent code listing. No claim about the magnitude that adjustment would produce is made.

**Focus area:** D, planned primary rescue analysis model/covariates. **Proposed review domain:** protocol_report. **Proposed relationship:** P_R. **All relationships:** P_R.

**Affected result or extracted value:** Rescue treatment effect OR 4.17 (1.32–13.16), RD 34.2% (6.6–52.6), P=.02, and whether it represents the prespecified primary Aim 2 comparison controlling first-drug failure reason or the planned supplementary site-adjusted comparison.

**Why context does not resolve it:** The final SAP explicitly separates primary reason-adjusted rescue analysis from secondary site-adjusted analysis, whereas the article uses the general treatment/site model and provides one rescue estimate under prespecified outcomes. The article does not identify a separate reason-adjusted estimate or explain a replacement of the planned primary model. Immediate context cannot determine which model generated the displayed result or whether the planned primary comparison is also represented.

**Supported alternatives:** The result may be the SAP's secondary site-adjusted rescue analysis; the reported model footnote may abbreviate a model that also included failure reason; or the reported analysis may have replaced the planned primary model. The supplied package does not distinguish these possibilities. The SAP's permission for supplemental models does not by itself identify the displayed estimate as its reason-adjusted primary result.

**Human verification steps:** Confirm the covariates used for the switched-drug row and corresponding P value/intervals; distinguish primary Aim 2 reason-adjusted analysis from secondary site-adjusted analysis; clarify the relationship between the reported rescue result and the prespecified primary rescue comparison. No preferred analysis or final correction is proposed.
