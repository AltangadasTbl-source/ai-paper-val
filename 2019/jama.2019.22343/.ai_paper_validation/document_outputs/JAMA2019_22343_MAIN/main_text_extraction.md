# Main-article result evidence

- **Document ID:** `JAMA2019_22343_MAIN`
- **Source file:** `jama_wilson_2020_oi_190154.pdf` (11 PDF pages; JAMA printed pp. 455-465)
- **Article:** Wilson ME et al. *Association of Home Noninvasive Positive Pressure Ventilation With Clinical Outcomes in Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis.*
- **Scope:** Main-article reported results only. This is an extraction record, not an assessment of reporting accuracy.

## Outcome definitions and analysis conventions

- **Location:** PDF p. 1 / printed p. 455, Abstract, “Main Outcomes and Measures”; PDF p. 3 / printed p. 457, “Outcome Measures” and “Data Synthesis and Analysis.”
- **Primary outcomes:** mortality, all-cause hospital admissions, need for intubation, and quality of life, at the longest follow-up.
- **Key secondary outcomes:** hospital admissions for respiratory causes, emergency-department visits, ICU admissions, COPD exacerbations, activities of daily living, dyspnea, sleep quality, exercise tolerance, and adverse events.
- **Reported analysis conventions:** OR and risk difference (RD) for binary outcomes; rate ratio (RR) for count outcomes; standardized mean difference (SMD) for differently scaled continuous outcomes. Random effects were used except fixed effect when fewer than 3 studies. Two-tailed P < .05 was the stated significance threshold. Prespecified subgroups: initiation in stable COPD vs within 1 month after exacerbation; post hoc subgroups: study design and PaCO2 threshold. The article states that no interaction tests were conducted and subgroup analyses were exploratory.

## Other device-comparison results

- **Location:** PDF pp. 6-7 / printed pp. 460-461, “Other Device Comparisons.”
- HMV vs BPAP and HMV vs CPAP: no mortality studies reported. One observational study (48,856 patients) reported HMV, compared individually with BPAP and CPAP, significantly associated with fewer all-cause hospital admissions (SOE low); HMV vs CPAP was also significantly associated with fewer patients with respiratory-cause hospital admission. The report says no no-positive-airway-pressure control data/results could be included, so these findings were excluded from forest plots and other analyses.
- BPAP vs CPAP: RCT, 49 participants; patients with COPD exacerbation 30.43% vs 53.85%; RD −23% (95% CI, −50% to 3%); OR 0.38 (95% CI, 0.12-1.22); P = .10; no significant difference.
- BPAP duration: RCT, 26 participants; >6 months BPAP associated with a 43% increase in 6-minute walk distance vs 11% reduction with <6 months; P = .04.
- BPAP adherence: observational study, 54 participants; adherent (≥4 h/d on ≥70% of days) vs nonadherent: all-cause hospital admissions 0.4 vs 1.0 per patient, P = .006; ICU admissions 0.6 vs 1.2 per patient, P = .37.
- High- vs low-intensity HMV/BPAP mix: RCT, 14 participants; quality of life (COPD Assessment Test) WMD 2.30 (95% CI, −2.23 to 6.83), P = .32; no significant difference.
- BPAP volume-assured pressure-support vs BPAP spontaneous/timed: RCT, 40 participants; mortality 5.00% vs 10.00%, RD −5% (−21% to 11%), OR 0.47 (0.04-5.69), P = .56; quality of life (SGRQ) WMD −4.70 (−15.97 to 6.57), P = .41; shuttle walk WMD −4.00 m (−54.24 to 46.24), P = .88; sleep quality (Epworth) WMD −2.70 (−6.07 to 0.67), P = .12. No significant differences reported.
- Home vs hospital initiation of BPAP spontaneous/timed: RCT, 67 participants; mortality 6.06% vs 2.94%, RD 3% (−7% to 13%), OR 2.13 (0.18-24.67), P = .55; quality of life (Severe Respiratory Insufficiency Questionnaire) WMD −1.20 (−9.92 to 7.52), P = .79; dyspnea WMD 0.10 (−0.50 to 0.70), P = .74; 6-minute walk WMD −19.00 m (−64.60 to 29.60), P = .41; number of all-cause admissions WMD −0.10 (−0.60 to 0.40), P = .40. No significant differences reported.
- HMV pressure-controlled vs pressure-support: RCT, 17 participants; quality of life WMD −0.14 (−4.90 to 4.60), P = .95; 6-minute walk WMD 14 m (−42 to 70), P = .58. No significant differences reported.

### PaCO2 initiation threshold (post hoc)

- **Location:** PDF p. 7 / printed p. 461, “Subgroup Analyses by PaCO2 Initiation Threshold.”
- The authors state that no studies directly compared threshold effects, and no significant differences in mortality or all-cause hospital admissions were found by threshold. They state higher PaCO2 may be associated with improved quality of life: ≥52 mm Hg, SMD 0.18 (−0.05 to 0.40), 2 studies/311 participants; 50-51 mm Hg, SMD 0.97 (0.36 to 1.58), 1 study/49 participants; 45-49 mm Hg, SMD −0.06 (−0.28 to 0.17), 2 studies/102 participants.

## Adverse events

- **Location:** PDF p. 8 / printed p. 462, “Adverse Events” (continuation into “Discussion”).
- In 6 direct-comparison studies (414 participants), total adverse events were 0.18 vs 0.17 per patient; RR 1.08 (95% CI, 0.52-2.21); P = .84; I2 = 36.7%; no statistically significant difference reported.
- NIPPV pooled incidence rates: total adverse events 0.21 per patient (95% CI, 0.12-0.37; I2 = 75.2%); serious adverse events 0 per patient (95% CI, 0.00-0.01; I2 = 89.6%); nonserious adverse events 0.24 per patient (95% CI, 0.12-0.47; I2 = 82.5%).
- The most common serious adverse event was acute respiratory failure. Reported nonserious events included skin, eye, nose/mouth, gastrointestinal, and device/mask-intolerance symptoms. The article states that mortality, all-cause hospital admission, and intubation were primary outcomes and were not rereported as serious adverse events.

## Main reported conclusion

- **Location:** PDF p. 9 / printed p. 463, “Conclusions”; also PDF p. 1 / printed p. 455, Abstract, “Conclusions and Relevance.”
- The authors conclude that, in COPD with hypercapnia, home BPAP vs no device was associated with lower risk of mortality, all-cause hospital admission, and intubation, with no significant quality-of-life difference; HMV vs no device was associated with lower all-cause hospital admission, with no significant mortality difference. They qualify that evidence quality was low to moderate, quality-of-life evidence insufficient, and some analyses were based on few studies.
