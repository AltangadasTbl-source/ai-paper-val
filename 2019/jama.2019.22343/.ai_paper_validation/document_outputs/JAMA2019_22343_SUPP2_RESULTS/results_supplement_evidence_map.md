## eTables 8–9 — reported risk-of-bias evidence

### RCTs (eTable 8, PDF p. 41)

The overall ratings are **High** for Bhatt 2013, Casanova 2000, Cheung 2010, Clini 2002, Dreher 2010, Duiverman 2017, Duiverman 2019, Garrod 2000, Gay 1996, Köhnlein 2014, Murphy 2017, Oscroft 2014, and Zhou 2017 (13 studies); **Moderate** for De Backer 2011, Duiverman 2008/2011, Funk 2011, Marquez-Martin 2014, McEvoy 2009, Oscroft 2010, Sin 2007, and Struik 2014 (8 studies). The table identifies sequence generation, allocation concealment, participant/personnel and assessor blinding, incomplete data, selective reporting, and other sources of bias for every row.

### Observational studies (eTable 9, PDF p. 42)

The overall ratings are **Low** for Budweiser 2007, Clini 1998, Durao 2018, Galli 2014, and Paone 2014 (5 studies); **Moderate** for Gad 2015, Oscroft 2010, Vasquez 2017, and Satici 2018 (4 studies); and **High** for Clini 1996, Heinemann 2011, and Tsolaki 2008 (3 studies). The row domains are representativeness, exposure ascertainment, outcome assessment, follow-up adequacy, and conflict of interest.
