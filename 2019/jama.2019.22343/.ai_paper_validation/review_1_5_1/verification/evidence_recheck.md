## C001 — BPAP mortality pooled confidence-interval lower endpoint differs across matched main-article displays

- **Cited location found:** [DOC-001 PDF p. 1, abstract](../../../jama_wilson_2020_oi_190154.pdf#page=1), [DOC-001 PDF p. 4, Figure 1](../../../jama_wilson_2020_oi_190154.pdf#page=4), and [DOC-001 PDF p. 5, BPAP Compared With No Device narrative](../../../jama_wilson_2020_oi_190154.pdf#page=5) were found at the cited PDF pages.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** The abstract on PDF p. 1 and the results narrative on PDF p. 5 each print BPAP versus no device mortality OR 0.66 with 95% CI 0.51 to 0.87, P = .003, 13 studies, and 1423 patients.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** Figure 1 on PDF p. 4 prints the BPAP-versus-no-device subtotal as OR 0.66 with 95% CI 0.50 to 0.87. The figure contains 13 BPAP study rows. Its displayed group denominators sum to 744 NIPPV patients and 679 no-NIPPV patients, or 1423 total, matching the narrative population.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** The outcome, intervention, comparator, effect measure, point estimate, study count, patient total, confidence level, and displayed precision are matched. No distinct population, time point, model, or confidence level is stated. Under those printed conditions, the rounded pooled interval should be the same across displays.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The point estimate and upper endpoint agree. The lower endpoints differ by `0.51 - 0.50 = 0.01`. The Figure 1 denominators reproduce `744 + 679 = 1423` patients.
- **Necessary inputs available and exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package supplies the matched labels, rounded estimates, confidence level, study identities, group denominators, and total. It does not supply the unrounded pooled lower endpoint, analysis output, pooled standard error, model command, or figure-versus-narrative rounding/export rule needed to identify which display arose from the intended final calculation.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** Both displays could have been generated from a common unrounded value near a two-decimal boundary using different display or export routines. The supplied pages do not state such a distinction.
- **Direct observation:** The direct PDF prints 0.51 as the lower endpoint in two narrative locations and 0.50 in the matched forest-plot subtotal.
- **Inferred explanation:** A boundary-rounding difference, export-version difference, or transcription difference is possible, but the source does not identify the production mechanism.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which lower endpoint is intended for the 13-study, 1423-patient BPAP mortality result, and were the abstract, Figure 1, and results narrative generated from the same unrounded final analysis under one display rule?

## C003 — Quality-of-life direction label conflicts with the stated standardized direction

- **Cited location found:** [DOC-001 PDF p. 3, Data Synthesis and Analysis](../../../jama_wilson_2020_oi_190154.pdf#page=3), [DOC-001 PDF p. 5, Figure 4](../../../jama_wilson_2020_oi_190154.pdf#page=5), [DOC-001 PDF p. 8, Table 2 footnote b](../../../jama_wilson_2020_oi_190154.pdf#page=8), and [DOC-003 PDF p. 15, eTable 3](../../../joi190154supp2_prod.pdf#page=15) were found at the cited PDF pages.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** The methods on DOC-001 PDF p. 3 say, “We standardized the direction of the measures and used higher scores to represent better outcomes.” Table 2 footnote b on DOC-001 PDF p. 8 says, “Higher scores indicate worse quality of life.”
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** DOC-003 PDF p. 15 directly shows mixed native directions: higher COPD Assessment Test and St George's Respiratory Questionnaire scores indicate more severe impact or more limitations, while higher Chronic Respiratory Disease Questionnaire and Severe Respiratory Insufficiency Questionnaire scores indicate better outcomes. Figure 4 on DOC-001 PDF p. 5 labels the negative side of zero “Favors NIPPV” and the positive side “Favors No NIPPV.” The corrected stable ledger now records this direct orientation.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Once mixed native scales are standardized to one stated direction, the sign of the SMD, the table's higher-score footnote, and the forest plot's favor labels should all express that same polarity. The stated p. 3 convention implies a positive standardized score difference represents a better outcome, whereas Figure 4's positive side favors no NIPPV and Table 2 says higher scores represent worse quality of life.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The direct polarity comparison is `positive SMD = higher standardized score`. Under the p. 3 statement, that maps to `better outcome`; under Table 2 footnote b, it maps to `worse quality of life`. Figure 4 maps the positive side to `Favors No NIPPV`, while the intervention is NIPPV. These mappings cannot all describe one standardized sign convention without an additional scale-specific explanation.
- **Necessary inputs available and exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package supplies the general standardization statement, Figure 4 favor labels, Table 2 footnote, study-level instrument names, and native instrument directions. It does not supply each study's extracted group means, the actual sign-reversal operations, an explicit declaration of which group was subtracted from which, or a statement that Table 2 intentionally reverted to native-scale polarity.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** Table 2 footnote b may have been intended to describe selected original scales rather than the standardized SMD, and Figure 4 may have used the opposite group-subtraction orientation. That reading would require an explicit distinction because the p. 3 methods state that directions were standardized, and eTable 3 shows that the native scales do not share one direction.
- **Direct observation:** The methods print higher standardized scores as better; Table 2 prints higher scores as worse; Figure 4 labels the positive side as favoring no NIPPV; and the supplement prints mixed native-scale directions.
- **Inferred explanation:** A footnote written for native scales, a forest-plot favor-label reversal, an unstated subtraction convention, or an incomplete description of sign transformations could account for the observed labels. The source does not establish which explanation applies.
- **Exact remaining human question:** For every quality-of-life result, were study effects sign-transformed so that a higher standardized score means a better outcome, and, under the actual group-subtraction convention, what favor labels and Table 2 footnote were intended for positive SMD values?

## C004 — High-versus-low intensity CAT confidence interval differs between the main article and supplement

- **Cited location found:** [DOC-001 PDF p. 7, Other Device Comparisons narrative](../../../jama_wilson_2020_oi_190154.pdf#page=7) and [DOC-003 PDF p. 43, eTable 10](../../../joi190154supp2_prod.pdf#page=43) were found at the cited PDF pages.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** DOC-001 PDF p. 7 prints an RCT of 14 patients comparing high-intensity HMV/BPAP pressure-controlled ventilation with low-intensity HMV/BPAP pressure-support ventilation for quality of life measured by the COPD Assessment Test: WMD 2.30, 95% CI -2.23 to 6.83, P = .32.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** DOC-003 eTable 10 on PDF p. 43 prints the same comparison, outcome, one-RCT design, and 14-patient total with WMD 2.30 and 95% CI -2.35 to 6.95. The table states that higher COPD Assessment Test scores represent worse outcomes.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** The comparison, outcome instrument, design, study count, patient count, point estimate, confidence level, and displayed precision are matched. No distinct analysis set, time point, or calculation is stated, so the rounded confidence interval should be the same.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The lower endpoints differ by `-2.23 - (-2.35) = 0.12`; the upper endpoints differ by `6.95 - 6.83 = 0.12`. Each interval is centered on the printed WMD: `(-2.23 + 6.83) / 2 = 2.30` and `(-2.35 + 6.95) / 2 = 2.30`. Their half-widths are 4.53 and 4.65, respectively.
- **Necessary inputs available and exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package supplies the matched labels, WMD, interval, study count, patient count, and the main-article P value. It does not supply the group summary data, exact standard error, confidence-interval construction, analysis output, or version history needed to reproduce either half-width from first principles.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The article and supplement may reflect different analysis or export versions, or one may use an unstated standard-error calculation. Neither page names such a distinction.
- **Direct observation:** The same 2.30 point estimate and 14-patient comparison are printed with two symmetrically different interval widths.
- **Inferred explanation:** A calculation-version, input-version, export, or transcription difference is possible, but the source does not establish its mechanism.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which 95% confidence interval belongs to the 14-patient high-versus-low intensity COPD Assessment Test result, and were the article and eTable 10 generated from the same group data and interval calculation?

The matched main-article narrative on DOC-001 PDF p. 6 identifies one RCT of 49 patients for BPAP versus CPAP and cites its own reference 20; DOC-001's reference list identifies reference 20 as the same Cheung 2010 pilot trial.

- **Direct observation:** The direct supplement prints group counts of 24 and 23 in the Cheung baseline row and prints 49 patients for the same trial's BPAP-versus-CPAP effectiveness result; the main article also prints 49.
- **Inferred explanation:** Different analysis populations, missing baseline data, or a transcription difference could produce the two-person gap. The package does not identify which mechanism applies.

## C006 — Final-report meta-analysis model rule differs from the protocol rule for syntheses with 3 through 18 studies

- **Cited location found:** [DOC-002 PDF p. 11, Data Synthesis](../../../joi190154supp1_prod.pdf#page=11), [DOC-001 PDF p. 3, Data Synthesis and Analysis](../../../jama_wilson_2020_oi_190154.pdf#page=3), [DOC-001 PDF p. 4, Figures 1 and 2](../../../jama_wilson_2020_oi_190154.pdf#page=4), and [DOC-001 PDF p. 5, Figures 3 and 4](../../../jama_wilson_2020_oi_190154.pdf#page=5) were found at the cited PDF pages.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** The protocol on DOC-002 PDF p. 11 says meta-analysis is considered when more than two studies address the same PICOTS. It then states that DerSimonian-Laird random effects will be used when the number of included studies is larger than 18 and that otherwise DerSimonian-Laird random effects with the Knapp-Hartung variance adjustment will be used.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** The final article on DOC-001 PDF p. 3 states that the DerSimonian-Laird random-effect method was used except when fewer than three studies were included, when a fixed-effect Mantel-Haenszel model was used. The article does not mention the protocol's Knapp-Hartung variance-adjustment branch. Concrete displayed syntheses in the overlapping range include the 13-study BPAP mortality subtotal and 15-study overall mortality result in Figure 1, the 5-study BPAP and 6-study overall hospital-admission results in Figure 2, the 3-study intubation result in Figure 3, and the 9-study quality-of-life result in Figure 4.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Combining the protocol's meta-analysis threshold of more than two studies with its model branch assigns DerSimonian-Laird plus Knapp-Hartung to `3 <= k <= 18`. The final article's printed rule assigns its described DerSimonian-Laird random-effect method to every `k >= 3` synthesis and does not state whether Knapp-Hartung was retained. The two source descriptions therefore differ specifically for the printed 3-, 5-, 6-, 9-, 13-, and 15-study results. This is a concrete reporting and model-label comparison tied to displayed syntheses, not a general concern about method choice.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Protocol branch: `k > 18` gives DerSimonian-Laird without the separately named adjustment; `3 <= k <= 18` gives DerSimonian-Laird with Knapp-Hartung. Final-article branch: `k < 3` gives fixed-effect Mantel-Haenszel; `k >= 3` gives the stated DerSimonian-Laird random-effect method. The intersection `3 <= k <= 18` contains every listed printed synthesis count.
- **Necessary inputs available and exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package supplies both written model rules and the study counts for the displayed syntheses. It does not supply per-synthesis statistical commands or output, degrees of freedom, critical-value rule, variance-estimator details, an amendment to the protocol, or a reconciliation stating whether Knapp-Hartung was actually used but omitted from the article's abbreviated description.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The final article may provide only a high-level random-effects label while silently retaining the protocol-specified Knapp-Hartung variance adjustment, or the analysis plan may have been amended after the protocol. Either interpretation is compatible with the supplied prose, but no supplied amendment or model-output record distinguishes them.
- **Direct observation:** The protocol explicitly names Knapp-Hartung for the 3-through-18-study branch; the final article gives a different threshold description and omits that adjustment; the figures directly print several study counts inside that overlapping range.
- **Inferred explanation:** Whether the actual calculations departed from the protocol cannot be inferred from the confidence intervals alone without the missing analysis settings and output. The directly reproducible issue is the source-to-source model-rule description for concrete printed results.
- **Exact remaining human question:** For each printed synthesis with 3 through 18 studies, was the Knapp-Hartung variance adjustment used, and, if not, where is the analysis-plan amendment; if it was used, why is it absent from the final article's model description?
