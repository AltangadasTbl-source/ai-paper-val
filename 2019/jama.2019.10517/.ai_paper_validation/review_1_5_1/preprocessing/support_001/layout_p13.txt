591   increments to reach a target dose of 150 mg/day by the end of the first week of treatment. Participants who did
592   not experience at least partial improvement (based on operationalized criteria) in depressive symptoms, after
593   taking sertraline at a dosage of 150 mg/day for 4 weeks, will have the sertraline dose increased to 200 mg/day,
594   as tolerated. Olanzapine will be started at 5 mg/day and increased by 5 mg increments to reach a target dose
595   of 15 mg/day by the end of the first week of treatment. If there is no improvement or inadequate improvement
596   in psychosis after 7 days of 15 mg/day of olanzapine, the dosage of olanzapine will be increased to 20 mg/day,
597   as tolerated. Slower titration or temporary dose reductions of one or both medications will be allowed if there
598   are clinically significant side effects, but a concerted effort will be made to achieve the target dosages, as
599   tolerated.
600   Stabilization Phase: When participants meet remission criteria, they will continue with open-label sertraline
601   and olanzapine during the 8-week stabilization phase. Since the goal of this phase is to consolidate stability of
602   remission, adjustment of the doses of study medications will be allowed, if necessitated by clinical worsening
603   or significant side effects. The maximum allowable daily dosages of sertraline and olanzapine, respectively, will
604   be 200mg and 20mg, and the minimum allowable daily dosages will be 50mg and 5mg. Because sustained
605   remission of psychosis during the stabilization phase will be required for eligibility for the RCT, participants will
606   leave the stabilization phase if they experience a relapse of delusions or hallucinations during that time as
607   determined by SCID interview.
608   Randomized Phase: Participants who meet the study’s remission criteria will be randomly allocated, following
609   completion of the stabilization phase, to either continue olanzapine or switch, over a period of 4-weeks, from
610   olanzapine to placebo. All participants will take open-label sertraline for the duration of the RCT. The goal will
611   be to maintain sertraline at the same acute dose that the participant was prescribed at the time of
612   randomization to the RCT. Because participants will have taken sertraline for a minimum of 10 weeks before
613   entering the RCT, we anticipate that the dose of sertraline will rarely need to be adjusted in the RCT because
614   of side effects. However, a reduction of the dosage of sertraline will be allowed if the site PI agrees. An
615   increase in dose of sertraline will not be permitted at any time during the RCT, because it will confound testing
616   of H1. The goal will be to maintain olanzapine/placebo at the same acute dose that the participant was
617   prescribed at the time of randomization to the RCT. However, a change in dose of olanzapine/placebo will be
618   permitted, if necessitated by adverse effects or clinical worsening during the RCT, following discussion with the
619   site PI. The rationale for allowing a change in olanzapine/placebo dose, is that the primary aim of the study is
620   to assess whether olanzapine, administered within a clinically relevant dose range, prevents relapse, not
621   whether a specific dose of olanzapine prevents relapse. Dose changes of sertraline and olanzapine/placebo
622   will be carefully recorded and will be reported in a descriptive fashion for each treatment arm.

624   3.10 DATA ANALYSIS
625   3.10.1 Data Management is described as a part of Quality Control in Section 4.1.6.
626   3.10.2 Randomization: We will randomize 88 participants to each of the two treatment arms using a 1:1
627   allocation ratio, stratified by age, remission group at randomization, and site. In order to reduce the probability
628   that a disproportionate number of subjects are randomized to any one level of the factors a blocking strategy
629   will be used. Randomization will be conducted by Dr. Leon, the study’s statistician, using the Dallal software.
630   3.10.3 Data Analytic Procedures: Initially, baseline demographic and clinical characteristics will be
631   examined. Frequency distributions will be produced. Descriptive and graphical displays will be produced.
632   Transformations will be used when distributional assumptions are not fulfilled for inferential tests. Treatment
633   groups will be compared on baseline demographic and clinical variables using t-tests for continuous variables,
634   Mann-Whitney tests for ordinal variables, and chi-square tests for categorical variables. If significant baseline
635   group imbalance is detected on any particular variable, that variable will be included as a covariate in the
636   inferential analyses if its correlation with outcome is 0.30 or greater. Dropouts and completers will also be
637   compared on baseline variables using t-tests, Mann-Whitney tests, or chi-square tests. The primary analyses
638   will adhere to the Intent-to-Treat principle. Each statistical test for H1-3 will involve a two-tailed alpha of .05.
