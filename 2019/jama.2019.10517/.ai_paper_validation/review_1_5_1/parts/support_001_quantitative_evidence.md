## Page-complete evidence map

### PDF p. 1 — contents (no result-relevant quantitative relationship)

The contents identifies the protocol sections, including Research Design and Methods, Data Analysis, Human Subjects, and Appendix 1. Its printed internal pagination is not a result, measure, or statistical assertion.

### PDF p. 2 — project summary

- Historical contextual prevalence: 19%-45% of inpatients with major depression have psychotic features. The proposed RCT is 36 weeks, double blind, and substitutes placebo for olanzapine in half the group after sustained remission.
- The primary comparative statement is continuation sertraline+olanzapine versus sertraline+placebo for prevention of relapse. Planned exploratory domains are age, genetic polymorphisms, and population pharmacokinetics.

### PDF p. 3 — aims and planned contrasts

- Acute open-label sertraline+olanzapine: maximum 12 weeks; stabilization: an additional 8 weeks; randomized double-blind treatment: 36 weeks.
- **H1 / primary:** sertraline+olanzapine has less relapse risk than sertraline+placebo.
- **H2 / secondary:** sertraline+olanzapine has higher weight, total cholesterol, and triglycerides than sertraline+placebo in the randomized phase.
- **H3 / secondary:** older age is associated with less weight gain in the open-label phase.
- Exploratory: age moderates randomized-phase changes in weight/metabolic variables; selected genetic polymorphisms associate with response, relapse, and weight/metabolic variables across phases.

### PDF pp. 4-5 — contextual quantitative information

- Lifetime psychotic features: 15%-20% of people with major depression; psychosis among inpatients with major depression: 19%-45% (p. 4).
- Historical relapse ranges: naturalistic PD 17%-50% (mean 30%) over 12 months; following ECT 35%-50% at 12 months; approximately 35% of relapses in one chart review followed antipsychotic discontinuation; a prospective 30-person study reported 27% over 8 months; maintained-treatment studies reported 0%-6% at 12 months (p. 4).
- Historical pilot: 16 younger adults receiving aripiprazole+citalopram, 63% akathisia, mean aripiprazole dose 14.4 +/- 5.5 mg. Olanzapine sales: 25% of US atypical-antipsychotic sales and 30% worldwide (p. 5). These are contextual, not proposed-RCT endpoints.

### PDF p. 6 — historical STOP-PD results cited by the protocol

- Randomized total 259 = 142 aged >=60 + 117 aged <60; site subtotals 56 Cornell + 59 Massachusetts + 63 Pittsburgh + 81 Toronto = 259.
- Combination versus monotherapy remission over the trial: OR 1.28, 95% CI 1.12-1.47, P<.001. Last assessment remission: 41.9% versus 23.9%; chi-square(1)=9.53, P=.002; NNT 5.6. Completed-12-week remission: 66.7% versus 49.2%; chi-square(1)=4.4, P=.036; NNT 5.7.
- Age comparison of remission: OR 1.05, 95% CI 0.80-1.37, P=.75.
- Weight gain is printed as older 7.3 +/- 10.3 lb versus younger 13.9 +/- 4.0 lb; F(1,226)=14.51, P=.0002. Younger subjects only: fasting glucose mean change 8.4 +/- 41.3 mg/dL from baseline 93.6 +/- 20.4 mg/dL; t(211)=2.65, P=.009. Adverse-effect withdrawal: 5%.
- Pharmacokinetic pilot: n=66 young + n=102 old = 168. Exposure variability: 0.98 +/- 0.27 versus 0.96 +/- 0.30, t(166)=0.36, P=.72. Exposure magnitude: 1128.9 +/- 737.6 versus 1027.1 +/- 477.6 ug/mL*h, unequal-variances t(102)=1.0, P=.32.

### PDF p. 7 — historical stabilization data and planned subject flow

- Historical stabilization cohort: 74 = 29 young + 45 old. Of 48 combination-treatment remitters, 10.4% later relapsed. Weight-gain rates: approximately 1.5 lb/month in stabilization versus 4.4 lb/month in STOP-PD. Age-by-time glucose F(3,149)=3.16, P<.03; triglycerides P=.08, total cholesterol P=.07, LDL cholesterol P=.09.
- **Figure 1 planned flow:** Acute N=392, balanced ages n=196 age 18-59 and n=196 age >=60. Figure indicates 50% meeting remission/near-remission -> stabilization N=196, then 10% relapse/withdraw -> discontinuation RCT N=176. The rounded flow is checkable: 196 x 0.90 = 176.4, displayed as 176.
- Acute target doses: sertraline 150 mg/day and olanzapine 15 mg/day, up to 12 weeks. Full remission requires no delusions/hallucinations plus 17-item HAM-D <=10 at two consecutive assessments. Near remission requires CGI “very much”/“much improved,” HAM-D 11-15, and >=50% baseline HAM-D reduction during the last two acute assessments. Stabilization is 8 weeks; RCT is 36 weeks. RCT MMSE threshold is >=24, subject to documented case-by-case exception below 24.

### PDF p. 8 — recruitment/flow arithmetic

- Timeline: 4-month start-up, 51-month acute/stabilization/RCT period including 38 months recruitment, and 5-month analysis/write-up period.
- Recruitment target 392 over four sites and 38 months is 392/(4 x 38) = 2.58, consistent with the printed “2.6 participants enrolled per site per month.”
- Historical STOP-PD combination arm: 63/129 = 48.8% full remission; 10/129 = 7.8% near-remission definition. Combined 73/129 = 56.6%, consistent with printed 56.6% after rounding.
- Proposed planning assumptions: 60% acute-phase remission/near-remission expected, but 392 are recruited so that a 50% rate gives 196 stabilization entrants. Expected 10% noncompletion in stabilization gives 196 x 0.90 = 176.4, consistent with target RCT N=176 after whole-person rounding.
- One-third of STOP-PD combination remitters achieved remission only between weeks 6 and 12 (p. 8).

### PDF p. 9 — eligibility definitions and duration rationale

- Stabilization chosen as 8 weeks. RCT is 36 weeks; the stated high-risk period after discontinuation is 3 months. The taper is 4 weeks; cited open-label study had no discontinuation side effects or re-emergence of psychosis during that taper.
- Near-remission eligibility permits HAM-D 11-15 while requiring >=50% baseline improvement. Conventional non-PD remission cut point is described as <8-10 HAM-D.
- At end of stabilization, MMSE <24 excludes from RCT unless explained by non-cognitive factors; historical stabilization sample: 1/37 remitters had MMSE <24.

### PDF pp. 10-11 — measures and Table 1 schedule

- Primary ratings: SCID-IV, GRID 17-item HAM-D, DAS, SADS delusion/hallucination items, SSI, CGI, and MMSE. Secondary relapse-risk covariates are anxiety, medical burden, executive dysfunction, psychomotor change, and treatment resistance.
- Anthropometrics: weight, BMI, waist circumference. BMI is defined as weight relative to height; waist circumference is measured at the umbilicus. Pre-morbid weight is average weight in the year before the index depression and is used in a secondary weight-change analysis.
- Metabolic measures: HOMA is the primary insulin-resistance/glycemic measure, calculated from fasting glucose and insulin using an established formula; HbA1c is secondary. Triglycerides and total/LDL/HDL cholesterol are measured.
- **Table 1:** acute phase variable 4-12 weeks; stabilization 8 weeks; discontinuation RCT 36 weeks. The layout schedules ratings, weight/waist, height, metabolic labs, drug levels, genetics, biomarkers, vital signs, cognitive and safety assessments across named weeks. Footnote C specifies telephone evaluations at stabilization weeks 2 and 6 and RCT weeks 10,14,18,22,26,30,34. Footnote F defines primary clinical ratings. Footnote H defines the cognitive testing schedule. Footnote J defines the terminal blinding assessment as testing whether participant/rater treatment guesses exceed chance.
- Genotyping quality control: re-genotype ambiguous results; re-genotype 10% to assess error; stated laboratory test error rate 0.5% (p. 11).

### PDF p. 12 — pharmacokinetics and outcome definition

- Population structure plan: 96 ancestry-informative markers, admixture model with correlated frequencies, 20,000 burn-ins and 20,000 repetitions; membership probabilities are pharmacogenetic-analysis covariates.
- Drug-exposure definitions: magnitude is average concentration or AUC; variability is predicted-to-observed concentration ratio Cpred/obs. Sparse sampling is 2-4 plasma samples per patient.
- Primary outcome relapse includes any one of: major depressive episode; HAM-D >=18 plus mean absolute increase >=5 points relative to RCT entry; psychosis plus SADS delusion/hallucination severity >=3; high suicide risk, mania/hypomania, or psychiatric hospitalization. Preferred persistence is >=1 week over two consecutive weekly visits for specified symptomatic criteria. Time to relapse is weeks/randomization-to-first established relapse or hospital start.
- Expected relapse service burden: average 3 cases per year per site (a planning statement).

### PDF p. 13 — treatment ranges and randomization

- Acute target doses: sertraline 150 mg/day (50-mg increments; potentially 200 mg/day after 4 weeks at 150) and olanzapine 15 mg/day (5-mg increments; potentially 20 mg/day after 7 days at 15). Stabilization ranges: sertraline 50-200 mg/day; olanzapine 5-20 mg/day.
- RCT: 4-week switch from olanzapine to placebo, continued open-label sertraline; no sertraline dose increase; olanzapine/placebo changes permitted for adverse effects/worsening and recorded by arm.
- Randomization: 176 total, 88 per arm, 1:1 allocation, stratified by age, remission group, and site; blocking is used.
- General analysis rules: transformations if distributional assumptions fail; t tests continuous, Mann-Whitney ordinal, chi-square categorical; baseline covariate enters inferential analyses only if group imbalance is significant and correlation with outcome is >=0.30. ITT governs primary analyses; H1-H3 tests are two-tailed alpha .05.

### PDF pp. 13-14 — planned statistical models

- H1: Cox proportional hazards model; survival time in weeks, randomization to relapse; censor at discontinuation or end follow-up. Kaplan-Meier curves are descriptive. Mandatory Cox predictors: treatment and stratification variables (site, full/near remission, age 18-59/>=60). Conditional predictors: the five baseline risk variables if group P<=.05 and correlation with relapse r>.30. Planned RCT attrition <=10%; noninformative censoring check compares prior-week HAM-D among dropout/non-dropout; constant hazards check uses treatment x vulnerable-period interaction.
- H2: separate mixed-effects linear regressions for weight, cholesterol, triglycerides. Up to 15 repeated weights and 5 repeated cholesterol/triglyceride measurements. Successive likelihood-ratio tests assess quadratic time, treatment-by-site, and treatment-by-time; H0 is rejected if treatment-by-time is significant. Later terms remain only when significant. Weekly indicators and splines assess nonlinear time. After lipid-treatment change, relevant subsequent metabolic values are excluded from the mixed model; expected <5%, stated as <10 RCT participants.
- H3: mixed-effects model of acute-baseline weight change; up to 6 assessments over 12-20 weeks; age is primary fixed effect; LR tests of quadratic time and age-by-time; H0 rejected if the interaction is significant.
- E1: randomized age moderation uses mixed models for weight, total cholesterol, triglycerides, insulin resistance and is expressly magnitude-focused with no significance testing. E2 genotype QC excludes tag SNPs call rate <95% or MAF <10%; discards HWE failures at P<.001; then uses one genotype/model Cox models for time to remission/relapse and mixed models for weight/metabolic outcomes. Haplotypes <10% frequency are excluded.

### PDF pp. 15-16 — adverse events, power, attrition, and Tables 2-3

- Nonmetabolic safety reports N and percent among those not meeting the event criterion at acute baseline. Thresholds include >=2-item UKU increase or item score 3; AIMS global >=2; Barnes global >=2; Simpson-Angus mean >=2; SSI item 4/5 >=1; standing systolic decrease >=20 mm Hg or diastolic decrease >=10 mm Hg within 3 minutes; >=1 fall. Separate N/% is reported from RCT week 8 onward. These are descriptive because of many variables.
- H1 power assumptions: clinically meaningful relapse-rate difference 20 percentage points, 15% olanzapine vs 35% placebo; absolute risk difference .20 implies NNT=1/.20=5. Expected RCT attrition <=10%.
- **Table 2:** total N=176 (88/group), two-tailed alpha .05, simulation using S+ `survfit`. Printed scenarios (placebo relapse / olanzapine relapse / attrition / power): 40%/15%/10%/.95; 40%/15%/15%/.94; 35%/15%/10%/.84; 35%/15%/15%/.82; 35%/10%/10%/.98; 35%/10%/15%/.97.
- H2 assumptions: continued olanzapine weight gain average 1 lb/month up to 9 months in cited RCTs; STOP-PD stabilization 1.5 lb/month; triglyceride increase 8-10 mg/dL/month in cited RCTs and ~9 mg/dL/month in STOP-PD; placebo trends toward acute-baseline values.
- **Table 3:** mixed-effects power. Four observations/subject: standardized effects .35/.40/.45 give lipid powers .74/.84/.89 (ICC=.50) and weight powers .77/.85/.93 (ICC=.95). Eight observations/subject: .35/.40/.45 give weight powers .80/.89/.95. Assumption: at least 8/15 repeated weight assessments and 4/5 metabolic assessments. Claimed >=.80 power detects endpoint standardized differences >=.35 for weight and >=.40 for triglycerides/cholesterol, represented as 4.9 lb, 38 mg/dL, and 22 mg/dL. H3 assumes at least 4/6 weights; power >.80 for standardized difference .40. Historical 12-week gains 16.6 +/- 16.5 lb young and 10.7 +/- 11.3 lb old, standardized difference .42.
- Attrition strategy: H1 ITT continues assessment through randomized treatment/relapse despite nonadherence. Mixed models assume ignorable attrition; diagnostic/sensitivity comparisons add dropout-pattern main effect or Intent-to-Attend covariate.
