1. PROJECT SUMMARY 30
Psychotic depression (PD) is a severe disabling disorder with considerable morbidity and mortality. Between 31
19% and 45% of inpatients with major depression have psychotic features, with greater prevalence in older 32
patients. Although electroconvulsive therapy has well-established efficacy in the treatment of PD, its use is 33
limited by several factors. As a result, the pharmacologic treatment of PD is common. Expert guidelines 34
recommend the combination of antidepressant and antipsychotic medications in the pharmacologic treatment 35
of PD. The recently completed Study of the Pharmacotherapy of Psychotic Depression (STOP-PD) was the 36
first NIMH-funded randomized controlled trial (RCT) to examine the efficacy and tolerability of newer 37
antidepressant and antipsychotic medications in the acute treatment of younger and older persons with PD. 38
The combination of sertraline and olanzapine was significantly more efficacious than olanzapine combined with 39
placebo. Both treatments were equally well tolerated, but were associated with clinically significant weight gain 40
and elevation of lipids. Older persons, however, had significantly less weight gain than younger persons. 41
42
Little is known about the continuation treatment of PD. Of particular concern, it is not known whether 43
antipsychotic medication needs to be continued once an episode of PD responds to pharmacotherapy. This 44
issue has profound clinical relevance. On the one hand, the unnecessary continuation of antipsychotic 45
medication exposes a patient to adverse effects, such as weight gain and metabolic disturbance. On the other 46
hand, premature discontinuation of antipsychotic medication has the potential risk of early relapse of a severe 47
disorder. The primary goal of this Renewal application, therefore, is to assess the risks versus benefits of 48
continuing olanzapine in younger and older patients with PD, once the episode of depression has responded to 49
treatment with sertraline and olanzapine. This goal will be addressed through a 36-week double-blind RCT, in 50
which placebo is substituted for olanzapine in half the study group, following a period of sustained remission. 51
We hypothesize that sertraline+olanzapine will be more efficacious than sertraline+placebo in preventing 52
relapse of PD. This study provides the unique opportunity to systematically assess the effect of antipsychotic 53
discontinuation (as opposed to switching from one antipsychotic to another) on olanzapine-related weight gain 54
and metabolic disturbance. Additional innovative aims of the study are to examine age and genetic 55
polymorphisms as predictors/moderators of treatment variability, potentially leading to more personalized 56
treatment of PD, and to employ population pharmacokinetics to determine the magnitude and consistency of 57
exposure to study drugs. Olanzapine is selected because it is the only atypical antipsychotic with established 58
efficacy and tolerability in the treatment of both younger and older adults with PD . This research will be 59
transformative, by providing clinicians with a much-needed evidence base to guide the continuation treatment 60
of one of the most disabling and lethal psychiatric disorders. 61

2. SPECIFIC AIMS 85
2.1 INTRODUCTION TO STUDY AIMS: Major depression with psychotic features (psychotic depression; PD) 86
is a severe disorder associated with considerable morbidity and mortality.1
Although ECT has well-established 87
efficacy as a treatment for PD,2,3
its use is limited by several factors: a high rate of relapse following completion 88
of ECT,4,5
lower rates of response in non-academic centers,6
significant disparities in its availability,7
and the 89
fact that many patients prefer pharmacologic treatment, because they find the concept of ECT and the 90
potential side effect of cognitive impairment unacceptable. Because of these limitations, the pharmacologic 91
treatment of PD is common.8
Expert guidelines recommend the combination of antidepressant and 92
antipsychotic medications as an alternative to ECT in the treatment of PD.9,10
The recently completed Study of 93
the Pharmacotherapy of Psychotic Depression (STOP-PD)11
, which involved collaboration of the current group 94
of principal investigators, was the first NIMH-funded RCT to examine the efficacy and tolerability of 95
combination therapy using newer antidepressant and antipsychotic medications in the acute treatment of PD. 96
The combination of sertraline and olanzapine was significantly more efficacious than olanzapine monotherapy, 97
and the two treatments had comparable tolerability.11
Nevertheless, both treatments were associated with 98
increases in weight and lipids over the 12-week study.11
99
Once an episode of major depression responds to antidepressant medication, the antidepressant must be 100
continued to prevent relapse.12
However, it is not known whether antipsychotic medication needs to be 101
continued once an episode of PD has responded to combined antidepressant-antipsychotic treatment. This 102
issue is of profound clinical importance. On the one hand, the unnecessary continuation of antipsychotic 103
medication exposes a patient to adverse effects, some of which have considerable public health significance. 104
On the other hand, premature discontinuation of antipsychotic medication has the potential risk of early relapse 105
of a severe, disabling disorder. The main goal of this collaborative Renewal Application, therefore, is to assess 106
the benefits and risks of continuing olanzapine in older and younger persons with PD, once the episode of 107
depression has responded to treatment with sertraline and olanzapine. This goal will be addressed through a 108
RCT, in which placebo is substituted for olanzapine in half the study group, following a period of sustained 109
remission. Specifically, we propose a three-phase study. In the Acute Phase, participants will receive open- 110
label sertraline and olanzapine for a maximum of 12 weeks. Subjects who respond to treatment will enter the 111
Stabilization Phase, where they will continue to receive open-label sertraline and olanzapine for an additional 8 112
weeks. Participants who meet remission criteria will then be randomized to 36 weeks of double-blind treatment 113
with either sertraline+olanzapine or sertraline+placebo. 114
