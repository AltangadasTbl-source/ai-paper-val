8.3 Choice of acetylsalicylic acid dose
Most participants in our previous study (7) used acetylsalicylic acid in a dose of 100 mg per
day. This dosing scheme leads to predictable suppression of platelet function after repeated
administration. However, the onset of effects is slow and maximal suppression is achieved
only after about 5 days (27). Therefore, a loading dose of 500 mg is given in clinical routine
when a rapid onset of thrombocyte inhibition is required. Based on published data a dose of
300 mg seems to be similarly effective (48, 49). However, an enteric-coated preparation of the
same dose was associated with submaximal inhibition (49) and will therefore not be used in
our study.

8.4 Potential benefits of the study medication
The participants in this study have no indication for acetylsalicylic acid and no individual
benefit from taking one dose of acetylsalicylic acid. Should this study show that one dose of
acetylsalicylic acid strongly increases the sensitivity of the iFOBTs without relevant loss in
specificity, these findings may improve diagnostic procedures in future patients, which might
affect also study patients if repeated screening is required.
