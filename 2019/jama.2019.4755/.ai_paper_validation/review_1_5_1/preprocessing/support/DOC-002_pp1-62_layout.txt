1. SUMMARY


Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most common cancer and the fourth most common
cancer cause of death worldwide, with over 1.2 million new cases and over 600,000 deaths
annually. The commonly slow progression from detectable and fully curable precursors
(adenomas) to still-curable early-stage CRC to advanced CRC provides much better
perspectives for prevention by early detection than for most other cancer types. Thus, the
majority of CRCs could be prevented by early detection and removal of adenomas. However,
participation of patients in endoscopic screening programs is low, likely due to its
invasiveness. Higher participation rates can be achieved with non-invasive stool- or blood-
based screening tests. However, such tests suffer from poor sensitivity in detecting precursors
for CRC.
In a recent study by our group (Division of Clinical Epidemiology and Aging Research),
sensitivity for detecting advanced colorectal neoplasms by immunochemical fecal occult
blood test (iFOBT) was 70.8% among users of low-dose acetylsalicylic acid compared with
35.9% among non-users (p=0.001), whereas there were only very small differences in
specificity. In ROC analysis, the area under the curve (AUC) was much higher for
acetylsalicylic acid users than for non-users, with particularly strong differences in men (0.87
versus 0.68, p=0.003). This finding suggests that use of acetylsalicylic acid before conduction
of iFOBT might be a promising approach to improve non-invasive screening for CRC.
In the randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study that we propose, the diagnostic
performance (sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive values, likelihood ratios,
area under the curve) of two iFOBTs for detecting advanced colorectal neoplasms after a
single low dose of acetylsalicylic acid (300 mg) compared to placebo will be evaluated.
Acetylsalicylic acid or placebo will be administered at least 5 days before a planned, study-
independent colonoscopic screening in 2,400 participants aged 40 to 80 years (i.e. there will
be at least 4 full days between drug intake and colonoscopy). Stool samples will be obtained
before and on three days after the single dose of acetylsalicylic acid/placebo.
The diagnostic performance of the iFOBTs will be compared to the results of the colonoscopy
as a gold standard for the diagnosis of colorectal neoplasms. Additional study questions
include gender-specific performance of the tests and gain in diagnostic performance by test
application on multiple days. In addition, optional blood samples are taken for future
biomarker analyses.

3. STUDY SYNOPSIS

3.1 Title
Use of Acetylsalicylic Acid (ASA) for Enhanced Early Detection of Colorectal Neoplasms


3.2 Objectives
Main objective
To evaluate diagnostic performance (sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive
values, likelihood ratios, area under the curve) of 2 immunochemical Fecal Occult Blood
Tests (iFOBTs) for detecting advanced colorectal neoplasms after a single dose of
acetylsalicylic acid as compared to placebo.

Secondary objectives
To study gender-specific performance of the 2 iFOBTs and the possible gain in diagnostic
performance by stool sampling on multiple days.
To study the safety of single-dose acetylsalicylic acid in the selected population.
To collect blood samples for additional biomarker analyses (optional)


3.3 Intervention
Experimental intervention
Single oral dose of acetylsalicylic acid 300 mg in the form of a tablet without enteric coating.

Control intervention
Single oral dose of a placebo tablet.

Study duration for each participant
5 days (in which day 1 = day on which the study medication is taken). The colonoscopy is not
part of the study, but is performed within the framework of the national screening program.

Primary endpoint
Sensitivity at predefined cutpoints of test positivity of the FOBGold®Tube Screen test to
detect advanced neoplasms.

Secondary endpoints

   x   specificity and the area under the curve in ROC analyses
   x   likelihood ratios
   x   gender-specific performance of the tests
   x   gain in diagnostic performance by test application on multiple days
   x   serious adverse events

4. BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE


4.1 Background
Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most common cancer and the fourth most common
cancer cause of death globally, with more than 1.2 million new cases and more than 600,000
deaths annually (1). In Germany, there are over 70,000 new cases and almost 30,000 deaths
due to CRC per year (2). The commonly slow progression from detectable and fully curable
precursors (adenomas) to still-curable early-stage CRC to advanced CRC provides much
better perspectives for prevention by early detection than for most other forms of cancer, and
screening for CRC is a highly cost-effective, if not cost-saving, means to reduce the burden of
the disease (3).
According to recent evidence from randomized and observational studies, the majority of
CRCs (60-90%) could be prevented by endoscopic detection and subsequent removal of
adenomas (4, 5). Since October 2002, colonoscopy is offered as a primary screening
examination in Germany. However, participation rates in endoscopic screening programs are
low, probably due to the invasiveness of the procedure. Higher participation rates can likely
be achieved with non-invasive stool- or blood-based screening tests. Randomized studies have
demonstrated that CRC incidence and CRC-associated mortality may be reduced by 20-30%
with fecal occult blood test (FOBT) screening (6), despite the low sensitivity for detection of
colorectal adenomas of conventional (guaiac based) FOBTs. Substantially higher sensitivity
for detection of colorectal adenomas can be achieved with newer, immunochemical FOBTs
(iFOBTs), but sensitivity for detecting advanced neoplasms still remains below 50% at cut
points yielding 90-95% specificity required for population-based screening. Thus, there is an
unmet medical need for non-invasive, and, at best, inexpensive and easy-to-implement,
screening methods for detection of high-risk colorectal adenomas in addition to CRC.
In a recent prospective screening study conducted among 1979 participants of screening
colonoscopy in Germany, sensitivity for detecting advanced colorectal neoplasms by iFOBT
was 70.8% among users of low-dose acetylsalicylic acid compared with 35.9% among non-
users (p=0.001), whereas there were only very small differences in specificity (85.7% for
users compared with 89.2 to 91.1% for nonusers) (7). In ROC analysis, the area under the
curve (AUC) was much higher among users than among non-users, with differences being
particularly pronounced among men (0.87 versus 0.68, p=0.003, Figure 1). This AUC for
users of low-dose acetylsalicylic acid is the highest AUC of any non-invasive test for
detecting advanced colorectal neoplasms reported to date and suggests that use of
acetylsalicylic acid prior to conduction of iFOBT might be a particularly promising approach
for enhancing non-invasive screening for colorectal cancer. It is hypothesized that the
underlying mechanism could be an enhanced tendency of (micro-)bleeding of colorectal
neoplasms.

heme moiety of hemoglobin, which remains quite stable throughout the gastrointestinal tract,
whereas the iFOBTs respond to globin, which is degraded in the gastrointestinal tract (7-11).

5. STUDY DESIGN
The planned study is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter study.
Overall, 2,400 eligible participants with an age of 40 to 80 years and with no recent use of
acetylsalicylic acid will be recruited into this study when visiting one of the participating
gastroenterology private practices or hospitals (outpatient clinics) for an informative
appointment, which is routinely scheduled a few weeks before colonoscopy. In addition to
participants scheduled for a screening colonoscopy, patients visiting the study centers for a
diagnostic colonoscopy but excluding patients with a history of colorectal cancer (see list of
in- and exclusion criteria for details) will also be asked to participate. Thus, the colonoscopy
procedure is planned and conducted independently from our study.
During this first appointment (day 0), participants will be informed about the study, will
receive the printed study information, will have enough time for consideration and will have
the opportunity to ask questions. After obtaining written informed consent a detailed medical
history and information on the use of medication will be collected and in- and exclusion
criteria will be checked. Women who are not postmenopausal have a negative pregnancy test.

7. STATISTICAL APPROACH

7.1 Analysis
Standard techniques for the analysis of diagnostic tests will be used (18, 19). Diagnostic
accuracy of the two immunochemical FOBTs at the different time points of stool collection
will be quantified by estimating sensitivity, specificity, likelihood ratios, positive and negative
predictive values (and corresponding 95% confidence intervals) at cut-points yielding levels
of specificity typically required for routine testing of average-risk populations (>90%), using
the results of colonoscopy as the gold standard. These results will be compared between
participants randomized to receive acetylsalicylic acid and those randomized to receive
placebo in an intention-to-treat analysis. For the primary endpoint, sensitivity for predefined
(test-specific) cutpoints of test positivity of the FOBGold®TubeScreen Haemoglobin test will
be compared between users and non-users of acetylsalicylic acid. The stool samples taken on
day 3 will be used for these primary analyses.
In addition, the tests will be evaluated over the whole range of cut-points by receiver
operating characteristics (ROC) curve analyses and the area under the ROC curve (AUC) will
be determined. All analyses will be performed for the total study population, as well as
stratified on gender and separately for the different colonoscopy results (advanced adenomas
and carcinomas). Analyses will be carried out for stool tests taken at baseline before
commencing the study medication and on different days after a single dose of the study drug.
Furthermore, analyses will be carried out in which results of tests from stool samples taken on
multiple (2 or 3) different days are combined to assess the gain in diagnostic performance by
test application on multiple days. Diagnostic performance and its enhancement by
acetylsalicylic acid will be evaluated and compared for both of the two iFOBTs employed.
Safety analyses will be performed in the total study population (i.e. all randomized patients,
independent from the availability of stool samples or colonoscopy reports).
The primary endpoints will be analyzed with confirmatory aim; the other statistical analyses
have an exploratory character. The statistical analysis will be carried out in cooperation with
the department of Biostatistics of the DKFZ under guidance and supervision of Prof. Dr. A.
Kopp-Schneider, the study statistician.


7.2 Proposed sample size / Power calculations
Diagnostic performance of the test with and without prior use of acetylsalicylic acid will be
evaluated using the results of colonoscopy as the gold standard. For the primary endpoint,
sensitivity and specificity for predefined (test-specific) cutpoints of test positivity and the area

8.1 Potential risks of the study medication
Acetylsalicylic acid is a very well-known and well-characterized drug that has been on the
market for more than 100 years (since 1899). Only one dose is given in this study and this
dose (300 mg) is lower than the normal adult dose required for pain relief (500 mg) and is

myocardial infarction (where doses from 75 to 325 mg are used). Furthermore, patients with
risk factors (e.g. bleeding diathesis) are excluded. Thus, the occurrence of serious adverse
reactions is considered very unlikely.
However, acetylsalicylic acid affects platelet function and as such, it is known to increase the
risk of bleeding events, although spontaneous bleeding has been reported to be rare and fatal
bleeding events to be exceptional in the setting of primary or secondary prevention of
coronary artery disease (20). The absolute risk of upper gastrointestinal events in long-term
use of acetylsalicylic acid is estimated to be increased by 0.12% per year of treatment (21).
Excess mortality attributable to 1-week use of acetylsalicylic acid is estimated as 185 per 100
million or 0.000185% overall and 0.000417% in people over the age of 60 (22). Thus,
although very unlikely, it is theoretically possible that a participant experiences a hemorrhagic
event during the study period. Given the short duration of treatment (one dose) and the fact
that potential participants with an increased risk of bleeding are excluded, we estimate the risk
of such an event to be extremely low.
Candidate-participants in the study will be excluded if they use potentially interacting
medication or have illnesses that may be worsened by participation in this study (for details,
see exclusion criteria). In the study information documents, participants will be informed
about all relevant potential adverse effects.
Before being asked to participate in the study, the participants have all decided to undergo
colonoscopy as a screening or diagnostic procedure. The time between drug intake and the
planned colonoscopy allows for recovery of haemostatic functions (see below). Thus, the
colonoscopy is not considered to be a part of the study.


8.2 Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of acetylsalicylic acid
Acetylsalicylic acid is eliminated rapidly from the body. The active metabolite, salicylic acid,
has a half-life of about 2-3 hours. Thus, one day after administration salicylic acid is
eliminated from the body almost completely. However, acetylsalicylic acid-induced platelet
dysfunction lasts several days because of the irreversible effects on platelets (i.e. the
individual thrombocyte does not recover, but platelet aggregation recovers due to release of
newly generated thrombocytes from the bone marrow). As platelet life span is about 10r1
days (23, 24)it is sometimes assumed that the time to normalization of platelet aggregation
after administration of acetylsalicylic acid is also 10 days. This assumption appears to be
consistent with some laboratory findings where quantified markers (e.g. serum TxB2 using
intensive ex vivo thrombocyte stimulation) normalizes within 2 weeks in most patients (25-
28). However, due to experimental conditions, these markers likely reflect the number of
functioning platelets but not overall aggregatory function, since complete exchange of
thrombocytes is not required to restore normal aggregation. Early on it was recognized that ex

8.3 Choice of acetylsalicylic acid dose
Most participants in our previous study (7) used acetylsalicylic acid in a dose of 100 mg per
day. This dosing scheme leads to predictable suppression of platelet function after repeated
administration. However, the onset of effects is slow and maximal suppression is achieved
only after about 5 days (27). Therefore, a loading dose of 500 mg is given in clinical routine
when a rapid onset of thrombocyte inhibition is required. Based on published data a dose of
300 mg seems to be similarly effective (48, 49). However, an enteric-coated preparation of the
same dose was associated with submaximal inhibition (49) and will therefore not be used in
our study.

8.4 Potential benefits of the study medication
The participants in this study have no indication for acetylsalicylic acid and no individual
benefit from taking one dose of acetylsalicylic acid. Should this study show that one dose of
acetylsalicylic acid strongly increases the sensitivity of the iFOBTs without relevant loss in
specificity, these findings may improve diagnostic procedures in future patients, which might
affect also study patients if repeated screening is required.

11.2 Pharmacological properties
Acetylsalicylic acid is an anti-platelet agent, which inhibits platelet (thrombocyte)
aggregation. Its antithrombotic properties are based on the fact that it irreversibly acetylates
the enzyme cyclo-oxygenase-1 (COX1) in thrombocytes (platelets), rendering COX1
incapable of converting arachidonic acid to prostaglandin H2, a precursor of thromboxane A2.
Under normal circumstances, thromboxane A2 stimulates platelet aggregation and
vasoconstriction. Acetylsalicylic acid also acetylates fibrinogen, which plays a role in the
stabilization of the thrombus. Low doses of acetylsalicylic acid are used in primary and
secondary prevention of vascular complications due to thromboembolic events. As platelets
are anuclear, they are incapable of producing new enzymes. Therefore, the inhibition of a
platelet lasts for the duration of the life of the platelet and decreases progressively with the
generation of new platelets. Per day, 10-15% of platelets are replaced (17, 20, 50, 51).
However, aggregatory functions normalize already after approximately 4-6 days, because
50% functioning platelets are sufficient to restore aggregatory functions (as discussed above).


11.3 Pharmacokinetic properties
Acetylsalicylic acid undergoes a first-pass effect in the bowel and the liver by hydrolyzation,
leading to the active metabolite salicylic acid. The time to peak concentration in the blood
(Tmax) is 0.5-2 hours. The volume of distribution is 0.16 l/kg. Salicylic acid binds for more
than 90% to plasma protein (mostly to albumin). Only the free form has therapeutic potential.

APPENDIX 1: Definition of Adverse Drug Events

Definition of an Adverse Event (AE)
According to GCP, an adverse event (AE) is defined as follows: Any untoward medical
occurence in a subject administered a pharmaceutical product and which does not necessarily
have a causal relationship with this treatment. An AE can therefore be any unfavourable and
unintended sign (including an abnormal laboratory finding), symptom, or disease temporally
associated with the use of a medicinal (investigational) product, whether or not related to the
medicinal (investigational) product.
An AE may be:
        - New symptoms/ medical conditions
        - New diagnosis
        - Changes of laboratory parameters
        - Intercurrent diseases and accidents
        - Worsening of medical conditions/ diseases existing before clinical trial start
        - Recurrence of disease
        - Increase of frequency or intensity of episodical diseases.
A pre-existing disease or symptom will not be considered an adverse event unless there will
be an untoward change in its intensity, frequency or quality. This change will be documented
by an investigator.
Surgical procedures themselves are not AEs; they are therapeutic measures for conditions that
require surgery. The condition for which the surgery is required may be an AE. Planned
surgical measures permitted by the clinical trial protocol and the condition(s) leading to these
measures are not AEs, if the condition leading to the measure was present prior to inclusion
into the trial.
AEs are classified as "non-serious" or "serious".

Definition of a Serious Adverse Event (SAE)
A serious adverse event is an adverse event that at any time during the period of observation:
   x results in death
   x is life-threatening
   x requires or prolongs hospitalization
   x results in persistent or significant disability or incapacity
   x involves congenital anomaly

Definition of Unexpected Adverse Event
An unexpected adverse event is an adverse event of which the nature and severity is not
consistent with the applicable product information.

other drugs or chemicals. The response to withdrawal of the drug (dechallenge) should be
clinically plausible. The event must be definitive pharmacologically or phenomenologically,
using a satisfactory rechallenge procedure if necessary.
Probable: A clinical event, including laboratory test abnormality, with a reasonable time
sequence to administration of the drug, unlikely to be attributed to concurrent disease or other
drugs or chemicals, and which follows a clinically reasonable response on withdrawal
(dechallenge). Rechallenge information is not required to fulfill this definition.
Possible: A clinical event, including laboratory test abnormality, with a reasonable time
sequence to administration of the drug, but which could also be explained by concurrent
disease or other drugs or chemicals. Information on drug withdrawal may be lacking or
unclear.
Unlikely: A clinical event, including laboratory test abnormality, with a temporal relationship
to drug administration which makes a causal relationship improbable, and in which other
drugs, chemicals or underlying disease provide plausible explanations.
Not assessable: Reports which for one or another reason are not yet assessable, e.g. because of
outstanding information (can only be a temporary assessment).
Not related: A clinical event, including laboratory test abnormality, with no relationship to
drug administration, and in which other drugs, chemicals or circumstances provide plausible
explanations.

Handling and storage of blood samples
After blood withdrawal, serum samples will be kept at room temperature for 30 minutes and
for a maximum of 60 minutes, to allow complete coagulation of the serum sample. Followed
by 10 minutes centrifugation at 2500-3500 RPM. Blood samples (EDTA tubes) will be gently
shaken (turned upside down) 8 to 10 times to prevent coagulation. Both serum and plasma
samples will be stored at the centers at 4°C to 8°C until being picked up by the Limbach
Laboratory within 2 to 3 days of blood withdrawal. Blood samples will be transported (on
ice). At the laboratory of the Division of Clinical Epidemiology and Aging Research
(C070)blood samples will be centrifuged (2500-3500 RPM) and aliquoted on the same day.
After centrifugation the buffy coat (white blood cells) will be collected from the plasma
samples. Aliqouts from both serum and plasma will be stored at -20°C.

Blood samples may also be transported to laboratories of cooperating partners (including
international partners) for future studies on biomarkers for risk and early detection of
advanced adenomas / colorectal cancer including genetic factors. All laboratory analyses will
be done in a blinded fashion with respect to both treatment given and clinical/colonoscopy
data.
The handling and storage of the blood samples will be conducted in a similar fashion as is
currently done in the ongoing observational studies. The blood samples will only be used for
research with the specific aims outlined above. Participants will be informed about this and
will have the option of participating in the main study without providing a blood sample. In
addition, participants will be informed that they can request destroying their blood samples at
any time. In that case participants have to contact their study center, which in turn will give
the specific participant number to the coordinating center. Participants will not be informed
about the results of such analyses, as they have no clinical relevance to them and it is unclear
