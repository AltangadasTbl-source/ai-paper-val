# Numeric relationship inventory

## Scope and rules

Five direct PDFs, 88 physical pages, mapped completely by the current source maps: 85 reusable-backed units and three freshly inspected protocol pages (16, 20, 49). This inventory contains 107 stable numeric relationship groups, each with all constituent cells rather than a sample of rows. N001–N048 crosswalk one-to-one to MAIN-N001–MAIN-N048; N049–N068 cover every eTable 2 row and column; N069–N077 every eTable 3 row; N078–N086 every eTable 4 row joined to the same eTable 5 row; N087–N095 every eTable 6 row; N096–N107 cover applicable plan inputs and the boundary catalogue. All groups were checked. There are no scientific coverage gaps.

REPORT: main article, result supplement, availability statement. PLAN: protocol and SAP. Source PDFs are authority; current mapper outputs and source-matched extraction are locators. Exact mapper crosswalks preserve all numeric input detail, confidence limits, and matching occurrences in extraction/main_quantitative_evidence.md and extraction/support_quantitative_evidence.md. Inferential compatibility, power reproduction, and statistical design comparisons also have independent S tracking; N tracking records count/denominator and quantity identity, without duplicating the S inventory.

Each displayed decimal x with last-place unit u retains [x−u/2,x+u/2]; each displayed whole number retains [x−0.5,x+0.5] for display diagnostics. Exact integer count arithmetic also was checked at the printed center. A percentage comparison succeeds if the interval of the computed ratio intersects the displayed percentage interval. Differences use the complete inputs, not subtraction of already rounded percentages. Every reconciled comparison below has at least the printed-center solution inside these allowed intervals. Diagnostic formulas: sensitivity=100 TP/(TP+FN), specificity=100 TN/(TN+FP), PPV=100 TP/(TP+FP), NPV=100 TN/(TN+FN); differences are aspirin minus placebo in percentage points. No odds ratio, risk ratio, hazard ratio, person-time rate, or standardized effect is reported. Adjusted intervals and model SDs are not equated with crude risk quantities.
