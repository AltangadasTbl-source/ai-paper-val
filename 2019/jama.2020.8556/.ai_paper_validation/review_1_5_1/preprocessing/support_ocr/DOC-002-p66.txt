6.4.5 ESTIMATING HEIGHT FROM ULNA LENGTH
An exploratory analysis will be carried out to assess the agreement between measured height and
height predicted from ulna length. Participants who are unable to measure height or ulna length will
be excluded from the analysis. Appropriate descriptive summaries and graphical illustrations of
baseline demographic including age, gender, ethnicity and clinical frailty scale will be presented split
by those who are included and excluded from the analysis to assess the missing pattern.
Participant’s height will be measured in metres, and ulna length will be measured in centimetres. The
predict height and ulna length will be converted using the Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool
(MUST) table. Summary statistics (n, minimum, maximum, mean, SD) on the difference between these
two measures and the measured and predicted values using the MUST table will be described with
respect to gender and overall. The correlation between the different methods will be evaluated using
intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) to assess the agreement between these two methods. Bland—
Altman analysis will be used to assess any systematic differences between the two methods, with 95%
limits of agreement also calculated.
6.4.6 ESTIMATING BMI FROM MID UPPER ARM CIRCUMFERENCE
Ina similar way, an exploratory analysis will be carried out to assess the agreement between measured
body mass index (BMI) categories and BMI categories predicted from mid upper arm circumference
(MUAC).
Participant’s weight will be measured in kilograms. BMI will be calculated using formula: weight
(kg)/[height (m)’].The predicted BM! categories and MUAC will be converted using the MUST tool.
Summary statistics (n, sensitivity, specificity) on the measured and predicted categories will be
described with respect to gender and overall. Agreement will be by calculating using weighted kappa
statistics.
6.4.7 AGREEMENT BETWEEN METHODS USED TO MEASURE STUDY PRODUCT USE
We will conduct exploratory analysis comparing the agreement between study product use as
assessed by weekly records (i.e. primary measure of study product use) and capsule counts. We will
also investigate participant and care home predictors of disagreement. The model will account for the
presence, direction, and extent to which there is disagreement.
