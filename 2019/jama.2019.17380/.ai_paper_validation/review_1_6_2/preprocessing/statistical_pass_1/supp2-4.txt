eAppendix. Supplementary Methods


       Laboratory drift and shift due to changes in reagents, calibrator lots, equipment, or

equipment settings can introduce selective or nonselective bias into analyses.1,2 Therefore, we

prospectively incorporated 5 levels of quality control (QC) into our measurements of creatinine,

cystatin C, and urinary albumin in order to prevent lab error and, if present, to effectively

detect and account for changes over time.3


       First, assay controls provided by reagent manufacturers were run daily prior to clinical

samples to verify proper analyzer function. Second, we generated aliquots of “high” and “low”

serum and urine standards, prepared by the hundreds and frozen at -80°C, and measured them

daily in parallel with clinical samples to monitor for drift.


       Third, we generated a “QC set” of 20 serum samples and a parallel “QC set” of 20 urine

samples in order to harmonize laboratory data across the study if shift or drift was detected.

For the serum QC set, the 20 samples were selected to span large ranges of serum creatinine

and cystatin C concentrations. The urine QC set of 20 samples was similarly designed to span

large ranges of urine creatinine and albumin concentrations. The samples for each QC set were

aliquoted and frozen at -80°C and measured throughout the study. Samples of the serum and

urine QC sets were each measured a minimum of 8 times over the course of sample analysis.


       Fourth, blind replicates (5-10%) were analyzed to ensure the integrity of the entire assay

and data management process, including the identification and labeling of samples and the

incorporation of results into master databases. Fifth, national and international standards were

       During the course of measurement, a one-time shift in serum cystatin C measurements

was observed using “high” and “low” serum standards as well as the serum QC set. This shift

corresponded with a change in reagent lot number and calibrators for cystatin C and can be

seen in the Supplementary Methods Figure as a systematic increase in each sample in the QC

set comparing the last three days on which the QC set was run to the first five days on which

the QC set was run (upper and lower left panels).


       Cystatin C standard ERM-DA471/IFCC was run 8 times after the shift was observed. The

observed mean concentration (5.96 mg/L) was slightly higher than the expected concentration

(5.49 mg/L).


       We transformed our cystatin C concentrations to calibrate them with ERM-DA471/IFCC

and harmonize them across the study. First, we calibrated post-shift cystatin C concentrations

(including study samples and QC set samples) by multiplying each value by the ratio of the

expected (5.49 mg/L) to the observed (5.961 mg/L) reference mean. After calibration, we

calculated the mean cystatin C concentration for each of the 20 samples in the QC set

separately for days 1-5 (before the shift) and days 7-9 (after the shift, now calibrated to ERM-

DA471/IFCC). We then used the pairs of mean values of the 20 samples in the QC set to

generate an equation harmonizing cystatin C concentrations measured before the shift to those

measured after the shift. The regression equation was: 0.006801 + 1.037603*(pre-shift cystatin
