## C001 — Vitamin-D concentration unit differs at the 20-unit baseline threshold

- **Cited location found:** The cited Results sentence is present in “Baseline Characteristics” on [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 6](<../../../jama_okereke_2020_oi_200066.pdf#page=6>). The comparators are present in Table 1 on [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 4](<../../../jama_okereke_2020_oi_200066.pdf#page=4>), Figure 4 on [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 8](<../../../jama_okereke_2020_oi_200066.pdf#page=8>), eTable 14a and its footnote on [DOC-003 Supplement 2 — PDF p. 38](<../../../joi200066supp2_prod.pdf#page=38>), the eTable 14c footnote on [DOC-003 Supplement 2 — PDF p. 39](<../../../joi200066supp2_prod.pdf#page=39>), and the “Description of Results from eTable 14” on [DOC-003 Supplement 2 — PDF p. 40](<../../../joi200066supp2_prod.pdf#page=40>).
- **Printed source value/text matched:** DOC-001 PDF p. 6 prints, “The mean 25-hydroxyvitamin D level was 31.1 ng/mL and 11.6% of participants had levels lower than 20 mg/mL.” Visual inspection distinguishes the printed `mg/mL` threshold unit from the `ng/mL` mean unit in the same sentence.
- **Comparator matched:** DOC-001 PDF p. 4 labels the Table 1 analyte “Serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D level, mean (SD), ng/mL,” places the categories `<20` and `≥20` beneath that label, and prints the footnote, “To convert 25-hydroxyvitamin D to nmol/L, multiply by 2.5.” DOC-001 PDF p. 8 labels the subgroup “Baseline 25-hydroxyvitamin D level, ng/mL” and prints `<20` and `≥20`. DOC-003 PDF p. 38 states, “Low vitamin D is considered 25(OH)D <20 ng/ml”; PDF p. 39 repeats the same definition; and PDF p. 40 twice uses `<20 ng/ml` in its narrative. The `mL` versus `ml` capitalization differs across documents, but all comparator occurrences attach the nano- prefix, not the milli- prefix, to the 20 threshold.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** A repeated threshold for the same 25-hydroxyvitamin-D concentration requires a consistent unit when no conversion or alternative threshold is stated. Rounding cannot reconcile different unit prefixes.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The Table 1 low-category counts give `630 + 698 = 1,328`, and the available-measurement denominators give `5,739 + 5,678 = 11,417`. Thus `1,328 / 11,417 × 100 = 11.63%`, which rounds to the p. 6 narrative value of `11.6%` and links that narrative percentage to the `<20 ng/mL` comparator category. Under the standard metric-prefix relation used as a dimensional diagnostic, `20 mg/mL × 1,000,000 ng/mg = 20,000,000 ng/mL`, not `20 ng/mL`.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The supplied PDFs provide the analyte name, threshold value, p. 6 percentage, Table 1 counts and available-measurement denominators, Figure 4 threshold label, Supplement 2 low-vitamin-D definition, and the article’s printed `ng/mL × 2.5` conversion to `nmol/L`. These inputs are sufficient to reproduce the printed unit difference and to connect the 11.6% statement to the baseline `<20 ng/mL` group.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not provide an authoritative statement of the unit intended for the p. 6 threshold, production-source or typesetting history for that sentence, an author-query record, or an alternative laboratory-unit definition that would make `20 mg/mL` the intended threshold.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The p. 6 threshold could refer to a different unstated analytic unit. That reading is not supported elsewhere in the supplied package: the same sentence uses `ng/mL` for the mean, the reproduced 11.6% corresponds to the Table 1 `<20 ng/mL` counts, and the figure and supplement define the same 20 threshold in `ng/mL`. A production or transcription mechanism remains possible, but the PDFs do not establish one.
- **Direct observation:** The p. 6 narrative prints `20 mg/mL`; the matched Table 1, Figure 4, and Supplement 2 definitions print the 20 threshold with `ng/mL` or `ng/ml`. The count calculation reproduces the p. 6 percentage from the comparator category.
- **Inferred explanation:** Any account involving an unintended unit prefix, typesetting, transcription, or a prior document version is inferred because the supplied sources do not describe how the differing label arose.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What concentration unit was intended for the `20 mg/mL` threshold printed on DOC-001 PDF p. 6, and what authoritative production or author record establishes that intent?

## C002 — Protocol ICD-9-code prose cites Table 3 while the code list is Table 1

- **Cited location found:** The referring sentence and adjacent code-list table are present on [DOC-002 protocol — PDF p. 18](<../../../joi200066supp1_prod.pdf#page=18>). The separately numbered Table 3 is present on [DOC-002 protocol — PDF p. 23](<../../../joi200066supp1_prod.pdf#page=23>).
- **Printed source value/text matched:** DOC-002 PDF p. 18 prints, “ICD-9 codes will be used to identify depression (Table 3), medical comorbidities and exclusionary conditions.” The same paragraph later states that a complete list of codes is provided in Appendix C.
- **Comparator matched:** Immediately below the p. 18 paragraph, the displayed caption is “Table 1. ICD-9 Codes Identifying Relevant Depressive Disorders,” followed by depressive-disorder names and ICD-9 codes. DOC-002 PDF p. 23 instead captions Table 3 as “Power for effects of a single agent on recurrent depression in VITAL, over 5 years of follow-up” and displays expected risk ratios, participant-group sizes, and power percentages rather than ICD-9 codes.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** A numbered table locator attached to the statement that ICD-9 codes identify depression should identify the displayed table containing those depression codes. No rounding or numerical tolerance applies to a table number or content identity.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The locator comparison is `Table 3 ≠ Table 1`. The content comparison is also nonidentical: p. 18 Table 1 contains psychiatric-disorder labels and ICD-9 codes, whereas p. 23 Table 3 contains recurrent-depression power scenarios. Therefore, the displayed Table 3 in the supplied protocol cannot serve as the printed ICD-9-code table named by the p. 18 sentence.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The supplied protocol provides the complete referring sentence, the adjacent code-list caption and contents, and the title and contents of its displayed Table 3. These inputs are sufficient to reproduce the locator and content comparison within the supplied PDF.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not provide document-version history, source cross-reference fields, a record of table renumbering, an author-query record, or another authoritative protocol version in which an ICD-9-code list is numbered Table 3. The supplied reference to Appendix C does not state that Appendix C itself is Table 3.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The p. 18 parenthetical could have been retained from a protocol version with different table numbering, or the adjacent code-list caption could have changed while the prose remained unchanged. Appendix C may contain a longer code list, but that does not make the supplied p. 23 Table 3 an ICD-9-code table.
- **Direct observation:** On p. 18, the prose prints `(Table 3)` for depression-identifying ICD-9 codes and the adjacent relevant table is captioned Table 1. On p. 23, Table 3 is visibly a recurrent-depression power table.
- **Inferred explanation:** Carryover from an earlier numbering scheme, incomplete cross-reference updating, and any relationship to an unavailable version are inferred explanations; none is documented in the supplied PDF.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which table was the p. 18 parenthetical intended to identify in the authoritative protocol, and does any authoritative version or source cross-reference record contain a depression ICD-9-code table numbered Table 3?

These inputs are sufficient to reproduce the current-package locator comparison.

No such layout is supplied. In the supplied main article, the numerical match is Figure 4 and Figure 3 is a different PHQ-8 distribution graphic.
