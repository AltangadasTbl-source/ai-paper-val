# Main-article results extraction

- **Document ID:** DOC-001-main-article
- **Source PDF:** `jama_saynorea_2019_oi_190106_1635377898.43062.pdf`
- **Article:** Sayón-Orea et al., *Effect of a Nutritional and Behavioral Intervention on Energy-Reduced Mediterranean Diet Adherence* (JAMA 2019;322:1486-1499).
- **Scope examined:** PDF pp. 1-11 (journal pp. 1486-1496); normalized text plus temporary visual corroboration of PDF pp. 3, 5, 9, and 10. Values below are reported evidence, not an assessment of accuracy.
- **Analysis population / model stated by authors:** principal analyses included randomized participants with baseline nutrition data; missing follow-up values were multiply imputed. Mixed-effects linear models used site and intracluster (couple) correlations as random factors (PDF p. 4, *Statistical Analysis*; Tables 2-3 footnote b). All secondary analyses were exploratory and no adjustment for type I error was conducted (PDF p. 4, *Statistical Analysis*).

## Outcomes prespecified for this interim analysis

- **Primary:** baseline-to-12-month change in the er-MedDiet score (range 0-17; higher = greater adherence; minimally clinically important difference [MCID] 1 point). Location: PDF p. 4, *Outcomes*; also Abstract, PDF p. 1.
- **Key secondary dietary scores:** MEDAS (0-14), MDS (0-9), and PDQS (0-42), all with higher score = better dietary quality. Other secondary outcomes: nutrients, listed foods, and cardiovascular risk factors. Location: PDF p. 4, *Outcomes*.
- **Clinically meaningful risk-factor thresholds:** ≥5% reduction in BMI, body weight, waist, total/LDL/non-HDL cholesterol, and total cholesterol:HDL ratio; ≥5% increase in HDL; ≥10% triglyceride reduction; ≥5-mm-Hg systolic or ≥2.5-mm-Hg diastolic blood-pressure reduction. Location: PDF p. 4, *Outcomes*.

## Cardiovascular risk-factor claims and figures

- The authors state that “with few exceptions, such as LDL cholesterol,” the intervention versus control showed significant and clinically meaningful favorable 12-month changes in body weight, waist circumference, BMI, HDL cholesterol, non-HDL cholesterol, total cholesterol:HDL ratio, triglycerides, and systolic/diastolic blood pressure. Location: PDF p. 8, *Risk Factors*.
- Narrative exact examples: baseline waist mean 108 cm in each group; 12-month between-group difference −3.3 cm (95% CI, −3.6 to −2.9), P<.001. ## Subgroups and sensitivity claims

- The main article does **not** present a subgroup-effect table, interaction estimate, or subgroup-specific outcome claim in PDF pp. 1-11. It describes randomization stratification by center, sex, and age (<65, 65-70, >70 years) (PDF p. 2, *Methods*), but does not report subgroup effects.
- Sensitivity analyses were post hoc completer-only analyses and analyses replacing missing values with baseline values (PDF p. 4, *Statistical Analysis*). The main text says PDQS differences and food findings were maintained / similar in those analyses; detailed estimates are in Supplement 3 (PDF p. 6, *Other Dietary Scores* and *Foods and Food Groups*).

## Main article’s interpretation statements

- “Improvements in diet quality, energy intake, and cardiovascular risk factors also were observed in the energy-reduced Mediterranean diet group.” PDF p. 8, *Discussion*.
- It states these are “interim and preliminary analyses” of an ongoing RCT and says long-term cardiovascular/other health-outcome effects are unknown. PDF p. 11, *Limitations* and *Conclusions*.
