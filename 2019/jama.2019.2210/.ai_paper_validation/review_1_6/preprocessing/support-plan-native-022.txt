Hypothesis
Mechanisms of anti-cancer effects by vitamin D
Serum levels of the active vitamin D precursor, 25(OH)D increase in response to exposure
to sunlight or a vitamin D-rich diet or vitamin D supplementation. In contrast, levels of
1,25(OH)2D that is activated in the kidneys remain constant and are least affected by
lifestyle. Cancer cells expressing both 1D-hydroxylase and vitamin D receptor (VDR) uptake
25(OH)D and convert it into 1,25(OH)2D, which binds to VDR in cancer-cell nuclei. This
signaling influences gene expression, which consequently induces cell proliferation and
apoptosis, and inhibits proliferation, angiogenesis, and metastasis.
