# Mechanical Evidence Recheck

## Scope and method

This record mechanically rechecks all seven stable candidate IDs, C001 through C007, against the cited pages of the three supplied PDFs. Targeted native layout extraction and page rendering were performed directly from the source PDFs. Reusable OCR, text, tables, and prior checker artifacts were used only as locators. The recheck covers 13 cited PDF-page locations. Each candidate remains **Pending Human Adjudication**; the observations below do not assign an adjudicative disposition.

## C001 — HbA1c daily-rate unit conflicts with the table scale

- **Cited location found:** Yes. The HbA1c daily-rate statement is present in the abstract at [DOC-001, PDF p. 1](../../../jama_flint_2019_oi_190079.pdf#page=1) and in the Secondary Outcomes narrative at [DOC-001, PDF p. 7](../../../jama_flint_2019_oi_190079.pdf#page=7). The HbA1c table label is present in Table 4 at [DOC-001, PDF p. 8](../../../jama_flint_2019_oi_190079.pdf#page=8).
- **Source printed value or text matched:** Yes. PDF p. 1 prints `HbA1c levels (-0.0002 mg/dL; 95% CI, -0.0021 to 0.0016)`. PDF p. 7 repeats `HbA1c levels (-0.0002 mg/dL [95% CI, -0.0021 to 0.0016], adjusted P = .99)`.
- **Comparator printed value or text matched:** Yes. Table 4 on PDF p. 8 labels the analyte `HbA1c, %`; its displayed HbA1c baseline and termination values are on that percent scale. The table contains raw within-group summaries rather than a numerical copy of the adjusted treatment-by-time coefficient, so the matched comparator is the named analyte's scale, not the table's change estimate.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. For the same named measured analyte, a reported daily-rate coefficient should identify a unit coherent with the response scale or state a transform that explains a different unit. Percent and milligrams per deciliter are different units.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The comparison is categorical: `mg/dL` does not equal `%`, and no numeric rounding operation converts one unit to the other. The estimate and interval match between PDF pp. 1 and 7, so the comparison isolates the unit label.
- **Necessary inputs available and exact missing inputs or definitions:** The source supplies the repeated estimate, interval, textual unit, and Table 4 analyte scale. It does not supply the exact modeled response-scale definition, any transformation of HbA1c, or a statement that the coefficient is expressed as percentage points per day.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The `mg/dL` label may have been carried over from the adjacent lipid and glucose results, while the intended HbA1c scale may be the percent scale printed in Table 4. A different modeled scale is also possible, but no such transform is stated on the cited pages.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Direct observations are the two `mg/dL` occurrences, the matching estimate and interval, and the Table 4 `%` label. Carryover from adjacent analytes or use of an unreported transform is inferred explanation.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What response scale, unit per unit time, and any transformation were used for the HbA1c treatment-by-linear-time coefficient, and which unit should accompany the printed estimate?
- **Status:** Pending Human Adjudication.

## C005 — Egger estimate and standard error do not reproduce the printed t statistic at displayed precision

- **Cited location found:** Yes. The complete Egger line appears below eFigure 3 at [DOC-003, PDF p. 21](../../../joi180151supp2_prod.pdf#page=21).
- **Source printed value or text matched:** Yes. The line prints `Egger Test: -0.47 (standard error: 0.77); t = -0.59, P = 0.57.`
- **Comparator printed value or text matched:** Yes. The estimate, standard error, t statistic, and P value occur together in the same line. No other Egger calculation or definition is printed on the page.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Conditionally yes. If `-0.47` is the coefficient estimate and `0.77` is the standard error used for the displayed coefficient test, the ordinary identity is `t = estimate / standard error`. The figure does not define a distinct statistic, but that missing definition prevents treating the identity as source-explicit.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The point calculation is `-0.47/0.77 = -0.610389...`, which prints as `-0.61`, not `-0.59`. Under ordinary nearest-hundredth rounding, the estimate magnitude lies from `0.465` through values below `0.475`, and the standard error lies from `0.765` through values below `0.775`. Therefore the attainable t-magnitude interval is from `0.465/0.775 = 0.600000` through values below `0.475/0.765 = 0.620915...`. A t statistic printed as `-0.59` requires a magnitude from `0.585` through values below `0.595`. The two intervals do not overlap. This resolves the pass-1 versus numeric diagnostic disagreement at the stated nearest-rounding convention: the displayed-precision interval does not support the pass-1 claim of finite-precision compatibility.
- **Necessary inputs available and exact missing inputs or definitions:** The displayed estimate, standard error, t statistic, and P value are available and suffice for the conditional ratio and display-interval checks. Missing are the unrounded estimate and standard error, the exact Egger regression/test definition, confirmation that the printed standard error is the one used for the printed t statistic, degrees of freedom, and sidedness. Those missing items prevent an independent P-value reconstruction.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The t statistic may come from a different parameter, variance estimate, or software-output field than the adjacent estimate and standard error, despite their presentation on one line. The supplied page does not identify such a distinction. Greater hidden precision alone cannot bridge the nonoverlapping nearest-hundredth intervals.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** The four printed values are direct observations. The ratio identity and rounding-interval bounds are diagnostic calculations conditional on the estimate and standard error belonging to the displayed t test. A distinct parameter, variance estimate, or transcription mechanism is inferred explanation.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which exact unrounded estimate and standard error, and which precise Egger test formula or software-output fields, generated `t = -0.59`; were the adjacent `-0.47` and `0.77` intended to be that t statistic's numerator and denominator?
- **Status:** Pending Human Adjudication.
