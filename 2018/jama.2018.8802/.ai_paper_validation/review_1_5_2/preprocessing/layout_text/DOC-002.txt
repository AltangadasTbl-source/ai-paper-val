42                                         Introduction

43     Stroke is a major cause of death and long-term disability in China. The annual cost

44   of stroke care is about 5.76 billion US dollars.1-3 Improving stroke care quality and

45   clinical outcomes is a national priority.4 Large-scale randomized trials and systematic

46   reviews have established the efficacy and safety of interventions for acute ischemic

47   stroke (AIS), such as intravenous recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (IV rtPA),

48   antiplatelet therapy, anticoagulation for patients with atrial fibrillation, deep vein

49   thrombosis (DVT) prophylaxis, swallowing dysfunction management, statin treatment,

50   antihypertensive and hypoglycemic treatment.5-16 Nevertheless, the adherence to these

51   evidence-based interventions is suboptimal in China.17-19 Research studies in other

52   countries have demonstrated that quality improvement programs can be effective in

53   improving the quality of stroke care. Interventions include clinical pathway or

54   standardized order sets, training and distribution of educational materials to heath care

55   providers, and audit with timely feedback.20-22 Combination of different methods

56   targeting different barriers have been shown effective in patients with acute coronary

57   syndromes.23, 24 However, to date these quality improvement interventions have been

58   limited to high income countries and not evaluated in less well developed health care

59   settings such as China.

60     Using hospitals participating in the China National Network of Stroke Research, we

61   design a cluster-randomized trial to evaluate the effectiveness of a multifaceted

62   quality improvement initiative to bridge the gap of evidence-based medicine in AIS

63   management in China (GOLDEN BRIDGE-AIS).

64   Study aim

65   The aim is to evaluate whether a multifaceted quality improvement intervention will

66   improve the adherence to individual, composite and all-or-none measures for patients

67   with AIS presenting within the first 7 days from stroke symptom onset and reduce the

68   incidence of in-hospital death, a new vascular event, disability, and all-cause death at

69   3, 6, and 12-months after the initial symptom onset.

70   Design

71     Golden Bridge-AIS is a multicenter, 2-arm, open label, parallel, cluster-randomized,

72   controlled trial with blinded adjudication of outcomes and intention-to-treat analysis

73   to assess the feasibility and efficacy of this intervention (Figure 1). Forty hospitals

74   from Mainland China participate in the trial.25

81   and that meet the inclusion and exclusion criteria (Table 1) are eligible for inclusion in

83   developed by the National Center of Quality Management in Stroke Care includes 563

84   hospitals from 27 provinces and 4 municipalities in Mainland China, with about 20

85   hospitals in each province or municipality.

 87     This trial is specifically designed for patients with acute ischemic stroke and

 88   excludes those with final diagnosis of other cerebrovascular disease (hemorrhagic

 89   stroke or transient ischemic attack, cerebral venous sinus thrombosis, and so on) or

 90   non-cerebrovascular disease. Head computed tomography (CT) or magnetic

 91   resonance imaging (MRI) scan is required for every suspected stroke patient enrolled

 92   in the study no matter they were admitted to an intervention or control site.
 93   Clusters randomization and blinding
 94     Forty eligible clusters in Mainland China are stratified by the following

 95   characteristics: province, hospital grade (secondary or tertiary), and baseline level of

 96   stroke care quality. Stratification details are offered in a de-identified form to an

 97   independent statistician not otherwise involved in the study to guarantee blindness of

 98   allocation. They are randomized within the strata and assigned (1:1) to a multifaceted

 99   quality improvement intervention (experimental group) or to standard of care (control

100   group) using random number generating software (SAS Version 9.3 software, SAS

                                                                                         2014/4/17
                                                                                   Page 8 / 25


101   Institute, Cary, NC) by the independent statistician who conceal the sequence until the

102   intervention is assigned. In view of the nature of the multifaceted intervention in the

103   GOLDEN BRIDGE-AIS, only the independent outcome evaluators are blinded to the

104   intervention.

105   The stroke quality improvement interventions

106     The multifaceted quality improvement intervention includes an evidence-based

107   clinical pathway, written care protocols for implementation of performance measures

108   (Figure 2), a full-time quality coordinator, and monitoring and feedback system for

109   the AIS performance measures. A local quality care initiative (QCI) team at each

110   intervention hospital is established to take responsibility for the implementation of the

111   multifaceted intervention. Two health care providers of the QCI from all intervention

112   clusters (a director of department of neurology who acts as the local investigator and a

113   physician or nurse who acts as a quality coordinator) attend a 2-day workshop on how

114   to perform the GOLDEN BRIDGE-AIS quality improvement intervention. These two

115   trained professionals are charged with sharing these operational methods with cluster

116   personnel who care for the AIS patients, complemented by training video or slides.

117   Each cluster provides to the central coordinating center formal documentation of

118   completed training such as photos and attendance logs to guarantee adequate

119   knowledge translation on the implementation of each quality improvement

120   intervention component.

128   Evidence-based clinical pathway
129     The stroke clinical pathway is added into the care plan on each eligible stroke

130   admission, either in the emergency department or inpatient ward. It contains general

131   guideline-based recommendations about acute stroke management and detailed daily

132   care plan for each of the first 7 days of the acute admission and at discharge. The

133   daily activities are listed under medical, nursing, therapist and discharge planning

134   headings. Daily each item is to be checked, initialed and dated upon completion by

135   the staff on a daily basis.26 The stroke clinical pathway is developed by a panel of

136   stroke clinical experts according to peer-reviewed empirical literature, consensus

137   statements and guidelines.5, 27

139   Written care protocols for implementations of performance measures


                                                                                      2014/4/17
                                                                                   Page 10 / 25


140   1. Intravenous IV rt-PA protocol for AIS patients who arrive within 2 hours after last

141      seen normal and are treated within 3 hours contains mainly indications,

142      contraindications, dosage weight table, the specific user method of operation,

143      management of side effects of rt-PA, assessment of National Institutes of Health

144      Stroke Scale (NIHSS), specific job responsibilities of emergency department

145      physicians and acute stroke care team.

146   2. Deep venous thrombosis (DVT) prophylaxis protocol contains mainly the

147      evaluation scale of risk factors associated with DVT, Wells scale and the detailed

148      approaches for DVT prophylaxis including non-pharmacological interventions

149      and anticoagulant drugs.

150   3. Swallowing dysfunction management protocol contains mainly the flow chart for

151      screening and assessment of swallowing function, detailed operation of clinical

152      examination of swallowing function, and methods of training patients with

153      swallowing dysfunction.

154   4. Evidence-based medications protocol includes the indications, contraindications,

155      and dosage recommendations of antithrombotic therapy by the end of day two,

156      and discharge antithrombotic therapy, anticoagulation for patients with atrial

157      fibrillation, statin, antihypertensive, and hypoglycemic medications as

158      appropriate.

216     Adherence is expressed as a composite measure or all-or-none measure. The

217   composite measure is defined as the total number of interventions performed among

218   eligible patients divided by the total number of possible interventions among eligible

219   patients. 28 The all-or-none measure is defined as the proportion of eligible patients

220   who receive all of the performance measure interventions.28

221

222   Secondary outcomes

223     The secondary outcomes include in-hospital death, a new clinical vascular event

224   (ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, myocardial infarction, or vascular death),

225   disability measured by Modified Rankin Score (mRS = 3-5), and all-cause mortality

226   at 3, 6, and 12-months after initial symptom onset.

227

228     Trained research personnel at Beijing Tiantan Hospital, blinded to the intervention

229   and using standardied scripts, will contact patients by telephone to collect follow-up

230   information at 3, 6, and 12 months after initial symptom onset. When it is difficult to


                                                                                       2014/4/17
                                                                                    Page 17 / 25


231   communicate with the patient or when the information provided by the patient is

232   regarded as unreliable by the interviewer, the specific care-giver will be contacted and

233   interviewed. During the follow-up periods, just stroke recurrences and

234   rehospitalization will be tracked back to the attended hospitals where the care are

235   provided in order to guarantee accuracy in diagnosis. Each case fatality will either be

236   identified by a death certificate from the local citizen registry or from the attended

237   hospital where the patient is treated. In the event of lack of local citizen registry

238   information or death without hospitalization, case fatality is deemed to be reliable if

239   death is reported on two consecutive follow-up periods from different agencies.

241   Sample size

242     A pre-randomization survey at participating clusters was performed. The mean

243   composite score of stroke care quality is 80%. To detect a 5% improvement in the

244   composite measure of evidence-based performance measures in patients with AIS,

245   with 80% power, 5% significance level, and an intracluster correlation coefficient of

246   0.02 (value determined according to pre-randomization pilot data), we need to

247   randomize 40 clusters and approximately 4800 patients (considering a median of 120

248   AIS patients per cluster). It is estimated that three monitoring, feedback, and

249   improvement cycles, would be implemented to achieve the predefined improvement

250   of the composite measure; therefore, 30 patients will be enrolled per cycle at every

251   cluster (Figure 4).

253   Statistical analysis plan

254     An “intention-to-treat” analysis will be performed. Baseline characteristics of

255   hospitals and patients will be analyzed to assess cluster differences between treatment

256   and control groups. Possible control variables will be identified and included in the

257   final models. Categorical variables will be summarized as proportion; continuous

258   variables with normal distribution will be summarized with mean and standard

259   deviation (SD), and those with skewed distribution will be expressed with median and

260   interquartile range.

261

262     In univariate analysis for comparisons between groups, χ2 test will be used to

263   compare categorical variables, and Student t test or Mann-Whitney U test will be used

264   to compare continuous variables.

265

266     In multivariate analysis, a generalized estimation equation accounting for

267   within-hospital correlation will be used to compare between the intervention and

268   control groups at the patient and hospital level for modeling proportions and

269   quantitative variables. The effects of intervention will be demonstrated according the

270   features of variables. Qualitative and quantitative variables and will be expressed as a

271   population average odds ratio and the mean difference separately, both with 95%

272   confidence intervals (CIs). The composite measure was expressed as the odds of

273   fulfilling care opportunities that each measure for which a patient was eligible for.

274   Thus in the analysis of composite measure, each care opportunity contributed an


                                                                                       2014/4/17
                                                                                   Page 19 / 25


275   observation in the analysis, and the outcome was a dichotomized (1: measure met

276   versus 0: measure not met).7, 11 For example, if a particular patient was eligible for 5

277   of the 7 performance measures and only received 3 of them, this patient contributed 5

278   observations to the analysis, and 3 of them has outcome value as 1 and 2 had outcome

279   value as 0.

280     The intervention effects on clinical vascular events and mortality at discharge and

281   at 3, 6, 12 months after initial symptom onset will be demonstrated by Kaplan-Meier

282   curves and compared by using proportional hazards Cox models. A sensitivity

283   analysis will be performed including patients with contraindications for

284   evidence-based interventions in the denominator of the overall population. All

285   statistical analyses will be performed by using SAS Version 9.3 software (SAS

286   Institute, Cary, NC). The internal statistical analyses will be performed by the Beijing

287   Key Laboratory of Translational Medicine for Cerebrovascular Disease and external

288   analyses will be verified by the Department of Statistics & Operations Research,

289   University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill.

291   Ethical considerations

292     This study is performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and

293   evidence based clinical practice guidelines. The trial has been approved by the central

294   institutional review board at Beijing Tiantan Hospital, all participating clusters submit

295   the study protocol for approval by their research ethics board, and written consent is

296   obtained at the cluster. This is a common and well-accepted approach; the objective of


                                                                                        2014/4/17
                                                                                 Page 20 / 25


297   such an approach is to avoid selection bias that may arise from different consent

298   refusals rates between clusters.29 However, written consent is signed by patients or

299   their family representative to give the permission so that researchers can contact them

300   after 3,6, and 12 months after initial symptom onset for a telephone interview.

302   Organizational structure

303      This study is administered by an academic steering committee composed of the

304   principle investigator and members. This committee is responsible for the research

305   directions and supports, addressed policy issues about the protocol, and met

306   periodically to evaluate the progress of study. The quality improvement committee is

307   composed of clinical and management experts from the steering committee. This

308   committee is responsible for overseeing the current adherence of the key performance

309   indicators, analyzing the potential causes of low implementation rate, and setting new

310   goals for improvement and measures by conducting conference calls with the QCI

311   team at every cluster. The executive committee is composed of senior experts from

312   the academic steering committee. The mission of this committee is to assess the

313   progress and safety of the study and make any decisions regarding early termination,

314   modification, or continuation of the trial (see Appendix Ⅱ for committee member

315   list).

318        In all participating clusters, data are captured prospectively and entered in the

319   Web-based data capture system by a trained independent research coordinator who is

320   not involved in the care of patients with AIS. Range checks will be used for

321   inconsistent or out-of-range data and prompt the user to correct or review data entries

322   that outside the predefined range. The system also provides pre-defined logic checks

323   to identify erroneous, illogical data entries. A sample of 20% of all recruitments will

324   be selected at random and checked by research specialists from an independent

325   contract research organization (CRO) throughout the study period. Finally, a data

326   quality meeting will be held monthly to review all the hospital enrollment records and

327   registry data.
