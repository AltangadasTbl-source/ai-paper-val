**Rule and calculation:** A two-sided paired-t interval produced with the same standard error/test is d±t(.975,ν)×SE. Thus |d|/h=t_observed/t(.975,ν), where h=(U−L)/2; it must reach t(1−P/2,ν)/t(.975,ν) for the printed P. For all positive Student-t degrees of freedom, the quantile ratio needed at a small P is at least its normal-limit ratio. SF-36 h=5.14 and |d|/h=6.78/5.14=1.3191. With every two-decimal input allowed its half-unit interval, maximum ratio is6.785/5.135=1.3213. Even treating P=.001 as rounded, P<.0015 requires a ratio at least approximately3.1747/1.9600=1.6198. EMG h=.44, ratio=.67/.44=1.5227; the maximum precision-compatible ratio is.675/.435=1.5517. P<.001 requires at least approximately3.2905/1.9600=1.6788. Both maximum ratios are below their respective necessary thresholds. This comparison does not select an unreported ν, SE, covariance or variance estimator. A normal-limit diagnostic gives P values of approximately.010 and.0028 respectively; these are labelled diagnostics, not replacements for the reported paired-t analyses or proposed values.
