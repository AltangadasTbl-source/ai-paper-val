187   STUDY SCHEMA
188           This study is a cluster randomized, stepped wedge clinical trial assessing
189   implementation and effect of a locally-developed quality improvement toolkit for patients
190   admitted with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) in Kerala, India. Hospitals will be
191   randomized after stratification for size to one of five steps. After a four-month baseline
192   period, the quality improvement toolkit will be implemented in a random subset of
193   hospitals in step 1 (cohort 1). Through a one-way crossover design, these hospitals will
194   continue to use the quality improvement toolkit through the end of the trial for all acute
195   coronary syndrome (ACS) patients. Cohorts 2 through 5 will implement the quality
196   improvement toolkit every 4 months at months 8, 12, 16 and 20, continuing the use of
197   the toolkits from that time forward to the end of the study. The primary outcome is 30-
198   day major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) rates. Rates will be continuously
199   collected and compared at one interim time point for safety and efficacy. Final analysis
200   compares MACE rates before and after implementation of the quality improvement
201   toolkits, accounting for cluster effects of hospital, cohort, and time.

212   1.0   BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE

213         1.1   Disease Background
214               Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is the leading cause of death worldwide
215               with 80% of deaths occurring in low- and middle-income countries
216               (LMIC).1 In India, a World Bank LMIC, coronary heart disease (CHD)
217               causes 40% of deaths in urban areas and 30% of deaths in rural areas.2
218               CHD prevalence in India has increased with a recent study estimating that
219               30 million individuals in India have CHD.
220
221               In addition to increased prevalence, the burden of CVD in India may be
222               higher than in developed and other developing countries due to
223               differences in age-standardized death rates. WHO reported 2004 age-
224               standardized CVD death rates for all ages are 174.7 per 100,000 in
225               Britain, 178.8 per 100,000 in United States, 279.5 per 100,000 in China,
226               and 381.5 per 100,000 in India.3 Increased burden at a younger age leads
227               to loss of productivity, depleted economic growth and increased social
228               challenges.4 The World Health Organization (WHO) proposes a three-
229               pronged approach to curtailing global trends in CVD; surveillance and
230               monitoring, reduction of risk factors, and improved management and
231               healthcare through early detection and timely treatment.

232         1.2   Study Agent(s)/Devices Background and Associated Known
233               Toxicities
234               Studies demonstrating improved outcomes with use of guideline-based
235               therapies in high-income countries have demonstrated improvements in
236               clinical outcomes in ACS patients.5-9 Similar large-scale studies have not
237               been carried out in India, although pilot investigation shows a potential
238               benefit of implementation of education and standardized health system
239               interventions.10

240         1.3   Rationale
241               System-level quality improvement initiatives in ACS, while successful in
242               high-income countries, have not been extensively evaluated in LMIC, yet
243               could be sources of innovation in the field of cardiovascular
244               implementation science.

246          2.1    Primary Objectives

247                 2.1.1   Compared with usual care, to evaluate the effect of a locally-
248                         developed, evidence-based health care quality improvement toolkit
249                         on 30-day major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in patients
250                         admitted with acute coronary syndrome.

251          2.2    Secondary Objectives

252                 2.2.1   To evaluate health related quality of life in post-ACS patients (30-
253                         days) using a translated and validated version of the Seattle Angina
254                         Questionnaire (SAQ).

255                 2.2.2   To evaluate individual- and household-level impoverishing effects
256                         of an ACS event in the context of the recent implementation of a
257                         national government insurance program for families below the
258                         poverty line.

259                 2.2.3   Compared with usual care, to evaluate the effect of a locally-
260                         developed, evidence-based health care quality improvement toolkit
261                         on in-hospital and discharge medication prescription rates,
262                         discharge advice relative to healthy lifestyles, and in-hospital and
263                         30-day expanded MACE.

264                 2.2.4   To evaluate the association between concordance with locally-
265                         defined performance measures for ACS care and in-hospital and
266                         30-day MACE.

267          2.3    Endpoints
268                 Composite of 30-daymajor adverse event rate, defined as death,
269                 myocardial infarction, stroke, and major bleeding defined by GUSTO
270                 criteria.

289   4.0   TREATMENT PLAN

290         4.1   Treatment Dosage and Administration

291               4.1.1   Quality Improvement Toolkits
292                       Quality improvement toolkits for ACS patients (clinical standards,
293                       guidelines, pathways, electronic audit-feedback, and cardiac arrest
294                       code teams) will be developed through workshops with providers
295                       from the Kerala ACS registry.
296
297                       Toolkits will be implemented at each step in randomly selected
298                       cohorts of hospitals, according to the stepped wedge design (five
299                       steps planned over two years). At a minimum, toolkits will include
300                       locally-developed standard admission and discharge order sets,
301                       poster-size clinical pathways and algorithms, standardized patient
302                       lifestyle counseling advice, and electronic audit-feedback systems.
303
304                       At baseline, all Hospitals will provide ACS care per normal
305                       procedures for no shorter than a period of 4 months. At 4 months,
306                       one cohort will randomly be selected through randomization
307                       stratified by hospital size. This step will implement the quality
308                       improvement toolkit and continue the use of the toolkit through the
309                       end of the trial (2 years). Additional cohorts will be sequentially
310                       randomized at months 8, 12, 16, and 20.
311
312                       Patients presenting at participating hospitals will be treated per the
313                       ACS care and procedures in place at the hospital at the time of
314                       presentation. Patients will be followed up at 30-days either by
315                       telephone or in-person visit to determine the occurrence of any
316                       major adverse cardiovascular events.

317               4.1.2   Seattle Angina Questionnaire & Micro-Economic Assessment
318                       A 2,200 subset of patients will also be asked to complete the
319                       Seattle Angina Questionnaire and a Micro-Economic Assessment.

320                     Completion of these additional questionnaires necessitates an in-
321                     person visit.

442   6.0   ADVERSE EVENTS

443         6.1   Adverse Event Monitoring
444               Adverse event data collection and reporting, which are required as part of
445               every clinical trial, are done to ensure the safety of Subjects enrolled in the
446               studies as well as those who will enroll in future studies using similar
447               agents.

448         6.2   Definitions

449               6.2.1   Adverse Event (AE):
450                       Any untoward medical occurrence associated with the use of a drug
451                       in humans, whether or not considered drug related. An adverse
452                       event (also referred to as an adverse experience) can also be any
453                       unfavorable and unintended sign (e.g., an abnormal laboratory
454                       finding), symptom, or disease temporally associated with the use of
455                       a drug, without any judgment about causality. An adverse event
456                       can arise from any use of a drug (e.g., off label use, use in
457                       combination with another drug) and from any route of
458                       administration, formulation or dose, including overdose.

459               6.2.2   Serious Adverse Event (SAE):
460                       An adverse event or suspected adverse reaction is considered
461                       "serious" if, in the view of either the investigator or sponsor, it
462                       results in any of the following outcomes:
463                           Death,
464                           a life-threatening adverse reaction,
465                           inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of existing
466                              hospitalization,
467                           a persistent or significant incapacity or substantial disruption
468                              of the ability to conduct normal life functions,
469                           or a congenital anomaly/birth defect.


                                                                                              15
                    ACS QUIK Protocol Version 22 September 2014


470                 Important medical events that may not result in death, be life-
471                 threatening, or require hospitalization may be considered serious
472                 when, based upon appropriate medical judgment, they may
473                 jeopardize the patient or subject and may require medical or
474                 surgical intervention to prevent one of the outcomes listed above.

475         6.2.3   Expected Adverse Event
476                 This study assesses the effect of a quality improvement toolkit on
477                 30-day major adverse cardiovascular event (MACE) rates defined
478                 as one or more of the following:
479                     Death
480                     Myocardial infarction
481                     Stroke
482                     Major bleeding defined by GUSTO criteria
483
484                 These adverse events are expected as inherent to the trial.

485         6.2.4   Unanticipated Adverse Event (uAE)
486                 An adverse event or suspected adverse reaction is considered
487                 "unexpected" if it is not listed in the investigator brochure or is not
488                 listed at the specificity or severity that has been observed; or, of an
489                 investigator brochure is not required or available, is not consistent
490                 with the risk information described in the general investigational
491                 plan or elsewhere in the current application. "Unexpected" as used
492                 in this definition, also refers to adverse events or suspected
493                 adverse reactions that are mentioned in the investigator brochure
494                 as occurring with a class of drugs or as anticipated from the
495                 pharmacological properties of the drug, but are not specifically
496                 mentioned as occurring with the particular drug under investigation.

497   6.3   Reporting Requirements for Adverse Events

498         6.3.1   Routine Reporting
499                 All SAEs and AEs will be routinely captured through the electronic
500                 data capture form and reported semi-annually and at the interim
501                 safety analysis.

502         6.3.2   Expedited Reporting
503                 In addition to routine reporting, the Principal Investigator and site
504                 Principal Investigator must be notified within 24 hours of learning of
505                 any unanticipated SAEs, regardless of attribution, occurring during
506                 the study or within 30 days of treatment. Unanticipated SAEs are
507                 reported within 10 business days to institutional officials.
508
509                 Unanticipated SAEs are followed to resolution or stabilization, and
510                 any follow-up information for an unanticipated SAE is reported


                                                                                         16
                          ACS QUIK Protocol Version 22 September 2014


511                       within the same timelines as the original SAE. If the investigator
512                       becomes aware of an unanticipated SAE occurring within 15 days
513                       of patient withdrawal from the study intervention, it is immediately
514                       reported to the CCDC and site IRB.

515               6.3.3   Additional Reporting
516                       In addition to adverse event reporting, the institutional officials must
517                       be notified within 10 business days of “any unanticipated problems
518                       involving risk to subjects or others” (UPR/UPIRSO).
519                       The following events meet the definition of UPR:
520                           1. Any serious event (injuries, side effects, deaths or other
521                              problems), which in the opinion of the Principal Investigator
522                              was unanticipated, involved risk to subjects or others, and
523                              was possibly related to the research procedures.
524                           2. Any serious accidental or unintentional change to the IRB-
525                              approved protocol that alters the level of risk.
526                           3. Any deviation from the protocol taken without prior IRB
527                              review to eliminate apparent immediate hazard to a
528                              research subject.
529                           4. Any new information (e.g., publication, safety monitoring
530                              report, or updated sponsor safety report), interim result or
531                              other finding that indicates an unexpected change to the
532                              risk/benefit ratio for the research.
533                           5. Any breach in confidentiality that may involve risk to the
534                              subject or others.
535                           6. Any complaint of a subject that indicates an unanticipated
536                              risk or that cannot be resolved by the Principal Investigator.

537         6.4   Stopping Rules
538                     Data safety monitoring board (DSMB) will provide recommendation
539                     after one interim data analysis at one-year to stop the trial based on

570               optimal in-hospital medication use, optimal discharge medication use,
571               tobacco cessation advice”.
572

574               Power and sample size are covered in detail within the ACS QUIK
575               Statistical Analysis Plan (Version 22 September 2014). In brief “At an

577               80% power to detect a 2.4% difference from a baseline 10.4% 30-day
578               MACE rate. This sample size is increased to 15,750 to account for up to
579               5% drop out between discharge and 30-day follow-up.”
