30   eMETHODS

31   Ventilation protocol

32   The allowed ventilation modes were volume-controlled ventilation and pressure

33   support ventilation (pressure-controlled ventilation, modes volume combining

34   controlled and spontaneous ventilation, and automated modes of ventilation were not

35   allowed). The inspiration-to-expiration ratio with volume-controlled ventilation was 1:2.

36   With volume-controlled ventilation the inspiration time and pause were set at 25% and

37   10% respectively. With pressure support ventilation the highest possible pressure rise

38   was chosen, and cycling off was set at 25%.

39          The inspired oxygen fraction was 0.21 or higher to maintain oxygen saturation

40   90 to 92% and/or PaO2 > 7.3 to 10.7 kPa (55 to 80 mmHg). The respiratory rate was

41   to be adjusted to maintain an arterial pH of 7.25 to 7.45. In case of a metabolic acidosis

42   or – alkalosis, a lower or higher than normal PaCO2 was to be accepted, left to the

43   discretion of the attending physician. The lowest level of positive end–expiratory

44   pressure was 5 cm H2O; allowed FiO2–PEEP combinations according to previous used

45   strategy.1 Recruitment maneuvers were allowed, when deemed necessary by the

46   attending physician.

47   Standard Care

48   Standard care followed local clinical guidelines and was performed by independent

49   board-certified ICU physicians and board-certified ICU nurses not involved in the trial.

50   The nurse-to-patient ratio in the participating centers varied from 1:1 to 1:2, depending

51   on severity of illness. ICU physicians rotated in 24/7 shifts, meaning that there was

52   always an ICU physician present in the ICU. Physician and nursing staffs used

53   restrictive sedation, preferring analgo-sedation over hypno-sedation, and restrictive

54   intravenous fluid resuscitation. ICU nurses performed standard airway care, including



     © 2018 American Medical Association. All rights reserved.
55   endotracheal suctioning, and tracheostomy care if applicable. Endotracheal suction

56   was performed only when clinically indicated and according to current guidelines.2 All

57   patients received humidification (passive or active) of the inhaled air.

58   Infection prevention

59   Infection prevention strategies consisted of frequent oral care (tooth brushing and

60   rinsing of the oral cavity every six hours), head–of–bed elevation, hand washing, and

61   use of selective decontamination of the digestive tract.3
