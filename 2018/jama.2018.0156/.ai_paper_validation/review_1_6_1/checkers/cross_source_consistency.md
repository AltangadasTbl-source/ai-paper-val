### F-XS-001

The final protocol's primary endpoint includes survival with unchanged pre-existing neurological disability; the report specifies CPC 1–2 only.

- **Locations and document roles:** PLAN [DOC-002 p 71, final protocol §4.1.1](../../../joi180004supp1_prod.pdf#page=71), and PLAN [DOC-002 p 110, amendment primary-endpoint comparison](../../../joi180004supp1_prod.pdf#page=110); REPORT [DOC-001 p 1, abstract Main Outcomes and Results](../../../jama_jabre_2018_oi_180004.pdf#page=1), REPORT [p 3, Outcomes](../../../jama_jabre_2018_oi_180004.pdf#page=3), REPORT [p 4, Primary Outcome](../../../jama_jabre_2018_oi_180004.pdf#page=4), REPORT [p 6, Table 2 CPC rows and footnote b](../../../jama_jabre_2018_oi_180004.pdf#page=6), and REPORT [DOC-003 p 3, both day 28 CPC≤2 post-hoc outcome labels](../../../joi180004supp2_prod.pdf#page=3). Earlier PLAN [DOC-002 p 15, original endpoint](../../../joi180004supp1_prod.pdf#page=15), PLAN [p 122, SAP §6.1](../../../joi180004supp1_prod.pdf#page=122) and its PLAN [p 131 scanned counterpart](../../../joi180004supp1_prod.pdf#page=131) corroborate the strict threshold; they do not turn a disclosed endpoint amendment into an independent plan-version finding.
- **Printed statements and values:** Final §4.1.1 first defines favorable neurological function as CPC≤2, then adds: “In case of neurological disability before randomization, the survival associated the same degree of disability will be considered a favorable neurological function.” The amendment p 110 explicitly adds the same provision to the original endpoint. Main p 3 defines the primary endpoint as day 28 survival with CPC≤2; abstract p 1 says category 1 or 2; Table 2 footnote says CPCs 1 and 2 were counted as success. The primary ITT numerator 44 BMV equals 35+9 and 43 ETI equals 37+6; reported groups are 44/1018 and 43/1022, difference 0.11%, lower one-sided 97.5% CI−1.64%, NI P=.11. PP numerator 43 equals 35+8 and 40 equals 34+6. Supplement sensitivity outcomes are labeled CPC≤2.
- **Comparison logic:** Match the same primary day 28 favorable neurological survival endpoint and BMV−ETI comparison. The final protocol adds a success route based on unchanged baseline disability without limiting that disability to CPC≤2. The report's definition and success-coding footnote restrict successes to CPC 1/2. This is a categorical endpoint-definition difference, unaffected by display precision. No claim is made that any particular participant actually qualified under the additional route.
- **Focus area:** D, planned versus reported primary endpoint definition.
- **Proposed review domain:** protocol_report. **Proposed relationship type:** P_R. **All relationships:** P_R.
- **Affected result or extracted value:** The definition attached to primary favorable day 28 neurological survival and its ITT 44/1018 versus 43/1022 and PP 43/995 versus 40/943 success counts, including corresponding NI estimates, and the same endpoint label in sensitivity results.
- **Why context does not resolve it:** A reader can extract the strict CPC 1/2 result from the report, but cannot determine whether this is the amended primary endpoint, whether the baseline-disability provision was applied through an unstated coding convention, or whether the reported primary result uses the original definition. The explicit amendment identifies the extra provision as part of the primary endpoint; the immediate report definition/footnote does not mention it. The supplied material does not specify a restriction of the amendment to baseline CPC 1/2 or state that no participant could qualify outside that range.
- **Supported alternatives and missing definition:** The report could have used the original strict CPC endpoint; the amended provision could have been implemented without changing observed success counts; or the report could have omitted the amended coding rule from its endpoint description. The source does not identify the actual rule used for survivors with pre-randomization disability or its applicability counts. These alternatives do not assert changed event totals.
- **Classification ambiguity:** Original protocol and SAP predate the disclosed endpoint amendment. Their strict CPC wording may simply be retained historical material. This finding rests on final PLAN versus REPORT, not on treating every version difference as an inconsistency.
- **Human verification steps:** Read the final endpoint and p 110 amendment alongside main Outcomes and Table 2 footnote; establish which version governed the primary endpoint; confirm how unchanged pre-existing disability was coded and whether the printed primary counts correspond to that definition. Compare both sensitivity outcome labels if the amendment also governed those analyses.

### F-XS-002

The SAP specifies both randomized-group and actual-treatment safety analyses, while the reported key airway outcomes use only the actual-treatment safety population.

- **Locations and document roles:** PLAN [DOC-002 p 124, SAP §8.2.4 Safety Analyses](../../../joi180004supp1_prod.pdf#page=124) and PLAN [p 133, scanned counterpart](../../../joi180004supp1_prod.pdf#page=133); PLAN [p 121, ITT and AT definitions §§5.1/5.3](../../../joi180004supp1_prod.pdf#page=121) and PLAN [p 130, scanned counterpart](../../../joi180004supp1_prod.pdf#page=130). REPORT [DOC-001 p 3, Statistical Analysis safety definition](../../../jama_jabre_2018_oi_180004.pdf#page=3), REPORT [p 4, Figure/Baseline Characteristics/Adverse Events Analysis](../../../jama_jabre_2018_oi_180004.pdf#page=4), REPORT [p 6, Table 3](../../../jama_jabre_2018_oi_180004.pdf#page=6), and REPORT [p 1, abstract complications](../../../jama_jabre_2018_oi_180004.pdf#page=1). REPORT DOC-003 pages 1–3 contain investigator contributions and efficacy post-hoc results, with no randomized-group safety display.
- **Printed statements and values:** SAP §8.2.4 says, “The safety analysis will be carried out on the ITT and AT analysis.” ITT §5.1 assigns randomized adults in cardiac arrest to the randomized group regardless of technique actually received; AT §5.3 assigns by technique really received. Main p 3 defines safety as all treated patients according to actual treatment, attributing adverse events of unsuccessful attempted ETI to ETI. Main Figure shows 14 assigned ETI but receiving BMV added to safety BMV, and excludes 9 no-resuscitation patients and 1 under 18 from ETI safety; safety totals 1028 BMV/999 ETI differ from ITT 1018/1022. Abstract, adverse-event narrative and Table 3 report difficulty 186/1027 versus 134/996, difference 4.7%,95% CI 1.5%–7.9%,P=.004; failure 69/1028 versus 21/996, difference 4.6%,CI 2.8%–6.4%,P<.001; regurgitation 156/1027 versus 75/999, difference 7.7%,CI 4.9%–10.4%,P<.001. They all use the described actual-treatment safety analysis; no ITT safety estimate is displayed.
- **Comparison logic:** Match difficulty, procedure failure and regurgitation, all key secondary outcomes reported in the abstract. SAP requires safety by both ITT and AT. The report defines and displays actual-treatment safety only, and the supplied results supplement supplies no ITT safety comparison. The actual-treatment analysis cannot simultaneously represent randomized-group ITT: the Figure explicitly moves 14 randomized ETI patients into BMV safety, and population sizes differ. Missing outcome-specific observations explain 1027/996 denominators but do not explain omission of the distinct randomized-group analysis. Display precision does not alter the population distinction.
- **Focus area:** D, planned versus reported analysis population and prespecified key secondary safety analyses.
- **Proposed review domain:** protocol_report. **Proposed relationship type:** P_R. **All relationships:** P_R.
- **Affected result or extracted value:** Whether the abstract's difficulty, failure and regurgitation contrasts represent the complete planned safety analysis or only its AT component; the planned randomized-group estimates for those same outcomes are unavailable for extraction.
- **Why context does not resolve it:** The report clearly identifies actual-treatment safety, but gives no randomized-group safety result or statement replacing the SAP requirement. A reader cannot reconstruct the randomized-group event numerators from actual-treatment counts because the safety outcomes of the patients reassigned between groups are not separated. Thus the displayed actual-treatment effect cannot supply the planned ITT safety effect at once.
- **Supported alternatives and missing definition:** The ITT safety analyses may have been performed but not displayed; the reporting plan may have been changed to actual-treatment-only safety without an identified amendment; or a further ITT display may exist outside the supplied package. The supplied protocol's generic adverse-event tabulation paragraph does not explicitly rescind SAP §8.2.4. No alternative ITT counts or corrected effect values are proposed.
- **Classification ambiguity:** The supplied SAP carries an original version date and also includes retrospective final-analysis statements; no supplied amendment expressly removes ITT safety. This finding is a report-versus-supplied-SAP discrepancy, without claiming nonperformance or noncompliance.
- **Human verification steps:** Read SAP §8.2.4 and §§5.1/5.3 alongside the main safety definition and flow; locate any disclosed change to the safety population requirement; establish whether the randomized-group safety analysis was reported elsewhere or intentionally replaced; confirm the key outcome event counts under the randomized-group set if needed for extraction.
