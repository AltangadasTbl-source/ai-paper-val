 60   1. Abstract
 61   Background: Acute respiratory failure (ARF) is the leading reason for ICU admission in
 62   immunocompromised patients. Usual oxygen therapy involves administering low-to-medium oxygen
 63   flows through a nasal cannula or mask [with or without a bag and with or without the Venturi system]
 64   to achieve SpO2≥95%. Based on a landmark trial by Hilbert et al. published in 2001, oxygen therapy is
 65   usually combined with non-invasive ventilation [NIV] providing both end-expiratory positive pressure
 66   and pressure support. However, in a recent trial by our group (in press), NIV was not superior over
 67   oxygen without NIV. High-flow nasal oxygen [HFNO] therapy is a focus of growing attention as an
 68   alternative to usual oxygen therapy. By providing warmed and humidified gas, HFNO allows the
 69   delivery of higher flow rates [of up to 60 L/min] via nasal cannula devices, with FiO2 values of nearly
 70   100%. Physiological benefits of HFNO consist of higher and constant FiO2 values, decreased work of
 71   breathing, nasopharyngeal washout leading to improved breathing-effort efficiency, and higher positive
 72   airway pressures associated with better lung recruitment. Clinical consequences of these physiological
 73   benefits include alleviation of dyspnoea and discomfort, decreases in tachypnoea and signs of respiratory
 74   distress, a diminished need for intubation in patients with severe hypoxemia, and decreased mortality in
 75   unselected patients with acute hypoxemic respiratory failure. However, although preliminary data
 76   establish the feasibility and safety of this technique, HFNO has never been properly evaluated in
 77   immunocompromised patients.
 78   Hypothesis: HFNO is not inferior to the usual care [low/medium-flow oxygen and/or NIV] in
 79   minimising day-28 mortality.
 80   Design: Randomised multicentre (26 centres) open-label controlled non-inferiority trial.
 81   Intervention: Continuous HFNO only vs. usual care [low/medium-flow oxygen and/or NIV]
 82   Inclusion criteria: Only patients meeting all five of the following criteria can be included: 1) adult; 2)
 83   known immunosuppression defined as any of the following: a) immunosuppressive drugs/long-term [>3
 84   months] or high-dose [>0.5 mg/kg/day] steroids; b) solid organ transplant; c) solid tumour; d)
 85   haematological malignancy; e) HIV infection; 3) ICU admission for any reason; 4) oxygen therapy
 86   indicated by any of the following: a) respiratory distress with tachypnoea [respiratory rate >30/min]; b)
 87   cyanosis; c) laboured breathing; d) SpO2<90%; e) anticipated respiratory deterioration (procedure), 5)
 88   written informed consent from the patient or next of kin. Patients with do-not-intubate orders [DNI] are
 89   eligible.
 90   Exclusion criteria: Only patients meeting none of the following criteria can be included: 1) patient
 91   expected, at ICU admission, to die in the ICU; 2) patient or next of kin having refused study
 92   participation; 3) hypercapnia [which requires NIV, according to current guidelines], 4) isolated
 93   cardiogenic pulmonary oedema [which requires NIV, according to current guidelines], 5) pregnancy or
 94   breastfeeding, 6) anatomical factors precluding insertion of a nasal cannula; and 7) no coverage by the
 95   French statutory healthcare insurance system.
 96   Primary endpoint: all-cause mortality 28 days after ICU admission
 97   Secondary endpoints: intubation rate, comfort, dyspnoea, respiratory rate, oxygenation, ICU length of
 98   stay, ICU-acquired infections, time to resolution of pulmonary infiltrates, oxygen-free survival,
 99   ventilation-free survival, re-intubation, lowest median SpO2 while intubated, mortality after HFNO
100   failure, patient satisfaction, and physician satisfaction
101   Sample size estimation: Based on an expected 26% mortality rate in the control group, and using a 9%
102   non-inferiority margin, error rate set at 5% and a statistical power at 80%, 408 patients are required in
103   each treatment group [816 patients overall].
104   Participating centres: 26 centres belonging our study group.
105   Randomisation: randomised controlled open-label trial (patient as the unit of randomisation).
106   Study period: 30 months, i.e., 24 months for patient recruitment with 6 months of additional follow-
107   up.
