# Numeric consistency review — N001–N047

## Scope and method

Complete mechanical review of the 47 canonical numeric/reporting relationships in `relationships/numeric_relationship_inventory.md`. Evidence was restricted to the fresh mapping assets, fresh native/layout text, and the three supplied PDFs at the cited pages. Percentages were tested against their printed numerator/denominator using ordinary rounding to the displayed whole percent (tolerance: 0.5 percentage point); displayed absolute differences were tested at the same precision. A range was required to have lower endpoint less than or equal to upper endpoint. Where the sources specify different analysis populations or planned versus implemented definitions, that distinction was retained rather than treated as an arithmetic error.

## Provisional candidate records

### NC-01 — Reversed displayed endpoints for the all-patient first-attempt-duration interval

- **Category:** Numeric or arithmetic inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** [DOC-001 p. 7](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=7); comparator [DOC-003 p. 2](../../../joi180054supp2_prod.pdf#page=2).
- **Printed values:** Main Table 3 prints all-patient median duration 38 vs 36 seconds, difference `1 (4 to -1)`. The physician-clustered eTable prints `1 s (-1 s to 4 s)` for the matched all-patient duration result.
- **Rule and calculation:** A reported interval must have lower endpoint <= upper endpoint. `4 > -1`, so the main interval order fails; reordering yields `-1 to 4`, matching the supplementary display.
- **Tolerance:** None for endpoint order.
- **Direct observation versus inference:** Direct: the two printed strings. Inference: the main ordering is likely a transposition, but the source does not state a correction.
- **Alternative interpretation:** The main table may use an unstated reversed endpoint convention, although its other intervals use lower-to-upper ordering.
- **Quality-control relevance:** A reversed interval can be copied incorrectly into outcome extraction or evidence tables.
- **Human question:** Should the main Table 3 all-patient duration interval read `-1 to 4` seconds, or is another documented convention intended?

### NC-02 — Unexplained two-patient denominator difference between matched ETT+stylet waveform-dependent outcomes

- **Category:** Denominator, proportion, or total inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** [DOC-001 p. 7](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=7); [DOC-001 p. 9](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=9).
- **Printed values:** Table 3 reports ETT+stylet first-attempt success without hypoxemia as `282/366`; Table 5 reports ETT+stylet hypoxemia as `50/364`, both identifying valid pulse-oximetry waveform availability as the data constraint.
- **Rule and calculation:** For matched all-patient, arm-specific outcomes using the same hypoxemia definition and waveform-availability qualification, the stated evaluable denominator should be identified consistently or a distinct eligibility rule supplied. `366 - 364 = 2` patients are unexplained by either table.
- **Tolerance:** Zero patients for an identity claim; no matching-population definition is supplied to justify a two-patient difference.
- **Direct observation versus inference:** Direct: the two denominators and shared waveform qualification. Inference: they appear intended to describe different evaluable populations, but no reason is printed.
- **Alternative interpretation:** Table 3 may additionally require first-attempt outcome ascertainment, or Table 5 may apply a further complication-specific exclusion; neither rule is stated.
- **Quality-control relevance:** Different denominators change the reported hypoxemia percentage and can affect data extraction.
- **Human question:** What eligibility or missing-data rule accounts for the 366 versus 364 ETT+stylet denominator, and should it be disclosed in the relevant table footnotes?

### NC-03 — Hypoxemia observation-window definition differs between protocol and reported results

- **Category:** Measure, label, or scale inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** [DOC-002 pp. 9-10](../../../joi180054supp1_prod.pdf#page=9); [DOC-001 p. 7](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=7); [DOC-001 p. 9](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=9); [DOC-003 p. 3](../../../joi180054supp2_prod.pdf#page=3).
- **Printed values/statements:** Protocol: record hypoxemia from first-attempt start until one minute after ETT cuff inflation. Main Table 3/Table 5 and supplement eTable footnote: hypoxemia occurs during or within one minute after completion of the intubation attempt.
- **Rule and comparison:** Endpoint labels for the same named outcome should agree after matching population and measure. “Cuff inflation” and “completion of the attempt” are distinct printed endpoints; the protocol itself defines time to cuff inflation separately from the attempt boundary.
- **Tolerance:** None for nonidentical event-window endpoints.
- **Direct observation versus inference:** Direct: the quoted endpoints. Inference: the reporting definition may be an implemented amendment/clarification, but no supplied source identifies it as such.
- **Alternative interpretation:** Cuff inflation may usually occur before blade removal, and the authors may have treated these operationally as equivalent; the package does not establish equivalence.
- **Quality-control relevance:** A different observation window changes which hypoxemia events are eligible for the reported outcome.
- **Human question:** Which observation window governed the analyzed hypoxemia outcomes, and was the change from cuff-inflation to attempt-completion endpoint intentional and documented?

### NC-04 — First-attempt-duration endpoint differs between protocol and reported results

- **Category:** Measure, label, or scale inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** [DOC-002 p. 9](../../../joi180054supp1_prod.pdf#page=9); [DOC-001 p. 3](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=3); [DOC-001 p. 7](../../../jama_driver_2018_oi_180054.pdf#page=7); [DOC-003 p. 3](../../../joi180054supp2_prod.pdf#page=3).
- **Printed values/statements:** Protocol defines “time to intubation” from attempt start to ETT cuff inflation in the trachea. Main Table 3 and supplement eTable define “intubation duration” from blade entering mouth to blade removal; main reports all-patient medians 38 vs 36 seconds under that label.
- **Rule and comparison:** Matched outcome labels should disclose a changed endpoint when start/end events differ. Cuff inflation and blade removal are distinct procedural events, so the measures are not definitionally identical.
- **Tolerance:** None for distinct endpoint definitions.
- **Direct observation versus inference:** Direct: the printed definitions and reported medians. Inference: the reported measure may intentionally differ from the protocol’s planned time-to-intubation measure, but the supplied documents do not say why.
- **Alternative interpretation:** The protocol outcome may have been replaced by an implementation measure; this cannot be resolved without amendment/analysis documentation.
- **Quality-control relevance:** Readers extracting a duration effect may treat the protocol and reported outcome as the same measure despite different endpoints.
- **Human question:** Was first-attempt duration intentionally redefined from cuff inflation to blade removal, and where is that change and its rationale documented?
