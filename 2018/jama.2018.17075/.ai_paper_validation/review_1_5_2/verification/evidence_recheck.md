The article states that adverse events were compared with an unadjusted chi-square test for equal proportions and reported with relative risk and risk difference.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** For an unadjusted two-group relative risk, the point estimate must equal the ratio of the two printed event proportions, subject only to rounding. - **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The crude relative risk is `(47/260)/(37/240) = 1.1726`, which rounds to `1.17`, not `1.23`. These diagnostic results align with the point estimate/CI printed on the neighboring extracranial row of Table 2 and with eTable 6's intracranial P value, not with Table 2's intracranial `1.23 (0.43-3.5); P = .70` display.
- **Necessary inputs available; exact missing inputs or definitions:** Event counts, group denominators, percentages, the stated chi-square method, and both printed P values are available, so the crude RR incompatibility and cross-location P-value disagreement are reproducible. Exact SAS options, unrounded calculated statistics, and the production source that assigned effect tokens to rows are not supplied. Participant-level data are also absent, although they are not needed for the displayed crude 2x2 diagnostics.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** Table 2 could be displaying a differently modelled effect, but its header says `Relative Risk`, its footnote describes the intention-to-treat population, and no row-specific adjusted analysis is identified. The neighboring extracranial counts mathematically produce the displayed `1.23 (0.43-3.5)` and approximately `.70`, providing a source-grounded token-assignment hypothesis, but that production explanation is not established by the PDFs.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Direct observations: the crude RR does not match the effect printed on the intracranial row, and eTable 6 assigns `.43` to the same outcome for the same groups while Table 2 assigns `.70`. Inferred explanation only: the relative-risk/CI/P tokens may have been transposed between the two adjacent bleeding rows.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which relative risk, 95% CI, and P value were intended for new or increased intracranial bleeding, and was any analysis other than the stated unadjusted comparison used?
