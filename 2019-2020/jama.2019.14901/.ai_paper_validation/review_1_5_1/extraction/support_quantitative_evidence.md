### Protocol population, interventions, endpoints, and definitions

- Study design: prospective, multicenter, open-label randomized controlled comparison, allocation ratio 1:1; HFNC alone versus HFNC interspersed with NIV. Randomization is centrally Internet-based and stratified by center and PaCO2 at/near the end of the weaning trial (>45 versus nonhypercapnic). Source: DOC-002 pp. 10, 28-30.
- Eligibility: mechanical ventilation before extubation >24 h (synopsis says at least 24 h); planned extubation after a successful weaning trial; at least one high-risk factor: age >65 years or chronic cardiac/lung disease. Cardiac definitions include LVEF <=45%, documented ischemic disease, chronic atrial fibrillation, or history of cardiogenic pulmonary edema. Lung definitions include COPD, obesity-hypoventilation syndrome, or restrictive disease; suspected disease thresholds include PaCO2 >45 mm Hg and BMI >30 kg/m2 for obesity-hypoventilation. Source: DOC-002 pp. 10-11, 26.
- Primary endpoint: reintubation within 7 days after planned extubation. Secondary endpoints: reintubation at 48 h, 72 h, and until ICU discharge; acute respiratory-failure criteria within 7 days; treatment continued past 48 h; ventilatory-support-free days (intubation/NIV/HFNC) in 14 days; ICU and hospital length of stay; ICU, hospital, day-28 and day-90 mortality. Source: DOC-002 pp. 9-10, 25.
- Control: continuous HFNC for >=48 h, flow 50 L/min and FiO2 adjusted for SpO2 >=92%; if symptom-free at 48 h, stop/switch after a 30-L/min, FiO2 30% HFNC weaning test. Continue/restart in 24-h periods if ARF persists/occurs. Source: DOC-002 p. 29.
- Intervention: NIV immediately after extubation, first session >=4 h then >=2-h sessions, including 22:00-06:00 when possible, total >=12 h/day, plus HFNC 50 L/min between NIV sessions; support >=5 cm H2O targeting tidal volume around 6-8 mL/kg, PEEP 5-10 cm H2O, FiO2 for SpO2 >=92%, ramp 0.1-0.2 s, cycling-off 25%-30% of peak inspiratory flow. Both groups treated >=48 h; beyond this, 24-h extensions for persisting/incident ARF. Source: DOC-002 pp. 29-30.
- ARF used for post-48-h treatment continuation: at least two of respiratory rate >25/min for >=2 h; clinical distress/fatigue; pH <7.35 plus PaCO2 >45 mm Hg; FiO2 >=50% to retain SpO2 >=92% or PaO2/FiO2 <=150 mm Hg. Reintubation respiratory-failure definition also requires at least two criteria, but uses RR >35/min and FiO2 >=80% to retain SpO2 >=92% or PaO2/FiO2 <=100 mm Hg; other reintubation triggers are SAP <90 mm Hg or MAP <65 mm Hg requiring vasopressors, Glasgow <12/agitation, or cardiac/respiratory arrest. Therapeutic NIV after reintubation criteria may be attempted according to center practice, but persistent ARF 2 h after its initiation should result in intubation. Source: DOC-002 pp. 30-31.
- Weaning trial: T-piece or pressure support 7 cm H2O without PEEP, lasting 30 min-2 h; PaCO2 assessed at end of trial (otherwise last mechanical-ventilation blood gas). Page-42 visual form additionally specifies screening: RR <=35/min and SpO2 >=92% with FiO2 <=40% and PEEP <=8 cmH2O; no vasopressors; awake/cooperative with RASS +1 to -2; no continuous sedation. The form’s poor-tolerance fields are O2 flow >6 L/min, SpO2 <90%, RR >35/min, HR >140/min, SAP <90 or >180 mm Hg, and distress signs; it stops at 1 h or after one poor-tolerance criterion. It scores cough 0-3, secretions 0-3, defines congestion risk when secretions 2/3 plus cough 0/1, and records cuff-leak risk. Source: DOC-002 pp. 27-28, 42 (visual confirmation).
- Follow-up fields: inclusion/extubation; H1; H6, H12-H24, H24-H48; daily day 3-day 7; day 14; ICU discharge; and day 90. Variables include vital signs, HFNC/NIV settings, blood gas, number/duration of sessions, ARF/reintubation status, death, intubation/tracheotomy etiology/duration, lengths of stay, and withdrawal/withholding of life-sustaining treatment. Source: DOC-002 pp. 31-32, 41.

### Planned statistical framework and sample size

- Analysis population: intention to treat after blinded review-committee validation of inclusion/exclusion. Continuous variables: median/IQR/extremes or mean/SD with missing-data number; categorical variables: frequency and percentage. Source: DOC-002 p. 33.
- Primary comparison: percentage reintubated by day 7 compared between groups with chi-square. Predictors: t test/Mann-Whitney for continuous variables, chi-square/Fisher for categorical variables, univariate logistic regression reporting OR and 95% CI; multivariable logistic regression tests interactions, initially includes variables with p<0.20 plus forced literature-relevant variables, uses descending elimination and retains p<0.05 plus forced variables. Time to reintubation: Kaplan-Meier/log-rank and Cox backward selection (univariate p<0.20, final p<0.05), reported as adjusted relative risk and hazard ratio with 95% CI. Source: DOC-002 p. 33.
- Secondary rates use the primary method. Ventilator-free days and lengths of stay use t test/Mann-Whitney. Mortality at ICU/hospital/day 28/day 90 uses Kaplan-Meier/log-rank and Cox selection under analogous p<0.20/p<0.05 rules. Source: DOC-002 pp. 33-34.
- Prespecified subgroup comparisons: hypercapnic (PaCO2 >45 mm Hg at end of spontaneous breathing trial) versus nonhypercapnic; chronic cardiac/lung disease; and T-piece versus minimal pressure-support weaning. Source: DOC-002 p. 34.
- Sample-size statement: 590 patients provides 80% power, two-sided alpha 0.05, to show 8 percentage-point absolute reintubation difference: 18% control HFNC alone versus 10% HFNC+NIV. Planned 10% increase for secondary exclusions gives target n=650. Source: DOC-002 pp. 11, 34.
- Expected rates: NIV+standard oxygen pilot 15% reintubation at day 7 and 10% at 48 h; HFNC+NIV expected day-7 rate 10%. Standard oxygen pilot day-7 rate 28%; HFNC control expected approximately 15%-20% at day 7. Source: DOC-002 pp. 34-35.

### Historical quantitative context and Table 1

- Protocol background says overall reintubation/extubation failure about 15%, exceeding 20%-25% in high-risk patients; high-risk patients represent around one half of ICU extubations. In one preliminary cohort of 168 planned extubations with 26 failures, reintubation was 29% if age >65 years, 23% with any chronic cardiac/respiratory disease, 34% with both factors, and 9% with neither. A before-after comparison reports 28% to 15% reintubation within 7 days, p=.03. Source: DOC-002 pp. 8, 14, 18-20.
- Table 1 (DOC-002 p. 17) reports external historical RCTs as `NIV versus standard oxygen`: Nava 2005, n=48 vs 49: reintubation 4 (8%) vs 12 (24%), p=.027; ICU death 3 (6%) vs 9 (18%), p=.064. Ferrer 2006, n=79 vs 83: postextubation ARF 13 (16%) vs 27 (33%), p=.029; reintubation 9 (11%) vs 18 (22%), p=.12; nosocomial infection 18 (23%) vs 27 (33%), P=NS; ICU death 2 (3%) vs 12 (14%), p=.015. Ferrer 2009, n=54 vs 52, PaCO2 >45: ARF 8 (15%) vs 25 (48%), p<.0001; reintubation 6 (11%) vs 10 (19%), p=.37; pneumonia 3 (6%) vs 9 (17%), p=.12; ICU death 3 (6%) vs 4 (8%), p=.71; day-90 death 6 (11%) vs 16 (31%), p=.024. Khilnani 2011, n=20 vs 20: reintubation 5 (25%) vs 3 (15%), p=.44; ICU stay 18.3 +/-7.9 versus 16.1 +/-6.3 days, p=.34. Su 2012, n=202 vs 204: extubation failure 30 (15%) vs 27 (13%), p=.62; reintubation 21 (10%) vs 16 (8%), p=.37; ICU death 3 (1.5%) vs 2 (1%), p=.64. Ornico 2013, n=20 vs 18: reintubation 1 (5%) vs 7 (39%), p=.016; hospital death 0% versus 4 (22%), p=.041.
