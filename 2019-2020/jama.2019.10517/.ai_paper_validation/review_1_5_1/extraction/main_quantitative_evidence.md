## PDF p. 1 (printed p. 622): abstract

The 36-week, four-center trial randomized 126 participants: sertraline plus olanzapine, n=64; sertraline plus placebo, n=62. Mean (SD) age was 55.3 (14.9) years and 78/126 were women (61.9%). The reported completion count was 114/126 (90.5%). At randomization, median sertraline dosage was 150 mg/d (IQR 150-200) and median olanzapine dosage was 15 mg/d (IQR 10-20).

Relapse was 13/64 (20.3%) with olanzapine and 34/62 (54.8%) with placebo; the reported Cox hazard ratio (HR) was 0.25 (95% CI 0.13-0.48; P<.001). Abstract daily treatment-by-time effects were: weight 0.13 lb (95% CI 0.11-0.15); waist circumference 0.009 inches (0.004-0.014); total cholesterol 0.29 mg/dL (0.13-0.45); LDL cholesterol 0.04 mg/dL (-0.01 to 0.10); HDL cholesterol -0.01 mg/dL (-0.03 to 0.01); triglyceride -0.153 mg/dL (-0.306 to 0.004); glucose -0.02 mg/dL (-0.12 to 0.08); and HbA1c -0.0002 mg/dL (-0.0021 to 0.0016). The conclusion states reduced relapse risk over 36 weeks, balanced against adverse effects including weight gain.

## PDF pp. 2-4 (printed pp. 623-625): design, populations, definitions, and models

Participants were aged 18-85 years at acute enrollment. Acute-phase remission required absent delusions/hallucinations and 17-item HDRS <=10 for two consecutive weeks. Near remission required absent delusions/hallucinations, HDRS 11-15 with >=50% baseline HDRS reduction, and CGI very much/much improved. At the end of the 8-week stabilization phase, full/near remission plus MMSE >=24 qualified participants for the 36-week RCT.

Randomization was 1:1 with blocks of 4-8 and strata age 18-59 versus 60-85 years, remission versus near remission, and site. The original power calculation was n=176 for 80% power to detect a 20% absolute relapse-risk difference with up to 15% attrition; the approved revised target was n=128 because overall relapse risk was higher than anticipated.

Primary analysis: all randomized participants; Cox proportional-hazards model with treatment plus the three stratification variables. A post hoc sensitivity Cox model excluded participants who discontinued either/both study medications but remained under research assessment. Anthropometric/metabolic analyses used linear mixed models with participant random intercept and random continuous-time slope, fixed site/time/treatment/treatment-by-time terms. Simpson-Angus score used overdispersed Poisson mixed-effects regression. Tests were two-sided at overall 5%; multiple-secondary-outcome P values were post hoc Holm-stepdown adjusted. Pattern-mixture models assessed early-termination missingness; triglyceride estimates were pattern-mixture averaged.

## Direct-source consistency locators for later review

The relapse count/proportion pair is reproduced concordantly on PDF pp. 1, 2, 6, and 7; Figure 1 arm totals are 64 and 62. Table 1 count/percentage entries reconcile with their printed arm denominators at stated rounding. Flow subtotals reconcile at every explicitly partitioned node. Figure 2 week-0 risk sets match randomized arm totals and week-36 risk sets match completed-in-remission/near-remission counts in Figure 1. Table 3 is explicitly multi-event, so event-row sums are not relapse totals. Table 4 explicitly cautions against deriving its reported differences from displayed baseline/termination values when missing data differ. Table 5 percentages reconcile to n=64/n=62 at one decimal place.

Potential QC observations for later independent checking, without candidate registration or adjudication: (1) the reported 114/126 trial-completion measure (PDF p. 1) is not defined in the Figure 1 terminal-status categories and should not be equated automatically with the 43+24 remission/near-remission completions; (2) HbA1c is expressed as percent in Tables 2 and 4 but the daily treatment-by-time effect is labelled `mg/dL` in the abstract and results narrative (PDF pp. 1 and 7). Both are source-location and label checks, not conclusions.
