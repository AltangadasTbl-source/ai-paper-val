2.2.2 STUDY OBJECTIVE
The primary objective of this trial is to evaluate the effect of a daily dose of oral probiotics on CAAD
for all-cause acute infections.
The secondary objectives of this trial are to determine the effect of daily probiotic intake on:
a) CAAD for the following sub-categories of infection: respiratory tract infections, urinary tract
infections, skin infections, gastrointestinal infections, unexplained fever and other infection
b) Site, incidence and duration of infection
c) Prevalence of C. difficile infection
d) Health Utility (EQ-5D-5L(7})
e) Wellbeing (ICECAP-O(8))
f} Service Use (hospitalisation)
g) Mortality
h) Antibiotic associated diarrhoea
3. STUDY MATERIALS
3.1 TRIAL DESIGN
The PRINCESS trial is a two-arm double-blind individually-randomised controlled trial which will take
place approximately across 20 care homes in Wales and England. The trial will evaluate the efficacy
and mechanisms of a nutritional intervention (daily dose of oral probiotics) on cumulative antibiotic
administration days for all-cause infections and incidence and duration of infections in care home
residents over a 12 month period. Treatment allocation is a 1:1 ratio. Care home residents are
randomised to either daily probiotic supplement or matched placebo for up to a 12-month period.
3.2 RANDOMISATION
The researcher (either data manager, research nurse, or trial manager) will randomise the participants
on an individual basis using the online computerised randomisation system (Sortition) provided by the
University of Oxford Primary Care Clinical trials Unit. The system will be operational 24 hours a day.
Allocation ratio will be one-to-one and stratified by care home, and minimised by gender of
participant. The algorithm will allocate new participants to the trial arm that minimises imbalance with
respect to gender and within care homes with probability of 0.8. Full details of the randomisation
process are described in the trial protocol and randomisation protocol.
