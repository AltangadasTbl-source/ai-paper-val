## C001 — Primary-outcome 72-hour clock origin is not stated consistently

- **Cited location found:** The cited primary-outcome wording and matched result were found in DOC-001 [p. 1](../../../jama_paterson_2024_oi_240139_1741633738.12862.pdf#page=1), [p. 5](../../../jama_paterson_2024_oi_240139_1741633738.12862.pdf#page=5), and [p. 6](../../../jama_paterson_2024_oi_240139_1741633738.12862.pdf#page=6). The planned endpoint definition was found in DOC-003 [p. 9](../../../joi240139supp2_prod_1741633738.17362.pdf#page=9).
- **Source value or text matched:** DOC-001 p. 1 says “return of gut function at 72 hours after surgery” and reports 160/279 (57.3%) versus 164/278 (59.0%), adjusted difference −1.9% (95% CI, −8.0% to 4.2%), and RR 0.97 (95% CI, 0.88 to 1.07). DOC-001 p. 5 says the same primary outcome was met “at 72 hours after operation,” with the same counts and effect values. DOC-001 p. 6 labels the same Table 2 result “achieved GI-3 recovery by 72 h after operation,” again with 160 versus 164 and RR 0.97.
- **Comparator matched:** DOC-003 p. 9 defines the primary outcome as achievement of the same two-part GI-3 composite “at 72 hours after the start of operation.” The outcome composition, treatment contrast, primary analysis population, counts, and reported effect identify this as the planned definition corresponding to the article result.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** A binary endpoint evaluated at a fixed 72-hour cutoff needs a consistent clock origin when the same result is named across sources. The rule applies to the time-window label; it does not require the article and SAP to use identical prose if the shorter article wording is explicitly or operationally defined as the same origin.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The numeric result matches across article occurrences. The logical comparison is `72 hours after surgery/operation` versus `72 hours after the start of operation`. Only the SAP phrase expressly identifies operation start as time zero; the article phrases do not expressly identify a start timestamp.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The GI-3 composition, 72-hour duration, analysis-group counts, denominators, percentages, adjusted difference, RR, and SAP clock-origin phrase are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not supply the final outcome-derivation specification, case-report-form timestamp fields, analysis code or dataset used to calculate the cutoff, or a definition stating that article wording “after surgery/operation” means “after the start of operation.”
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The article may use “after surgery/operation” as shorthand for the SAP’s operation-start origin. Alternatively, the final derivation may have used completion of surgery, start of anaesthesia, or another timestamp not named in the article.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Direct observations are the three article phrases, the SAP phrase, and the otherwise matching result. Any claim that the article used a different timestamp, or that its wording is harmless shorthand, is an inferred explanation.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What timestamp was used as time zero for the published 72-hour GI-3 cutoff, and should each article occurrence explicitly identify that timestamp?

## C002 — Postoperative opioid-consumption window is 24 hours versus 72 hours

- **Cited location found:** The article endpoint description was found in DOC-001 [p. 4](../../../jama_paterson_2024_oi_240139_1741633738.12862.pdf#page=4), and the displayed values were found in DOC-001 [p. 6](../../../jama_paterson_2024_oi_240139_1741633738.12862.pdf#page=6). The planned definitions were found in DOC-002 [p. 17](../../../joi240139supp1_prod_1741633738.16362.pdf#page=17) and DOC-003 [p. 11](../../../joi240139supp2_prod_1741633738.17362.pdf#page=11).
- **Source value or text matched:** DOC-001 p. 4 names “postoperative opioid consumption (up to 24 hours).” DOC-001 p. 6 reports “Postoperative opioid analgesia up to 24 h, median (IQR), OME mg”: n=210 and 70.6 (30.0 to 150.0) for IV lidocaine, and n=210 and 45.0 (17.1 to 98.6) for placebo.
- **Comparator matched:** DOC-002 p. 17 defines “Total postoperative opioid consumption,” measured as morphine-equivalent doses, with the specific time point “cumulative total until 72 hours after start of operation.” DOC-003 p. 11 schedules “Total opioid consumption in-hospital up to 72 hours.” The measure, postoperative setting, and morphine-equivalent framing align; the printed accumulation windows do not.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Cumulative drug consumption over 24 hours is a different quantitative measure from cumulative consumption over 72 hours unless a supplied definition establishes equivalence or identifies them as separately intended endpoints.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `72 hours − 24 hours = 48 hours`. The displayed 24-hour median cannot be recalculated as a 72-hour median from the supplied aggregate values, and a cumulative 72-hour amount cannot generally be assumed equal to the cumulative 24-hour amount.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The article’s 24-hour label, both group medians, IQRs, sample sizes, OME unit, and the protocol/SAP 72-hour definitions are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** No final amendment, final outcome dictionary, 72-hour participant-level consumption data, populated 72-hour analysis output, or derivation record for the published 70.6-mg and 45.0-mg medians is supplied.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The published analysis may intentionally report a distinct 24-hour collected measure, or the planned 72-hour endpoint may have been changed before final analysis. The planning sources do not document either explanation.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** The 24-hour and 72-hour labels and the published medians are direct observations. Whether an endpoint change occurred, whether 72-hour values were also analysed, and whether the published values were relabelled are inferred possibilities.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which accumulation window generated the published 70.6-mg and 45.0-mg medians, and where is any change from the planned 72-hour endpoint to a 24-hour endpoint documented?

## C003 — Unplanned-readmission window is 90 days versus 30 days

- **Cited location found:** The article’s endpoint definition was found in DOC-001 [p. 4](../../../jama_paterson_2024_oi_240139_1741633738.12862.pdf#page=4), and the displayed readmission result was found in DOC-001 [p. 8](../../../jama_paterson_2024_oi_240139_1741633738.12862.pdf#page=8). Planning comparators were found in DOC-002 [p. 18](../../../joi240139supp1_prod_1741633738.16362.pdf#page=18) and DOC-003 [p. 10](../../../joi240139supp2_prod_1741633738.17362.pdf#page=10) and [p. 27](../../../joi240139supp2_prod_1741633738.17362.pdf#page=27).
- **Source value or text matched:** DOC-001 p. 4 names the tertiary objective “unplanned readmission within 30 days of surgery.” DOC-001 p. 8 instead labels the Table 3 row “Unplanned readmission after discharge and within 90 d of operation” and prints 31/279 (11.1%) for IV lidocaine and 34/278 (12.2%) for placebo.
- **Comparator matched:** DOC-002 p. 18 specifies “Unplanned re-admissions within 30 days of date of operation,” number of patients readmitted, with the specific time point “30 days from date of operation.” DOC-003 p. 10 lists the same 30-day tertiary outcome, and the DOC-003 p. 27 dummy-table heading again specifies unplanned readmissions within 30 days of operation.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** A participant count and proportion for readmission within 30 days are not interchangeable with a participant count and proportion for readmission within 90 days. The same endpoint label should retain its ascertainment window unless a source identifies a redefinition.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `90 days − 30 days = 60 days`. The displayed percentages reproduce from the Table 3 denominators at one decimal place: `31 / 279 × 100 = 11.11%`, printed as 11.1%, and `34 / 278 × 100 = 12.23%`, printed as 12.2%. That arithmetic identifies the published denominators but cannot identify whether the 31 and 34 events were accumulated for 30 or 90 days.
- **Necessary inputs available:** Both article windows, both group counts and denominators, the reproduced percentages, and three independent 30-day planning/method labels are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package lacks participant-level readmission dates, final analysis output showing the cutoff, a final endpoint-derivation dictionary, and an amendment history that selects 30 or 90 days for these counts.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** Table 3 may contain 30-day counts under a 90-day label, or the final endpoint may have changed to 90 days while the article Methods and planning documents retained 30 days.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** The 30-day and 90-day phrases, counts, denominators, and percentages are direct observations. Which window produced the counts, and whether a label or endpoint definition changed, are inferred explanations.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Do the 31 and 34 counts represent readmissions within 30 days or within 90 days of operation, and what final approved definition and analysis output establish that window?

## C004 — ERAS subgroup cut points differ from the protocol bands

- **Cited location found:** The protocol’s ERAS criteria begin in DOC-002 [p. 17](../../../joi240139supp1_prod_1741633738.16362.pdf#page=17), and its grading phrase was found in DOC-002 [p. 18](../../../joi240139supp1_prod_1741633738.16362.pdf#page=18). The SAP’s category names were found in DOC-003 [p. 15](../../../joi240139supp2_prod_1741633738.17362.pdf#page=15). The eFigure thresholds and subgroup display were inspected directly in DOC-004 [p. 2](../../../joi240139supp3_prod_1741633738.18862.pdf#page=2).
- **Source value or text matched:** DOC-002 pp. 17-18 describes ERAS protocol compliance from listed care variables and says the participant metric is a “graded proportion e.g 0-30% compliant; 30-60% compliant; >60% compliant.” DOC-003 p. 15 names “ERAS protocol compliance (High, moderate, low).” DOC-004 p. 2 displays high n=191, moderate n=274, and low n=92 and states that high adherence means meeting 7 or more of 10 criteria while low adherence means meeting fewer than 5 criteria.
- **Comparator matched:** Both sources concern categorical ERAS adherence used for the primary-outcome subgroup. The protocol provides example percentage bands; the eFigure provides a 10-criterion count scale and explicit high and low thresholds. The SAP supplies the same three category names but no cut points.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** A participant’s criterion count should map unambiguously to one category under the categorization rule used for the subgroup analysis. Comparability to the protocol bands requires knowing whether those bands were prescriptive and how their boundaries were implemented.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `4 / 10 × 100 = 40%`. The eFigure rule places 4/10 in low because 4 is fewer than 5. Forty percent lies in the protocol’s printed 30%-60% band, not its 0%-30% band. The eFigure counts also reproduce the primary-analysis total: `191 + 274 + 92 = 557`. A moderate range of 5-6 criteria is logically implied only if the three eFigure categories are exhaustive and mutually exclusive; the eFigure does not print that range expressly.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The protocol’s example percentage bands, the ten-item denominator used by the eFigure, the eFigure’s high and low thresholds, the three category labels, and the three subgroup counts are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package lacks the final categorization code, an express definition of the moderate category, the participant-level number of eligible criteria and missing-item denominator rule, documentation stating whether the protocol’s “e.g.” bands were illustrative, and a record showing whether those bands were superseded.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The protocol’s “e.g.” may mark the percentage bands as examples rather than the final algorithm, and a deliberate final rule may have used 0-4, 5-6, and 7-10 criteria. Alternatively, the protocol bands may have remained operative and the eFigure threshold may not represent the same scale.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** The printed bands, eFigure thresholds, counts, and 4/10 arithmetic are direct observations or direct arithmetic. Treating the protocol bands as mandatory, inferring moderate as 5-6, or asserting that a final algorithm superseded the protocol are interpretations.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What exact code, eligible-item denominator rule, and category boundaries produced n=191, n=274, and n=92, and were the protocol’s printed percentage bands illustrative or superseded?

## C005 — The eFigure assigns both 99% and 95% labels to the same intervals

- **Cited location found:** The graphical legend, right-hand interval values, and caption were inspected directly in DOC-004 [p. 2](../../../joi240139supp3_prod_1741633738.18862.pdf#page=2). The planned subgroup confidence convention was found in DOC-003 [p. 15](../../../joi240139supp2_prod_1741633738.17362.pdf#page=15).
- **Source value or text matched:** In DOC-004 p. 2, the forest-plot legend labels the solid horizontal interval line “99% CI.” The sentence immediately below the plot says, “The numbers on the right are the within-subgroup relative risks and 95% confidence interval.” The right side prints subgroup estimates and intervals, including intended 6 hours 0.92 (0.73, 1.15) and 12 hours 1.03 (0.82, 1.28).
- **Comparator matched:** DOC-003 p. 15 states that subgroup analyses use a two-sided 1% significance level and that corresponding 99% confidence intervals will be calculated. This independently matches the legend’s 99% label while differing from the eFigure caption’s 95% label.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** One displayed within-subgroup interval set must have one confidence level. A legend and caption referring to the same subgroup interval representation should not assign mutually different confidence levels unless they clearly distinguish different interval sets.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `99% − 95% = 4 percentage points`. The plot places one horizontal interval and one parenthesized numerical interval beside each subgroup estimate; the legend calls the horizontal intervals 99% CIs while the caption calls the right-hand within-subgroup intervals 95% CIs. No rounding convention can reconcile the explicit 95% and 99% labels.
- **Necessary inputs available:** Both labels, the printed interval values, the visual linkage of each subgroup bar to its right-hand value, and the SAP’s planned 1%/99% framework are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not supply the populated statistical output, interval-construction command output, standard errors, covariance details, or metadata recording the confidence level actually used for each displayed interval.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The caption may have used a generic 95% phrase while subgroup intervals were calculated at 99%. A distinction between the overall estimate’s 95% CI and subgroup estimates’ 99% CIs may have been intended, but the caption expressly refers to the plural within-subgroup intervals and does not state that distinction. The legend itself could instead carry the unintended label.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** The 99% legend, 95% caption, SAP 99% statement, and printed interval values are direct observations. Which label reflects the calculations, and whether an overall-versus-subgroup distinction was intended, are inferred explanations.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What confidence level was used to calculate each displayed subgroup interval, and what confidence-level wording should the legend and caption use to distinguish any overall and subgroup intervals?

## C006 — Day-5 EQ-5D inference conflicts with blanket no-difference prose

- **Cited location found:** The Results statement was found in DOC-001 [p. 5](../../../jama_paterson_2024_oi_240139_1741633738.12862.pdf#page=5), and the day-5 EQ-5D-5L row was found in DOC-001 [p. 6](../../../jama_paterson_2024_oi_240139_1741633738.12862.pdf#page=6). The confidence-interval framework was found in DOC-003 [p. 13](../../../joi240139supp2_prod_1741633738.17362.pdf#page=13); its continuation and secondary-outcome model statement were found in DOC-003 [p. 14](../../../joi240139supp2_prod_1741633738.17362.pdf#page=14).
- **Source value or text matched:** DOC-001 p. 6 prints postoperative day 5 EQ-5D-5L means of 0.555 (SD 0.257; n=89) and 0.621 (SD 0.247; n=96), with treatment contrast −0.057 (95% CI, −0.111 to −0.003) and P=.04. DOC-001 p. 5 states there was “no significant difference” in quality of life (EQ-5D-5L) between groups.
- **Comparator matched:** DOC-003 p. 13 says treatment effects use 95% CIs apart from subgroup analyses. DOC-003 p. 14 states there is no adjustment to secondary outcomes for multiple testing, identifies repeated-measures analysis for EQ-5D, and says treatment effects are estimated from time-by-treatment interactions at each time point. These statements align the day-5 row with the printed 95% time-specific framework but do not define the scope of the article’s prose statement.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Under the printed time-specific 95% framework, a confidence interval for a difference that excludes zero and a two-sided P value below .05 indicate a nominal non-null contrast for that row. An unqualified statement covering EQ-5D as a whole needs either to accommodate that row or to identify a different endpoint-level decision rule.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The estimate is inside the ordered interval: `−0.111 < −0.057 < −0.003`. Zero is outside because the upper endpoint is `−0.003 < 0`. The printed P value satisfies `.04 < .05`. These comparisons reproduce the nominal row-level inference; they do not establish an overall EQ-5D treatment effect or a clinical interpretation.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The group means, SDs, day-5 denominators, adjusted contrast, 95% CI, P value, repeated-measures/time-by-treatment description, and no-secondary-multiplicity-adjustment statement are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not state whether the prose refers to an omnibus treatment-by-time test, a prespecified EQ-5D time point, an endpoint-level hierarchy, a clinical-importance threshold, or another narrative decision rule. No overall EQ-5D test statistic or populated model output is supplied.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The prose may summarize an overall repeated-measures pattern rather than every time-specific contrast, or it may apply an unstated endpoint-level interpretation despite the nominal day-5 row. It may also be a broad summary that did not separately mention one nominal time-point result.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** The day-5 values, interval, P value, prose, and SAP framework are direct observations; interval containment and null exclusion are direct logical comparisons. Any overall-test, hierarchy, or narrative-intent explanation is inferred.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What estimand or decision rule governs the article’s EQ-5D prose, and was the nominal day-5 time-specific contrast intended to be included in or distinguished from that statement?

## C007 — ERAS subgroup is labeled as two levels versus three levels

- **Cited location found:** The article subgroup wording was found in DOC-001 [p. 5](../../../jama_paterson_2024_oi_240139_1741633738.12862.pdf#page=5). The planned category names were found in DOC-003 [p. 15](../../../joi240139supp2_prod_1741633738.17362.pdf#page=15), and the displayed subgroup levels, counts, estimates, and interaction P value were inspected directly in DOC-004 [p. 2](../../../joi240139supp3_prod_1741633738.18862.pdf#page=2).
- **Source value or text matched:** DOC-001 p. 5 describes the subgroup as “high vs low enhanced recovery protocol adherence.” DOC-004 p. 2 displays one enhanced-recovery-adherence interaction P=.966 and three rows: high n=191, RR 0.96 (0.82, 1.12); moderate n=274, RR 0.99 (0.76, 1.29); and low n=92, RR 1.01 (0.49, 2.08).
- **Comparator matched:** DOC-003 p. 15 names the subgroup factor “ERAS protocol compliance (High, moderate, low).” The SAP and eFigure therefore identify the same three category names, whereas the article phrase names only high and low.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** A categorical subgroup label should identify the levels represented in the reported interaction. A two-level phrase does not fully describe a three-level displayed factor unless it is explicitly defined as shorthand or as a separate extreme-level contrast.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The three eFigure counts sum to the full primary-analysis population: `191 + 274 + 92 = 557`. The figure prints one interaction P value beside the three-row factor. This supports a three-level display, but the supplied sources do not expose the model matrix needed to prove how the interaction test was coded.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The article’s two named levels, SAP’s three named levels, eFigure’s three rows, subgroup counts, RRs, intervals, interaction P=.966, and primary-analysis total 557 are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The final model formula, factor coding, degrees of freedom for the interaction test, contrast matrix, and populated analysis output are absent. The article does not define “high vs low” as shorthand.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** “High vs low” may be shorthand for a categorical adherence subgroup that includes moderate, or it may describe an intended extreme-category contrast while the eFigure displays a separate three-level interaction. The package does not distinguish these possibilities.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** The two-level article phrase, three-level SAP/eFigure labels, counts, estimates, and interaction P value are direct observations; the count sum is direct arithmetic. Whether all three levels entered the interaction model, or whether the article phrase was shorthand, remains inferred.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Did the reported ERAS interaction model include high, moderate, and low as three factor levels, and what level description should the article use for the analysis actually performed?
