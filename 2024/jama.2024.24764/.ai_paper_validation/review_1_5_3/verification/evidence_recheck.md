## C001 — Smoking percentages use a different denominator from the printed smoking totals

- **Cited location found:** Yes. The group headers and smoking rows are together in Table 1 at [DOC-001 PDF p. 5](<../../../jama_atherton_2025_oi_240145_1741627844.85412.pdf#page=5>).
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. The table labels the columns `No. of participants (%)`, with iNPWT `n = 411` and surgeon's preference `n = 410`. Under `Smoking status`, it prints `Total No.` values 405 and 402. The three iNPWT rows are 220 (53.5), 95 (23.1), and 90 (21.9); the three surgeon's-preference rows are 223 (54.4), 70 (17.1), and 109 (26.6).
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** Yes. The printed smoking totals are 405 and 402, while the printed column-header denominators are 411 and 410.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. A variable-specific `Total No.` normally supplies the denominator for that variable's percentages unless the table states that percentages instead use a different denominator. The page supplies no smoking-specific denominator note.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The smoking counts reconcile with the variable-specific totals: 220 + 95 + 90 = 405 and 223 + 70 + 109 = 402. Against the column-header denominators, 220/411 = 53.53%, 95/411 = 23.11%, 90/411 = 21.90%, 223/410 = 54.39%, 70/410 = 17.07%, and 109/410 = 26.59%; these round to all six printed percentages. Against the printed smoking totals, the corresponding percentages are 54.32%, 23.46%, 22.22%, 55.47%, 17.41%, and 27.11%, which do not round to the printed percentages. The gaps between the group headers and smoking totals are 411 - 405 = 6 and 410 - 402 = 8.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The two possible denominator pairs, all six numerators, and all six printed percentages are available. The missing definition is the table's intended denominator convention for smoking percentages and whether the six and eight records absent from the smoking total are treated as denominator members.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The percentages may intentionally use every participant in each randomized group, with `Total No.` denoting only participants with observed smoking status. This interpretation exactly reproduces the percentages, but the page does not state that convention or identify the missing smoking values.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Direct observation is limited to the printed headers, totals, counts, percentages, and absence of a smoking-specific note on the page. The proposition that `Total No.` should control the percentages, or alternatively that randomized-group denominators were intentionally used despite missing smoking data, is interpretive.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which denominator was intended for each smoking percentage, and, if 411 and 410 were intentional, how should the page define the role of the six and eight participants not included in the printed smoking totals?

Robust sandwich variance estimation is a separate choice that can coexist with a working covariance structure; its omission from the article does not by itself establish that it was not used.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** No arithmetic is applicable. The source-to-source logical comparison is `unstructured` in the SAP versus `independent` in the final article. The SAP additionally names robust sandwich standard errors, while the cited final description is silent about variance estimation.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The planned and published named covariance structures and the SAP's planned variance estimator are available. - **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The final article may give a condensed description of an amended analysis, or `independent` may describe the final working covariance while robust sandwich standard errors were retained. The cited sources do not identify the executed variance estimator or provide an amendment rationale.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** The two covariance names, the SAP's sandwich-estimator statement, and the final article's silence about robust standard errors are direct observations. - **Exact remaining human question:** Which covariance structure and variance estimator generated the published SF-12 and EQ-5D-5L estimates and P values, and what contemporaneous record, if any, documents the reason for the specification used?

## C007 — Length-of-stay effect measure and model differ between SAP/protocol and final article

- **Cited location found:** Yes. The SAP length-of-stay plan is at [DOC-002 PDF p. 25](<../../../joi240145supp1_prod_1741627844.87412.pdf#page=25>); the protocol's skewness contingency is at [DOC-004 PDF p. 39](<../../../joi240145supp4_prod_1741627844.90412.pdf#page=39>); the final analysis description is at [DOC-001 PDF p. 4](<../../../jama_atherton_2025_oi_240145_1741627844.85412.pdf#page=4>); and the published results and footnote are at [DOC-001 PDF p. 8](<../../../jama_atherton_2025_oi_240145_1741627844.85412.pdf#page=8>).
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. The SAP states that length of hospital stay will use mixed-effects linear regression to estimate an adjusted mean difference and 95% CI for UK participants. The protocol states that the outcome will use an adjusted mean difference from mixed-effects linear regression, but, if the data are skewed, it will be summarized with medians and IQRs and an unadjusted difference between medians will be reported.
- **Comparator printed value/text matched:** Yes. The final article says the data were not normally distributed, were log-transformed before mixed-effect linear model fitting, and model estimates were exponentiated to obtain an adjusted ratio of geometric means. Table 3 reports median (IQR) summaries and adjusted ratios of geometric means of 0.91 (95% CI, 0.82 to 1.02) for UK participants and 0.96 (95% CI, 0.88 to 1.06) for UK and Australian participants; footnote c confirms the log transformation, exponentiation, and ratio interpretation.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. An adjusted mean difference and an unadjusted median difference are additive effect measures expressed in days; a ratio of geometric means is a multiplicative, dimensionless effect measure. These are different estimands or reporting scales even when they concern the same length-of-stay endpoint. The UK-only analysis provides the directly matched population across the SAP and final article.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The logical comparison is reproduced: the SAP specifies an adjusted arithmetic-scale mean difference; the protocol's skewness branch specifies an unadjusted median difference; the final article reports adjusted exponentiated log-model coefficients as ratios of geometric means. The reported ratios cannot be arithmetically reconstructed from the printed medians and IQRs because the group geometric means, fitted log-scale coefficients, and participant-level values are not printed.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The planned effect measures, the final transformation and effect-measure description, the two final ratios, and their intervals are available. Missing inputs are the executed model code or output, group geometric means or log-scale coefficients, participant-level length-of-stay values, and any prespecified decision rule or amendment authorizing the log-transformed ratio analysis.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The final log transformation may be a documented analytic response to the nonnormal distribution explicitly noted in the article, and the protocol's skewness branch shows that distributional shape was anticipated. However, the printed protocol branch specifies a median difference rather than a ratio of geometric means, and the cited sources do not supply an amendment or decision rule selecting the final scale.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** The planned mean-difference and median-difference language, the final log-transformation and ratio language, and the reported ratios are direct observations. Characterizing the final model as a documented or undocumented change, and attributing it to a distributional diagnostic, requires inference beyond the cited text.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Was the log-transformed adjusted ratio-of-geometric-means analysis selected under a prospectively documented rule or amendment, and which effect measure and population should be treated as the intended length-of-stay result for extraction and interpretation?
