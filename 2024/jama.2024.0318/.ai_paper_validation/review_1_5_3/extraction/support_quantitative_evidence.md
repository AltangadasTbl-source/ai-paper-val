### Protocol design, population, outcome, and time keys

1. **Primary endpoint and analysis population.** PDF pp. 7, 16-18, 36: the primary endpoint is the between-group percent change in HbA1c from baseline; groups are surgery versus non-surgical medical/lifestyle treatment according to original randomization (ITT). All participants consented to the observational follow-up are the analysis population. Main-paper matching key: primary HbA1c change by randomized medical/lifestyle versus bariatric-surgery group.
2. **Follow-up horizon.** PDF pp. 7, 16-17: baseline-to-7-year comparison for all, and up to 13 years for earliest enrollees; p. 17 says data common across sites would be collected for five additional years. These are protocol-plan time windows, not a reported follow-up result. Main-paper key: annual results through 12 years.
3. **Population eligibility.** PDF pp. 7, 17, 20: adults with T2D, BMI 27-45 kg/m2, originally randomized at one of four sites, who initiated allocated treatment. Original criteria state age 20-65 years (p. 17); pregnancy test negative at screening/baseline where applicable. The protocol estimates approximately 302 eligible re-consenting participants; p. 16 states 302 randomized participants across four parent RCTs would receive seven additional years of follow-up. Main-paper matching key: pooled four-RCT randomized/treatment-initiating population.
4. **Primary and secondary outcome definitions.** PDF p. 18 and Table A1 (pp. 29-30): glycemic control = HbA1c <7.0% on or off diabetes medication; diabetes remission = HbA1c <6.5% off diabetes medication; partial remission = HbA1c <6.5% with or without medication; relapse = time from initial remission, defined in Table A1 as HbA1c >=6.5% and/or restarting diabetes medication. Continuous outcomes are percent change in body weight, BMI, UKPDS CHD risk score, quality of life and SBP/DBP/LDL/total cholesterol/urine microalbumin; micro/macrovascular events (stroke, retinopathy, nephropathy, foot ulcer) are binary; safety endpoints are time to first event. Main-paper matching keys: remission, HbA1c <7%, weight and clinical/laboratory outcomes.
5. **Measurement/schedule definitions.** PDF pp. 22-25: annual visits centered on original-RCT anniversary, preferred ±3 months and accepted up to ±6 months; interim contacts at 3, 6 and 9 months, preferred ±1 month and accepted to ±1.5 months. Height is measured at first annual visit only; weight, waist circumference and BP at each annual visit. The schedule lists fasting glucose/insulin, HbA1c, CBC, Chem 20, C-peptide, hsCRP, vitamin D, PTH, lipid profile, iron/ferritin/TIBC/B12 and microalbumin:creatinine ratio. Units/footnote: Chem 20 includes ALT/AST in IU/L and creatinine in mg/dL; urine measure is morning-void microalbumin:creatinine ratio.

### Statistical definitions, formulas, and planned sensitivity/subgroup analyses

6. **Primary mixed model A1.** PDF pp. 26-27: Y_ijt is HbA1c percent change for subject j at site i and visit t; X_ij=1 surgery and 0 non-surgical; Z is a four-dimensional site indicator; fixed effects include group, annual visit, group-by-visit and site; patient random effect c_ij is Gaussian N(0,sigma_c^2); residual vector is MVN(0,Sigma_e), whose form is chosen by AIC. beta_7 is the surgery-minus-nonsurgical mean percent-change difference at year 7. Primary null H0: beta_7=0, rejected at two-sided P<0.05. Main-paper key: primary ITT linear mixed-effects group comparison at year 7.
7. **Binary and relapse analyses A2.** PDF p. 27: logit(P_ijt)=alpha_t+beta_t X_ij+gamma^T Z+b_i+c_ij for longitudinal glycemic control; beta_7 is the log odds ratio at year 7 (surgery relative to non-surgical). Same approach planned for remission and partial remission. Relapse is descriptive Kaplan-Meier only; no group comparison because few expected non-surgical remissions/relapses.
8. **Primary-outcome power assumptions.** PDF pp. 27-29: observed/planning values: 1-year HbA1c percent changes -24.0% surgery and -9.1% non-surgical; three-year values -21.6% and -0.1%. Assumed year-5 surgery -20%, increasing linearly to -15% at year 7; non-surgical year-7 range -7% to +2%; observed crossover rate 0.8%. Of 355 originally randomized, 325 initiated treatment, 20 (6%) lost to follow-up and 3 (0.9%) died. At least 80% of available cohort 302 expected at year 7 (n approximately 242). Simulated variance components sigma_b=0.06, sigma_c=0.15, sigma_e=0.10, AR(1) rho=0.13; 500 simulations and two-sided alpha 0.05; Figure A1 reports power >90% except 30% missing.
9. **Secondary-outcome power assumptions.** PDF p. 28: remission rates at year 1 46% surgery/1% non-surgical, year 3 32%/0%; assumed year-7 20%/5%, 500 simulations, 94.6% power at 20% missing and two-sided alpha .05. Partial-remission assumed 27%/7%, power 98.6%. Glycemic-control observed 75%/52% at year 1, 63%/38% at year 3 and 48%/22% at year 5; assumed year 7 39%/11%, power >99% (about 98% at 40% missing). No relapse power calculation.
10. **Missing-data and crossover sensitivity definitions.** PDF pp. 30-31: mixed models presume missing at random. Planned MICE with Rubin's rule and pattern-mixture models (including death versus other dropout). ITT retains randomization assignment despite crossover. Planned MSM with stabilized IPTW, logistic treatment models based on treatment history and baseline covariates; final weight = IPTW × censoring weight. Per-protocol analysis discards outcomes after crossover. Main-paper key: ITT versus IPW per-protocol sensitivity analysis.
11. **Intervention-comparison models A3/A4 and tables.** PDF pp. 31-34: A3 replaces binary group with four categories (RYGB, LAGB, SG, non-surgical); uses least-square estimates per year and Bonferroni multiple-pairwise-comparison adjustment. Table A2 average first-year component doses (hours/year): CROSSROADS supervised/unsupervised/total exercise 176/196/372, nutrition 32, other lifestyle 26; SLIMM-T2D 12/222/234, 22, 22; STAMPEDE 0/183/183, 2, 2; TRIABETES 0/174/174, 28, 28. Table A3 surgery counts total n=192: RYGB 104 (50/20/19/15 by STAMPEDE/TRIABETES/SLIMM-T2D/CROSSROADS), SG 49 (all STAMPEDE), AGB 39 (21 TRIABETES/18 SLIMM-T2D). Table A4 RYGB N=104: pouch 15-20/20/15-20/40 mL; Roux limb 150/150/75/100-150 cm; BP limb 50/50/50/30-50 cm. A4 defines W_ijt as surgery dummies plus supervised/unsupervised exercise hours, nutrition-counselling hours, medication variables, provider and other lifestyle hours; beta_7,1 -100 beta_7,4 is the stated RYGB-alone versus 100 supervised-hours/year non-surgical contrast.
12. **Subgroup and Aim-2 power.** PDF pp. 34-35: all analyses are planned for baseline BMI <35 kg/m2. Table A5 at 80% power, two-sided alpha .05 and 20% missing: continuous-outcome detectable standardized difference Delta=0.47 at rho=.11 and .43 at rho=.8; binary OR=3.03 for 10% surgical event rate and OR=2.46 for 20%. Example residual SD=6% and autocorrelation about .7 yields detectable approximately 2.8% group difference; stated observed three-year weight difference about 20%.
13. **Aim-3 time-to-event and power definitions.** PDF pp. 35-36: remission/relapse predictors use univariate then adaptive-lasso multivariable Cox models (hazard-ratio multiplicative scale); proportional hazards assessed by Schoenfeld residuals; accelerated-failure-time sensitivity models use log time/additive covariate effects; site strata and randomized-group adjustment. A multi-state Markov model has initial, remission and death states. Overall preliminary remission about 23%; assuming 30%, 80% power to detect HR 1.36 per 1-SD predictor at two-sided alpha .05 and R2=.1. Table A6 minimum HRs: overall remission 20%: 1.46/1.50/1.67 at R2 .1/.2/.5; 30%: 1.36/1.39/1.52; 40%: 1.31/1.33/1.43.

### Measurement and ancillary-plan definitions

17. **SF-36.** PDF pp. 57-59: RAND SF-36, 36 questions, measures eight health concepts and can score eight-scale plus physical/mental health summaries; several questions use past four weeks, 3-level activity limitation, yes/no, 5-level and 6-level response scales. No score/result is printed.
18. **AUDIT-C.** PDF p. 60: 3 items, 0-12 score; five answers per item, a-e score 0-4; scores 0 indicate no alcohol use; positive screen >=4 for men and >=3 for women. No observed study screening result is printed.
19. **Ancillary economic endpoint.** PDF pp. 61-65: BMI 30-35 with T2D; state-transition/Markov microsimulation compares surgery with intensive lifestyle medical intervention. Primary endpoint ICER < $100,000 per QALY, third-party payer perspective, benefits in QALYs, probabilistic sensitivity analysis. Data restricted to BMI <=35; planned model-development timeline three months plus 1-2 months analysis. This is an ancillary plan, not a reported result and has no required main-paper outcome match.

19a. **Protocol background quantities (contextual prior-evidence and pooled-cohort keys).** PDF pp. 9-14: national estimates 29.1 million Americans/9.3% with diabetes and 34% obese; four RCTs had at least two years' follow-up, 35% BMI <35 kg/m2, 355 randomized and 302 then available. RYGB/LSG/LAGB stated to comprise at least 95% of US bariatric operations; approximately 70% of severely obese people with diabetes undergoing RYGB experience remission. Prior Cleveland retrospective cohort: n=217 at >=5 years; complete remission (HbA1c<6%, glucose<100 mg/dL) 24%, partial remission (HbA1c 6.0-6.4%, glucose 100-125) 26%, improvement 34%, recurrence after remission 19%. Prior RCT review: 11 RCTs, 794 total patients, trial sizes 38-150, follow-up six months to three years (one to five years); short-term mean weight difference -26 kg (95% CI -31 to -21; P<.001); complete-case remission RR 22.1 (95% CI 3.2-154.3; P=.002) and conservative RR 5.3 (1.8-15.8; P=.003). Look AHEAD cited 8.6% weight loss and HbA1c 7.3% to 6.6% at 12 months. Protocol states parent-trial medical arms at one year had 5-10% weight loss and HbA1c <=6.5% in about 16-35%. These are cited prior/contextual data, not the main paper's pooled outcomes; matching keys are baseline BMI, parent cohort, one-year parent results and long-term durability rationale.

### Participants, data collection, and statistical definitions

20. **Pooled population and parent-trial allocation.** PDF p. 3: eligible participants had been randomized in STAMPEDE, SLIMM-T2D, TRIABETES or CROSSROADS to RYGB/SG/AGB or medical/lifestyle and underwent allocated intervention. Parent criteria: T2D, BMI 27-45 kg/m2, age 18-65, anesthesia/exercise eligibility and baseline pregnancy test where applicable. Site allocation: Cleveland RYGB/SG versus medical; Joslin/BWH procedure preference then procedure-versus-medical randomization; Pittsburgh RYGB/AGB/medical; UW/Kaiser RYGB/medical. Main-paper matching keys: Figure 1 participant flow, pooled treatment groups, parent trials.
21. **Follow-up and retention.** PDF pp. 3-4: annual visit is centered on parent-RCT randomization anniversary within a 12-month window (±6 months); missed prior visit did not preclude annual follow-up. At pooled-study initiation, 305 of 316 originally randomized/intervention patients were available. Central labs changed from Northwest Lipid Research Laboratories through 05/2020 to University of Minnesota ARDL thereafter. Main-paper matching keys: annual visit/time horizon and participant flow.
22. **Primary/secondary model definitions.** PDF pp. 4-5: ITT. Primary HbA1c mixed model fixed effects group, visit, group×visit, site and baseline HbA1c; participant Gaussian zero-mean random intercept; LS means by group/visit and linear contrast at year 7 primary. Four-category secondary procedure analysis uses multiplicity adjustment. Continuous secondary outcomes use same approach and control site and baseline value. Binary remission/glycemic-control/diabetes-medication outcomes use GEE with group, visit, group×visit, site and baseline HbA1c; LS-estimate SEs use sandwich method. Secondary results are hypothesis-generating and not adjusted for multiple testing. Main-paper keys: primary analysis, secondary outcomes, statistical methods.
23. **ITT/PP estimand and IPW formula.** PDF p. 5: for change Y_t, ITT E(Y_t)=alpha_t+beta_t X+gamma^T Z, X=0 medical/lifestyle and 1 surgery, Z=site plus baseline HbA1c; beta_t=E(Y_t|X=1)-E(Y_t|X=0), years 1-12. PP uses time-varying X_t: for a crossover between s-1 and s, X_t=0 for t<s and 1 for t>=s; no surgery-to-medical crossovers. Stabilized weight w_t=product k=1 to t of P(X_k|X history)/P(X_k|X history,Y history,Z); numerator logistic model uses prior assignments, denominator adds site/HbA1c; R `ipw`, truncated at 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles; weighted mixed model and robust sandwich SE. Validity assumptions named: no unmeasured confounding, positivity and correct IPW models. Main-paper matching keys: per-protocol sensitivity / crossover analysis.
24. **Missing data and event tests.** PDF pp. 5-6: all participants have 7-year minimum and range 7-15 years; post-year-7 absence may reflect shorter planned follow-up. Missed-visit clinical/lab data recovered from electronic records where available. Death/loss are assumed random censoring/MAR; LMM/GEE account for MAR. Adverse events/outcomes summarized as percentages and compared with chi-square or Fisher exact tests. Main-paper keys: follow-up completeness and safety/event analysis.
