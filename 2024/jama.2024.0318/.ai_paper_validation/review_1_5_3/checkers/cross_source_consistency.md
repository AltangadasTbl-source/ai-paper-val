# Cross-Source Consistency Check

## Scope and method

- **Assigned direct sources checked:** DOC-001 main article, PDF pp. 1-11; DOC-002 protocol/SAP, PDF pp. 1-65; DOC-003 Supplement 2, PDF pp. 1-22; and DOC-004 data-sharing statement, PDF p. 1 (99 PDF pages total).
- **Evidence maps used as locators:** `extraction/main_quantitative_evidence.md` (51 numeric/reporting and 37 inferential relationship records) and `extraction/support_quantitative_evidence.md` (40 support relationship groups). Exact candidate comparators below were re-read in the direct PDF text/layout or source rendering; the maps were not treated as final authority.
- **Matching rule:** values were compared only after matching the randomized medical/lifestyle versus bariatric-surgery population, the stated time point, outcome, direction, model/estimand, measure/scale, and displayed precision. Protocol planning quantities, parent-trial context, sparse unlabelled figure coordinates, distinct within-group versus between-group tests, and different observed-data denominators were not treated as matched results.
- **Coverage result:** abstract, key points, results narrative, Tables 1-3, Figures 1-3 and captions/footnotes in DOC-001; all protocol/SAP definitions and quantitative plan/table units in DOC-002; all DOC-003 eMethods, eFigures, eTables, captions, and footnotes; and DOC-004 were checked for cross-document or internal cross-location quantitative matches. No supplied structured data file was present.
- **Candidate proposals:** 4 distinct proposals. These are observations for **Pending Human Adjudication** only. They have no C IDs and no AI disposition.

## Candidate proposal 1 — eTable 2 calls its results year 12 but its quantitative footnotes call them year 7

- **Category:** Measure, label, or scale inconsistency; cross-location time-label inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** [DOC-003 Supplement 2 — PDF p. 15](../../../joi240004supp2_prod_1721756962.82552.pdf#page=15) (eTable 2 title, columns, values, footnotes a-b); [DOC-003 Supplement 2 — PDF p. 16](../../../joi240004supp2_prod_1721756962.82552.pdf#page=16) (footnote c); [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 3](../../../jama_courcoulas_2024_oi_240004_1721756962.76052.pdf#page=3) (narrative explicitly identifies eTable 2 as the 12-year HbA1c result).
- **Printed comparators:** The table title is “Laboratory and clinical outcomes at year 12 and changes from baseline,” and both group columns are headed “Year 12.” Its HbA1c row prints Year-12 values of 7.9 (1.4) and 7.3 (1.5), with a difference in change of −1.1 (−1.7, −0.5). In contrast, footnote a says values are “presented for baseline and year-7 data”; footnote b defines the urine ratio as “year 7 data over baseline data” and binary odds ratios as “7-year over baseline”; and footnote c defines the numeric difference as surgery’s “7-year change” minus medical/lifestyle’s “7-year change” and the binary difference as the odds ratio “at year 7.” The main narrative states that the −1.1% (95% CI, −1.7% to −0.5%) result is “at 12 years (eTable 2 in Supplement 2).”
- **Comparison logic:** The title, column headings, displayed values, and main-text pointer identify the same table as a 12-year result. The table’s three footnotes instead repeatedly label the descriptive data, changes, and binary/numeric comparison definitions as year-7 quantities. These are mutually exclusive time labels for the same table outputs; no different analysis set, model, contrast, scale, or precision is identified.
- **Supported source-grounded alternatives:** The repeated year-7 references may be unrevised carryover wording from Table 2 rather than the intended time point. Alternatively, the year-12 heading could be wrong. The printed 12-year values and the main-text pointer support the former possibility, but the supplied sources do not resolve which labels were formally intended.
- **Human verification steps:** Inspect the statistical output/program specification for eTable 2 and the journal production proof. Confirm the intended time point for every eTable 2 column and revise all affected title/column/footnote labels consistently, if warranted.

## Candidate proposal 2 — Year-12 HbA1c comparison has identical estimate and interval but conflicting printed P values

- **Category:** Cross-document numeric inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 1](../../../jama_courcoulas_2024_oi_240004_1721756962.76052.pdf#page=1) (Abstract Results); [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 3](../../../jama_courcoulas_2024_oi_240004_1721756962.76052.pdf#page=3) (Results, citing eTable 2); [DOC-003 Supplement 2 — PDF p. 15](../../../joi240004supp2_prod_1721756962.82552.pdf#page=15) (eTable 2, HbA1c row).
- **Printed comparators:** The abstract and Results each print the year-12 between-group HbA1c-change difference as “−1.1% (95% CI, −1.7% to −0.5%; P = .002).” eTable 2 prints the same group difference in change, “−1.1 (−1.7, −0.5),” but prints “<.001” in its P-value column.
- **Comparison logic:** These locations identify the same randomized-group contrast, baseline-to-year-12 change, HbA1c percentage-point scale, direction (surgery minus medical/lifestyle), displayed estimate, and displayed 95% interval. The P-value displays are not the same at their printed precision: `.002` is greater than `.001`, whereas `<.001` is strictly less than `.001`. This is not a display-zero issue.
- **Supported source-grounded alternatives:** The two P values could arise from distinct test/variance/rounding pipelines, but neither location labels a different model, sidedness, adjustment, analysis population, or estimand. The supplied protocol/Supplement methods describe the same mixed-model/linear-contrast framework for the year-7 primary comparison and annual follow-up estimates, but do not identify a separate year-12 P-value convention that reconciles these displays.
- **Human verification steps:** Retrieve the exact year-12 model contrast and its degrees of freedom/variance method from the analysis output. Confirm whether `.002` or `<.001` belongs to the printed −1.1 (−1.7 to −0.5) contrast, or document a named analytic distinction and label it at both locations.

## Candidate proposal 3 — Abstract percentage for four deaths does not reconcile with the displayed group counts and denominators

- **Category:** Denominator, proportion, or total inconsistency; cross-document numeric inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 1](../../../jama_courcoulas_2024_oi_240004_1721756962.76052.pdf#page=1) (Abstract Results); [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 3](../../../jama_courcoulas_2024_oi_240004_1721756962.76052.pdf#page=3) (enrolled randomized groups: 96 medical/lifestyle and 166 surgery); [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 7](../../../jama_courcoulas_2024_oi_240004_1721756962.76052.pdf#page=7) (Results death narrative); [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 8](../../../jama_courcoulas_2024_oi_240004_1721756962.76052.pdf#page=8) (Table 3).
- **Printed comparators:** The abstract says, “There were 4 deaths (2.2%), 2 in each group.” The Results narrative says, “There were 4 deaths over 12 years: 2 in the medical/lifestyle group … and 2 in the bariatric surgery group.” Table 3, titled “Major Adverse Events and Events of Interest Through 12 Years,” prints death as 2 (2.1) for medical/lifestyle `(n = 96)` and 2 (1.2) for bariatric surgery `(n = 166)`.
- **Comparison logic:** The explicitly matched enrolled analysis groups total 262 (96 + 166), and the Table 3 death counts total four. Four divided by 262 is 1.53%, which rounds to 1.5% to one decimal place, not 2.2%. The separate Table 3 percentages also derive from the displayed counts and group denominators (2/96 = 2.1%; 2/166 = 1.2%). The abstract supplies no alternative denominator for its 2.2%.
- **Supported source-grounded alternatives:** The abstract percentage may have used a distinct, unstated risk set or may be a transcription/rounding error. The sources do not identify a denominator of approximately 182 that would yield 2.2% from four deaths, and the Results/Table 3 presentation supports 262 as the relevant long-term enrolled cohort.
- **Human verification steps:** Verify the death-event denominator in the analysis dataset and the abstract proof. If the denominator differs from 262, add its population definition and reconcile it with Table 3; otherwise verify the appropriate combined percentage and revise the abstract if needed.

## Candidate proposal 4 — Narrative describes the Table 2 glycemic threshold as ≤6.5%, while the referenced table labels it <6.5%

- **Category:** Measure, label, or scale inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 4](../../../jama_courcoulas_2024_oi_240004_1721756962.76052.pdf#page=4) (Results narrative); [DOC-001 main article — PDF p. 6](../../../jama_courcoulas_2024_oi_240004_1721756962.76052.pdf#page=6) (Table 2, HbA1c threshold row); [DOC-003 Supplement 2 — PDF p. 4](../../../joi240004supp2_prod_1721756962.82552.pdf#page=4) (supplementary analysis definition: remission HbA1c <6.5% without medication; used as supporting threshold-definition context).
- **Printed comparators:** The Results state, “Similar differences were observed using the threshold of HbA1c less than or equal to 6.5% (P = .002; Table 2).” Table 2’s matched year-7 row is labelled “HbA1c <6.5%, %” and prints medical/lifestyle 17.3%, bariatric surgery 37.7%, group OR 2.89 (95% CI, 1.48 to 5.64), `P = .002`.
- **Comparison logic:** The shared `P = .002`, immediate Table 2 citation, year-7 randomized-group comparison, and glycemic threshold identify the narrative and table as the same result. “Less than or equal to 6.5%” includes values exactly 6.5%, while “<6.5%” does not; the printed measure definitions therefore differ even though the estimate/P-value are matched.
- **Supported source-grounded alternatives:** The narrative’s “less than or equal to” may be imprecise prose, or the table label may omit an intended equality sign. The supplied source does not report how many observations equal exactly 6.5%, so it cannot establish whether the numerical estimate would change under the two cutoffs.
- **Human verification steps:** Check the outcome-program condition and the analysis data dictionary for the threshold used for the row with `P = .002`. Align the narrative, Table 2 label, and any protocol/SAP definition to that verified condition.

## Checked matched results with no candidate proposal

- The year-7 HbA1c estimate/interval/P, remission percentages, medication-use direction, year-7 and year-12 weight-loss values, BMI-subgroup values, crossover counts, and Table 3 group death counts matched across their like-for-like main-text, table, figure, and Supplement 2 occurrences after allowing for stated model-derived estimates versus raw observed counts.
- Protocol/SAP planned enrollment, power assumptions, parent-trial results, and ancillary endpoints were not matched to the later observed ARMMS-T2D outcomes because their populations, periods, or estimands differ.
- Repeated P values for longitudinal eFigures versus single-year Table 2 results were retained as different comparisons where the source labels an overall-over-time versus a year-specific contrast.
- No candidate was proposed for a small-P display convention. No `P = 0` or equivalent display-zero was used as a basis for a candidate.

## Limitations

- Some Supplement 2 figures provide plotted proportions or least-square trajectories without exact point labels. They were checked against only the exact values/denominators printed in their captions, tables, or matching narrative; no visual interpolation was treated as a numeric comparator.
- DOC-002 is a protocol/SAP and contains planning, historical, and parent-trial quantities. Those were used for definition/estimand matching only, not as competing values for later observed results unless a direct shared result was named.
- This artifact reports all qualifying discrepancies observed in the assigned cross-source scope. It does not determine correction, importance, validity, or any adjudication outcome.
