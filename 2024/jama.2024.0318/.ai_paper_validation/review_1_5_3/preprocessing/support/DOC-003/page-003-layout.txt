Methods Supplementary Appendix
Participants:
Eligible participants for ARMMS-T2D included those who were originally randomized in one of the four
original trials (STAMPEDE, SLIMM-T2D, TRIABETES or CROSSROADS) to bariatric surgery (Roux-
en-Y gastric bypass [RYGB], sleeve gastrectomy [SG] or adjustable gastric banding [AGB]) or
medical/lifestyle treatment and who underwent their allocated treatment intervention post-randomization.
Other inclusion criteria from the parent studies were: (a) diagnosis of T2D, (b) BMI 27-45 kg/m2, (c) age
18-65 years, (d) candidate for general anesthesia, (e) ability to perform exercise and other components of
the lifestyle intervention, and (f) negative urine pregnancy test at baseline (if applicable). T2D was
confirmed by requiring diabetes medication and/or having a diabetes diagnosis according to American
Diabetes Association criteria.1,2 Sex/gender data were collected by self-report with options of male,
female, other provided. Details of the original recruitment and randomization processes including the
methods of allocation concealment for each of the four original trials are available in these publications.3-
14
   None of the study teams were masked to group assignment.
All eligible participants were invited to continue long-term observation at their original site, and
voluntary informed written consent was obtained for participation in the pooled consortium study. The
study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Boards at each site. A diagram depicting the
flow of patients from the original trials into the ARMMS-T2D follow-up study is shown in Figure 1,
main paper.
Procedures:
Randomization occurred at each of the 4 sites independently and differed by site. At Cleveland Clinic,
participants were randomized to surgery (RYGB or SG) vs. medical/lifestyle therapy; at the Joslin
Diabetes Center/Brigham and Women’s Hospital participants chose which surgical procedure they
preferred and were then randomized to that procedure or to medical/lifestyle therapy; at the University of
Pittsburgh participants were randomized to RYGB, AGB, or medical/lifestyle therapy; and at University
of Washington/Kaiser Permanente Washington, to RYGB or medical/lifestyle. All surgical procedures
were performed laparoscopically. Those randomized to medical/lifestyle received individualized nutrition
counselling, instruction on exercise and self-monitoring of glucose, plus behavioral education and
counselling for at least 1 year after randomization. The protocols for delivery of the medical/lifestyle
intervention varied across sites and have been published with the original trials.3-14 After the first year, all
patients at University of Pittsburgh and Cleveland Clinic remained in a low level lifestyle intervention
including exercise and nutritional counseling monthly and participants at Joslin Diabetes Center/Brigham
and University of Washington/Kaiser Permanente Washington were encouraged to maintain participation
in supervised or unsupervised exercise, as well as to continue behavioral and nutritional counseling.
Data collection and oversight:
Annual study visits were scheduled within a 12-month window (±6 months) and centered at the
anniversary date of randomization in their original trial. Participants were eligible for an annual follow up
visit even if they had missed a prior visit. Blood and urine samples were collected and sent to the central
laboratory (Northwest Lipid Research Laboratories at University of Washington until 05/2020, thereafter
at Advanced Research and Diagnostic Laboratory at the University of Minnesota). Weight, waist
circumference, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, and the use of glucose-lowering and other
medications were obtained. Annual visits were scheduled at study centers. If in-person visits were not
