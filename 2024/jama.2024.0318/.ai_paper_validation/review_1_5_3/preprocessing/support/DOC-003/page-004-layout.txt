Data harmonization:
The primary outcome of the parent RCTs was based on change in HbA1c. Although the assays were
performed at different laboratories, the efforts and success of the National Glycohemoglobin
Standardization Program to standardize clinical laboratories against a universal calibrator and to reduce
assay variability enabled the use of these baseline measures across centers. For measurement of height
and weight, wall-mounted stadiometers and calibrated scales were used at each center. Blood pressure
measurements were all obtained in the seated position after 5 minutes of rest, using the same arm for each
participant.
Participant retention:
At the initiation of the pooled study, 305 of the 316 patients who were originally randomized and
underwent an intervention in one of the four original trials were available for follow-up (Figure 1, main
paper). Participant retention strategy plans were crafted by the coordinators specific to the study
populations in their specific geographical site. Specific strategies included telephone contact at 3-month
intervals, evening and/or weekend options for study-related visits, newsletters to update participants on
study progress, review of annual participant retention survey data, a study web site
(https://www.lerner.ccf.org/quantitative-health/armmst2d/about/), monetary renumeration/reimbursement,
and active outreach effort to maintain current contact information.


Statistical Supplementary Appendix
Statistical analysis:
Baseline participant characteristics were summarized as appropriate descriptive statistics for the
medical/lifestyle group, bariatric surgery group and each of the three surgical procedures. The analyses
followed the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle. The primary outcome of change in HbA1c was assessed
using a linear mixed-effect model, which included group (medical/lifestyle vs. surgery), visit, their
interaction, site, and baseline HbA1c as fixed effects. The models also included a random intercept at the
participant level. The random intercept was assumed to follow a Gaussian distribution with a zero mean.
Least-square means were derived at each visit by group, and the group comparison at each visit was
performed by applying appropriate linear contrast to the model. The group comparison at year 7 was the
primary comparison. A secondary analysis was conducted where the binary group assignment was
replaced by a 4-category variable representing the three surgical procedures and the medical/lifestyle
intervention, allowing pairwise comparisons among the four groups with multiplicity adjustment.

Secondary outcomes for longitudinal continuous variables such as the percent changes of weight and
cardiometabolic risk factors (e.g., blood pressure and lipid parameters) were analyzed in the same manner
as the primary outcome. Each model controlled for site and the baseline value as the covariates. For
longitudinal binary outcomes such as diabetes remission, glycemic control and the use of medications for
diabetes, the generalized estimating equation approach was used, where the models included group, visit,
their interaction, site and baseline HbA1c as covariates. Least-square (LS) estimates were derived by visits
