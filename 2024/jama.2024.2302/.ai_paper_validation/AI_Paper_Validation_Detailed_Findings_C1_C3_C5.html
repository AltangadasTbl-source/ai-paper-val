Finding C1

Abstract Misstates the Number of Infants Who Underwent Operative Repair

Error Type

Presentation inconsistency—Major. The abstract assigns the operative-repair label to the 320-infant postwithdrawal cohort, although the article documents 281 infants who actually underwent hernia repair.

Error Explanation

The abstract states that, among 338 randomized infants, “320 underwent operative repair.” The number 320 does not correspond to the reported operative-repair population. It is the number remaining after 9 infants in each randomized arm were withdrawn:

338 randomized − 9 early-arm withdrawals − 9 late-arm withdrawals = 320

The Surgery Characteristics section reports that 152 of 163 infants in the early-repair arm and 129 of 157 infants in the late-repair arm underwent hernia repair:

152 early repairs + 129 late repairs = 281 operative repairs

Figure 1 independently confirms the same total. In the early arm, 147 infants underwent repair before neonatal intensive care unit discharge and 5 underwent repair after discharge. In the late arm, 90 underwent repair after reaching 55 weeks’ postmenstrual age and 39 underwent repair before reaching 55 weeks.

(147 + 5) + (90 + 39) = 281 operative repairs

The difference is 39 infants. Figure 1 and the Surgery Characteristics text report 11 early-arm and 28 late-arm infants who did not undergo hernia repair:

320 postwithdrawal infants = 281 repaired + 39 not repaired
Study Population Early Arm Late Arm Total Meaning
Randomized population 172 166 338 All infants randomized to a timing strategy.
Postrandomization withdrawals 9 9 18 Withdrawn by parents or guardians after randomization.
Postwithdrawal cohort 163 157 320 The cohort mislabeled in the abstract as having undergone repair.
Did not undergo hernia repair 11 28 39 Infants explicitly reported as not receiving repair.
Operative-repair population 152 129 281 Infants who actually underwent hernia repair.
Primary-analysis population 159 149 308 Infants with complete primary-outcome data; this is a separate population from the operative-repair population.
Nature and limit of the finding. C1 identifies an incorrect population label in the abstract. It does not, by itself, show that the primary-outcome calculation or the randomized strategy comparison is invalid. The primary outcome is separately reported for 308 infants with complete data.

Finding C3

Figure 1 Omits Explicit Withdrawal and Analysis-Exclusion Branches

Error Type

Participant flow inconsistency—Minor. Figure 1 includes matching 9-infant categories in both randomized arms but does not explicitly identify them as postrandomization withdrawals excluded from the primary analysis.

Error Explanation

Figure 1 shows the following sequence in the early arm: 172 randomized, 163 “Received treatment as randomized,” 9 “Did not undergo treatment as randomized,” 4 lost to follow-up, and 159 included in the primary analysis. The late arm shows 166 randomized, 157 “Received treatment as randomized,” 9 “Did not undergo treatment as randomized,” 8 lost to follow-up, and 149 included in the primary analysis.

The Results text and Table 2 note a identify the two 9-infant categories as infants who were withdrawn after randomization and excluded from analysis. Figure 1 does not use the term “withdrawn,” does not state “excluded from primary analysis,” and does not draw a separate withdrawal/exclusion branch before the primary-analysis boxes.

Arm Randomized Withdrawn After Randomization Remaining After Withdrawal Lost to Follow-up Included in Primary Analysis
Early repair 172 9 172 − 9 = 163 4 163 − 4 = 159
Late repair 166 9 166 − 9 = 157 8 157 − 8 = 149
Total 338 18 320 12 308

If a reader follows only the explicitly labeled follow-up exclusions in Figure 1, the apparent calculations are 172 − 4 = 168 and 166 − 8 = 158. These do not produce the displayed analysis populations. The reader must infer an additional, unstated step: the two 9-infant categories were withdrawals excluded from analysis.

Why the terminology matters

“Did not undergo treatment as randomized” describes treatment receipt or adherence. “Withdrawn after randomization and excluded from analysis” describes study and analysis status. These concepts are not interchangeable. A participant may deviate from assigned treatment yet remain in the randomized analysis group. Therefore, the Figure 1 wording does not independently communicate the analysis exclusion documented elsewhere.

Nature and limit of the finding. C3 is a flow-label and branch omission, not an arithmetic error. The final analysis counts are reconcilable and agree with Table 2. The issue is Minor because the Results text and Table 2 note provide the missing status and because the omission does not, by itself, establish an incorrect outcome calculation.

Finding C5

Enrollment-Details Cross-Reference Incorrectly Includes Outcome-Analysis eTable 2

Error Type

Presentation inconsistency—cross-reference mismatch—Minor. The Patient Characteristics section directs readers seeking additional enrollment details to “eTables 1–2,” but only eTable 1 contains the relevant enrollment, eligibility, consent, and refusal information.

Error Explanation

The Patient Characteristics section discusses 613 parents or guardians who refused participation. It reports that 280 preferred early repair, 196 preferred late repair, 71 wanted the clinician to decide, and 66 had other reasons. It then states that “additional enrollment details appear in eTables 1-2.”

eTable 1 directly supports and expands this paragraph. Its title is “Additional information related to trial enrollment,” and it lists eligibility-related factors, reasons for refusal by parents or guardians, reasons for refusal by physicians, and other reasons eligible infants were not consented.

280 + 196 + 71 + 66 = 613 parent/guardian refusals

eTable 2 has a different purpose. Its title is “Frequentist primary and major secondary outcome analyses.” It reports serious-adverse-event results, hospital-day results, effect estimates, confidence intervals, P values, and statistical models. It does not provide the refusal breakdown referenced by the Patient Characteristics paragraph.

Information Sought by the Reader eTable 1 eTable 2
Additional trial-enrollment information Yes No
Reasons for parent/guardian refusal Yes No
Reasons for physician refusal Yes No
Other reasons eligible infants were not consented Yes No
Primary serious-adverse-event outcome analysis No Yes
Major secondary hospital-days analysis No Yes
Frequentist confidence intervals and P values No Yes

Figure 1 note b correctly directs additional enrollment information to eTable 1 alone. The Primary Outcome section correctly directs frequentist outcome-analysis readers to eTable 2. These two content-appropriate citations reinforce that the Patient Characteristics reference should identify eTable 1 rather than the range eTables 1–2.

Nature and limit of the finding. C5 is a cross-reference error, not a numerical or statistical-result error. The correct destination, eTable 1, is already included in the printed range, so readers can still locate the enrollment information. eTable 2 itself is not erroneous and is correctly cited elsewhere. These factors support Minor severity.
