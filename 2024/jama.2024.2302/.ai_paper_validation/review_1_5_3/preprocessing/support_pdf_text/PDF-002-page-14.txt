519    Interim Analyses and Early Stopping Rules: The primary statistician (Dr. Pedroza) will conduct a safety and
520   efficacy analysis in December 2019, when we expect to have 10-month outcome data for approximately 200
521   infants. Bayesian methods will be used to calculate the probability of harm using the pre specified outcomes of
522   death and prolonged hospital stay >30 days post surgery. We will also conduct an efficacy Bayesian interim
523   analysis to calculate the probability of benefit on the primary outcome, proportion of infants with an SAE, and
524   secondary outcome of total hospital length of stay. We will use neutral priors that a priori assume a 50-50
525   likelihood of either repair management being better. In contrast to frequentist analyses, Bayesian analyses
526   provide direct probabilities of benefit and may be more interpretable by clinicians. In deciding whether to stop
527   or continue the trial, we define the following stopping guidelines:
528        a. Stopping for safety: probability of increased harm >90% for either arm (for either death or prolonged
529           hospital stay)
530        b. Stopping for efficacy: probability of decreased SAEs >95% for either arm

535   Compliance and retention strategies. We will utilize other evidence-based strategies to increase adherence to
536   the trial protocol.102 A risk with the HIP trial is parental desire to request IH repair according to their preferences
537   rather than according to the study protocol. This is most likely to occur in infants assigned to late repair (i.e.
538   parents may want repair prior to 55-60 weeks PMA for a variety of reasons). Using clear and simple written
539   descriptions, we will develop a Parent Handbook with information about the HIP trial, including a list of names
540   and office numbers of the research team who may be contacted with questions and in case of emergencies.
541   The handbook will include important dates that will be personalized for each participant (written in at the site
542   level) regarding follow up appointments. We will include information regarding the importance of keeping the
543   research team updated with contact number changes. Sites will be encouraged to create materials that will
544   assist them in educating families involved in the HIP trial, e.g. parent handbook. The key to the success of
545   retention and follow-up will be compliance, attention to detail, consistency of the research team and trust
546   among the parents and research teams.

548     Every attempt will be made by the HIP Trial Study team to contact participating families regularly and keep
549   them from becoming lost to follow up. Identifying a stable contact person (grandparent, neighbor, friend) not
550   living with the enrolled infant but who will always know the family’s location will assist in tracking patients and
551   decrease the number lost to follow up. We will also encourage these individuals to participate in the parent
552   education visit prior to randomization in the trial. Drs. Tyson and Hintz have extensive experience in long-term
553   outcomes studies, including devising and implementing tracking strategies and assuring excellent follow-up
554   rates, and they will contribute to creating tracking and follow-up mechanisms.
