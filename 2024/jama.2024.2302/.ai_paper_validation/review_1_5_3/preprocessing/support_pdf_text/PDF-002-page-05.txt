208   Our proposal addresses what we regard to be a fundamental flaw in the current research agenda related to the
209   risk of anesthesia in infants -- the lack of surgeon involvement and leadership. Surgeons are key decision
210   makers regarding the necessity of interventions that require anesthesia and the timing of these procedures.
211   Early IH repair is likely to entail not only earlier exposure but also longer anesthesia times (due to increased
212   technical difficulty). The HIP trial offers a unique opportunity to evaluate what parents and providers care about
213   most, i.e. the combined, “real world” effects of surgery and anesthesia on the outcomes of vulnerable
214   premature infants, to be identified in a large pragmatic RCT that avoids the confounding that plagues
215   observational studies.

217   Background / Previous studies
218   Dr. Blakely previously performed a small pilot RCT addressing the timing of IH repair in premature infants.14
219   Early IH repair was compared to late repair at a goal PMA of 55 weeks; 39 of 50 eligible infants at 4
220   participating sites were randomized (78% randomization rate).

232   Methods
233   To test our hypotheses, multiple Participating Clinical Centers, including a Clinical Coordinating Center
234   (Vanderbilt), and a Data Coordinating Center (Vanderbilt) will conduct this multi-center, randomized clinical
235   trial. The acronym for the trial is the HIP trial (Hernia in Premies) and it has been registered with
236   Clinicaltrials.gov (NCT01678638).
