369    Study Procedures and Outcome Determination.
370    After treatment group assignment, each infant will receive care according to the practice at each site, in
371    keeping with a pragmatic trial design. Existing treatment practices that will be allowed include different
372    operative approaches (laparoscopic or open IH repair), anesthesia management (general or regional or other),
373    and other treatment considerations. All details will be documented for analysis. There are no prior studies
374    indicating an impact on the HIP trial outcome measures based on these differences in treatment.
375
376    Data will be collected from these broad temporal categories: demographics, perinatal, NICU course prior to
377    enrollment, surgical consult just prior to enrollment, status of infant at enrollment, information at NICU
378    discharge, intraoperative details (includes surgery and anesthesia-specific data), outpatient data and data at
379    the conclusion of the first phase of the trial (10 months after randomization). Ten months was selected as the
380    end of SAE and hospital days determination by assuming that the earliest PMA at NICU discharge would be at
381    35 weeks. An infant discharged at 35 weeks PMA who had very late IH repair at 65 weeks PMA would still
382    have a 1.5 - 2 month post-operative observation period. The aim is to have the same observation period for
383    infants in both treatment groups to decrease ascertainment bias. Infants in the early IH repair group will have
384    a longer observation period postoperatively, whereas those in the late IH repair group have a longer
385    observation period preoperatively. This study design represents clinical practice and will allow the detection of
386    SAE related to performing or delaying IH repair, which is our goal. After the first phase of the study (10 months
387    after enrollment), surveillance for SAEs will cease and the patient will then enter the “tracking phase” in
388    preparation for neurodevelopmental assessment at 22-26 months corrected age (chronological age - # weeks /
389    months premature).
390
391    Primary / secondary outcomes definitions.
392    Ascertainment of Primary Outcome (proportion of infants with >1 SAE in the 10 months after enrollment).
393    Specific definitions (based on standard definitions when available) are listed in the Appendix for all measured
394    SAEs. Trained coordinators will prospectively monitor all trial patients for SAEs from enrollment until 10
395    months afterwards. Measures to avoid bias and maximize reliability in assessing SAEs will include field testing
396    of these definitions, discussing definitions in conference calls with the coordinators to identify and resolve
397    problems in their assessment across different sites and settings, and assessing inter-coordinator reliability for a
398    random 10%. From the start of the IH repair operation until 24 hours post-operatively, the coordinators will
399    ascertain whether an SAE has occurred by briefly asking the clinical nurse, anesthesia provider, and surgeon if
400    they are aware of any SAE, in addition to medical record review. This face-to-face, very brief interaction (~5
401    minutes) has been shown to reliably track SAEs in prior studies.91 A primary outcome adjudication committee,
402    blinded to treatment group assignment, will review all case report forms during the trial and will make the final
403    assessment of SAE occurrence. Additionally, the committee will assign the highest level of harm for patients
404    that have had >1 SAE according to the National Coordinating Council for Medication Error Reporting and
405    Prevention (NCC MERP), as a secondary outcome.
