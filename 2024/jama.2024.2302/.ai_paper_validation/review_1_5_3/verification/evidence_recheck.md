- **Consistency rule applicable:** For a nonnegative integer event count `k`, `k > 1` means at least two events, whereas `k ≥ 1`, “at least 1,” and “any” include an infant with exactly one event. The labels differ precisely for `k = 1`.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `44 / 159 = 27.67%`, which displays as 28%, and `27 / 149 = 18.12%`, which displays as 18%. Thus the same displayed arm results sit under the two different threshold operators. Set comparison gives `{k: k > 1} = {2, 3, ...}` and `{k: k ≥ 1} = {1, 2, 3, ...}`; their difference is the exactly-one-SAE group.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The competing operators, matched arm counts, matched denominators, displayed percentages, and repeated “any”/“at least 1” wording are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not provide the number of infants with exactly one SAE, the final coded endpoint expression, the analysis dataset, the program that generated 44 and 27, or a document that explicitly declares which operator is the governing definition. Counts of individual SAE types cannot identify the number of unique infants with exactly one SAE because an infant may contribute more than one type.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The repeated “any,” “at least 1,” and `≥1` wording supports the possibility that `>1` was used as informal shorthand for more than zero. Whether any analyzed infant had exactly one SAE remains a missing input, not a source-grounded alternative.

## C003 — Bayesian intervention-prior ranges differ between the article and SAP

- **Location found:** [Main article — PDF p. 4](<../../../jama_blakely_2024_oi_240020_1710443209.74411.pdf#page=4>), Statistical Analysis; [Supplement 2 — PDF p. 8](<../../../joi240020supp2_prod_1710443209.75411.pdf#page=8>), section 7.3.1, Bayesian Prior Specifications.
- **Source value/text matched:** The article says that categorical and count outcomes used an intervention-effect prior centered at an odds ratio or relative risk of 1.0 with a 95% credible interval of 0.33 to 3.0.
- **Comparator matched:** The SAP gives the categorical intervention effect an odds-ratio prior centered at 1.0 with 95% credible interval 0.2 to 4 and specifies `Normal(0, SD=0.7)` on the log-odds-ratio scale. It gives the count intervention effect a relative-risk prior centered at 1.0 with 95% credible interval 0.33 to 3.3.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Prior descriptions for the same intervention-effect outcome class and scale must have matching parameters or be explicitly distinguished as different specifications. The printed interval endpoints are not identical.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** For categorical outcomes, the lower endpoints differ by `0.33 − 0.20 = 0.13` and the upper endpoints differ by `4.0 − 3.0 = 1.0`. For count outcomes, the lower endpoint matches at 0.33 while the upper endpoints differ by `3.3 − 3.0 = 0.3`. As a diagnostic of the SAP's stated log-scale parameters, `exp(−1.96 × 0.7) ≈ 0.253` and `exp(1.96 × 0.7) ≈ 3.945`; this shows that the SAP's textual 0.2-to-4 interval is itself approximate and does not make the article's common 0.33-to-3.0 range identical to the SAP's two class-specific descriptions.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The outcome classes, effect scales, centers, printed interval endpoints, and the SAP's categorical log-scale distribution are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not provide final model code, fitted-model prior summaries, a dated amendment, outcome-by-outcome prior assignments, or a statement that the article intentionally rounded or replaced the SAP specifications. The exact quantile convention and rounding rule behind each descriptive interval are not stated.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The supplied sources do not select between the article's common range and the SAP's class-specific ranges. Simplification, replacement, or amendment history is not supplied and remains a limitation.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** The centers, scales, and differing endpoints are direct observations; the endpoint arithmetic and log-normal calculation are reproduced diagnostics. Simplification, replacement, and amendment history are inferred explanations.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What prior distribution was actually passed to each final primary and major-secondary Bayesian model, and was the article's common 0.33-to-3.0 description an intentional final specification or a summary of the SAP's class-specific priors?
