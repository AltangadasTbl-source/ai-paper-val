eMethods
Analysis comparing explanatory capabilities of various quantitative ischemic injury estimates:

In order to provide a head-to-head comparison of explanatory capabilities of quantitative ischemic

injury estimates on CT Perfusion (using rCBF <30% threshold), Non-contrast CT (Manually

delineated CT hypodensity volume) and considering both images together (composite core

volume), information criteria and area-under-the-curve (for dichotomous functional independence

and independent ambulation) were reported for models including age, NIHSS-score at

presentation, time from last known well to randomization, and one imaging parameter (CT-

ASPECTS, CT-hypodensity volume, CT-perfusion/MR-diffusion core volume or composite core

volume) to compare model fit for functional outcomes. Lower information criteria values and

higher area-under-the-curve (AUC) values were considered better. By convention, Bayesian

information criterion (BIC) differences were categorized as 0-2 weak, 2-6 positive, 6-10 strong

and >10 very strong evidence of model superiority.1

Reference:

1.    Raftery AE. Bayesian model selection in social research. Sociol Methodol. Published

      online 1995:111-163.

eFigures
eFigure 1. Illustrative CT Perfusion imaging in a patient with occlusion of the M1 segment of
left middle cerebral artery.
The overall rCBF and Tmax maps are provided in the first series, whereas the second series
provides an illustration for maps using rCBF<30% and Tmax>6s thresholds. The region with
severely reduced relative cerebral blood flow (rCBF) <30% (pink region on the left) is estimated
to be irreversibly injured due to ischemia. The region with time to maximum intensity (Tmax) >6
s (green region on the right) represents delayed perfusion and thus brain area at the risk of
infarction in the absence of restored blood flow (reperfusion). The ratio of tissue volume with 1)
Tmax >6s and 2) rCBF <30% is termed the mismatch ratio. The volumetric difference between
the two regions is termed the mismatch volume and estimates the volume of ischemic penumbra
(brain tissue that is at risk of infarction but can be salvaged if blood flow to the area is rapidly
restored). For this illustrative case, the rCBF <30% volume was 94ml and Tmax >6s volume was
131ml. Thus, mismatch ratio was 1.4 and mismatch volume was 37 ml.

eFigure 18 Illustrative predicted probabilities of independent ambulation(mRS 0-3) and how
they change with increasing age and time from perfusion imaging acquisition to
reperfusion/end of the procedure with CTP/MRI core volume set at a) 70ml, b) 100ml and c)
150ml.
As age and time from perfusion imaging acquisition increases, the predicted probability of
independent ambulation decreases. This relationship is consistent across composite core volume
estimates, but overall predicted probability decreases as composite core volume estimate increases.
As age increases and time from imaging to reperfusion/end of the procedure lengthens, the
probability of independent ambulation decreases, such that it is very unlikely that a patient with
150mL core would achieve independent ambulation unless they are young and treated very rapidly.
