## C001 — ITT Figure 2 mRS distributions conflict with ITT eTable 2 threshold counts

- **Category:** Cross-document numeric inconsistency
- **Checker provenance:** Cross-source matched-result group 8; related numeric inventory relationships N015, N016, N069, N092, and N093.
- **Exact source locations:** [Main article Figure 2, PDF p. 7](<../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=7>); [Supplement 5 eTable 2, PDF p. 38](<../../joi240006supp5_prod_1708623115.01733.pdf#page=38>); population confirmation at [main Results, PDF p. 4](<../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=4>) and [Supplement 5 eFigure 3, PDF p. 8](<../../joi240006supp5_prod_1708623115.01733.pdf#page=8>).
- **Direct observation:** Figure 2 is titled as an intention-to-treat 90-day mRS distribution and its core-stratum denominators sum to EVT 117+51=168 and MM 119+49=168. eTable 2 is also explicitly intention-to-treat with EVT N=168 and MM N=168, but reports EVT mRS 0-2 as 34/168, mRS 0-3 as 65/168, and mRS 5-6 as 76/168; MM values are 12/168, 31/168, and 102/168.
- **Reproducible rule and calculation:** High-resolution legend/color inspection and nearest-whole-percentage feasibility, with each bar constrained to its printed denominator, uniquely reconstruct the mRS 0-6 category counts as EVT <100 mL `[2, 7, 21, 26, 17, 9, 35]`, EVT >=100 mL `[1, 3, 5, 10, 0, 5, 27]`, MM <100 mL `[0, 2, 9, 17, 27, 24, 40]`, and MM >=100 mL `[1, 0, 2, 8, 0, 8, 30]`. The Figure-derived thresholds are therefore EVT mRS 0-2 `39`, 0-3 `75`, and 5-6 `76`, versus eTable 2 `34`, `65`, and `76`; MM values are `14`, `39`, and `102`, versus `12`, `31`, and `102`. Thus mRS 5-6 and reconstructed mortality reconcile exactly, while mRS 0-2 and 0-3 do not under ordinary nearest-whole-percent rounding.
- **Observation versus inference:** The headings, denominators, percentages, and threshold counts are direct observations. It is an inference that at least one display, dataset label, denominator, or mRS segment is inconsistent; the package does not establish which production component caused the mismatch.
- **Source-grounded alternatives:** The figure and table may use different outcome derivations or versions despite identical ITT labels and arm totals; one or more Figure 2 percentages or segment labels may be wrong; eTable 2 may use an unstated outcome set; or an unstated non-nearest rounding, normalization, or weighting rule may invalidate the integer reconstruction.
- **Quality-control relevance:** If confirmed, an extractor could copy conflicting ITT functional-outcome counts or proportions from two supplied displays. This observation does not establish a change in the paper's overall conclusion.
- **Exact human question:** Do Figure 2 and eTable 2 use the same ITT population and 90-day mRS derivation; if so, which category counts or threshold totals should control, and if not, what source note distinguishes them?

## C002 — `aRR` is labeled as an absolute risk reduction but displayed and interpreted as a ratio

- **Category:** Measure, label, or scale inconsistency
- **Checker provenance:** Numeric provisional candidate; cross-source provisional candidate 2; statistical pass-1 candidate P1-S01; relationships N052, N056, N078, N110, N111, S022-S025, S028-S034, S036, S068-S069, and S071-S076.
- **Exact source locations:** [Main narrative, PDF p. 6](<../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=6>); [Main Table 2, PDF p. 7](<../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=7>); [Main Table 3, PDF p. 9](<../../jama_sarraj_2024_oi_240006_1708623114.96234.pdf#page=9>); [secondary-analysis SAP, PDF p. 4](<../../joi240006supp4_prod_1708623115.00733.pdf#page=4>); [trial SAP, PDF p. 17](<../../joi240006supp3_prod_1708623114.99733.pdf#page=17>); [Supplement 5 eTable 7, PDF p. 43](<../../joi240006supp5_prod_1708623115.01733.pdf#page=43>).
- **Direct observation:** The main narrative and Tables 2-3 expand `aRR` as “absolute risk reduction.” Table 2 says values greater than 1 indicate a higher rate ratio. Tables 2-3 print multiplicative estimates such as 0.89 (0.84-0.95), 1.05 (1.02-1.08), and 1.33 (0.52-3.44), while Table 3 separately reports `aRD` as an absolute risk difference. The SAPs specify RR estimates from modified Poisson regression with robust standard errors for these binary outcomes.
- **Reproducible rule:** An absolute risk reduction or risk difference is additive, has null value 0, and is expressed in risk units. A risk or rate ratio is multiplicative, dimensionless, and has null value 1. The printed values and explicit greater-than-1 interpretation are ratio-scale quantities and therefore do not reconcile with the printed expansion “absolute risk reduction.”
- **Observation versus inference:** The abbreviation expansions, model statements, values, and table interpretation are direct observations. It is an inference that the intended expansion may be “adjusted risk ratio” or “adjusted rate ratio”; this review does not prescribe the correction.
- **Source-grounded alternatives:** “Absolute risk reduction” may be a repeated abbreviation-key production error; “higher rate ratio” may instead be unintended; or a nonstandard internal term may have been intended but is not defined in the supplied package.
- **Quality-control relevance:** If confirmed, an extractor could assign a relative estimate to an absolute effect-measure class, changing the recorded scale and null value. This observation does not establish a change in the paper's conclusion.
- **Exact human question:** What expansion and effect-measure label was intended for `aRR`, and should the narrative, Tables 2-3, SAP-linked description, and supplement be standardized to distinguish it from `aRD`?
- **Workflow status:** Pending Human Adjudication
