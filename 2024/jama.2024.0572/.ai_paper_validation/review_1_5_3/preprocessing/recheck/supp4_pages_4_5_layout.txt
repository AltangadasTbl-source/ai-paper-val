•   Continuous variables will be included in regression models if no substantial deviation in linearity
    assumption is found. In cases with substantial deviation from linearity assumption, appropriate
    transformations of variables will be considered.
•   Predictive margins and marginal probabilities will be used to graphically illustrate how imaging
    characteristics affect the outcomes and thrombectomy treatment effect.
•   Patients with mRS scores of 6 (death) will be merged with patients who achieved scores of 5
    (bedridden with constant care required) to avoid counting a shift from mRS 6 to 5 as a treatment
    success, for analyses evaluating distribution of modified Rankin Scale scores.
•   Analyses will be conducted primarily using Stata and R statistical software.
•   Missing data will not be imputed.

5.2.         Outcomes definitions and analysis approach:
1) Distribution of modified Rankin Scale scores at 90-day follow-up:
       • Descriptive statistics including tabulation and stacked bar graphs may be used to illustrate
           the distribution of modified Rankin Scale scores across various categories of independent
           variable under consideration.
       • The association of a given imaging characteristics with distribution of modified Rankin
           Scale scores will be evaluated using probabilistic index model, adjusting for age, NIHSS at
           presentation and time from last known well to randomization and ischemic core volume
           as prognostic variables. Modified Rankin Scale scores of 5 and 6 will be merged to avoid
           counting a shift from mRS 6 to 5 as a treatment success, in consistency with the primary
           manuscript and analysis approach.
       • For subgroup analyses of one of these prognostic variables, that variable will be excluded
           from adjustment (e.g. analyses stratified by core volume will not adjust for core volume).
2) Proportion of patients achieving functional independence (mRS 0-2):
       • Descriptive statistics including tabulation and bar graphs may be used to illustrate the
           proportion of patients achieving functional independence across various categories of
           independent variable under consideration.
       • The association of a given imaging characteristics with functional independence (mRS 0-
           2) will be evaluated using a modified Poisson regression model with robust standard
           errors, adjusting for age, NIHSS at presentation, time from last known well to
           randomization and ischemic core volume as prognostic variables.
       • If the calibration of predicted probabilities is poor for the Poisson regression model and
           logistic regression produces better calibration, logistic regression will be used instead for
           the purposes of producing marginal probability plots.
       • For subgroup analyses of one of these prognostic variables, that variable will be excluded
           from adjustment (e.g. analyses stratified by core volume will not adjust for core volume)

3) Proportion of patients achieving independent ambulation (mRS 0-3):
      • Descriptive statistics including tabulation and bar graphs may will be used to illustrate the
           proportion of patients with independent ambulation across various categories of
           independent variable under consideration.
      • The association of a given imaging characteristics with independent ambulation (mRS 0-
           3) will be evaluated using a modified Poisson regression model with robust standard
               errors, adjusting for age, NIHSS at presentation, time from last known well to
               randomization and ischemic core volume as prognostic variables.
          • For subgroup analyses of one of these prognostic variables, that variable will be excluded
               from adjustment (e.g. analyses stratified by core volume will not adjust for core volume)
          • If the calibration of predicted probabilities is poor for the Poisson regression model and
               logistic regression produces better calibration, logistic regression will be used instead for
               the purposes of producing marginal probability plots.
          • In addition to the marginal predicted probabilities reported for other outcome measures,
               predicted probabilities of achieving independent ambulation based on the combination
               of age and time from last known well to randomization within the subgroup of patients
               who received EVT.
    4) Proportion of patients experiencing complete dependence or death (mRS 5-6):
          • Descriptive statistics including tabulation and bar graphs may will be used to illustrate the
               proportion of patients with independent ambulation across various categories of
               independent variable under consideration.
          • The association of a given imaging characteristics with death or severe disability (mRS 5-
               6) will be evaluated using a modified Poisson regression model with robust standard
               errors, adjusting for age, NIHSS at presentation, time from last known well to
               randomization and ischemic core volume as prognostic variables.
          • If the calibration of predicted probabilities is poor for the Poisson regression model and
               logistic regression produces better calibration, logistic regression will be used instead for
               the purposes of producing marginal probability plots.
          • For subgroup analyses of one of these prognostic variables, that variable will be excluded
               from adjustment (e.g. analyses stratified by core volume will not adjust for core volume)
    5) Proportion of patients experiencing symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage:
          • Symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage is defined as parenchymal hemorrhage type II or
               remote parenchymal hemorrhage on follow-up imaging associated with a 4-point or
               larger increase in NIHSS score at 4-hour follow-up window.
          • Due to very few events observed in the study, number & proportion of patients
               experiencing symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage will be reported but no further
               analysis or statistical testing or modelling will be performed.
