1. Introduction
The SELECT2 trial is an investigator-initiated, phase III, international, multicenter, prospective,
randomized, open-label, blinded-endpoint (PROBE) superiority study with adaptive enrichment two-
arm parallel design, with 1:1 assignment to thrombectomy plus medical management (EVT arm) vs
medical management only (MM arm). Randomization is conducted via a secure web-based interface.
The subjects are followed for 1 year after the procedure.
2. Primary study aim
The primary aim of the study is to evaluate thrombectomy efficacy and safety in patients with acute
ischemic stroke due to a large vessel occlusion in the anterior circulation (MCA M1 and ICA) and large
ischemic core on either non-contrast CT (Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score, ASPECTS: 3-5), CT or -
advanced perfusion imaging (relative cerebral blood flow, [rCBF<30% lesion volume 250cc)] on CTP or
diffusion-weighted MRI [(apparent diffusion co-efficient, ADC <620 x 10° mm7?/s lesion volume] on MRI:
250cc), treated within 0-24 hours from last known well.
3. Study Population
3.1 Inclusion criteria
1) Adults (18-85 years) with the final diagnosis of an acute ischemic stroke
2) NIHSS 26
3) Last known well to arterial puncture or medical management between 0 to 24 hours
4) Pre-stroke modified Rankin Scale score of 0-1
5) Eligible for thrombectomy or medical management
6) Signed Informed Consent obtained
7) Subject willing to comply with the protocol follow-up requirements
8) Anticipated life expectancy of at least 3 months
3.2 Exclusion criteria
1) Inability to undergo CT-Angiography and/or CT/MR Perfusion imaging (e.g., renal insufficiency,
iodine/contrast allergy)
2) Co-morbid psychiatric or medical illnesses that would confound the neurological assessments
3) Treatment with IV thrombolytic therapy beyond 4.5 hours from last known well
4) Treated with IV thrombolytic therapy 3-4.5 hours after last known well AND any of the following:
a. age >80
b. current anticoagulant use
c. history of both diabetes AND prior stroke
d. NIHSS >25
e. ischemic involvement of more than third of the MCA territory
5) Current participation in another investigational drug or device study.

4. Randomization
Randomization for the SELECT 2 trial takes place centrally using specifically designed randomization
module in the online web-based REDCap trial database management system with the implementation
of minimization with biased coin algorithm proposed by Pocock and Simon?. The randomization module
is dynamic and ensures that the stratification of subgroups across the treatment arms is well-balanced,
by balancing the treatment assignment along the marginal distribution of each stratification factors.
The dynamic randomization system measures the imbalance of various stratification factors using
predetermined weights at the onset of each randomization and assigns the treatment probability in
such a way, that the group with the smallest imbalance receives the largest probability of assignment
in the biased-coin randomization. Further details about the stratification factors and weights are
provided in Appendix A. The randomization procedure was implemented UTH Biomedical Informatics
group (UTH BIG) at University of Texas Health Science Center at Houston and maintained by Data
management core at Center for Clinical and Translational Sciences at UT McGovern Medical School.

All responsible investigators receive training in the systems for data collection and data entry and
training in Good Clinical Practice (GCP). All investigators responsible for patient assessment participate
in training in critical assessment items (NIHSS, mRS).

The treatment phase of the study is conducted in an open-label manner. The assessment of functional
outcomes is obtained by assessors blinded to the treatment arm and imaging parameters. Only the Data
and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) have access to interim data and results. The DSMB Co-Chairs are
Dr. Lawrence Wechsler (University of Pennsylvania, US) and Dr. Stephen Davis (University of Melbourne,
AU). Statisticians not involved in the DSMB will work on dummy datasets until the computer codes for
statistical analysis are validated and will only have access to full data after the study enrollment, follow-
up and data collection is completed and the dataset is locked.
7. Study design and sample size considerations
7.1 Adaptive enrichment design
The study implemented a novel adaptive enrichment design that not only allowed for the trial to
potentially stop early for efficacy, but also allowed for selection and evaluation of the treatment effect
in the most promising subpopulation if the futility rule was triggered at the interim (or final) analyses in
the overall population. This adaptive design has been developed, well-studied, and implemented in the
DEFUSE 3 trial, which assessed thrombectomy efficacy and safety in patients presenting within 6-16
hours from last known well. The details and theory of this adaptive enrichment design are summarized
before. This adaptive enrichment design is an extension of standard group sequential design with an
enrichment feature and is designed to test for efficacy or futility with two interim analyses and a final
analysis**. This adaptive design was chosen because the preliminary data from the SELECT study
suggested potential heterogeneity in thrombectomy treatment effect based on the different imaging
profiles. The aim of this adaptive design is to reallocate future accrual and study treatment effects only
in the most promising subpopulation if there was evidence that EVT was futile in the overall population
at interim (or final) analyses. By limiting the subsequent enrollment only in the most promising
subpopulation, a larger number of patients would eventually be enrolled in this subgroup for efficacy
testing compared to traditional designs.
This design uses the Generalized Likelihood Ratio (GLR) statistic (Kullback-Leibler criterion) as criteria to
identify the subgroup, which has the best chance of showing a benefit from EVT. Through these criteria,
it can achieve optimal balance simultaneously between the estimated size of the treatment effect and
the sample size of the subgroup. By considering the CT ASPECTS score, CTP core volume and treatment

7.2 Interim and final analyses
The study design incorporated two interim analyses after enrollment and completion of 90-day follow-
up of 200 and 380 patients to assess the efficacy of thrombectomy vs medical management in patients
with large core on one or both imaging modalities using primary outcome — distribution of mRS score
at 90-day follow-up. At each analysis, an efficacy boundary was set to decide if the study should be
stopped for efficacy while controlling the overall (one-sided) Type | error rate at 2.5%. At each interim
analysis, a futility boundary was set to decide if the study should continue recruitment in the overall
group, shift accrual and testing to a subgroup, or stop the trial entirely. If the futility boundary was
crossed and the optimal subgroup was to be selected, the maximum analyzed sample size would not
have been fixed but a random variable (smaller than the fixed maximum sample size 560). Due to the
reduction in the maximum number of patients available for analysis at the end of the study, our design
allowed for an easier futility stop after subgroup selection. The details regarding efficacy and futility
boundaries is provided in Appendix B.
At first interim analysis (enrollment and completion of 90-day follow-up for first 200 patients), the null
hypothesis is tested in the study cohort:
1. If neither efficacy nor futility boundary is crossed, the trial continues enrollment to the next
interim/final analysis.
2. If the efficacy boundary is crossed, the trial stops and efficacy is declared in the study cohort.
3. If the futility boundary is crossed, the optimal subgroup is selected based on the GLR statistic
and the null hypothesis is tested in this selected subgroup:
a. If neither boundary is crossed, the trial will continue with enrollment limited to the
selected subgroup

7.5 DSMB review after enrollment and completion of 90-day follow-up of 300
patients
First interim analysis after enrollment and study completion of 200 patients was conducted on February
2nd, 2022. The data safety monitoring board reviewed the results and recommended continuation of
the study.
Following the external signal constituted by the presentation and publication of the results of RESCUE
JAPAN Limit®, the data safety monitoring board requested to review the efficacy and safety outcomes
after enrollment and completion of 90-day follow-up of 300 patients. As the original study design
included pre-specified interim analyses and specified efficacy and futility boundaries at 200 and 380
patients as detailed in Appendix B, the SELECT2 steering committee met to discuss how to address the
DSMB request. At the meeting, the decision was made to provide the DSMB with the requested data
but without making any alteration to the study design or incorporating any additional interim analyses.
The DSMB met on September 9*", 2022 and reviewed the efficacy and safety data for first 300 patients
who completed the study. They also reviewed available outcomes (30d and 90d follow-up) for all
enrolled patients until September 1, 2022. Based on the review, using pre-specified boundaries for
interim analyses and after examination of the data from 300 pts completing 90-day follow-up, the DSMB
recommended that the SELECT 2 trial should immediately stop any further enrollment as the boundary
for efficacy has been crossed in favor of EVT.
Based on this recommendation, SELECT 2 steering committee stopped further recruitment of
participants and rejected null hypothesis of no difference in treatment effect between study arms at
the family-wise Type | error rate of 0.05.

e Discharge location

10. Statistical analysis
10.1 Analysis principles and general considerations

e The analyses are based on the total sample of 352 participants recruited prior to the study
stopping as described in Section 7.5.

e All outcomes and analyses are prospectively categorized as primary, secondary, tertiary or
exploratory.

e No formal adjustments will be undertaken to constrain the overall type | error associated with
the secondary or tertiary outcomes and exploratory analyses. Their purpose is to supplement
evidence from the primary analysis to fully characterize the treatment effect. Results from the
secondary and tertiary analyses will be interpreted in this context. All confidence intervals will
be reported at 95% level of confidence.

e Analysis for the primary outcome, and all secondary efficacy and safety outcomes analyses will
be conducted on an intention-to-treat (ITT) basis so that all patients will be analyzed in the group
to which they were randomized irrespective of whether they received the allocated treatment.
The primary analysis strategy based on ITT is defined as follows:

o Is based on an ITT design that aims to collect all outcome data on all randomized
subjects;

o Includes a main analysis that keeps subjects in their randomized groups, analyzes all
available outcome data, and is valid under a named plausible assumption about the
missing data;

o Includes tipping point analysis’ (that consider a range of alternative values for missing
outcomes data to evaluate the extreme distribution of missing data for primary outcome
where the results change) as an alternative to missing at random assumption

o Allindividuals are included in sensitivity analyses

e Patients with mRS scores of 6 (death) will be merged with patients who achieved scores of 5
(bedridden with constant care required) to avoid counting a shift from mRS 6 to 5 as a treatment
success® 10,

e Key analyses will be repeated in the subpopulations including 1) As-treated population and 2)
per-protocol population. Further details regarding these subpopulations are provided in section
11.

e Subgroup analyses will be carried out, irrespective of whether there is a significant treatment
effect on the outcome. Their purpose is to supplement evidence from the primary analysis to
help to fully characterize the treatment effect. Results from subgroup analyses will be

10.2.3.1.3 Treatment of missing values
All efforts are put forth to ensure complete follow-up. If the primary outcome (mRS at 90 days) cannot
be assessed in the clinic, it will instead be obtained by phone using a structured interview. Based on
prior stroke trials data, expected missingness in primary outcome is very low. Nevertheless, a small
amount of missing data may be inevitable. All randomized subjects are to be included in the primary
ITT outcome analysis so there is a need for a consistent ITT-based strategy of dealing with any missing
primary outcome values.
Following clinical input from, and discussions with, the SELECT2 Steering Committee, the primary
outcome is assumed to be missing-at-random (MAR), i.e. the missingness of the data may depend on
the observed data, but not on the missing data. Important explanatory and auxiliary variables are
collected and will be examined to assess the plausibility of the MAR assumption. We will perform
multiple imputations for missing data where data will be imputed 30 times by selecting random values
from a distribution of potential values based on strata (values) of all observed explanatory and auxiliary
variables. Specifically, we will build an ordinal logistic regression model including explanatory and
auxiliary variables to impute modified Rankin Scale scores for missing observations. Estimates will be
pooled across the imputed datasets in accordance with Rubin’s rule®.
Tipping point analysis’ (that consider a range of alternative values for missing outcomes data to
evaluate the extreme distribution of missing data for primary outcome where the primary results
change) will be performed as an alternative analysis not relying on missing at random assumption.
10.2.3.1.4 Analysis method

For primary outcome (distribution of 90-day mRS score), the treatment effect will be expressed as

e¢ The Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney measure of superiority (WMW), with its 95% confidence
interval and p-value, merging mRS categories 5 and 6 to avoid transition from mRS 6 to 5
counting as treatment success as they are assigned same utility scores % 1°.

For comparability with other studies, the following transformations of the WMW will also be reported:

e The average risk difference (ARD), defined as the probability that a randomly selected subject
from the endovascular group has a better functional outcome than a randomly selected
subject from the control group, with its 95% confidence interval, where ARD = 2(WMW — 0.5).

e The average number needed to treat for benefit (NNT), with its 95% confidence interval,
where NNT = 1/ ARD.

e The Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney Generalized Odds with its 95% confidence interval, merging
mRS categories 5 and 6 to avoid transition from mRS 6 to 5 counting as treatment success as
they are assigned same utility scores * 1°.
