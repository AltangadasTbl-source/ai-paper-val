10.2.3.1.3 Treatment of missing values
All efforts are put forth to ensure complete follow-up. If the primary outcome (mRS at 90 days) cannot
be assessed in the clinic, it will instead be obtained by phone using a structured interview. Based on
prior stroke trials data, expected missingness in primary outcome is very low. Nevertheless, a small
amount of missing data may be inevitable. All randomized subjects are to be included in the primary
ITT outcome analysis so there is a need for a consistent ITT-based strategy of dealing with any missing
primary outcome values.
Following clinical input from, and discussions with, the SELECT2 Steering Committee, the primary
outcome is assumed to be missing-at-random (MAR), i.e. the missingness of the data may depend on
the observed data, but not on the missing data. Important explanatory and auxiliary variables are
collected and will be examined to assess the plausibility of the MAR assumption. We will perform
multiple imputations for missing data where data will be imputed 30 times by selecting random values
from a distribution of potential values based on strata (values) of all observed explanatory and auxiliary
variables. Specifically, we will build an ordinal logistic regression model including explanatory and
auxiliary variables to impute modified Rankin Scale scores for missing observations. Estimates will be
pooled across the imputed datasets in accordance with Rubin’s rule®.
Tipping point analysis’ (that consider a range of alternative values for missing outcomes data to
evaluate the extreme distribution of missing data for primary outcome where the primary results
change) will be performed as an alternative analysis not relying on missing at random assumption.
10.2.3.1.4 Analysis method

For primary outcome (distribution of 90-day mRS score), the treatment effect will be expressed as

e¢ The Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney measure of superiority (WMW), with its 95% confidence
interval and p-value, merging mRS categories 5 and 6 to avoid transition from mRS 6 to 5
counting as treatment success as they are assigned same utility scores % 1°.

For comparability with other studies, the following transformations of the WMW will also be reported:

e The average risk difference (ARD), defined as the probability that a randomly selected subject
from the endovascular group has a better functional outcome than a randomly selected
subject from the control group, with its 95% confidence interval, where ARD = 2(WMW — 0.5).

e The average number needed to treat for benefit (NNT), with its 95% confidence interval,
where NNT = 1/ ARD.

e The Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney Generalized Odds with its 95% confidence interval, merging
mRS categories 5 and 6 to avoid transition from mRS 6 to 5 counting as treatment success as
they are assigned same utility scores * 1°.
