10. Statistical analysis
10.1 Analysis principles and general considerations

e The analyses are based on the total sample of 352 participants recruited prior to the study
stopping as described in Section 7.5.

e All outcomes and analyses are prospectively categorized as primary, secondary, tertiary or
exploratory.

e No formal adjustments will be undertaken to constrain the overall type | error associated with
the secondary or tertiary outcomes and exploratory analyses. Their purpose is to supplement
evidence from the primary analysis to fully characterize the treatment effect. Results from the
secondary and tertiary analyses will be interpreted in this context. All confidence intervals will
be reported at 95% level of confidence.

e Analysis for the primary outcome, and all secondary efficacy and safety outcomes analyses will
be conducted on an intention-to-treat (ITT) basis so that all patients will be analyzed in the group
to which they were randomized irrespective of whether they received the allocated treatment.
The primary analysis strategy based on ITT is defined as follows:

o Is based on an ITT design that aims to collect all outcome data on all randomized
subjects;

o Includes a main analysis that keeps subjects in their randomized groups, analyzes all
available outcome data, and is valid under a named plausible assumption about the
missing data;

o Includes tipping point analysis’ (that consider a range of alternative values for missing
outcomes data to evaluate the extreme distribution of missing data for primary outcome
where the results change) as an alternative to missing at random assumption

o Allindividuals are included in sensitivity analyses

e Patients with mRS scores of 6 (death) will be merged with patients who achieved scores of 5
(bedridden with constant care required) to avoid counting a shift from mRS 6 to 5 as a treatment
success® 10,

per-protocol population. Further details regarding these subpopulations are provided in section
11.
