6. Blinding
The treatment phase of the study is conducted in an open-label manner. The assessment of functional
outcomes is obtained by assessors blinded to the treatment arm and imaging parameters. Only the Data
and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) have access to interim data and results. The DSMB Co-Chairs are
Dr. Lawrence Wechsler (University of Pennsylvania, US) and Dr. Stephen Davis (University of Melbourne,
AU). Statisticians not involved in the DSMB will work on dummy datasets until the computer codes for
statistical analysis are validated and will only have access to full data after the study enrollment, follow-
up and data collection is completed and the dataset is locked.
7. Study design and sample size considerations
7.1 Adaptive enrichment design
The study implemented a novel adaptive enrichment design that not only allowed for the trial to
potentially stop early for efficacy, but also allowed for selection and evaluation of the treatment effect
in the most promising subpopulation if the futility rule was triggered at the interim (or final) analyses in
the overall population. This adaptive design has been developed, well-studied, and implemented in the
DEFUSE 3 trial, which assessed thrombectomy efficacy and safety in patients presenting within 6-16
hours from last known well. The details and theory of this adaptive enrichment design are summarized
before. This adaptive enrichment design is an extension of standard group sequential design with an
enrichment feature and is designed to test for efficacy or futility with two interim analyses and a final
analysis**. This adaptive design was chosen because the preliminary data from the SELECT study
suggested potential heterogeneity in thrombectomy treatment effect based on the different imaging
profiles. The aim of this adaptive design is to reallocate future accrual and study treatment effects only
in the most promising subpopulation if there was evidence that EVT was futile in the overall population
at interim (or final) analyses. By limiting the subsequent enrollment only in the most promising
subpopulation, a larger number of patients would eventually be enrolled in this subgroup for efficacy
testing compared to traditional designs.
