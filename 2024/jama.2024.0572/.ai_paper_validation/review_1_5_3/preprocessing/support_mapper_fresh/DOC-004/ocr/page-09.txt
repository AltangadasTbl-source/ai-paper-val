7.2 Interim and final analyses
The study design incorporated two interim analyses after enrollment and completion of 90-day follow-
up of 200 and 380 patients to assess the efficacy of thrombectomy vs medical management in patients
with large core on one or both imaging modalities using primary outcome — distribution of mRS score
at 90-day follow-up. At each analysis, an efficacy boundary was set to decide if the study should be
stopped for efficacy while controlling the overall (one-sided) Type | error rate at 2.5%. At each interim
analysis, a futility boundary was set to decide if the study should continue recruitment in the overall
group, shift accrual and testing to a subgroup, or stop the trial entirely. If the futility boundary was
crossed and the optimal subgroup was to be selected, the maximum analyzed sample size would not
have been fixed but a random variable (smaller than the fixed maximum sample size 560). Due to the
reduction in the maximum number of patients available for analysis at the end of the study, our design
allowed for an easier futility stop after subgroup selection. The details regarding efficacy and futility
boundaries is provided in Appendix B.
At first interim analysis (enrollment and completion of 90-day follow-up for first 200 patients), the null
hypothesis is tested in the study cohort:
1. If neither efficacy nor futility boundary is crossed, the trial continues enrollment to the next
interim/final analysis.
2. If the efficacy boundary is crossed, the trial stops and efficacy is declared in the study cohort.
3. If the futility boundary is crossed, the optimal subgroup is selected based on the GLR statistic
and the null hypothesis is tested in this selected subgroup:
a. If neither boundary is crossed, the trial will continue with enrollment limited to the
