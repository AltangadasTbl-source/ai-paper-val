## Extracted quantitative and statistical relationships

### A. Study identity, population, arms, and matching keys

1. **Protocol version and main-paper key — pages 1, 3, 7-12, 18-32.** This is the original BASIS protocol, V2.0 (June 25, 2018), for a multicenter, prospective, randomised, open-label, blinded-endpoint (PROBE) trial comparing balloon angioplasty plus aggressive medical management (AMM) with AMM alone. Match later sources only when the version, analysis population, endpoint definition, and time origin are aligned.
2. **Population definition — pages 7 and 18-21.** Adults 35-80 years, recent qualifying TIA `<90 days` or ischemic stroke `21-90 days` before enrollment in synopsis (p. 7) and `14-90 days` in the body eligibility text (p. 21), attributable to `70-99%` major intracranial-artery atherosclerotic stenosis. The same body section specifies terminal ICA C4-C7, MCA M1, VA V4, or BA; lesion length `<=10 mm`, diameter `>=1.5 mm`, normal distal artery, and WASID-method measurement. These are population/measurement keys, not observed participant results.
3. **Allocation — pages 18 and 24.** Experimental: balloon angioplasty plus AMM. Control: AMM alone. Central IWRS randomisation, stratified by study center, `1:1` allocation. This is the randomisation-level/matched-result key for arm-specific numbers.
4. **Operator/site qualification — page 18.** Each site transfers video for `3-5` angioplasty cases to the core laboratory; annual balloon-angioplasty volume is `>50` cases per neurointerventionalist. These are protocol implementation criteria, not trial outcomes.

### B. Endpoint definitions, outcome labels, and time windows

5. **Primary endpoint, synopsis — page 8.** “Stroke or death” within `30 days` after enrollment, or any ischemic stroke or qualifying-artery revascularisation after `30 days` through `12 months` after enrollment. This is an endpoint-definition comparator key for any main-paper composite endpoint count/risk/HR.
6. **Primary endpoint, body — pages 18-20.** Page 18 describes ischemic/hemorrhagic stroke or all-cause death within `30 days` after enrollment or after qualifying-lesion angioplasty during follow-up, or qualifying-artery ischemic stroke/revascularisation beyond `30 days` through `12 months`; page 19 gives “stroke or death” within `30 days` after enrollment, then ischemic stroke/revascularisation beyond `30 days` to `12 months`. Page 20 specifies symptomatic brain hemorrhage counts in the primary endpoint only if it occurs within `30 days` after enrollment or within `30 days` after angioplasty during follow-up. Preserve the stated source/time-origin wording when matching later protocol versions or results.
7. **Primary composite components — pages 19-20.** Ischemic stroke requires sudden new focal neurologic deficit confirmed on CT/MRI. Acute revascularisation requires acute qualifying-artery occlusion with deficit and specified rescue treatment. Elective revascularisation is symptom-driven and requires qualifying ischemic stroke or recurrent TIA lasting `>10 minutes`/new disabling neurologic symptoms. These definitions set the numerator identity for composite-event reporting.
8. **Secondary endpoints — pages 8 and 20-21.** (i) target-territory any stroke or all-cause death by `90 days`; (ii) outside-target-territory any stroke by `90 days`; (iii) mRS at `90 days`; (iv) target-territory any stroke or death by `12 months`; (v) outside-territory any stroke by `12 months`; (vi) qualifying-artery revascularisation by `12 months`; (vii) mRS at `12 months`; (viii) target-artery restenosis by `12 months`; (ix) combined stroke, myocardial infarction, and vascular death by `12 months`; (x) EQ-5D quality-of-life assessment by `12 months`.
9. **Restenosis label/scale — pages 8 and 21.** Restenosis is target-artery stenosis `>70%` or an increase of `30%`, based on follow-up neurovascular imaging. This is a definition key; it must not be conflated with residual stenosis after the index procedure.
10. **Outcome-scale labels — pages 8, 13-16, 20-21, 31-32.** mRS is modified Rankin Scale; EQ-5D is EuroQol five dimensions/quality-of-life questionnaire; NIHSS and MoCA are separately scheduled measures. The schedule records mRS at baseline and follow-up, NIHSS at baseline/discharge/90 days/1 year, and EQ-5D/MoCA at baseline and later annual/follow-up visits as marked. These are outcome-measure/time-point matching keys, not analysed values.

### C. Sample-size and interim-analysis specification

11. **Distribution/test/model — page 11.** Time to stroke/death is assumed exponential. The null hypothesis uses a two-sided log-rank test; sample-size software is SAS `9.3` or EAST `6.0`; bilateral `alpha = 0.05`. No hazard ratio, accrual duration, censoring distribution, or explicit power notation beyond the printed “90% confidence” wording is supplied in this segment.
12. **Control and experimental risk assumptions — page 11.** Cited SAMMPRIS 12-month target-vessel stroke/death in medical management: `12.2%`; assumed primary endpoint after balloon angioplasty: `6.25%`; subsequently stated sample risk rates: experimental `6.45%`, control `13.01%`. These are design assumptions, not BASIS observed event risks.
13. **Contamination/mixing formula — page 11.** Assumption: `5%` of control participants need balloon dilatation after `3 months`. Printed experimental-group balloon-dilation risk: `λmcb = 0.25λm + 0.75λb = 8.09%`; printed adjusted control-group risk: `λm* = 0.05λmcb + 0.95λm = 12.76%`. Retain symbols and displayed arithmetic verbatim for later formula/assumption checks; this shard does not infer unprinted parameter definitions beyond the prose.
14. **Planned events, N, effect target — pages 11-12.** Initial planned sample: `686` cases, `91` total endpoint events, a stated `90% confidence`, `50%` relative risk reduction target, and stated `6%` total type-I-error expansion from Lan-DeMets loss function. Mid-term-analysis calculation increases N to `728`; with `10%` attrition and the randomisation ratio, final planned N is `802`. These are cross-document keys for reported enrollment, analysis N, interim analysis, or SAP sample-size reporting.
15. **Interim/feasibility observation — pages 12 and 19.** First `100` participants complete a peri-operative (`30 days`) observation; purpose is primarily adverse-event collection and feasibility/continuation discussion. This is not presented as an efficacy result or formal decision threshold.

### D. Eligibility, baseline, procedure, and treatment thresholds

16. **Eligibility and exclusion thresholds — pages 9-11 and 21-23.** Exclusions include surgery within `30 days` or planned major/`>=3-grade` surgery within `90 days`; thrombolysis/neurologic worsening within `24 hours`; acute ischemic stroke onset within `21 days`; target-parent-artery stenosis `>50%`; contralateral/extracranial examples with stenosis `>70%`; baseline mRS `>=3`; intracranial hemorrhage within `3 months`; MI within `6 weeks`; expected survival `<1 year`; hemoglobin `<100 g/L`; platelet count `<100 x 10^9/L`; INR `>1.5`; ALT/AST `>3-fold` upper limit of normal; and creatinine `>2-fold` upper limit. These are population-definition exclusions, not observed baseline values.
17. **DSA/technical success labels — pages 25 and 27.** DSA records residual-vessel diameter at maximal stenosis and maximum stenosis length. Technical success is residual target-vessel stenosis `<=50%`; page 27 adds mTICI grade `3` and no flow-impairing arterial dissection. Do not conflate this procedure success criterion with the `>70%`/`+30%` follow-up restenosis outcome definition.
18. **Rescue-stent and procedural thresholds — pages 26-27.** Poor collateral is ASITN/SIR `<3`; balloon length covers the lesion and at least `2 mm` beyond each end; balloon diameter should be no more than `70%` of proximal-artery diameter; nominal-pressure inflation lasts `10-30 seconds` and may repeat `2-3` times. Rescue stenting is allowed if residual stenosis `>=70%` or unstable antegrade flow/mTICI `<2b`, flow-impairing dissection/mTICI `<2b`, or thrombosis/embolisation jeopardises distal perfusion; final DSA after `10-15 minutes` observation. These are procedure definitions/thresholds, not result rates.
19. **AMM regimen — pages 7-8, 19, 25, and 27.** Both arms receive aspirin `100 mg` daily through follow-up and clopidogrel `75 mg` daily for first `90 days` after enrollment; atorvastatin `20-80 mg/day` for `1 year` (dose target LDL `<1.8 mmol/L` or `<70 mg/dL`). In the angioplasty peri-procedural section, aspirin is `100 mg/day` for the entire follow-up and clopidogrel `75 mg/day` or ticagrelor `90 mg twice daily` for `90 days` after randomisation. Resistance/alternative treatment is tied to CYP2C19 loss-of-function or ADP platelet aggregation `>40%`. The differing time-origin wording (after enrollment vs after randomisation) is retained as a treatment-definition comparator key.
20. **Risk-factor thresholds — pages 7-8, 19, 28-29.** Synopsis/early design state BP target `130-140/90-100 mmHg`; detailed management sets BP `<=140/90 mmHg` without diabetes and `<=130/80 mmHg` with diabetes. LDL-c target `<1.8 mmol/L` or `<70 mg/dL`; non-HDL target `<100 mg/dL`; if non-HDL `>=100 mg/dL` or triglycerides `>=200 mg/dL` after larger statin dose, additional lipid-lowering treatment is recommended; HbA1c target `<7.0%`. These are target labels/thresholds, not observed trial measurements.
21. **Measurement timing and adherence fields — pages 28-29.** BP is checked at screening, randomisation, discharge, `30 days`, `90 days`, `180 days`, `1 year`, and end of trial. Fasting plasma glucose and HbA1c: baseline, `30 days`, `90 days`, and `1 year`. Lifestyle fields include BMI/weight, smoking status, and activity. Moderate exercise recommendation: `30 minutes/day`, `3 times/week`. Tablet counts assess clopidogrel/atorvastatin adherence. These are data-collection timing/variable keys.
22. **Drug-supply and named-dose entries — pages 29-30.** Clopidogrel is supplied for `3-5 days` during screening and after `90 days` of enrollment; atorvastatin may be supplied from enrollment to `1 year`. The protocol lists example antihypertensive doses: chlorthione `25 mg`; lisinopril `10` or `40 mg`; candesartan `16` or `32 mg`; atenolol `50` or `100 mg`; felodipine `5` or `10 mg`; clonidine `0.1 mg`; hydralazine `50 mg`. These are regimen details, not exposure results.

### E. Schedule, follow-up, and analysis-time labels

23. **Schedule of assessments table — pages 15-16.** Table columns are baseline (`15 days to 0 days`), angiogram (`0 days`), discharge, `30 days +/-7 days`, `90 days +/-7 days`, telephone interview `6 months +/-14 days`, `1 year +/-30 days`, and follow-up from 1 to 3 years at `6-month` intervals. The table marks AE/SAE/endpoints at baseline, angiogram, discharge, 30 days, 90 days, 6 months, 1 year, and later 6-monthly visits; footnote 8 says after one year follow-up focuses on AE, SAE, and endpoint events. This is the source timing framework for event-accounting and follow-up denominators.
24. **Schedule table definitions — pages 15-16.** Vital signs are BP, heart rate, respiration rate, body temperature. Blood laboratory testing includes routine/biochemistry/hepatic/renal testing. Coagulation tests are PT, APTT, TT, FIB, INR. HbA1c is optional but recommended for diabetes; platelet aggregation/CYPC219, MoCA, HR-VWI, and TCD are center-optional. CTP is optional but recommended for hemodynamic compromise, poor collateral, or perforator stroke. Lifestyle review records cigarettes/day, BMI/weight, weekly exercise count, and duration per exercise.
25. **Narrative follow-up — pages 30-32.** Follow-up continues to primary endpoint or study end and seeks complete endpoint information if a participant discontinues but agrees to provide it. Planned visits: baseline, DSA day, discharge, `30 +/-7 days`, `90 +/-7 days`, `6 months +/-14 days`, `1 year +/-30 days`, up to `3 years` at `6-month` intervals after year 1. Telephone follow-up at 6 months/post-year-1; other visits in person. Neurovascular imaging (DSA, CTA, or MRA) is required at one-year follow-up. These are censoring/follow-up and outcome-ascertainment keys, not achieved follow-up results.
26. **Baseline/treatment visit fields — pages 31-32.** Visit 1 includes demographic/history, vital signs, routine labs, optional fasting glucose/HbA1c, DSA/MRI or CT/CTP where indicated, mRS/NIHSS/MoCA/EQ-5D, optional platelet/CYP2C19 and TCD testing, AE/SAE/endpoint observation, and smoking/weight/exercise fields. Visit 2 treatment follows randomisation assignment; successfully randomised participants receive `30 days` follow-up research drug, while randomisation failures return screening-period research drug. These are collection/operational definitions, not outcome counts.
