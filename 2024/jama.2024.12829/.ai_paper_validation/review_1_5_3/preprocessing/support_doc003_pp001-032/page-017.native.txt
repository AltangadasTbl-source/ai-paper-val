4. Background

180

Intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis (ICAS) is a major etiology of stroke worldwide, which is

181

associated with a high rate of stroke recurrence, substantial morbidity, and mortality, accounting for

182

up to 50% of ischemic stroke in south and east Asia.1 Despite receiving treatment with aspirin and

183

standard medical management of vascular risk factors, patients with symptomatic ICAS (sICAS)

184

still had as high as 23% stroke recurrence rate at 1 year.2,3 Therefore, optimal secondary stroke

185

prevention and reducing mortality risk in patients with sICAS is a major global health problem.

186

Currently, treatment options for sICAS include aggressive medical management (AMM), balloon

187

angioplasty, and stenting (balloon angioplasty combined with stenting); however, the optimal

188

treatment for sICAS remains unclear.

189

The SAMMPRIS and VISSIT trials showed high perioperative complication rates and no

190

significant advantage of intracranial stenting compared to aggressive medical therapy (AMM) in the

191

long term. 4, 5 The CASSISS trial reported that stenting by experienced operators didn’t add

192

additional risks of stroke or death within 30 days compared to AMM for sICAS, although it failed

193

to demonstrate a benefit of stenting.6 As lower rates of short-term stroke or mortality

194

(peri-procedural or mean follow-up ≤ 3 months) were found in balloon angioplasty compared to

195

intracranial stenting,7 balloon angioplasty alone may be a feasible alternative to stenting for sICAS

196

treatment. Moreover, several meta-analyses also proved the safety and efficacy of balloon

197

angioplasty for sICAS, which suggested that balloon angioplasty alone may be a potentially

198

promising treatment for sICAS.8-10

199

To date, limited evidence is available from RCTs to determine the efficacy and safety of balloon

200

angioplasty for sICAS patients. Hence, we designed the BASIS (Balloon Angioplasty for

201

Symptomatic Intracranial Artery Stenosis) randomized controlled trial to explore whether balloon

202

angioplasty plus AMM is superior to AMM alone in patients with sICAS.
