1162   4. Background
1163   Intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis (ICAS) is a major etiology of stroke worldwide, which is

1164   associated with stroke recurrence, substantial morbidity, and mortality, accounting for up to 50%

1165   of ischemic strokes in south and east Asia.1 Despite receiving treatment with aspirin and

1166   standard medical management of vascular risk factors, patients with symptomatic ICAS (sICAS)

1167   still have as high as 23% 1-year stroke recurrence rate. 2,3 Therefore, secondary prevention of

1168   stroke and reducing mortality in patients with sICAS is a global major issue. Currently,

1169   treatment options for sICAS include aggressive medical management (AMM), balloon

1170   angioplasty, and stenting (balloon angioplasty combined with stenting); however, the optimal

1171   treatment for sICAS remains unclear.

1172      The SAMMPRIS and VISSIT trials have shown high perioperative complication rates and

1173   no significant advantage of endovascular stents compared to aggressive medical therapy (AMM)

1174   in the long term. 4, 5 However, the CASSISS trial reported that stenting by experienced operators

1175   didn’t add additional risk of stroke or death within 30 days compared to AMM for sICAS,

1176   although it failed to demonstrate a benefit of stenting.6 As lower rates of short-term stroke or

1177   mortality (peri-procedural or mean follow-up ≤ 3 months) were found in balloon angioplasty

1178   than stenting,7 balloon angioplasty alone may be a feasible alternative treatment to stenting for

1179   sICAS treatment. Moreover, several previous meta-analyses also proved the safety and efficacy

1180   of balloon angioplasty for sICAS, which suggested that balloon angioplasty alone may be a

1181   potentially promising treatment for sICAS.8-10

1182      To date, limited evidence is available from RCTs to determine the efficacy and safety of

1183   balloon angioplasty for sICAS patients. Hence, we designed the BASIS (Balloon Angioplasty

                                                        75
       BASIS                                                                         Number:2017YFC1307900



1184   for Symptomatic Intracranial Artery Stenosis) randomized controlled trial to explore whether

1185   balloon angioplasty plus AMM is superior to AMM alone in patients with sICAS.

1187   5. Objectives and Hypothesis
1188   5.1 Objectives

1189   To investigate whether balloon angioplasty combined with AMM is superior to AMM alone for

1190   patients with high-risk sICAS in the primary endpoint (ischemic/haemorrhagic stroke or

1191   all-cause death within 30 days after enrollment or after balloon angioplasty procedure of the

1192   qualifying lesion during follow-up, or any ischemic stroke or revascularization from the

1193   qualifying artery beyond 30 days through 12 months after enrollment).

1194   5.2 Hypothesis

1195   Compared to AMM alone, balloon angioplasty combined with AMM could decrease the risk of

1196   the primary endpoint by 50% in patients with high-risk sICAS over the 12-month follow-up

1197   period.

1396   12. Balloon Angioplasty
1397   Antithrombotic protocol

1398   Pre-procedure: All patients who are scheduled to undergo balloon angioplasty should receive

1399   dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin 100 mg per day and clopidogrel 75 mg per day) for at least

1400   three days before the procedure.

1401   Intra-procedure: Intravenous anticoagulant such as heparin during the procedure.

                                                        85
       BASIS                                                                        Number:2017YFC1307900



1402   Post-procedure: Aspirin 100 mg per day must be used for the entire duration of follow-up, and

1403   clopidogrel 75 mg per day or ticagrelor 90mg twice a day must be used for 90 days after

1404   randomization. After 90 days, whether to continue to take it will be assessed according to the

1405   patient's condition and the researcher's opinion. Atorvastatin will be taken 20mg-80mg/day for

1406   one year after enrollment; Whether to continue taking atorvastatin after one year will be

1407   assessed according to the patient's condition.

1408   Anesthesia strategy and arterial access

1409   The procedure should be performed under general anesthesia. Femoral artery access is

1410   recommended (radial artery access is allowed for patients with tortuosity of the aortic arch, or

1411   aorta).

1412   Procedure steps

1413   8.   A stable vascular access with a long sheath or guiding catheter (an intermediate catheter is

1414        recommended for participants with tortuous access), which should be placed as distal as

1415        possible, is suggested to provide adequate support for performing the balloon angioplasty.

1416        Collateral status assessment based on a whole brain DSA is recommended before balloon

1417        angioplasty according to the ASITN/SIR collateral flow grading system (poor collateral:

1418        ASITN/SIR < 3). 13

1419   9.   An 0.014-inch microwire with a microcatheter (or not) passes through the lesion to reach

1420        the distal branch of the target artery.

1421   10. A balloon (recommending the Neuro RX and Neuro LPS Intracranial Balloon Dilation

1422        Catheter [Sinomed Inc., Tianjin, China]) is navigated over the microwire to the lesion of the

1423        target artery.

                                                        86
       BASIS                                                                        Number:2017YFC1307900



1424       Balloon size selection: the balloon length should cover the lesion completely and cover at

1425       least 2 mm beyond each end of the lesion. The balloon diameter is determined according to

1426       the physician’s opinion (submaximal angioplasty is suggested: balloon with a diameter no

1427       more than 70% of the proximal artery diameter).

1428   11. The balloon should be slowly inflated to the nominal pressure, and maintained for 10-30

1429       seconds before deflating slowly. This step can be repeated 2-3 times if the dilation effect of

1430       balloon angioplasty is not satisfactory. For patients with extremely severe stenosis, a

1431       relatively smaller balloon can be used for pre-dilation.

1432   12. Technical success: residual stenosis ≤ 50% of the proximal artery diameter with modified

1433       Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction (mTICI)14 of 3 grade without arterial dissection that

1434       impairs the distal blood flow.

1435   13. Rescue stent implantation is allowed when: (1) residual stenosis ≥ 70% after balloon

1436       dilation or the antegrade flow is unstable (mTICI < 2b); (2) arterial dissection impairing the

1437       distal blood flow (mTICI < 2b); (3) development of thrombosis or embolization that

1438       jeopardizes distal perfusion. Other measures such as intravenous/intra-arterial thrombolysis,

1439       infusion of GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors, are based on the physician's experience, which can be

1440       used alone or in combination with rescue stenting.

1441   14. A final DSA should be performed after 10–15 minutes of observation.
