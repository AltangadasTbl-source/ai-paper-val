Subject: Guidance on Flag Adjudication and Temporal Analysis

Dear Dr. Lin,

I would appreciate your guidance on three issues arising from our review of the AI validation results.

1. Protocol-related discrepancies. Shiyin noted that differences between protocols and submitted manuscripts or supplements may reflect necessary changes during trial conduct. Should these flags therefore be excluded? My view is that, if we position agentic AI as a general tool for detecting reporting issues, such discrepancies remain useful candidates for human review. We could distinguish justified changes from reporting problems rather than exclude protocol-related flags as a group. To characterize the tool's capabilities, I propose focusing primarily on descriptive and exploratory analyses of the types of issues it identifies.

2. The secondary temporal comparison. We previously planned to compare pre- and post-pandemic flag counts to explore whether the wider availability of AI tools might be associated with improved reporting. However, protocol-related discrepancies may reflect practical trial constraints rather than reporting quality, potentially confounding the interpretation of temporal differences. For this analysis, I suggest focusing on statistical reporting errors and inconsistencies within the submitted manuscript and non-protocol supplements. This narrower scope may reduce that concern.

3. Proposed analysis. Applying the scope described above, preliminary screening leaves 287 of the original 412 flags after excluding protocol-versus-submitted-document and protocol-versus-protocol issues, before any rebuttal or human adjudication. To examine differences across years, I propose constructing count outcomes from this subset, including internal reporting inconsistencies, statistical reporting errors, and discrepancies in within-document totals. We could conduct an exploratory analysis using a hierarchical Bayesian count model or a GLM with standard errors clustered by paper, depending on how the observations are structured.

Would you consider this distinction between the tool's broader review scope and the narrower outcomes for temporal analysis appropriate? I would also welcome your advice on the proposed modeling approach and the bootstrap options for inference that we previously discussed.
