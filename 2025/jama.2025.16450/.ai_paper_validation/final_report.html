C-03 — Table 1 and Figure 2 differ by one control participant for baseline FIO2

  • Evidence status: Verified; retained by the critic.
  • Category: Presentation inconsistency.
  • Severity: Minor.
  • Exact source locations: jama_ambalavanan_2025_oi_250072_1761000786.6838.pdf, PDF p. 6 / journal p. 1457, Table 1 continuation, “FIO2 at baseline” and “High FIO2 (≥0.5) at baseline,” plus footnote b; and PDF p. 9 / journal p. 1460, Figure 2, subgroup “Baseline FIO2,” plus footnote c.
  • Direct source observations: Table 1 reports baseline FIO2 for Budesonide + Surfactant as [n=228], with high FIO2 86/228 (37.7%), and for Surfactant Alone as [n=230], with high FIO2 81/230 (35.2%). Figure 2 displays favorable-outcome fractions with subgroup denominators of 142 and 86 for Budesonide + Surfactant and 150 and 81 for Surfactant Alone at the <0.50 and ≥0.50 thresholds, respectively.

Reasoning procedure

  1. Match the variable, time point, and threshold. Table 1 footnote b describes baseline respiratory metrics as the last available data collected before study-drug initiation. Figure 2 footnote c describes baseline FIO2 as the last known level before treatment initiation. Both displays use the threshold 0.50 and concern the pretreatment baseline measurement in the intention-to-treat population. No visible note establishes a different baseline variable or subgroup eligibility rule between the two displays.

  2. Reconcile the Budesonide + Surfactant arm. Table 1 provides total n=228 and high-FIO2 count 86, so the complementary below-0.50 count is:

    228 − 86 = 142.

    Figure 2 displays 142 below 0.50 and 86 at or above 0.50:

    142 + 86 = 228.

    The arm therefore reconciles exactly between Table 1 and Figure 2.

  3. Reconcile the Surfactant Alone arm. Table 1 provides total n=230 and high-FIO2 count 81, so its complementary below-0.50 count is:

    230 − 81 = 149.

    Figure 2 instead displays 150 below 0.50 and 81 at or above 0.50:

    150 + 81 = 231.

    The figure therefore contains one more Surfactant Alone participant than Table 1 for the same displayed partition: 231 − 230 = 1.

  4. Perform the overall missingness cross-check recorded by the verifier. Figure 2 footnote c states that 182 of the 641 intention-to-treat participants lack baseline respiratory data. That implies:

    641 − 182 = 459 participants with baseline FIO2 data.

    Figure 2 totals:

    142 + 86 + 150 + 81 = 459.

    Table 1 totals:

    228 + 230 = 458.

    Thus, the figure's four subgroup denominators agree with its stated overall exclusion count, while Table 1 is one participant lower overall. This diagnostic check locates the presentation discrepancy; it does not establish which individual arm cell is correct.

  5. Locate the discrepancy. Because the Budesonide + Surfactant partition reconciles and the high-FIO2 Surfactant Alone count is 81 in both displays, the visible difference is between Table 1's implied below-0.50 count of 149 and Figure 2's displayed below-0.50 denominator of 150.

  6. Separate observation from derivation. All table totals, fractions, figure denominators, threshold labels, and footnote statements are direct source observations. Complements, arm sums, total sums, and the one-participant difference are derived calculations.

Existing supported conclusion: Table 1 and Figure 2 present Surfactant Alone baseline-FIO2 totals of 230 and 231, respectively, for the same pretreatment variable and threshold partition. No visible note explains the one-participant difference.

Limit on interpretation: The aggregate article does not establish whether Table 1 should contain 231, Figure 2 should contain 230, the below-0.50 subgroup should be 149 or 150, or a distinct analysis flag explains the difference. The discrepancy does not by itself show that the subgroup relative risks, the primary result, or any clinical conclusion changed.

Human verification instruction: Compare Table 1 and footnote b on PDF p. 6 with both baseline-FIO2 rows and footnote c in Figure 2 on PDF p. 9. Reconcile the participant-level baseline-FIO2 availability and subgroup flags in the analysis input. C-04 — eTable 4 incorrectly defines RR as risk difference

  • Evidence status: Verified; retained by the critic.
  • Category: Presentation inconsistency.
  • Severity: Minor.
  • Exact source locations: joi250072supp4_prod_1761000786.6988.pdf, PDF p. 7, eTable 4 estimate header and RR-labelled binary rows; PDF p. 8, abbreviation line and analysis note. Corroborating internal convention: jama_ambalavanan_2025_oi_250072_1761000786.6838.pdf, PDF p. 8 / journal p. 1459, Table 3 abbreviation line and methods footnote a.

Reported statements

The relevant wording is directly visible:

  1. eTable 4's estimate header reads: “Relative Risk (RR) or Mean Difference (MD) (95% CI).”
  2. Binary results are prefixed RR, including “Death before 120 days PNA,” reported as RR: 1.09 (0.76, 1.57).
  3. The eTable 4 analysis note states: “Binary outcomes report relative risks, estimated by robust Poisson regression, unless otherwise noted.”
  4. The eTable 4 abbreviation line nevertheless states: RR = risk difference.
  5. Main-article Table 3 separately defines RD as risk difference and RR as relative risk; its methods footnote likewise states that binary outcomes report RDs and RRs and that RRs were estimated by robust Poisson regression.

Reasoning procedure

  1. Identify the terms being reconciled. The comparison is terminological: the same abbreviation RR is used in the eTable 4 header, estimate labels, methods note, and abbreviation line.

  2. Establish the table's own meaning without external knowledge. The header explicitly pairs the words “Relative Risk” with (RR). The methods note explicitly says that binary outcomes report relative risks. The row prefix RR: follows that table-defined usage.

  3. Check internal journal convention. Main Table 3 distinguishes RD, risk difference from RR, relative risk. That direct internal definition confirms the same convention used in eTable 4's header and methods.

  4. Locate the isolated conflict. Only the supplement's p. 8 abbreviation line expands RR as “risk difference.” That expansion conflicts with all other cited source statements.

  5. Separate observation from conclusion. The quoted header, row, methods note, abbreviation line, and main-table definitions are direct observations. The conclusion that one expansion is internally inconsistent follows by direct term matching; no numerical or model assumption is required.

Existing supported conclusion: The eTable 4 abbreviation line incorrectly expands RR as “risk difference”; within the same table and the main article, RR is defined and used as “relative risk,” while RD denotes “risk difference.”

Limit on interpretation: This finding establishes a labeling conflict. It does not independently validate the fitted models or every displayed estimate, and it does not show that the numeric RR values were calculated as risk differences. The supplied text instead describes robust Poisson estimation of relative risks.

Human verification instruction: On supplement PDF p. 7, compare the estimate header and an RR-labelled binary row with the abbreviation and binary-outcome methods lines on p. 8; then compare the main-article Table 3 abbreviation line on main PDF p. 8. If the source production file matches the supplied display, change the eTable 4 abbreviation to RR = relative risk while leaving RD = risk difference where applicable.

C-05 — Proposed equality of GDB-status deaths and cumulative 120-day deaths

  • Evidence status: Rejected by the evidence verifier; not retained by the critic.
  • Proposed category: Arithmetic inconsistency.
  • Severity: Not applicable under the critic decision.
  • Exact source locations: joi250072supp4_prod_1761000786.6988.pdf, PDF p. 2, eTable 1, SAF “GDB status” block; PDF pp. 7–8, eTable 4, “Death before 120 days PNA” and notes; PDF pp. 15–16, eTable 8, title, death rows, and notes.

Reported values and statements

  • eTable 1's SAF column displays a four-category GDB-status distribution: discharged home 337, still in hospital at 120 days 189, transferred to another facility 16, and death 86. The visible counts total 628, although the SAF header is 635.
  • eTable 4 reports “Death before 120 days PNA” as 50/321 (15.6%) for Budesonide + Surfactant and 44/313 (14.1%) for Surfactant Alone.
  • eTable 4 states that clinical outcomes are assessed through GDB status at 120 days while in hospital, or at prior death, transfer, or discharge, and that death composites consider in-hospital deaths by 120 days PNA.
  • eTable 8 is titled “In-hospital Deaths by 120 Days' Postnatal Age, Safety Population” and repeats 50/321 and 44/313. Its note states that the table reports all-cause, in-hospital deaths through 120 days PNA.

Reasoning procedure supporting the locked rejection

  1. Reproduce the proposed numerical comparison. The eTable 1 displayed SAF disposition counts total:

    337 + 189 + 16 + 86 = 628.

    The eTable 4/eTable 8 arm-specific death counts total:

    50 + 44 = 94, with displayed nonmissing denominators 321 + 313 = 634.

    The two numerators differ by:

    94 − 86 = 8.

  2. Identify the equivalence the proposed candidate requires. Calling the eight-count difference an arithmetic inconsistency requires the eTable 1 GDB disposition category “Death” to be the same participant-level measure as the eTable 4/eTable 8 cumulative all-cause in-hospital death endpoint through 120 days.

  3. Compare the supplied definitions. eTable 1 is a mutually exclusive status distribution that also assigns participants to discharge, continued hospitalization, or transfer. eTables 4 and 8 report a cumulative in-hospital death outcome. The supplied notes do not state that the eTable 1 disposition category must equal the cumulative death endpoint.

  4. Compare the observed subsets. The eTable 1 status display contains 628 records, while eTables 4 and 8 display 634 participants with nonmissing death outcomes. The six-participant denominator difference independently prevents a direct row-total identity from being assumed.

  5. Identify the missing evidence. The aggregate tables provide no participant-level mapping between GDB disposition status and the cumulative in-hospital death outcome, and no crosswalk showing that the populations, status timing, and outcome rules are identical. Without that evidence, the equality 86 = 94 is an unsupported premise rather than an arithmetic requirement.

  6. Separate direct observations from the rejected inference. The labels, definitions, counts, and denominators are direct source observations; 94 − 86 = 8 is a valid descriptive calculation. The rejected step is the inference that the two differently described measures must be equal.

Existing supported conclusion: The supplied package does not establish that 86 and 94 represent the same measure in the same nonmissing subset. C-05 therefore does not support an arithmetic inconsistency and remains Rejected.

Limit on interpretation: Rejection does not establish that either count is clinically or analytically correct, does not explain the eight-count difference, and does not establish a participant pathway after discharge or transfer.
