## C001 — Conflicting confidence intervals for the matched any-stroke rate ratio

- **Cited location found:** Yes. DOC-001 abstract at [PDF p. 1](../../../jama_wijesurendra_2025_oi_250068_1760999665.28362.pdf#page=1), the results narrative at [PDF p. 5](../../../jama_wijesurendra_2025_oi_250068_1760999665.28362.pdf#page=5), and Figure 4B at [PDF p. 7](../../../jama_wijesurendra_2025_oi_250068_1760999665.28362.pdf#page=7). The PDF p. 3 statistical-analysis text was also inspected for the stated log-rank rate-ratio method.
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. PDF p. 1 states that stroke occurred in 69 intervention participants (2.7%) and 64 control participants (2.5%), followed by rate ratio 1.08 and 95% CI 0.76-1.53. PDF p. 5 states the same 69 (2.7%) and 64 (2.5%), followed by event RR 1.08 and 95% CI 0.77-1.51. Figure 4B on PDF p. 7 prints `Any stroke` as patch 69 (2.7%), usual care 64 (2.5%), rate ratio 1.08 (95% CI 0.77-1.51).
- **Comparator matched:** Yes. The three locations identify the patch/intervention group versus usual-care/control group, any stroke through 2.5 years, the same arm counts and percentages, and the same point estimate of 1.08. Figure 4B labels the comparison as a time-to-event analysis; PDF p. 3 states that stroke was analyzed using log-rank methods. No cited location labels a distinct population, time window, stroke definition, estimand, or interval procedure for the abstract result.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Repeated reporting of the same outcome, contrast, analysis window, counts, and effect estimate should use the same confidence-interval endpoints unless the source identifies a distinct analysis or method. The direct comparison rule is applicable because the matched labels and values are present. It does not determine which interval pair is intended.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The lower endpoints differ by `0.77 - 0.76 = 0.01`; the upper endpoints differ by `1.53 - 1.51 = 0.02`, with the abstract wider at both ends. The displayed crude count ratio is `69 / 64 = 1.078125`, which rounds to 1.08 and is compatible with the shared displayed point estimate, but it is not a reconstruction of the stated time-to-event estimator. A single identical unrounded endpoint cannot round conventionally to both 0.76 and 0.77, or to both 1.51 and 1.53, at two decimal places.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The printed outcome label, groups, follow-up horizon, counts, percentages, rate-ratio point estimate, both interval pairs, and general log-rank method are available. These inputs are sufficient to reproduce the reporting comparison and endpoint differences.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not provide the stroke event times, stroke-analysis risk sets, censoring pattern by arm, observed-minus-expected statistic (`O-E`), its variance (`V`), unrounded rate ratio or CI endpoints, exact confidence-interval construction, or an explanation that assigns different methods or estimands to the abstract and detailed displays. Those inputs are required to independently derive the intended interval and select between the printed pairs.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The abstract is a summary location and the narrative/Figure 4B are detailed locations; the supplied method text permits a time-to-event interval to depend on analysis quantities not recoverable from the displayed totals. Thus the two interval pairs could have come from separately generated analysis or reporting outputs. The source, however, does not label such a distinction, and the detailed narrative and Figure 4B agree with each other.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Direct observations are the three printed count/estimate combinations, the two different CI pairs, the common 2.5-year stroke context, and the absence of a labeled distinction at those locations. Possible separate calculation pipelines, a production update, or a transcription difference are inferred explanations; none is established by the supplied source.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which 95% CI pair is intended for the matched 2.5-year any-stroke rate ratio of 1.08, and was any difference in estimand, censoring, analysis snapshot, or interval method intended between the abstract and the detailed results/Figure 4B?

## C002 — Stroke subtype counts do not partition the displayed any-stroke count in the patch group

- **Cited location found:** Yes. DOC-001 Figure 4B at [PDF p. 7](../../../jama_wijesurendra_2025_oi_250068_1760999665.28362.pdf#page=7), specifically the `Presumed ischemic stroke`, `Hemorrhagic stroke`, and `Any stroke` rows and the adjacent Figure 4B explanatory text. The any-stroke totals are also repeated in the results narrative at [PDF p. 5](../../../jama_wijesurendra_2025_oi_250068_1760999665.28362.pdf#page=5) and the abstract at [PDF p. 1](../../../jama_wijesurendra_2025_oi_250068_1760999665.28362.pdf#page=1).
- **Source printed value/text matched:** Yes. Figure 4B prints, for patch/usual care respectively, presumed ischemic stroke 60 (2.4%)/58 (2.3%), hemorrhagic stroke 12 (0.5%)/6 (0.2%), and any stroke 69 (2.7%)/64 (2.5%). The figure heads the numeric columns `No. of events (%)`, gives `n = 2520` for each arm, describes time-to-event analyses with events between randomization and 2.5 years, and states that presumed ischemic stroke includes unspecified stroke types under the stated assumption.
- **Comparator matched:** The three rows use the same two randomized arms, arm denominators, 2.5-year window, and stroke-outcome section. The source supports comparing the displayed counts. It does not state that the subtype endpoints are mutually exclusive, that each cell counts unique participants rather than events, or that the two subtype rows must form an exhaustive partition of `Any stroke`.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** The component-sum identity is applicable only conditionally: if presumed ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke are mutually exclusive, exhaustive, participant-level components counted under the same rule, their counts should sum to `Any stroke`. The printed figure does not establish those necessary conditions, so the partition rule cannot be applied unconditionally.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Patch group: `60 + 12 = 72`, which is `72 - 69 = 3` above the displayed any-stroke count. Usual-care group: `58 + 6 = 64`, equal to the displayed any-stroke count of 64. The patch percentages sum to `2.4% + 0.5% = 2.9%`, compared with 2.7%; usual-care percentages sum to `2.3% + 0.2% = 2.5%`, equal to 2.5%. Direct denominator checks reproduce the printed percentages: `60/2520 = 2.381%`, `12/2520 = 0.476%`, and `69/2520 = 2.738%`, which round to 2.4%, 0.5%, and 2.7%. Therefore percentage rounding does not explain the integer-count relationship. Under the additional assumptions that the two subtype rows are exhaustive sets of participants and `Any stroke` is their unique-participant union, set-union arithmetic would imply three patch participants in both subtype sets (`60 + 12 - 69 = 3`); that overlap is a conditional inference, not a printed fact.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The row labels, arm denominators, event window, six integer counts, six percentages, and the figure's inclusion of unspecified stroke within presumed ischemic stroke are available. These inputs are sufficient to reproduce the arithmetic and identify the rule's dependency on counting conventions.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not provide participant-level outcome records; an explicit definition of whether each row counts unique participants, first events, or all events; the number of participants appearing in both subtype rows; whether multiple stroke types for one participant are permitted; whether the subtype rows are exhaustive; the deduplication rule for `Any stroke`; the handling of recurrent or reclassified events; or a hierarchy for conflicting primary- and secondary-care stroke classifications.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** Figure 4B expressly describes separate time-to-event analyses and labels its cells as numbers of events. The subtype rows may therefore be nonexclusive endpoints: one participant could meet both a presumed-ischemic and hemorrhagic endpoint during follow-up, while `Any stroke` could count that participant once or use a separate first-any-stroke analysis. This interpretation is compatible with the printed values, but the figure does not state the overlap or counting rule. The usual-care equality does not establish exclusivity because zero overlap in one arm can occur under a nonexclusive rule.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Direct observations are the printed row values, the shared denominators/window, the arithmetic totals of 72 versus 69 and 64 versus 64, and the absence of an explicit exclusivity or deduplication statement. Treating the subtype rows as a required partition is an inference. Possible three-person overlap, recurrent events, cross-source classification, or distinct endpoint-specific first-event counting are inferred explanations and are not established by the aggregate display.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Were Figure 4B `Presumed ischemic stroke` and `Hemorrhagic stroke` intended as mutually exclusive and exhaustive participant-level outcomes? If so, which displayed patch-group count, if any, is not the intended value; if not, exactly how many participants contributed to both subtype rows, what did each row count, and how was the `Any stroke` count of 69 deduplicated or otherwise derived?
