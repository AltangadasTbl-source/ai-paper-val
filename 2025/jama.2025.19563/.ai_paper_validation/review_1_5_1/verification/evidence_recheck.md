## C001 — Protocol and reported primary endpoint differ on the diabetes-range A1C failure condition

- **Status:** Pending Human Adjudication.
- **Cited location found:** Yes. [DOC-002 protocol PDF page 15](<../../../joi250084supp1_prod_1765403089.61351.pdf#page=15>) contains section 3.2, “PRIMARY ENDPOINT.” [DOC-001 main article PDF page 4](<../../../jama_mathioudakis_2025_oi_250084_1765403089.60451.pdf#page=4>) contains the reported primary endpoint definition. [DOC-003 Supplement 2 PDF page 56](<../../../joi250084supp2_prod_1765403089.61751.pdf#page=56>) contains eTable 13 with the 15 participants who had diabetes-range A1C. Citation precision note: the explicit statement that these participants were marked as having failed the endpoint is in the eTable 13 method description on [DOC-003 Supplement 2 PDF page 30](<../../../joi250084supp2_prod_1765403089.61751.pdf#page=30>), not in the participant table on page 56.
- **Source value/text matched:** The protocol defines the binary 12-month endpoint as success if at least one of three conditions is met: at least 5% weight loss; at least 4% weight loss plus at least 150 minutes/week of physical activity on average over months 6-11; or at least a 0.2% reduction in hemoglobin A1C by 12 months. It then states that the endpoint is 1 if at least one CDC success outcome is achieved and 0 if none is achieved. The main article adds that the A1C-reduction component is evaluated in participants who maintained A1C below 6.5% throughout the study. The Supplement 2 method description states that participants with diabetes-range A1C at the 6- and/or 12-month visit were marked as failing the endpoint regardless of body-weight and physical-activity improvements. eTable 13 lists 15 such participants and identifies values of at least 6.5% as diabetes range.
- **Comparator matched:** The compared definitions concern the randomized trial's binary primary endpoint at 12 months and use the same three component thresholds. The supplement's “regardless of body weight and physical activity improvements” clause makes the diabetes-range rule global rather than limited to the A1C-reduction component.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** A later failure condition that overrides all three success components changes the classification rule unless it was part of the prospectively governing endpoint definition.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** Let `A`, `B`, and `C` denote the three protocol success components and let `D` denote no A1C value of at least 6.5% at 6 or 12 months. The protocol rule is `A or B or C`; the reported/supplement rule is `(A or B or C) and D`. Therefore, anyone satisfying at least one component while not satisfying `D` can be classified differently. The supplied eTable identifies 15 participants for whom `D` is false, but it does not show whether any of those 15 met `A`, `B`, or `C`.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The component thresholds, timing, binary success framing, global failure statement, and the 15 diabetes-range instances are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The package does not supply a dated protocol amendment, a final statistical analysis plan revision, a version history establishing when the global failure rule was adopted, or the component-achievement status for each of the 15 listed participants. Consequently, the number of endpoint classifications affected cannot be reproduced.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** Protocol page 15 states that CDC standards could change during the trial and that outcomes would also be analyzed against an updated benchmark if the benchmark changed before trial completion. A later prespecified amendment or final analysis-plan version could therefore explain the added rule, but no such governing document is supplied. It is also possible to read the main-article grammar as attaching the below-6.5% condition only to the A1C-reduction component; the supplement's global-failure statement argues against that narrow reading.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** The three printed definitions, the global-failure clause, and the 15 listed instances are direct observations. Prospective amendment, production history, and any actual change to the reported 117 successes are inferred possibilities that the package cannot establish.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Was the global diabetes-range A1C failure rule prospectively adopted in a dated governing amendment or final analysis plan, and, under that governing version, how many of the 15 listed participants otherwise met a weight, physical-activity, or A1C-reduction success component?

## C007 — MICE pooled percentages and printed risk difference have incompatible signs

- **Status:** Pending Human Adjudication.
- **Cited location found:** Yes. The multiple-imputation method description is on [DOC-003 Supplement 2 PDF page 30](<../../../joi250084supp2_prod_1765403089.61751.pdf#page=30>). eTable 16 is on [DOC-003 Supplement 2 PDF page 59](<../../../joi250084supp2_prod_1765403089.61751.pdf#page=59>).
- **Source value/text matched:** The method description says that missing 12-month outcomes were addressed with multiple imputation by chained equations under a missing-at-random assumption; it names imputation-model inputs, specifies 20 imputed data sets, and says they were combined using Rubin's rules. eTable 16 prints MI-pooled outcome percentages of `32.2` for AI and `31.9` for human, followed by a risk difference of `-1.1` percentage points with a one-sided 95% bound of `-11.5`.
- **Comparator matched:** The eTable columns display AI first and human second, consistent with the package's AI-minus-human risk-difference direction. All values are presented for the randomized-population MICE sensitivity analysis of the primary 12-month outcome.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** If a risk difference is the direct subtraction of the two displayed marginal percentages in AI-minus-human order, its sign and approximate magnitude must agree with that subtraction after display rounding.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `32.2 - 31.9 = +0.3` percentage points, whereas the table prints `-1.1`. Under ordinary rounding to one decimal place, underlying percentages that display as `32.2` and `31.9` imply a difference of approximately `+0.2` to `+0.4` percentage points, so direct subtraction cannot yield a negative difference. The one-sided bound cannot be independently reproduced from these two percentages.
- **Necessary inputs available:** Displayed arm percentages, column order, displayed risk difference and bound, randomized population, number of imputations, imputation covariates, and Rubin's-rules statement are available.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The table does not state whether the risk difference is adjusted, standardized, model-based, or pooled on a different scale from the percentages. Full-precision per-imputation arm estimates, the analysis model, contrast coding, covariance estimates, pooling steps for the risk difference, and standard-error calculation are not supplied.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The risk difference could be an adjusted or standardized contrast pooled separately from the displayed marginal percentages, while the percentages are separately pooled descriptive values. The source does not label such a distinction. Separate nonlinear pooling can change magnitude, but without further specification it does not explain the displayed sign as a direct subtraction.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** The percentages, risk difference, confidence-bound label, MICE inputs, and Rubin's-rules statement are direct observations. Any adjusted estimand, separate pooling scale, contrast reversal, or table-production error is an inferred explanation.
- **Exact remaining human question:** What exact estimand, contrast order, model, and pooling calculation produced the `-1.1` percentage-point risk difference, and is it intended to be directly comparable with the displayed MI-pooled percentages `32.2` and `31.9`?
