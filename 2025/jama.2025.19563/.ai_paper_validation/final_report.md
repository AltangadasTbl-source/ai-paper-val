### C-06 — Figure 3B and eTable 14 use unreconciled HbA1c analysis-set sizes

- **Evidence status:** Verified and retained.
- **Category:** Cross-document inconsistency.
- **Severity:** Major.
- **Exact source locations:**
  - [jama_mathioudakis_2025_oi_250084_1765403089.60451.pdf](../jama_mathioudakis_2025_oi_250084_1765403089.60451.pdf#page=8), PDF p. 8, journal p. 2086, Figure 3B, “Change in HbA1c at 12 mo.”
  - [jama_mathioudakis_2025_oi_250084_1765403089.60451.pdf](../jama_mathioudakis_2025_oi_250084_1765403089.60451.pdf#page=3), PDF p. 3, journal p. 2081, Outcomes section.
  - [joi250084supp2_prod_1765403089.61751.pdf](../joi250084supp2_prod_1765403089.61751.pdf#page=57), PDF p. 57, eTable 14, “Change in A1C (Baseline to 12 Months) (%).”

**Reported values and statements**

Figure 3B has ranked Human-led DPP and AI-led DPP sequences whose visible axes end at 149 and 151, respectively. eTable 14 reports the HbA1c-change row as AI `N=106` and Human `N=103`; the same table reports `N=151` and `N=149` for percentage weight change, weight change, and average physical activity. The main article states that the HbA1c-change endpoint applies only to participants with baseline HbA1c `5.7% to 6.4%`.

**Reasoning procedure**

1. The comparison is between the population visibly represented by Figure 3B and the population explicitly reported for the same HbA1c-change outcome in eTable 14.
2. The complete-data/no-prohibited-medication population shown for the other Figure 3 outcomes is 151 AI and 149 Human. eTable 14 uses those same denominators for the non-HbA1c continuous outcomes.
3. For HbA1c change, eTable 14 instead reports 106 AI and 103 Human. The denominator differences are:
   - AI: `151 − 106 = 45`;
   - Human: `149 − 103 = 46`;
   - combined: `(151 + 149) − (106 + 103) = 300 − 209 = 91`.
4. The article’s explicit baseline-HbA1c restriction explains why an HbA1c-specific table analysis may be smaller, but Figure 3B contains no caption note explaining why its plotted representation is larger.
5. Direct vector inspection of the original Figure 3B identified 121 AI-colored and 117 Human-colored nonzero bar rectangles within the panel. Those observed minima already exceed the eTable 14 denominators:
   - AI: `121 − 106 = at least 15` additional plotted bars;
   - Human: `117 − 103 = at least 14` additional plotted bars.
6. The visible endpoints alone are not treated as an assertion of exact Figure 3B sample size. The retained conclusion rests on the visible bar counts already exceeding the table denominators and on the absence of a caption-level reconciliation.

The endpoint labels, eTable 14 denominators, and eligibility statement are direct source observations. The subtractions and bar-count comparisons are derived diagnostic checks.

**Existing supported conclusion**

The figure demonstrably represents a larger HbA1c analysis set than eTable 14 reports for the co-cited HbA1c-change outcome, without a caption explanation reconciling the inclusion rule.

**Limit on interpretation**

The supplied files do not contain the plotting data. This finding does not claim that the axis endpoints, by themselves, prove exact sample sizes, and it does not establish whether the figure, table, caption, or an undocumented inclusion rule is the source of the difference.

**Verification instruction**

Obtain and count the Figure 3B plotting-data records; document the figure inclusion criterion; then reconcile the plotted records with eTable 14’s AI `N=106` and Human `N=103` and the stated baseline HbA1c `5.7%-6.4%` restriction.

### C-01 — eTable 12 reports 10 of 59 as 19%

- **Evidence status:** Verified and retained.
- **Category:** Arithmetic inconsistency.
- **Severity:** Minor.
- **Exact source location:** [joi250084supp2_prod_1765403089.61751.pdf](../joi250084supp2_prod_1765403089.61751.pdf#page=55), PDF p. 55, eTable 12, second component-pattern row, Human-led DPP column.

**Reported values and statements**

eTable 12 identifies the Human-led DPP column as `N=59`. Its second component-pattern row—check marks for at least 5% weight loss and at least 4% weight loss plus at least 150 minutes/week of physical activity, with no HbA1c-reduction check mark—reports `10 (19%)`. The preceding all-three-components row reports the same Human-led numerator as `10 (17%)`. Footnote a states that percentages are calculated among participants who achieved the composite outcome within each study group and overall.

**Reasoning procedure**

1. The applicable denominator is the Human-led DPP column header, `N=59`.
2. The reported numerator is 10.
3. The source-based calculation is `10 ÷ 59 × 100 = 16.949...%`.
4. Rounded to the table’s displayed whole-percent precision, `16.949...%` is `17%`.
5. The adjacent `10 (17%)` entry demonstrates that the table itself displays the expected rounded percentage for the same numerator and denominator.
6. As a column-total check, the seven Human-led counts are `10 + 10 + 5 + 1 + 12 + 2 + 19 = 59`, confirming that the printed column denominator is internally supported.

The numerator, denominator, displayed percentages, footnote rule, and component pattern are direct source observations. Division, rounding, and column summation are derived calculations.

**Existing supported conclusion**

The second-row Human-led DPP entry `10 (19%)` does not reconcile with its stated denominator; it rounds to 17% under the table’s denominator rule.

**Limit on interpretation**

The evidence supports a displayed arithmetic inconsistency only. It does not establish whether the numerator, percentage, or an unreported intended denominator was the production element meant to differ.

**Verification instruction**

Visually inspect the eTable 12 cell and `N=59` header, recompute `10/59`, sum the Human-led column, and compare the result with the preceding `10 (17%)` row before making any production correction.

### C-04 — eTable 11 contains incompatible age and significance footnotes

- **Evidence status:** Verified and retained, narrowed to eTable 11.
- **Category:** Statistical reporting inconsistency.
- **Severity:** Minor.
- **Exact source locations:** [joi250084supp2_prod_1765403089.61751.pdf](../joi250084supp2_prod_1765403089.61751.pdf#page=53), PDF p. 53, eTable 11 and age row; and [the same PDF, p. 54](../joi250084supp2_prod_1765403089.61751.pdf#page=54), footnotes 1-2.

**Reported values and statements**

eTable 11 compares AI-based DPP `N=151` with Human-coach-based DPP `N=149`.

- Footnote 1 states that age differed between study groups, `P=.010`; sex differed, `P=.041`; and all other baseline characteristics were similar, `P>.05`.
- Footnote 2, marked on the age row, states that age differed between study groups, `P=.014`; and all other baseline characteristics were similar, `P>.05`.

The identical `P=.014` age footnote is also visible in eTables 5-7 on PDF pp. 43, 45, and 47. The verifier treated these repetitions as corroboration of a production pattern and narrowed the accepted candidate to the demonstrable eTable 11 contradiction.

**Reasoning procedure**

1. Within eTable 11, both footnotes are presented alongside the displayed restricted-population comparison.
2. Age is assigned two different P values without an identified distinction in population, model, or test: `.010` and `.014`.
3. Footnote 2 says “all other baseline characteristics were similar (`P>.05`).”
4. Sex is an “other” baseline characteristic relative to age, yet footnote 1 reports sex `P=.041`.
5. Because `.041 < .05`, footnote 2’s “all other” statement conflicts with the significant sex statement in footnote 1.
6. The repeated `.014` wording in eTables 5-7 supports the existing narrowed decision, but the workflow did not establish the correct P values for those other populations. They remain corroboration rather than separate findings.

The P values, sample sizes, footnote markers, and footnote wording are direct source observations. The threshold comparison `.041 < .05` and the table-local logical reconciliation are derived checks. No assumption is made that either P value tests a coefficient against zero or that the P values share a particular model.

**Existing supported conclusion**

eTable 11 assigns age two unexplained P values and includes an “all other” significance statement that conflicts with its reported significant sex result.

**Limit on interpretation**

The package does not establish which age P value is correct, whether one belongs to another population, or how the duplication occurred. The repeated-footnote observations in eTables 5-7 are corroborating evidence only.

**Verification instruction**

Review the eTable 11 analysis output for the `N=151/N=149` population; identify which age test produced `.010` or `.014`; retain or label the applicable statement and identify/remove the other footnote. Confirm that the resulting significance summary accounts for sex `P=.041`.

### C-07 — Supplement uses percent notation where the main article defines an absolute percentage-point HbA1c threshold

- **Evidence status:** Verified and retained.
- **Category:** Cross-document inconsistency.
- **Severity:** Minor.
- **Exact source locations:**
  - [joi250084supp2_prod_1765403089.61751.pdf](../joi250084supp2_prod_1765403089.61751.pdf#page=34), PDF p. 34, eFigure 3 row label and footnote 3.
  - [jama_mathioudakis_2025_oi_250084_1765403089.60451.pdf](../jama_mathioudakis_2025_oi_250084_1765403089.60451.pdf#page=3), PDF p. 3, journal p. 2081, Outcomes section.
  - [jama_mathioudakis_2025_oi_250084_1765403089.60451.pdf](../jama_mathioudakis_2025_oi_250084_1765403089.60451.pdf#page=7), PDF p. 7, journal p. 2085, Figure 2 row and footnote c.

**Reported values and statements**

The supplement uses “`0.2% A1C Reduction`” in the eFigure 3 row and repeats “`0.2% A1C reduction outcome`” in footnote 3. The main article defines the component as an **absolute decrease of at least `0.2 percentage points`**, and Figure 2 states “Reduction in HbA1c ≥0.2 percentage points.” Both figures display the same component counts: AI `35/130` and Human `35/130`.

**Reasoning procedure**

1. The same `35/130` counts in both arms and the same baseline-prediabetes restriction connect the supplement row to the main article’s HbA1c component.
2. The main article explicitly defines an absolute change in percentage points.
3. The supplement instead uses “0.2%,” which denotes percent rather than an absolute percentage-point change.
4. Percent and percentage points are not interchangeable descriptions of this threshold. For illustration only, a 0.2% relative reduction from a baseline HbA1c of 6.0% would equal `6.0% × 0.002 = 0.012` percentage points, not 0.2 percentage points.
5. The retained finding is the inconsistent endpoint labeling. The diagnostic illustration is not used to recalculate the `35/130` classifications.

The labels, counts, and endpoint definition are direct source observations. The distinction between relative percent and absolute percentage points, including the illustration, is explanatory.

**Existing supported conclusion**

The main article and supplement label the same HbA1c component differently: absolute percentage points in the main article and percent notation in the supplement.

**Limit on interpretation**

The record does not establish that the label affected analysis or participant classification, and it does not independently infer the intended calculation from the label alone.

**Verification instruction**

Compare the Outcomes definition, Figure 2, and eFigure 3. If all refer to the same `35/130` endpoint, revise the supplement label and footnote to preserve the main article’s absolute percentage-point definition.

The percentages, risk difference, CI lower bound, imputation description, and regression statement are direct source observations. The subtraction and 1.4-point comparison are diagnostic calculations only.

**Existing supported conclusion**

**Limit on interpretation**

**Verification instruction**
