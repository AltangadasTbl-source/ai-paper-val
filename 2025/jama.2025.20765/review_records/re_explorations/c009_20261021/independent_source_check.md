# C009 independent source check: missing primary outcomes

This bounded check used only `QUALITY_CONTROL_SCOPE.md`, the assigned source pages, source-derived images, and the main article's native text as a locator. Earlier candidate, checker, screening, recheck, audit, report, and re-exploration conclusions were not read. It does not rerun Workflow 1.6.2 or assess the separate model-family question.

**Independent assessment:** The sources reproduce an unresolved difference between the protocol's broad statement that outcomes will primarily be analysed in a missing-at-random framework and the explicit primary-outcome rule in the SAP and report assigning missing outcomes to continuing smoking/nonabstinence. The SAP and report agree. The evidence supports asking which primary analysis the protocol's framework describes and how the two specifications relate; it does not establish that a missing-at-random mechanism and negative-outcome assignment are universally logically incompatible.

## Reproduced arithmetic and display precision

The assigned source counts reconcile: 25 + 18 + 10 = 53; 27 + 9 + 6 = 42; 720 − 53 = 667; 360 − 42 = 318. Thus 53/720 and 42/360 are the arm-specific missing-primary-outcome counts supported jointly by Figure 1 and the narrative. The reported primary ITT denominators retain those participants; the complete-case denominators remove exactly those totals. The abstinent numerators remain 300 and 55.

The combined missing proportion is 100 × 95/1080 = 8.7963%, within the printed 8.8% interval [8.75%, 8.85%]. Counts are discrete exact inputs. Even allowing each displayed count a half-unit interval, the recomputed interval [100 × 94.5/1080.5, 100 × 95.5/1079.5] = [8.746%, 8.847%] overlaps the printed interval; gap and relative discrepancy are zero. Subtraction results are exactly the displayed denominators; their half-unit intervals also overlap. There is no numerical inconsistency in these counts. Display precision does not resolve the qualitative missing-data specification difference.

## Context and limits of the inference

**Economic adjacency:** The explicit economic complete-case paragraph concerns complete costs and QALYs, so it cannot be treated as an instruction about missing six-month abstinence. However, the MAR paragraph is beneath its own sibling `Statistical analysis` heading and describes binary-outcome relative risks and clustering. Economic adjacency alone does not establish that this paragraph applies exclusively to economic analyses. The text does not name a primary abstinence endpoint in that paragraph either; its exact scope remains undefined.

**Mechanism versus handling rule:** MAR describes a relationship between missingness and observed/unobserved data under a specified conditioning structure. Assigning an unobserved abstinence outcome to nonabstinence is an operational analysis rule. They are different concepts. A study can use such an assignment for one estimand/analysis and a MAR-based analysis for another; an analyst can also make an assumption about the original missingness while defining an analysis variable by a fixed assignment. Consequently the printed phrases alone do not establish a universal logical contradiction, an actual analysis implementation, or any direction of bias. The narrower unresolved issue is what analysis the protocol calls primary under its MAR architecture and how it relates to the explicit ITT assignment.

**Covariates and distinct sets:** No inspected statement limits the protocol's MAR framework to missing covariates. The SAP and article do explicitly separate primary ITT assignment from an available-outcome analysis. That is a plausible contextual explanation if the protocol's generic framework refers to the latter or another analysis, but the protocol does not make that mapping. The SAP/report agreement determines the reported handling rule clearly; it does not itself establish the intended scope of the generic protocol statement.

**Version supersession:** The p28 header identifies version 6.0 dated 07.03.25, while the SAP introduction cites version 6.0 dated 20.Mar.2025. Bundle order and this date difference do not establish a hierarchy or amendment history. The inspected change list supplies no missing-data amendment. No conclusion about prespecification timing, knowledge of outcomes, or an undisclosed change follows from these pages.
