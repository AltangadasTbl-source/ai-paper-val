C1 — Incorrect supplementary-figure citation for time to death

  • Evidence status: Verified; retained by the critic as a final scientific finding.
  • Category: Cross-document inconsistency.
  • Severity: Minor.
  • Exact source locations: jama_martin_2025_oi_250042_1753377747.91025.pdf — PDF p. 6, journal p. 403, “Primary and Secondary Outcomes”; joi250042supp2_prod_1753377747.93025.pdf — PDF p. 11, eFigure 4; and joi250042supp2_prod_1753377747.93025.pdf — PDF p. 12, eFigure 5.
  • Reported statements and values: The main article states, “Time to death (adjusted hazard ratio, 1.01; 95% CI, 0.96-1.05; eFigure 4 in Supplement 2).” Supplement PDF p. 11 labels eFigure 4 “Separation in FiO2 and SpO2 when receiving oxygen over time and by patient sequence within site for patients with enhanced data collection (N=2489).” Supplement PDF p. 12 labels eFigure 5 “Kaplan-Meier plot of cumulative all-cause mortality to 1 y following randomization” and displays “Hazard ratio, 1.01 (95% CI, 0.96-1.05); P=.82.”

Reasoning procedure

  1. The main-text result being traced is time to death, with adjusted hazard ratio 1.01 and 95% CI 0.96-1.05. Its printed cross-reference is eFigure 4.

  2. Direct inspection of eFigure 4 shows four oxygenation-separation panels. Their axes concern FiO2 or SpO2 against randomization date or patient sequence within site. The figure has no mortality title, time-to-death axis, or hazard-ratio annotation.

  3. Direct inspection of eFigure 5 shows a Kaplan-Meier cumulative all-cause mortality plot with time since randomization on the horizontal axis. Its displayed hazard ratio and CI exactly match the main text at the reported precision: 1.01 and 0.96-1.05.

  4. The comparison is therefore:

    • main article: time to death, HR 1.01, 95% CI 0.96-1.05, cited as eFigure 4;
    • eFigure 4: oxygenation separation, not mortality;
    • eFigure 5: all-cause mortality, HR 1.01, 95% CI 0.96-1.05, P=.82.

  5. No inferential recalculation is required. Outcome identity plus the exact HR/CI match establishes where the cited result appears. The CI includes the null value 1 and is consistent with the displayed P=.82, but this diagnostic observation is not needed to establish the cross-reference mismatch.

  6. The earlier “Oxygen Exposure” paragraph on main-article p. 6 also cites eFigure 4 for separation across time and patient sequence; that use is congruent with eFigure 4’s content. The mismatch is confined to the time-to-death cross-reference recorded in C1.

  • Existing supported conclusion: The main-text time-to-death statement points readers to eFigure 4, while the matching mortality result is in eFigure 5.
  • Limit on interpretation: This is a navigation/cross-reference inconsistency. It does not establish any error in the hazard ratio, confidence interval, P value, or mortality analysis.
  • Verification instruction: Open the main article at PDF p. 6 (journal p. 403), transcribe the time-to-death outcome, HR, CI, and cited figure number; then inspect the titles, axes, and annotations of supplement pp. 11-12. Confirm with the production source that the intended cross-reference is eFigure 5.

C2 — UK-ROX achieved-oxygenation summary does not identify its metric-specific source-row change

  • Evidence status: Verified; retained by the critic as a final scientific finding.
  • Category: Presentation inconsistency.
  • Severity: Minor.
  • Exact source locations: joi250042supp2_prod_1753377747.93025.pdf — PDF p. 21, eTable 5, patient-median and “when receiving O2” rows; joi250042supp2_prod_1753377747.93025.pdf — PDF p. 27, eTable 10, UK-ROX “Achieved oxygenation”; and jama_martin_2025_oi_250042_1753377747.91025.pdf — PDF p. 6, journal p. 403, “Oxygen Exposure”.
  • Reported values: eTable 10 gives UK-ROX SpO2 as 93.3% versus 95.2%, PaO2 as 71.5 versus 79.5 mm Hg, and FiO2 as 0.31 versus 0.35. The main article reports SpO2 93.3% versus 95.1%, PaO2 71.5 versus 79.5 mm Hg, and FiO2 0.31 versus 0.35.

The eTable 5 rows relevant to the source-value comparison are:

Metric Patient-median row “When receiving O2” row eTable 10 UK-ROX
SpO2 93.3% vs 95.1% 93.3% vs 95.2% 93.3% vs 95.2%
PaO2, mm Hg 71.5 vs 79.5 73.8 vs 81.4 71.5 vs 79.5
FiO2 0.31 vs 0.35 0.35 vs 0.37 0.31 vs 0.35

Reasoning procedure

  1. eTable 10’s SpO2 pair, 93.3% versus 95.2%, exactly matches eTable 5’s “when receiving O2” row. It does not match the patient-median SpO2 row, whose higher-target value is 95.1%.

  2. eTable 10’s PaO2 pair, 71.5 versus 79.5 mm Hg, exactly matches eTable 5’s patient-median PaO2 row. It does not match the oxygen-only pair, 73.8 versus 81.4 mm Hg.

  3. eTable 10’s FiO2 pair, 0.31 versus 0.35, exactly matches eTable 5’s patient-median FiO2 row. It does not match the oxygen-only pair, 0.35 versus 0.37.

  4. The displayed differences preserve that mapping:

    • eTable 10 SpO2: 95.2 - 93.3 = 1.9 percentage points, displayed at whole-percentage precision as 2%;
    • eTable 5 patient-median SpO2: 95.1 - 93.3 = 1.8 percentage points;
    • eTable 10 PaO2: 79.5 - 71.5 = 8.0 mm Hg;
    • eTable 10 FiO2: 0.35 - 0.31 = 0.04.

  5. The main article repeats the patient-median values from eTable 5: SpO2 93.3% versus 95.1%, PaO2 71.5 versus 79.5 mm Hg, and FiO2 0.31 versus 0.35.

  6. These are exact-value correspondences. The package does not explicitly state that eTable 10 was transcribed from eTable 5. For SpO2, the “when receiving O2” row is a conditional observation-period definition with its own available counts, not proof of a wholly different participant cohort. The report therefore does not overstate this as a proven participant-set error.

  7. eTable 10’s footnote cautions that reporting methods and data collection differ across trials, including means, medians, and time-weighted means. It does not identify the within-UK-ROX, metric-specific choice between the patient-median and oxygen-only rows.

  • Existing supported conclusion: Under its generic “Achieved oxygenation” presentation, eTable 10 uses the oxygen-only source row for SpO2 and the patient-median source rows for PaO2 and FiO2 without identifying that metric-specific definition change.
  • Limit on interpretation: The package supports an unlabeled metric-definition/conditional observation-subset presentation issue only. It does not establish that 95.2% or any other displayed value is numerically wrong, why the rows were mixed, or which row the authors intended.
  • Verification instruction: Compare all three UK-ROX pairs on supplement p. 27 with both corresponding rows on p. 21, including their labels and available counts, and then compare the patient-median values with the main-article p. 6 Oxygen Exposure paragraph. Confirm the intended definition for each metric and label it explicitly or harmonize the rows.

C5 — FiO2 fractions are mislabeled as percentages in eTable 6

  • Evidence status: Verified; retained by the critic as a final scientific finding.
  • Category: Presentation inconsistency.
  • Severity: Minor.
  • Exact source locations: joi250042supp2_prod_1753377747.93025.pdf — PDF p. 22, eTable 6, rows “Patient median FiO2, %” and “Patient median FiO2 when receiving O2, %”; comparison with joi250042supp2_prod_1753377747.93025.pdf — PDF p. 21, eTable 5 and joi250042supp2_prod_1753377747.93025.pdf — PDF p. 27, eTable 10.
  • Reported values and labels: eTable 6 appends % to both FiO2 row labels while displaying fractional values. The first-10 subgroup shows 0.32 versus 0.35 and 0.36 versus 0.37; the random-sample subgroup shows 0.30 versus 0.35 and 0.34 versus 0.37. eTable 5 uses corresponding FiO2 labels without percent signs and shows 0.31 versus 0.35 and 0.35 versus 0.37. eTable 10 defines FiO2 as “fraction of inspired oxygen” and shows 0.31 versus 0.35.

Reasoning procedure

  1. The p. 22 source observation is a unit-label/value combination: the two row labels assign percent units, but their displayed values are decimals between 0.30 and 0.37.

  2. On p. 21, the same measure is presented on the same decimal scale without a percent sign. On p. 27, FiO2 is explicitly defined as a fraction and again displayed on the approximately 0.3 scale.

  3. A fractional FiO2 of 0.32 corresponds numerically to 32%; a table that presents percent-valued numbers would therefore ordinarily display 32, not 0.32. This is a scale check, not a proposed correction.

  4. The within-package comparison is:

    • eTable 6: 0.32, 0.35, 0.30, 0.35, 0.36, 0.37, 0.34, and 0.37, labeled %;
    • eTables 5 and 10: comparable decimals presented as fractions without percent labels.

  5. The conflict also extends to associated differences and confidence intervals if percent units were truly intended: every fractional-scale entry would require a consistent factor-of-100 conversion. The package does not establish that this was the intended production repair.

  6. Small discrepancies that may arise from subtracting rounded means are not treated as a separate arithmetic issue; C5 remains limited to the locked unit/scale presentation conflict.

  • Existing supported conclusion: eTable 6 labels fractional-scale FiO2 values as percentages, contrary to the scale and terminology used for the same measure elsewhere in the package.
  • Limit on interpretation: The evidence does not establish whether the intended repair is to remove the percent signs or to convert every related value, dispersion measure, difference, and CI to percentage units.
  • Verification instruction: Confirm the intended units in the source table. If the values are fractions, remove the two percent signs; if percentage units were intended, verify a consistent factor-of-100 conversion for every associated entry before correction.

Uncertain Candidates

C3 — Blank overall event-count cells in eTable 9

  • Evidence status: Uncertain; not retained as an accepted scientific finding.
  • Category: Presentation inconsistency.
  • Potential severity: Uncertain.
  • Exact source location: joi250042supp2_prod_1753377747.93025.pdf — PDF p. 26, eTable 9, “Any serious adverse event”, under both “Events, No.” columns.
  • Reported values: Both overall event-count cells are blank. The adjacent “Patients, No. (%)” cells report 58 (0.7%) for conservative oxygen therapy and 29 (0.4%) for usual oxygen therapy. Event counts are printed for the specified and other serious adverse-event categories below the overall row.

Reasoning procedure

  1. Direct source observations are the two blank cells under columns explicitly labeled “Events, No.” and the adjacent patient totals, 58 (0.7%) and 29 (0.4%).

  2. The displayed conservative-therapy category counts sum as follows:

    • specified events: 2 + 12 + 16 + 19 + 7 = 56;
    • other events: 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 1 + 1 = 8;
    • total of all listed event rows: 56 + 8 = 64.

  3. The displayed usual-therapy category counts sum as follows:

    • specified events: 1 + 8 + 15 + 11 + 0 = 35;
    • other events: 0 + 0 + 0 + 0 + 0 + 0 + 2 + 0 + 0 = 2;
    • total of all listed event rows: 35 + 2 = 37.

  4. The calculations reproduce the sums of the visible category rows. They do not establish that the categories were intended to be exhaustive for an overall event total, that recurrent or multiple events should be summarized by simple summation in the overall row, or that 64 and 37 were intended as the blank entries.

  5. The patient totals and event totals are different estimands: one patient may experience more than one event. Therefore 58 and 29 neither confirm nor refute the diagnostic event sums.

  6. A directly supported alternative is that the overall row was designed to report only patients with any serious adverse event and the event-count cells were intentionally left blank. The supplied package cannot distinguish that design from an unintended omission.

  • Existing supported conclusion: The blank overall event-count cells and the diagnostic sums of 64 and 37 are document-grounded; whether totals were intended in those cells is not established.
  • Limit on interpretation: Neither sum may be treated as a confirmed missing printed value, and the blank cells may not be called a confirmed error without source-owner evidence.
  • Verification instruction: Ask the production/source-table owner whether the two overall “Events, No.” cells were intentionally blank. If not, confirm both the aggregation rule and the intended totals before making any correction.
