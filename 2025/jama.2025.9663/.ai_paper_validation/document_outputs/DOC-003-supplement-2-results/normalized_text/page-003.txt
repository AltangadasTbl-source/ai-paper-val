Consent procedures

Patients eligible for the            UK-ROX              trial become so during a period of critical illness.
Mechanical ventilation is an invasive procedure initiated as a life-saving measure, during an
emergency clinical situation. It requires the use of sedative and analgesic drugs  , meaning
most patients will lack mental capacity to provide consent. Additionally         , the urgent and life-
threatening nature of the                       clinical situation can cause profound distress  for relatives (including
the patient’s personal consultee). This raises ethical concerns around a relative’s ability to
process complex trial information at a time of great distress and to decide whether                                    the
patient should be enrolled into the trial. For these reasons, attempts to obtain either prior
informed consent from the patient, or the prior opinion of a p                                                                                                                                     ersonal consultee, were deemed
to be inappropriate.

The UK-ROX              trial therefore adopted                                                                                                                                                                                a research without prior consent (RWPC) model , also
referred to as deferred                                             consent, whereby eligible patients were             randomized to receive the
assigned treatment as soon as possible (and no later than 12 hours after fulfilling the
eligibility criteria). This is an                                             accepted consent model in adult emergency and critical care
research where participants lack mental capacity and minimizes                                                                                         the distress and additional
burden on families during a                                                                              distressing time. This consent model is covered by an
Emergency Waiver of Consent under the                                                                                    Mental   Capacity Act   and was approved by South
Central -              Oxford C Research Ethics Committee                                                                   (reference: 20/SC/0423).

In the very rare situation where a patient was                                                                    deemed by the treating clinical team to have
full mental capacity and was able to give informed consent at the point of meeting the trial’s
eligibility criteria, they were approached directly prior to randomization                                                                                                    for verbal or other
non-written consent to take part in the trial                                                                                                                . If they were able to provide verbal or                                                                                          other non-
written consent, they were            then followed up for full written informed consent                                                         at a later date. If

continuation in the trial.

UK-ROX trial, a delegated member of the site research team approached                                                                                                                             the participant’s
