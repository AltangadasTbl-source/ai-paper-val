12 STATISTICAL ASPECTS  
12.1 Description of planned statistical methods, including schedule of planned interim 
analyses 
A patient flow diagram will be drawn up to describe the number of eligible patients and the 
number of patients actually included and randomized in total and in each of the two arms, as 
well as patient follow-up (number of patients lost to follow-up per arm). This diagram will also 
show the number of patients analyzed in each arm. 
 
For each group and each evaluation date, qualitative variables will be described by their 
number, percentage and missing data per response modality, and quantitative variables by their 
number, mean and standard deviation. Quantitative variables not normally distributed will be 
presented with their median and interquartile range (1st quartile-3e quartile). 
 
Analysis of primary endpoint 
The primary endpoint will be analyzed sequentially in 2 stages: in an interim analysis planned 
at the end of the 6-month follow-up of 129 included patients (37.5%), approximately 15 months 
after the start of inclusions (9 months of inclusion + 6 months of follow-up), and at the end of 
the study (at the end of follow-up of the last included patient, data collection and control). This 
sequential analysis is based on a 2-stage Lan & DeMets approach with an O'Brien & Fleming 
alpha risk expenditure function. 
 
For the interim analysis, all patients randomized and for whom recurrence status at 6 months 
could be recovered will be analyzed, in their initially allocated randomization group. Patients 
who died without recurrence will be considered failures.  Recurrences of cSDH by group will 
be analyzed using a mixed logistic model adjusted for randomization stratification factors. 
Randomization group, anticoagulant/antiplatelet use and uni or bilateral cSDH will be included 
as fixed effects, and center will be introduced as a random effect. In the event of model non-
convergence, a Mantel-Haensel test (adjusted for center and, if possible, other randomization 
stratification factors) will be used.

In the event of an early stopping decision following this interim analysis, due to rejection of the 
hypothesis of equality of cSDH recurrence rates between the two groups at the nominal 
threshold of alpha=0.001, the final main analysis will include the patients analyzed leading to 
the stopping decision, as well as all patients for whom the primary endpoint could be collected 
subsequently (over-running patients). Similarly, included patients who had already undergone 
embolization will continue to be followed up until M6, and will be included in the final analysis. 
 
During the interim analysis, conditional power estimates will also be made. If the conditional 
power is deemed to be too low, the project may be discontinued on grounds of futility, after 
consultation with the DSMB. 
 
In the final primary analysis, the primary endpoint, cSDH recurrence rate, will be analyzed in 
the intention-to-treat population using a mixed logistic model adjusted for randomization 
stratification factors. Randomization group, anticoagulant/antiplatelet medication use and uni 
or bilateral cSDH distribution will be included as fixed effects, while center will be introduced 
as a random effect (to take into account the multicenter nature of the study). 
In the event of model non-convergence, a Mantel-Haensel test (adjusted for center and, if 
possible, other randomization stratification factors) will be used. Patients who die without 
recurrence will be considered as failures. 
 
The low significance level chosen for the interim analysis during the study leads to a virtual 
absence of alpha risk inflation; the final analysis will therefore be carried out at the conventional 
0.05 level, despite the interim analysis. 
 
A sensitivity analysis will be performed to exclude patients who did not benefit from 
embolization in the experimental arm after CT angiography.  
 
Two subgroup analyses will be carried out according to the uni or bilateral nature of cSDH and 
the use of anticoagulants/antiplatelet medication.

between the two arms of the study using a GEE logistic model, with a cluster effect on the

patient to account for intra-patient variability. This model will include as parameters the 
randomization arm, the visit as class and the visit*randomization arm interaction. The

test (or Fisher's exact test if appropriate). 
 
Adverse events observed at 1 and 6 months will be described globally and by treatment group, 
in terms of number, frequency and percentage of patients concerned. Serious adverse events 
will be described separately.  
 
12.2 Assumptions for calculating the number of subjects required and results  
To demonstrate a reduction in the recurrence rate from 15% (control group) to 5% (embolized 
group) with 80% power, and ensuring an overall bilateral alpha risk of 5%, 
142 patients per group are required. This number was estimated by taking into account the 2 
sequential tests planned according to the method of Lan and Demets with an O'Brien & Fleming 
alpha risk expenditure function (interim analysis after inclusion and end of follow-up of 129 
patients (information fraction of 37.5%) at nominal threshold alpha=0.001 and final analysis at 
threshold alpha=0.05). Assuming a lost-to-follow-up rate of 20%, a total of 342 patients (171 
per group) is required.  
 
12.3 Expected statistical significance 
All analyses will be performed at 5% two-sided alpha risk (with the exception of the interim 
analysis performed at the nominal threshold of 0.001 from 129 patients).

12.5 Method for taking into account missing, unused or invalid data 
The main analysis will be conducted on an intention-to-treat basis. All randomized subjects will 
be analyzed (in the randomization group initially allocated to them). In the main analysis, a 
multiple imputation method will be used for patients who cannot be evaluated for the primary 
endpoint. 
 
12.6 Manage changes to the initial strategy analysis plan. 
Any modification of the initial analysis plan will be justified in the study report. 
 
12.7 Population selection 
Intention to treat (ITT) 
The ITT population is defined as all patients randomized to the study, with each patient 
remaining in the arm to which he or she was randomly assigned. The analysis of this population 
constitutes the main analysis of the study. 
 
13 CONTROL AND quality assurance  
Each interventional research project involving humans undertaken by the AP-HP is classified 
according to the anticipated risk incurred by the people taking part in the research, using the 
classification of interventional research involving humans promoted by the AP-HP.  
 
13.1 General organization 
The sponsor must ensure the safety and respect of those who have agreed to take part in the 
research. They must set up a quality assurance system to monitor the progress of the research 
in the investigating centers.
