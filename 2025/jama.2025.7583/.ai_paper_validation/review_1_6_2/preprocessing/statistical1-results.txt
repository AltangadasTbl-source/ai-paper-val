Trial Design 
The study, registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04372147), followed the Declaration of 
Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. Ethical approval was obtained from the 
Comité de Protection des Personnes (CPP) Ile de France III on January 10, 2020 for the first 
version of the study protocol (reference no. 3754- I). All subsequent versions of the protocol, 
up to the last version (no. 5.0; June 7, 2022) were approved by the same research ethics 
committee. All protocol amendments were previously reported.1 All patients or their 
representatives provided written informed consent. 
The trial was designed by the first and two last authors. All investigators and study coauthors 
vouch for the rigorous fidelity of the trial to the protocol, the exhaustivity and accuracy of 
data reported, including the reporting of adverse events, and the fidelity of the analysis to the 
statistical analysis plan. The statistical analysis was performed by a biostatistician of the 
Clinical Research Unit under supervision of the lead study methodologist. The first draft of 
the manuscript was written by the first author and revised by the last two authors. The final 
draft was revised by all authors, who provided agreement for submitting the manuscript for 
publication.  
An independent and blinded committee adjudicated possible recurrence cases to ascertain the 
primary endpoint. An independent Data Safety Monitoring Board monitored safety data and 
interpreted the results of the planned interim analysis. The study was sponsored by Assistance 
Publique Hôpitaux de Paris (AP-HP) and funded by an academic grant from Programme 
Hospitalier de Recherche Clinique - PHRC 2018 (Ministère de la Santé) (PHRC-IR 
AOR18104). Merit Medical (South Jordan, UT, USA) and Balt (Montmorency, France)

Patients 
As reported in the main manuscript, Patients were not eligible for inclusion if CSDH 
evacuation was performed by craniotomy or twist-drill craniostomy rather than burr-hole. As 
previously reported, openings of the skull up to a diameter of 5 mm were classified as twist-
drill craniostomy, openings of up to 30 mm as burr-hole craniostomy, and larger openings as 
craniotomy.2–4  
Trial Procedures 
In the intervention group, patients underwent CT-angiography of the supra-aortic and 
intracranial vessels followed by MMA embolization within 7 days of surgery, along with best 
medical treatment. In the third amendment of the protocol (in effect on April 30, 2021), the 
CT-angiography was no longer mandatory if the patient had CT-angiography scans of the 
supra-aortic vessels in the past year that the investigator considered of sufficient quality. The 
CT-angiography was performed to check the feasibility and risk of arterial catheterization and 
embolization.5 Investigators were asked to check specific elements on the CT-angiography 
including anatomy of the supra-aortic vessels in order to plan for radial or femoral access, the 
presence of arterial thrombus in the aortic arch precluding aortic arch navigation, and 
anatomical variants contraindicating MMA embolization (MMA originating from the 
ophthalmic artery with agenesis of the foramen spinosum and absence of an MMA originating 
from the internal maxillary artery).

The embolization procedure was initially performed under local anesthesia or conscious 
sedation. Starting from June 10, 2020 (second amendment of the protocol), embolization 
could also be performed under general anesthesia if necessary. Via a femoral or radial access, 
the external carotid artery was catheterized with a regular 5F or 6F guiding catheter. Then the 
MMA was catheterized with a Progreat 2.4 (Terumo, Shibuya, Tokyo) microcatheter in a co-
axial fashion above the foramen spinosum and embolized by free-flow injection of 300-500 
µm EmboSphere (Merit Medical, South Jordan, UT, USA) triascryl gelatin microspheric 
(TAGM) particles. We chose 300-500 µm TAGM calibrated microspheres as the embolic 
agent because of previously reported non-randomized data suggesting a potential benefit.6 
This choice was also guided by safety concerns because these particles are not expected to 
penetrate the meningeal anastomotic arteries, which have been reported to be 50 to 300 µm in 
diameter, thus mitigating the potential risk for non-target embolization.1,7 If the selective 
MMA angiogram showed a dangerous anastomosis (an arterial anastomosis exposing to a risk 
of non-target embolic agent migration towards an eloquent structure such as the eye), the 
artery was embolized downstream of the anastomosis with a safety margin or the anastomosis 
was occluded using Optima coils (Balt, Montmorency, France) delivered via a Vasco 10 
(Balt) microcatheter. Technically, MMA embolization aimed to occlude both anterior and 
posterior MMA branches. This was achieved by injection from the common trunk or selective 
catheterization of each individual branch. Patients with a unilateral CSDH underwent 
embolization homolaterally to the CSDH. Patients with a bilateral CSDH underwent bilateral 
embolization. Patients who had unilateral surgery for a bilateral CSDH (i.e., if the CSDH was 
not compressive on one side and did not require surgery) underwent unilateral embolization 
on the same side as the surgery. In the specific setting in which a patient had bilateral surgery 
for a bilateral CSDH, then the CSDH recurred unilaterally and had unilateral surgery for this

recurrence and was included in the study, the embolization procedure was performed 
bilaterally. 
In the control group, patients received the best medical treatment alone, which included 
standard post-surgical care and nursing care, symptomatic treatments including pain 
management medication and rehabilitation if necessary. Corticosteroids were authorized if 
deemed necessary by the attending physician. Clinical and imaging follow-up beyond those 
planned in the protocol was organized as deemed necessary by the attending physician 
depending on patient status. 
 
Statistical Analysis 
Baseline characteristics are described in each group with median (interquartile range) for 
quantitative variables and number (percentage) for qualitative variables. 
In the primary endpoint analysis, a multiple imputation method was implemented for patients 
who could not be evaluated for the main endpoint except those who died from a neurological 
or unknown cause who were considered as having a CSDH recurrence. The Multiple 
Imputation by Chained Equations (MICE) technique was used to replace missing values.8 The 
variables used in the imputation phase were recurrence at 6 months, age, sex, unilateral or 
bilateral nature of the CSDH and use of anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy as well as the 
characteristics of CSDH at inclusion, namely type of CSDH (initial episode or recurrence), 
proven recent head trauma, Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score, American Society of 
Anesthesiologists (ASA) score, focal neurological deficit, hemiparesis, phasic disorder, gait 
disorder, headache, maximum thickness of the CSDH, Park classification9, type of surgery 
(burr hole, trephine craniostomy), complications (infection, subdural empyema, epileptic 
seizure during surgery or immediately afterward). Analyses involved using R software and

AI training, and similar technologies. 
the mice package. Ten datasets were generated. The results from each imputed dataset were 
pooled using Rubin's rules to obtain overall estimates and their standard errors. 
 
Data Sharing Statement 
Data are available upon reasonable request by a researcher independent of the study. The 
procedures carried out with the French data privacy authority (CNIL, Commission nationale 
de l'informatique et des libertés) do not allow the transmission of the database, nor the 
information and consent documents signed by the patients. Consultation by the editorial board 
or interested researchers of individual participant data that underlie the results reported in the 
article after deidentification may nevertheless be considered, subject to prior determination of 
the terms and conditions of such consultation and in respect for compliance with the 
applicable regulations.
