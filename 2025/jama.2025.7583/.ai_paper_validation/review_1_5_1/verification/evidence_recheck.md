The results supplement prints `≥ 5 mm`. The SAP prints `> 5mm`. Each occurrence is part of the six-month primary CSDH-recurrence definition and is followed by the same alternative symptomatic-homolateral-CSDH component.
- **Comparator matched:** Yes. The SAP boundary and the main/protocol/results-supplement boundaries refer to the same midline-shift component of the same primary endpoint, not to different outcomes or units.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. For a measured shift `x`, `x ≥ 5 mm` includes `x = 5 mm`, whereas `x > 5 mm` excludes it. “5 mm or greater” is equivalent to `x ≥ 5 mm`.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** The set difference is exactly the boundary value: `{x: x ≥ 5} \ {x: x > 5} = {5}`. This is a logical inequality comparison; rounding cannot make the printed operators equivalent.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The endpoint name, six-month time point, homolateral CSDH context, measurement unit, numerical boundary, inequality wording, and matched component wording are all printed. These inputs are sufficient to establish the cross-document boundary difference.

No supplied source explains the SAP/main-protocol-results version difference.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** Because the main article, two protocol occurrences, and the results supplement all use the inclusive boundary, the SAP’s strict inequality could be a typographic or version-specific expression. Alternatively, the SAP’s strict boundary could reflect the rule actually used. Neither explanation is stated in the supplied sources.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Directly observed: four supplied-source locations use an inclusive 5-mm boundary and the SAP uses a strict greater-than boundary for the matched endpoint component. Inferred only: that either expression is a typographic/version artifact, that one governed adjudication, or that the difference affected any participant’s classification.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Which boundary governed final endpoint adjudication and the reported analysis, was any participant classified at exactly 5 mm, and which supplied document expression—if any—requires annotation?

## C002

- **Status:** Pending Human Adjudication
- **Location found:** Yes. The full printed sequence appears in [protocol PDF p. 50](<../../../joi250033supp1_prod_1750956987.76581.pdf#page=50>) and across [SAP PDF p. 4](<../../../joi250033supp5_prod_1750956987.78281.pdf#page=4>) and [SAP PDF p. 5](<../../../joi250033supp5_prod_1750956987.78281.pdf#page=5>). The related summary appears in [main article PDF p. 3](<../../../jama_shotar_2025_oi_250033_1750956987.75881.pdf#page=3>).
- **Source value/text matched:** Yes. Protocol p. 50 prints 142 required patients per group, states that this number accounts for the two sequential tests, assumes a 20% lost-to-follow-up rate, and prints 342 total patients (171 per group). SAP pp. 4-5 prints the same 15%-to-5% effect, 80% power, overall two-sided 5% alpha, 142 per group, sequential design, 20% loss-to-follow-up assumption, and 342 total (171 per group). Main p. 3 repeats the 15% control rate, 10-percentage-point anticipated difference, 80% power, two-sided 5% alpha, 20% lost to follow-up, 342 total, and two sequential tests.
- **Comparator matched:** Yes. The 142-per-group requirement in the protocol and SAP is the stated pre-loss sample-size result for the same primary comparison whose enrollment target is 342. The main article independently repeats the same total and loss allowance, although it does not print 142 per group.
- **Consistency rule applicable:** Yes. If 20% of enrolled participants are expected to be lost and 142 evaluable participants per group are required, the retained fraction is 0.80, so enrolled participants must satisfy `enrolled × 0.80 ≥ required`.
- **Calculation or logical comparison reproduced:** `142 × 2 = 284` required evaluable participants. Direct loss-fraction inflation gives `284 / 0.80 = 355` total before balanced-arm integer rounding; per arm, `142 / 0.80 = 177.5`, requiring 178 per arm and 356 total for equal whole-number arms. Conversely, `342 × 0.80 = 273.6`, or `171 × 0.80 = 136.8` per arm. A different computation, adding 20% to the required count, gives `142 × 1.20 = 170.4`, which rounds upward to the printed 171 per group and 342 total.
- **Necessary inputs available:** The required 142 per group, equal two-group design, stated 20% loss rate, and printed 171-per-group/342-total enrollment target are available. The protocol and SAP also state that 142 already reflects the planned sequential tests.
- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The sources do not define the denominator or arithmetic convention used for “20%” loss, provide the unrounded software output for the sample-size calculation, state a separate design adjustment between 142 and 171, or explain why 20% was added rather than treated as a fraction of enrolled participants. They also do not supply a calculation trace that reconciles all printed values.
- **Source-grounded alternative interpretation:** The printed target is arithmetically compatible with treating 20% as an addition to 142 and rounding 170.4 upward to 171 per group. An unstated design-specific adjustment or unrounded underlying base could also be involved, but neither is documented on the supplied pages.
- **Direct observation versus inferred explanation:** Directly observed: 142 required per group, 20% loss assumed, and 171 enrolled per group/342 total are printed together. Direct arithmetic reproduces the mismatch under a loss-fraction convention and reproduces 171 per group under a 20%-addition convention. Inferred only: which convention the authors intended or whether an unreported sequential-design or rounding detail explains the target.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Was the printed 20% implemented as a 20% addition to the 142-per-group requirement, as a true 20% loss fraction, or through another documented sample-size calculation, and what exact unrounded calculation produced 171 per group?

- **Exact missing inputs or definitions:** The table does not define whether surgery-type rows are mutually exclusive at the participant level, whether bilateral procedures can use different techniques and be counted in both rows, whether numerators count participants or procedures when techniques overlap, or whether one printed numerator or denominator is a typographic error. Alternatively, one count or denominator could be typographic. The supplied table documents bilateral surgery in the same section but does not link any bilateral participant to both technique rows.

Inferred only: mutual exclusivity, overlap from bilateral procedures, or a typographic error.
- **Exact remaining human question:** Were surgery types mutually exclusive per participant, or was at least one standard-care participant counted in both technique rows; if overlap was allowed, what was the counting rule, and if not, which printed value requires review?
