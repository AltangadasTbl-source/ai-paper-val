The investigator must assess the seriousness of each adverse event, and record all serious and
non-serious adverse events in the e-CRF.

The investigator must document serious adverse events to the best of his/her ability, and give
the definitive medical diagnosis whenever possible.

The investigator must assess the intensity of adverse events:
- In general terms:
Mild: tolerated by the patient, does not interfere with daily activities
Moderate: sufficiently uncomfortable to affect daily activities
Severe: prevents daily activities

The investigator must assess the causal link between serious adverse events and each medical
device and/or the procedure used to install it and/or the specific acts/procedures/examinations
added by the research, and with any other treatments.

The method used by the investigator, based on the WHO (WHO Uppsala Monitoring Centre)
method, is based on the following 4 causal terms:
- Certain,
- Probable/plausible,
- Possible,
- Unlikely (not excluded).
Their definition is presented in the following table (extracted from WHO-UMC causality
categories, version of 17/04/2012).
