Chronic subdural hematoma (cSDH) is the most frequently
encountered pathology in emergency neurosurgery. The standard
treatment is burr-hole evacuation, with placement of a temporary
drain in the subdural space. Post-operative recurrence, estimated at
between 10% and 20%, is a major management challenge, due to its
frequency and the associated morbidity and mortality. Embolization
of the middle meningeal artery (MMA) has recently been proposed
as a treatment for cSDH. We hypothesize that embolization of the
MMA homolateral to a surgically evacuated cSDH in patients at high
risk of recurrence would significantly reduce the risk of postoperative
recurrence.
The main objective of the project is to evaluate the efficacy of MMA
embolization in reducing the risk of 6-month post-operative
recurrence of evacuated cSDH in patients at high risk of recurrence
compared with usual management.
The primary endpoint is the rate of cSDH recurrence 6 months after
index burr-hole surgery, defined as:
- the reappearance of a homolateral cSDH with a midline shift ≥ 5mm
or a symptomatic homolateral cSDH, including leading to death
- or, the presence of a homolateral SDH > 10mm in maximal thickness
on the 6 months control head CT scan
- or, the need for repeated surgery for a homolateral cSDH recurrence
- or, the need for a new hospital admission in relation to a
homolateral cSDH recurrence
The secondary objectives are to evaluate the efficacy of MMA
embolization in patients at high risk of recurrence compared with
usual management on the following criteria:
- the rate of repeated surgery for a homolateral cSDH recurrence
during the 6 months follow-up period
- the rate of disability and dependency at 1 and 6 months, defined by
a modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score ≥ 4.,
