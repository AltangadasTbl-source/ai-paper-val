### C02 — Inclusion label applied to an exclusion list

- **Evidence status:** Verified; accepted by the critic.
- **Category:** Presentation inconsistency.
- **Severity:** Minor.
- **Exact source locations:**
  - [jama_shotar_2025_oi_250033_1750956987.75881.pdf](../jama_shotar_2025_oi_250033_1750956987.75881.pdf), PDF p. 4, journal p. 130, Figure 1 footnote a.
  - [joi250033supp4_prod_1750956987.77981.pdf](../joi250033supp4_prod_1750956987.77981.pdf), PDF p. 8, eFigure 1 title and `Excluded (n=317)` box.
  - Context: [jama_shotar_2025_oi_250033_1750956987.75881.pdf](../jama_shotar_2025_oi_250033_1750956987.75881.pdf), PDF p. 2, journal p. 128, Methods, `Patients` subsection.
- **Reported statements and values:** The Figure 1 footnote directs the reader to reasons for excluding patients “with an inclusion criterion.” The eFigure 1 title repeats that phrase. The figure itself lists `Patient refusal (n=101)`, `Other pre-specified non-inclusion criteria (n=164)`, `Other reasons (n=18)`, and `Unknown reasons (n=34)`. The Methods separately says that specified patients “were not eligible for inclusion.”

**Reasoning procedure**

1. **Identify the compared source statements.** The main Figure 1 footnote and the supplementary eFigure 1 title describe the same detailed exclusion list. Both use “with an inclusion criterion.” The box they identify is explicitly an exclusion box and includes non-inclusion criteria, refusal, other reasons, and unknown reasons.
2. **Explain why they should agree.** The main footnote is a direct cross-reference to eFigure 1, so the cross-reference label should accurately characterize the linked figure. The eFigure title should likewise characterize the categories displayed immediately below it.
3. **Reproduce the participant-flow arithmetic.** These are derived checks from the printed values:
   - Top-level exclusions: `101 + 164 + 18 + 34 = 317`.
   - Prespecified non-inclusion subcategories: `38 + 35 + 29 + 19 + 19 + 11 + 8 + 5 = 164`.
   - Other-reason subcategories: `8 + 3 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 18`.
   - Flow into randomization: `659 assessed − 317 excluded = 342 randomized`.
4. **Perform within-figure and cross-document checks.** The main Figure 1 and supplement eFigure 1 agree on 659 assessed, 317 excluded, all four top-level exclusion counts, and 342 randomized. The error is therefore localized to the wording, not the participant arithmetic.
5. **Separate observations from derivations.** Direct observations are the exact title, footnote phrase, box label, and displayed counts. The sums above are derived arithmetic. The conclusion that a qualifier is missing is a direct linguistic comparison; no external eligibility convention is needed because the package itself uses “not eligible” and “non-inclusion.”
6. **Consider alternatives.** One possible reading is that “with an inclusion criterion” was intended as shorthand for people screened against inclusion criteria. That reading is not stated, and it does not account for refusal, other reasons, or unknown reasons. Another possibility is that a word such as “not meeting” or the prefix “non-” was lost during production; the supplied files do not establish how the wording arose.
7. **Resolve the alternatives.** The intended corrected phrase and production mechanism remain unproven, but neither is necessary to verify the inconsistency. The phrase as printed mischaracterizes the list it directly labels.

- **Supported conclusion:** The main Figure 1 cross-reference and supplementary eFigure 1 title apply an inclusion label to a displayed exclusion/non-inclusion list. This is a directly reproducible presentation inconsistency.
- **Limit on interpretation:** All displayed participant-flow totals reconcile. The evidence does not establish an incorrect exclusion count, randomization count, allocation, follow-up total, analysis population, or trial conclusion. The report does not assert a specific corrected wording or production mechanism.
- **Verification instruction:** Open the main PDF at p. 4 / journal p. 130 and read Figure 1 footnote a in full. Open the results supplement at PDF p. 8 and read the complete eFigure 1 title and the four top-level categories. Confirm that no “not meeting” or “non-” qualifier precedes “inclusion criterion,” then independently check `101 + 164 + 18 + 34 = 317` and `659 − 317 = 342`.

### C04 — MAGIC-MT usual-care event count omitted

- **Evidence status:** Verified; accepted by the critic.
- **Category:** Presentation inconsistency.
- **Severity:** Minor.
- **Exact source location:** [joi250033supp4_prod_1750956987.77981.pdf](../joi250033supp4_prod_1750956987.77981.pdf), PDF p. 14, eTable 4, MAGIC-MT row, `Primary outcome` column.
- **Reported values and statement:** The cell reports `24 patients (6.7%)` in the intervention group, followed by “as compared to (9.9%) in the usual-care group.” It then reports a between-group difference of `−3.3 percentage points` (95% CI, `−7.4 to 0.8`; *P* = `.10`). No usual-care event numerator appears before `(9.9%)`.

**Reasoning procedure**

1. **Identify the compared source values.** The comparison is between the event reporting for the intervention and usual-care groups within the MAGIC-MT primary-outcome cell, and between that cell’s formatting and the neighboring EMBOLISE and STEM cells.
2. **Explain why they should agree.** A two-arm comparison presented as counts and percentages should identify both displayed counts if both are claimed. The adjacent rows establish the table’s local pattern: EMBOLISE gives `8 patients (4.1%)` versus `23 patients (11.3%)`, and STEM gives `19 of 120 patients (16%)` versus `47 of 129 patients (36%)`.
3. **Show the relevant calculation.** From the rounded percentages alone, `6.7% − 9.9% = −3.2 percentage points`. The cell reports `−3.3 percentage points`. This 0.1-point difference is a diagnostic rounding check only and is not evidence of an error because the unrounded arm percentages are not supplied.
4. **Perform within-table checks.** Visual inspection at native page resolution confirms that the MAGIC-MT usual-care text begins with `(9.9%)`; there is no numeral between “compared to” and the opening parenthesis. The EMBOLISE and STEM rows immediately above contain numerators for both arms. The MAGIC-MT population cell reports 727 included and 722 in the intention-to-treat analysis but does not provide arm-specific denominators, so the missing numerator cannot be reconstructed from this table.
5. **Separate observations from derivations.** Direct observations are the printed intervention count, both percentages, the treatment-effect statistics, and the absence of a usual-care numerator. The `−3.2` percentage-point subtraction is derived and diagnostic. No replacement event count is derived or proposed.
6. **Consider alternatives.** The omitted count may be recoverable from an external MAGIC-MT report or underlying data, but external retrieval is outside scope. It might also be a typesetting omission, but the package does not establish a production mechanism. Neither possibility restores the count within the supplied cell.
7. **Resolve the alternatives.** The missing numerator is directly visible regardless of why it is missing. The remaining percentage, confidence interval, and *P* value do not by themselves establish a numerical contradiction.

- **Supported conclusion:** The MAGIC-MT primary-outcome summary omits the usual-care event numerator while presenting the corresponding 9.9% and while adjacent trial rows report counts for both arms. The comparative sentence is incomplete and cannot be count-verified from the supplied table.
- **Limit on interpretation:** The report does not infer the missing numerator, claim that 9.9% is wrong, or infer an error in the reported between-group difference, confidence interval, *P* value, MAGIC-MT analysis, or EMPROTECT conclusions.
- **Verification instruction:** Open the results supplement at PDF p. 14 and inspect the MAGIC-MT `Primary outcome` cell at native magnification. Confirm that no numeral precedes `(9.9%)`, compare the two-arm formatting with EMBOLISE and STEM, and confirm that the MAGIC-MT population field lacks arm-specific denominators needed to reconstruct the missing count.

### C01 — Treatment-type rows exceed a shared denominator

- **Evidence status:** Rejected.
- **Proposed category:** Arithmetic inconsistency.
- **Severity:** Not applicable because no inconsistency was verified.
- **Exact source location:** [jama_shotar_2025_oi_250033_1750956987.75881.pdf](../jama_shotar_2025_oi_250033_1750956987.75881.pdf), PDF p. 5, journal p. 131, Table 1, `Treatment` section, standard-care column and footnote e.
- **Reported values and statements:** `Trepanation burr hole craniostomy` is `146/163 (89.6%)`; `Trephine craniostomy` is `18/163 (11.0%)`. Footnote e says craniostomy procedures were performed either with a cranial drill or a skull trephine cylindrical saw. The same column reports `Unilateral CSDH surgery` as `137/163 (84.0%)` and `Bilateral CSDH surgery` as `26/163 (16.0%)`.

**Reasoning procedure**

1. **Identify the values compared.** The candidate adds the two adjacent craniostomy-technique numerators and percentages because both rows display denominator 163.
2. **Explain the proposed reconciliation rule.** The candidate would be verified only if the two technique rows were mutually exclusive patient-level categories that partitioned the 163 patients. Under that rule, the total could not exceed 163.
3. **Reproduce every arithmetic check.** These are derived from the printed values:
   - `146 + 18 = 164`, which is one more than 163.
   - `146 / 163 × 100 = 89.5706%`, which rounds to the reported 89.6%.
   - `18 / 163 × 100 = 11.0429%`, which rounds to the reported 11.0%.
   - `89.5706% + 11.0429% = 100.6135%`, or 100.6% after one-decimal rounding.
   - The laterality rows do partition the same denominator: `137 + 26 = 163` and `84.0% + 16.0% = 100.0%` as displayed.
4. **Perform within-table checks.** Each technique percentage is individually correct for denominator 163, so this is not a percentage-transcription problem. The embolization-arm technique rows also reconcile: `150 + 17 = 167` and `89.8% + 10.2% = 100.0%`.
5. **Separate observations from derivations.** The row values, laterality values, and footnote are direct observations. The sums and calculated percentages are derived. Mutual exclusivity at the patient level is not a direct observation.
6. **Consider alternatives.** The table documents 26 bilateral-surgery patients. A bilateral patient may have had more than one operative opening, and the footnote describes procedures/openings rather than explicitly defining mutually exclusive patient classifications. A patient could therefore potentially contribute to both technique rows. Conversely, the word “either” could be read as making the techniques exclusive for each procedure, but procedure-level exclusivity does not establish patient-level exclusivity.
7. **Resolve the alternatives.** The source package does not state the unit of counting for these two rows or provide patient-level procedure coding. Because a plausible package-grounded nonexclusive explanation remains and mutual exclusivity is unproven, the apparent excess cannot be called a document-verifiable arithmetic inconsistency.

- **Supported conclusion:** The printed technique numerators total 164 against a displayed denominator of 163, but the candidate’s necessary patient-level mutual-exclusivity premise is not established. The inconsistency as formulated is Rejected.
- **Limit on interpretation:** Rejection does not prove that both counts are correct. It means only that the supplied aggregate table cannot distinguish a legitimate multi-procedure count from a numerator, denominator, or labeling error. No corrected cell value is inferred.
- **Verification instruction:** Review the source case coding or an explicit table definition for the unit of count. Determine whether any bilateral-surgery patient received openings using both techniques. Call an arithmetic error only if the two technique categories were mutually exclusive per patient; otherwise clarify the table’s counting rule.

### C03 — Full-analysis-set labels beside observed denominators

- **Evidence status:** Rejected.
- **Proposed category:** Presentation inconsistency.
- **Severity:** Not applicable because the package resolves the candidate as formulated.
- **Exact source locations:**
  - [joi250033supp4_prod_1750956987.77981.pdf](../joi250033supp4_prod_1750956987.77981.pdf), PDF p. 9, eFigure 2 primary-analysis row.
  - [joi250033supp4_prod_1750956987.77981.pdf](../joi250033supp4_prod_1750956987.77981.pdf), PDF p. 15, eTable 4, EMPROTECT primary-outcome cell.
  - Resolving context: [jama_shotar_2025_oi_250033_1750956987.75881.pdf](../jama_shotar_2025_oi_250033_1750956987.75881.pdf), PDF p. 4 / journal p. 130, Figure 1 primary-analysis boxes; PDF p. 6 / journal p. 132, Table 2 primary row and footnotes a–c.
- **Reported values and statements:** eFigure 2 labels the primary population `Full analysis set, multiple imputation` and displays `24/162` versus `33/157`, adjusted OR `0.64` (95% CI, `0.36 to 1.14`), *P* = `.13`. eTable 4 repeats `24/162 (14.8%)` versus `33/157 (21.0%)`, followed by `ITT with imputation` and the same OR, CI, and *P* value. Main Figure 1 shows 171 participants per arm in the primary analysis, with 162 evaluable plus 9 imputed cases and 157 evaluable plus 14 imputed cases. Main Table 2 footnote a states that case numbers and percentages are observed values before multiple imputation; footnotes b–c describe post-imputation treatment-effect estimation.

**Reasoning procedure**

1. **Identify the compared values and labels.** The candidate compares the full-analysis-set / intention-to-treat imputation labels with the displayed observed fractions `24/162` and `33/157`.
2. **Explain why they might appear not to reconcile.** If the fractions were asserted to be event counts over the imputed analysis denominator, the denominators would be expected to be 171 and 171. The supplementary displays do not repeat the main table’s explanatory footnote next to the fractions.
3. **Reproduce the denominator and percentage checks.** These are derived checks:
   - `162 evaluable + 9 imputed = 171 randomized/analyzed` in the embolization arm.
   - `157 evaluable + 14 imputed = 171 randomized/analyzed` in the standard-care arm.
   - `24 / 162 × 100 = 14.8148%`, which rounds to the displayed 14.8%.
   - `33 / 157 × 100 = 21.0191%`, which rounds to the displayed 21.0%.
   - Diagnostic only: `24 / 171 = 14.0351%` and `33 / 171 = 19.2982%`. These are not authoritative imputed event rates because multiple imputation does not convert pooled inference into a single integer numerator over 171.
4. **Perform the cross-document check.** Main Table 2 footnote a explicitly assigns the displayed case numbers and percentages to observed pre-imputation values. Footnotes b and c assign the adjusted absolute difference, OR, CI, and *P* value to the post-imputation intention-to-treat/full-analysis-set model. The same split presentation explains eFigure 2 and eTable 4.
5. **Separate observations from derivations.** Direct observations are the population labels, fractions, effect estimates, Figure 1 analysis counts, and Table 2 footnotes. The sums and percentage recalculations are derived. The hypothetical 24/171 and 33/171 calculations are explicitly diagnostic and are not proposed corrections.
6. **Consider alternatives.** In isolation, eFigure 2 and eTable 4 could be clearer by labeling the fractions as observed. However, a package-level ambiguity is not the same as a contradiction when the main article explicitly defines the descriptive-versus-inferential split. The alternative reading—that the full-analysis-set label applies to the fitted effect estimate while the fractions remain observed descriptors—is directly supported by Table 2 footnotes a–c.
7. **Resolve the alternatives.** Because the supplied package expressly explains the mixed display, the candidate does not establish an inconsistency. No assumption about the exact imputed event total is needed or permitted.

- **Supported conclusion:** The observed fractions and the multiply imputed inferential result refer to different but explicitly defined parts of the display. C03 is Rejected as a reporting inconsistency.
- **Limit on interpretation:** The supplementary figure and table may be less self-contained than the main table, but this report does not elevate that possible clarity concern to a verified presentation inconsistency. Exact reproduction of the adjusted, multiply imputed OR requires the patient-level data, imputation model, fitted models for all imputed datasets, and Rubin-rule pooling output.
- **Verification instruction:** Read eFigure 2 and eTable 4 together with main Figure 1 and Table 2 footnotes a–c. Confirm the two denominator sums, reproduce the observed percentages, and verify that footnote a governs counts/percentages while footnotes b–c govern post-imputation effects. Use original model output—not 24/171 or 33/171—to audit the adjusted imputed estimate.
