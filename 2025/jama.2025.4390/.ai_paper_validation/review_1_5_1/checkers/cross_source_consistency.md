## Candidate proposals for human verification

### Proposal A — Figure 3 all-patient rate values conflict with the matched primary-outcome rate

- **Category:** Cross-document numeric inconsistency; measure, label, or scale inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** DOC-001 Figure 3, `jama_garrison_2025_oi_250019_1749674951.29054.pdf#page=9`; DOC-001 Table 2, `jama_garrison_2025_oi_250019_1749674951.29054.pdf#page=8`; abstract/narrative, `...pdf#page=1` and `...pdf#page=6`.
- **Printed values:** Figure 3 identifies the same composite primary outcome and all-patient event counts, 163 bedtime and 173 morning, then prints `71.0` and `71.0` under columns headed `Rate per 100 patient-years`. Table 2 prints the same allocation-specific composite event counts, with rates `2.30` and `2.44` per 100 patient-years; abstract/narrative print `2.3` and `2.4`.
- **Comparison logic:** Population, ITT arms, outcome, event counts, and rate unit match. A precision difference could account for 2.30 versus 2.3, but cannot account for 71.0 versus either 2.30 or 2.44. Thus the Figure 3 all-patient values do not reconcile with the matched rate values under the printed unit.
- **Supported alternatives:** The Figure 3 header or values may contain a production/transcription error; `71.0` may be an unintended quantity with a different unprinted denominator/scale; or Table 2 and the narrative may use a different rate calculation despite their matching outcome and all-patient event counts. The supplied package does not define an alternative rate scale for Figure 3.
- **Human verification steps:** Inspect the journal production source/figure dataset; calculate both arm-specific person-time rates from the locked analysis dataset; confirm whether Figure 3 should display 2.30/2.44 or another explicitly labeled measure; then verify all subgroup-column values and header together.

### Proposal B — Bedtime diuretic six-month timing counts differ between eFigure 4 and eTable 6

- **Category:** Cross-document numeric inconsistency; denominator, proportion, or total inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** DOC-004 eFigure 4, `joi250019supp3_prod_1749674951.30054.pdf#page=26`; DOC-004 eTable 6, `...pdf#page=42` (table begins p.41).
- **Printed values:** For the same six-month bedtime/PM diuretic class total of 424, eFigure 4 displays `278` as allocated, `138` off allocation, and `8` twice or more daily. eTable 6 displays `277/424 (65.3%)`, `139/424 (32.8%)`, and `8/424 (1.9%)`, respectively.
- **Comparison logic:** The population (bedtime allocation), time point (six months), class (diuretic), three mutually exclusive timing categories, and total (424) are identical. Both triplets sum to 424, but their allocation-category counts differ by one in opposing directions. eFigure's 278/424 rounds to 65.6%, not its table's 65.3%; eTable's displayed counts are arithmetically consistent with its displayed percentages at normal one-decimal rounding.
- **Supported alternatives:** One source may have been updated after the other; the figure may reflect a different coding cut or pre-final dataset; or one of the two category labels/counts may have been transposed. The captions and table notes provide no different analysis-set or timing definition that resolves the difference.
- **Human verification steps:** Reproduce the diuretic medication-level records used for eFigure 4/eTable 6, verify the once-daily/timing coding for the one discrepant medicine, determine the intended locked table, and correct/relabel the discordant display if necessary.

### Proposal C — eTable 5 prints an `Other` ethnicity row that duplicates White/Caucasian values

- **Category:** Numeric or arithmetic inconsistency; measure, label, or scale inconsistency.
- **Exact source locations:** DOC-004 eTable 5, `joi250019supp3_prod_1749674951.30054.pdf#page=37`; randomized baseline eTable 3, `...pdf#page=29`.
- **Printed values:** eTable 5 is explicitly for unable-to-follow participants, morning n=44 and bedtime n=57. Under ethnicity it prints White/Caucasian `40 (90.9%)` and `53 (93.0%)`; it later prints `Other` with exactly the same `40 (90.9%)` and `53 (93.0%)`. In the randomized baseline eTable 3, `Other` is only 9/1677 (0.5%) bedtime and 5/1680 (0.3%) morning, whereas White is 1565/1677 and 1587/1680.
- **Comparison logic:** A mutually exclusive ethnicity row labeled `Other` cannot contain the same near-total counts and percentages as the preceding White/Caucasian row in the same n=44/n=57 table. It also exceeds the entire randomized baseline `Other` totals in the corresponding allocation arms (40>5 morning and 53>9 bedtime). This is an exact printed-row/label contradiction, not a rounding difference.
- **Supported alternatives:** The `Other` values may be a duplicated White row caused by table-production error; `Other` may have been intended to display a different row's values; or a label could have been misplaced. The source does not state that ethnicity categories are overlapping, and its row layout presents them as standard categories.
- **Human verification steps:** Inspect the eTable 5 analysis export and raw baseline-ethnicity coding for the 44/57 unable-to-follow groups; verify whether ethnicity rows are mutually exclusive; replace the duplicated values or row label with the validated values; recheck the ethnicity P value after correction.
