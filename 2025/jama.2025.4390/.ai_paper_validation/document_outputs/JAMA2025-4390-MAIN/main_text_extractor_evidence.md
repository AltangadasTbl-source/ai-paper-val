## Design, population, and outcome definitions

| Evidence | Exact location |
|---|---|
| Open-label, pragmatic randomized trial; 1:1 bedtime vs morning use of all once-daily antihypertensive medicines. Randomized: bedtime **n=1677**, morning **n=1680**; total **N=3357**. | Abstract, PDF p. 1 / printed p. 2061; Results—Trial Participants, PDF p. 4 / printed p. 2064; Figure 1, same page. |
| Primary outcome: time to first all-cause death or hospitalization/emergency-department (ED) visit for stroke, acute coronary syndrome, or heart failure. | Methods—Outcomes—Primary Outcome, PDF p. 3 / printed p. 2063. |
| Secondary outcomes: each primary-component outcome; all-cause unplanned hospitalization/ED visit; nursing-home admission; nonvertebral fracture; new glaucoma diagnosis; 18-month cognitive decline. Other safety outcomes include subjective vision worsening, new impairment consistent with dementia, light-headedness, fainting, falling, hip fracture, and nocturia. | Methods—Outcomes—Secondary Outcomes, PDF p. 3 / printed p. 2063. |
| Median follow-up was **4.6 years** in each group. The abstract reports 56.4% female, median age 67 years, and 53.7% taking monotherapy. | Abstract, PDF p. 1 / printed p. 2061. |

## Main-article conclusion claim

The conclusion states that bedtime administration was safe but did not reduce cardiovascular risk, and that administration time did not affect the benefits or risks of BP-lowering medication. This conclusion is stated in the Abstract (PDF p. 1 / printed p. 2061) and Conclusions section (PDF p. 10 / printed p. 2070).
