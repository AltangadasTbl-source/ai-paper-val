C01 — Period 3 augmented-protein sex counts do not reconcile to the displayed denominator

  • Evidence status: Verified; accepted by critic.
  • Category: Arithmetic inconsistency.
  • Severity: Minor.
  • Exact source locations: joi250040supp3_prod_1753124024.38098.pdf, PDF p. 10, supplemental p. 10, eTable 4, “Period 3 – Augmented Protein” Sex rows; aggregate comparison in jama_summers_2025_oi_250040_1753124024.36498.pdf, PDF p. 5, journal p. 323, Table 1, Sex rows.
  • Reported values: eTable 4 displays Period 3 augmented protein as 4 units, n = 551, Male 359 (65.2), and Female 190 (34.5). The other augmented-protein periods report male/female counts of 303/177, 187/111, and 220/132. Table 1 reports overall augmented-protein Male 1070 (63.7) and Female 611 (36.3) among n = 1681. No missing/other sex row or explanatory footnote is displayed in eTable 4.

Reasoning procedure

  1. Direct within-column count check. The two displayed Period 3 sex categories sum to

    359 + 190 = 549.

    Compared with the displayed Period 3 denominator,

    551 − 549 = 2.

    The two visible sex counts therefore leave two of the 551 participants unreconciled.

  2. Direct within-column percentage check. The printed percentages sum to

    65.2% + 34.5% = 99.7%,

    leaving 100.0% − 99.7% = 0.3 percentage points. Separately, using the printed denominator gives 359 / 551 × 100 = 65.154%, which rounds to 65.2%, and 190 / 551 × 100 = 34.483%, which rounds to 34.5%. Thus, each printed percentage is reproducible using 551 even though the displayed category counts total only 549.

  3. Other-period control check. The other augmented-protein sex rows each reconcile exactly to their period denominator:

    • Period 1: 303 + 177 = 480.
    • Period 2: 187 + 111 = 298.
    • Period 4: 220 + 132 = 352.

    This confines the visible within-period deficit to Period 3.

  4. Between-table denominator check. The four augmented-protein period denominators sum to

    480 + 298 + 551 + 352 = 1681,

    matching the overall augmented-protein denominator in Table 1. Across those periods, however:

    • Male: 303 + 187 + 359 + 220 = 1069, versus 1070 in Table 1; deficit 1.
    • Female: 177 + 111 + 190 + 132 = 610, versus 611 in Table 1; deficit 1.
    • Combined: 1069 + 610 = 1679, versus 1681; deficit 2.

  5. Observation versus derivation. The denominators, counts, percentages, row labels, and absence of another displayed category are direct source observations. The sums, deficits, and recalculated percentages are derived arithmetic from those printed values.

  6. Alternative interpretations. The visible record is compatible with an omitted/missing category, one or more incorrect period counts, or another production/data-display explanation. The package does not establish which mechanism occurred and does not establish corrected participant-level values. Those alternatives do not remove the locked finding that the displayed table fails to reconcile; they limit the conclusion to presentation of the reported counts.

  • Existing supported conclusion: The displayed Period 3 augmented-protein sex counts are two below the displayed period denominator and also leave one male and one female unreconciled against the aggregate Table 1 totals.
  • Limit on interpretation: This finding does not identify which participant records, counts, or categories should change and does not allege an error in trial outcomes or conclusions.
  • Verification instruction: Re-add the two Period 3 sex counts and all four augmented-period male and female counts. Reconcile them with n = 551 and the Table 1 totals 1070 + 611 = 1681. Confirm whether the table requires corrected counts or disclosure of a missing/other category.

C02 — Protocol-deviation participant percentage matches the event count rather than the participant count

  • Evidence status: Verified; accepted by critic.
  • Category: Arithmetic inconsistency.
  • Severity: Minor.
  • Exact source location: joi250040supp3_prod_1753124024.38098.pdf, PDF p. 16, supplemental p. 16, eTable 8, “Total protocol deviations” in the Augmented Protein column.
  • Reported values: The augmented-protein column header is n = 1681. Under “Total protocol deviations, n (%)”, the table reports “Number of participants with at least one event” as 151 (9.4) and the next row, “Number of events,” as 158.

Reasoning procedure

  1. Identify the denominator rule. The column supplies the treatment-group denominator 1681, and the section label specifies n (%). The percentage beside the participant count is therefore checked against 1681.

  2. Reproduce the participant percentage. From the displayed participant count:

    151 / 1681 × 100 = 8.982748...%.

    Rounded to the one decimal place used in the table, this is 9.0%, not 9.4%.

  3. Compare the event count. From the separately displayed number of events:

    158 / 1681 × 100 = 9.399167...%,

    which rounds to the printed 9.4%.

  4. Observation versus derivation. The denominator, labels, participant count, event count, and printed percentage are direct observations. The percentages above are derived from the printed values.

  5. Alternative interpretations. The exact match to 158/1681 supports the completed workflow’s localized explanation, but the package does not establish the production mechanism. The report therefore does not claim that an event count was literally copied or used in source code.

  • Existing supported conclusion: The printed 9.4% beside 151 participants is not the one-decimal percentage of 151/1681 and numerically equals the one-decimal percentage of 158/1681.
  • Limit on interpretation: The finding does not dispute the participant count, event count, denominator, or the clinical meaning of the deviations; it identifies a displayed percentage inconsistency only.
  • Verification instruction: Recalculate 151/1681 and 158/1681 to one decimal place from the editorial source table. Confirm whether the participant entry should be 151 (9.0%) or whether another displayed value or denominator requires correction.

C03 — Figure 1 uses patient-level randomization wording although ICUs were randomized

  • Evidence status: Verified; accepted by critic.
  • Category: Presentation inconsistency.
  • Severity: Minor.
  • Exact source locations: jama_summers_2025_oi_250040_1753124024.36498.pdf, PDF p. 3, journal p. 321, Figure 1; comparison statements in PDF p. 2, journal p. 320, Design/Trial Procedures and PDF p. 8, journal p. 326, Limitations.
  • Reported statements and values: Figure 1 contains patient-count boxes reading 1043 Randomized to augmented protein group, 1015 Randomized to usual protein group, 650 Randomized to augmented protein group and included in primary outcome analysis, and 703 Randomized to usual protein group and included in the primary outcome analysis. The Methods call the trial cluster randomized, state that ICUs were randomly assigned, and say participants commenced the formula to which the ICU was randomized. The Limitations explicitly state that randomization occurred at the cluster level rather than the patient level.

Reasoning procedure

  1. Identify the compared units. The Methods identify eight ICUs as the clusters and state that ICUs were randomly assigned to treatment sequences. Figure 1 correctly begins with eight ICUs and then two branches of four ICUs, but four downstream boxes attach “Randomized to” directly to patient counts.

  2. Check the patient pathways. The figure’s counts reconcile arithmetically:

    • 1130 screened − 87 excluded = 1043 patients in an augmented-protein period.
    • 1040 screened − 25 excluded = 1015 patients in a usual-protein period.
    • 683 screened − 33 excluded = 650 patients in an augmented-protein period.
    • 746 screened − 43 excluded = 703 patients in a usual-protein period.
    • Augmented primary analysis: 1043 − 12 consent withdrawals + 650 = 1681.
    • Usual primary analysis: 1015 − 2 consent withdrawals + 703 = 1716.
    • Overall primary analysis: 1681 + 1716 = 3397, matching the Results text.

    These checks support the critic’s restriction of the issue to terminology rather than participant-flow arithmetic.

  3. Compare the grammatical claim. A patient box saying “1043 Randomized to…” grammatically presents those patients as the randomized units. That wording conflicts with the explicit statement that clusters/ICUs, not patients, were randomized. Participants were exposed or assigned according to the ICU’s randomized sequence.

  4. Observation versus derivation. The labels, Methods statements, and displayed counts are direct observations. The pathway sums are derived checks showing that the counts themselves reconcile.

  5. Alternative interpretations. Readers may understand “randomized to” as shorthand for assignment under a cluster-randomized design, particularly because the upper figure boxes identify randomized ICUs. That recoverable context supports the locked Minor severity, but it does not eliminate the mismatch between the downstream wording and the stated randomization unit.

  • Existing supported conclusion: The four downstream patient boxes use patient-level randomization language inconsistent with the article’s explicit ICU/cluster randomization unit.
  • Limit on interpretation: The finding does not allege incorrect participant counts, incorrect sequence allocation, or a failure of randomization.
  • Verification instruction: Compare the four Figure 1 patient-box labels with the Design, Trial Procedures, and Limitations statements. Confirm the reconciled counts above, then assess replacing patient-level Randomized to with Assigned/allocated to, Received treatment during, or equivalent cluster-period wording.

C04 — Bayesian primary-outcome row labels median/IQR values as mean (SD)

  • Evidence status: Verified; accepted by critic.
  • Category: Statistical reporting inconsistency.
  • Severity: Minor.
  • Exact source locations: jama_summers_2025_oi_250040_1753124024.36498.pdf, PDF p. 7, journal p. 325, Table 2, Primary outcome secondary analyses, Bayesian quantile mixed-model row; comparison in joi250040supp3_prod_1753124024.38098.pdf, PDF p. 27, supplemental p. 27, eFigure 6.
  • Reported values and labels: The Bayesian quantile mixed-model row ends with mean (SD) but reports augmented protein 62.0 (0 to 77), usual protein 64.0 (0 to 77), and Median difference, −1.50 (−3.86 to 0.90). The primary quantile mixed-model row immediately above labels the identical group summaries median (IQR). eFigure 6 reports Median Difference: −1.50 (95% CrI: −3.86, 0.90).

Reasoning procedure

  1. Within-table row comparison. The primary row identifies 62.0 (0 to 77) and 64.0 (0 to 77) as median (IQR). The Bayesian row repeats both three-number summaries exactly but changes the descriptive label to mean (SD).

  2. Model and effect-scale comparison. The row is named a Bayesian quantile mixed model and its effect is explicitly a median difference, not a mean difference. eFigure 6 independently repeats the point estimate and interval on the median-difference scale.

  3. Interval integrity check. The figure and table agree on the point estimate and endpoints: −3.86 ≤ −1.50 ≤ 0.90. The problem is therefore not endpoint order, point-estimate containment, or transcription of the Bayesian result; it is the group-summary label.

  4. Format check. A mean (SD) display ordinarily contains one center and one SD, such as the adjacent linear mixed-model cells 47.5 (34.0) and 48.9 (33.5). By contrast, the Bayesian cells contain a center plus lower and upper endpoints, exactly matching the median (IQR) row.

  5. Observation versus derivation. The labels, model names, values, and eFigure annotation are direct source observations. The equality and containment comparisons are derived logical checks.

  6. Alternative interpretations. The two values inside parentheses cannot be reconciled with the row’s literal one-SD label, and the package provides convergent median terminology. The report does not infer any change to the Bayesian estimate, credible interval, posterior probability, or model.

  • Existing supported conclusion: mean (SD) is inconsistent with the displayed group summaries and the median-based Bayesian analysis; the completed record supports median (IQR) as the intended descriptive label.
  • Limit on interpretation: The finding is confined to the row label and does not challenge the estimate −1.50, its 95% credible interval, or the Bayesian analysis.
  • Verification instruction: Compare the two Table 2 primary-outcome rows cell by cell and confirm eFigure 6’s effect label and endpoints. Determine whether the Bayesian row label should be corrected from mean (SD) to median (IQR).

C05 — Main-table ventilation summaries are labeled mean (SD) while the supplement identifies median (IQR) summaries

  • Evidence status: Verified; accepted by critic.
  • Category: Cross-document inconsistency.
  • Severity: Minor.
  • Exact source locations: jama_summers_2025_oi_250040_1753124024.36498.pdf, PDF p. 7, journal p. 325, Table 2, “Duration of invasive ventilation” row; joi250040supp3_prod_1753124024.38098.pdf, PDF p. 18, supplemental p. 18, eTable 10 and footer; and joi250040supp3_prod_1753124024.38098.pdf, PDF p. 4, supplemental p. 4, eMethods, “Duration of invasive ventilation”.
  • Reported values and labels: Table 2 labels the row Duration of invasive ventilation, mean (SD), h but gives augmented protein 84.0 (35.0 to 178.9) and usual protein 78.0 (33.2 to 161.0). Its model effect is Mean difference, 6.8 (−3.0 to 16.5). eTable 10 presents period-level “Hours of invasive mechanical ventilation in ICU” as three-number summaries and states, Data presented as median (IQR) or n (%). The eMethods say a tobit mixed-effects model compared mean duration and that the treatment effect estimate is presented as a difference in means with 95% CI.

Reasoning procedure

  1. Main-table format check. Each group cell has a center plus two endpoints: 84.0, 35.0, 178.9 and 78.0, 33.2, 161.0. This does not match the literal mean (SD) format, which would contain a center and one SD.

  2. Cross-document outcome match. eTable 10 uses the same outcome (hours of invasive mechanical ventilation in ICU) and the same three-number descriptive pattern across periods. Its footer explicitly defines continuous entries as median (IQR). The supplement therefore identifies the descriptive convention applied to the period-level versions of this outcome.

  3. Separate descriptive summaries from model effect. The eMethods specify that the tobit mixed-effects model’s treatment effect estimate is a difference in means. Table 2 follows that instruction with Mean difference, 6.8 (−3.0 to 16.5). This model-effect scale does not make the separate treatment-group descriptive cells means (SDs).

  4. Interval integrity check. The treatment-effect interval is ordered and contains the point estimate: −3.0 ≤ 6.8 ≤ 16.5. The locked issue does not concern that interval.

  5. Observation versus derivation. The labels, cells, footer, and eMethods wording are direct observations. The format distinction and containment check are derived comparisons.

  6. Alternative interpretations. The main-table group values are overall treatment-group summaries, whereas eTable 10 displays summaries by period. That difference in aggregation does not erase the label conflict: both use three-number group summaries for the same outcome, and the supplement expressly labels the descriptive convention median (IQR). The package does not establish that the model effect should change.

  • Existing supported conclusion: The main article’s mean (SD) label conflicts with the displayed three-number group summaries and the supplement’s median (IQR) convention for the same outcome.
  • Limit on interpretation: This finding does not challenge the tobit model, the model-based mean difference of 6.8 hours, its confidence interval, or the clinical interpretation of the outcome.
  • Verification instruction: Compare the Table 2 group-cell format with the eTable 10 ventilation row and footer, then read the eMethods sentence defining the modeled treatment effect. Determine whether the main-table group-summary label should be median (IQR) while retaining the separately labeled mean-difference effect if correct.

C06 — Discussion calls day-10 urea concentrations means although displayed summaries are medians (IQR)

  • Evidence status: Verified by evidence verifier; Uncertain and not accepted as a final issue by critic.
  • Category: Statistical reporting inconsistency.
  • Potential severity: Uncertain.
  • Exact source locations: jama_summers_2025_oi_250040_1753124024.36498.pdf, PDF p. 8, journal p. 326, Discussion; jama_summers_2025_oi_250040_1753124024.36498.pdf, PDF p. 5, journal p. 323, Biochemical Outcomes; and joi250040supp3_prod_1753124024.38098.pdf, PDF p. 19, supplemental p. 19, eTable 11, Blood urea, Day 10.
  • Reported statements and values: The Discussion states, In this trial, mean urea concentrations at day 10 were higher in the augmented protein group. The Results state that day-10 median (IQR) blood urea was 13.0 (8.2–18.8) mmol/L in the augmented-protein group and 10.6 (7.1–15.4) mmol/L in the usual-protein group. eTable 11 repeats those values, with sample sizes n = 439 and n = 417, and its footer states Data are Median (IQR).

Reasoning procedure

  1. Match outcome, time point, groups, and direction. All cited passages concern blood urea at day 10, compare augmented with usual protein, and describe the augmented group as having the higher central value.

  2. Identify the terminology difference. The numeric Results and eTable 11 explicitly report medians and IQRs, whereas the Discussion uses the word mean without displaying arithmetic mean values.

  3. Diagnostic numeric comparison. The displayed medians differ by 13.0 − 10.6 = 2.4 mmol/L. This calculation describes the printed median contrast only; it is not an estimate of the unreported difference in arithmetic means.

  4. State the missing inferential information. The package does not provide day-10 arithmetic means, SDs, an analysis table identifying a mean contrast, or source model output showing that the Discussion sentence refers to calculated means. It also does not state that “mean” was intended as a synonym for the displayed medians.

  5. Why the critic retained uncertainty. A distribution may simultaneously have a higher mean and a higher median. Therefore, the real terminology mismatch verifies the evidence basis of the candidate, but the supplied documents alone do not prove that the statement about means is false or that it purports to restate the displayed medians. This is why the verifier’s Verified status and the critic’s Uncertain/not accepted decision can both be preserved without conflict.

  6. Observation versus derivation. The Discussion wording, median labels, values, IQRs, and sample sizes are direct observations. The 2.4 mmol/L median difference and the logical possibility of distinct mean and median comparisons are explanatory checks.

  • Existing supported conclusion: The package directly supports a terminology difference between the Discussion’s mean wording and the only displayed day-10 summaries, which are medians (IQR).
  • Limit on interpretation: The package does not establish that the augmented group’s arithmetic mean was not higher, so it does not prove a substantive contradiction or justify changing the locked critic decision.
  • Verification instruction: Obtain the editorial analysis output or source data summary for day-10 blood urea. Determine whether arithmetic means were calculated and compared. If no mean analysis exists and the Discussion refers to the displayed summaries, assess changing mean to median; if a mean comparison exists, report or document the corresponding mean values and analysis so the sentence is traceable.
