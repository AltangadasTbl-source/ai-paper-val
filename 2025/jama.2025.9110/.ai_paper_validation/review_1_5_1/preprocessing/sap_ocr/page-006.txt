113 BACKGROUND

114 Approximately one in four critically ill patients who receive enteral nutrition (EN) in the
115 intensive care unit (ICU) do not survive to hospital discharge(1). For those that do survive,
116 rapid and substantial muscle wasting occurs(2, 3), which is strongly associated with adverse
117 patient-centered outcomes(4-6). International critical care nutrition guidelines recommend
118 protein delivery of at least 1.2g/kg body weight/day(7, 8). Critically ill patients typically
119 receive dietary protein at rates <1.0 g/kg body weight/day(9, 10) and it has been proposed
120 that augmenting dietary protein in critical illness could improve outcomes(11, 12). However,
121 it is unclear from existing data whether augmenting dietary protein in the ICU improves
122 patient outcomes, with existing meta-analysis indicating that increased protein could reduce
123 mortality by up to 12% or increasing mortality by up to 11%(13, 14).

124

125 The TARGET Protein trial is a cluster randomized, cross-sectional, double cross-over,
126 __registry-embedded, pragmatic open-label clinical trial with the aim of evaluating the effect of
127 _ higher enteral protein delivery (augmented protein) on clinical outcomes of critically ill adult
128 patients when compared to usual care(15). This manuscript describes the statistical analysis
129 plan for the TARGET Protein trial and supersedes the plan provided in the previously
130 published protocol(15) and trial registration (Australian New Zealand Clinical Trials
131 Registry, ACTRN12621001484831).
