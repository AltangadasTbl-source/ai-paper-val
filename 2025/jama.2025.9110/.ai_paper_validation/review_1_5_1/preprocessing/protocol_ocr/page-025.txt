813 2.3.10 (a) involvement in the research carries no more than low risk to participants; This study is
814 comparing two protein prescriptions using enteral formulae with differing protein content that are
815 frequently used in ICU patients in Australia (ie. the protein prescriptions being compared are neither
816 new nor experimental). The TARGET Protein study is low risk to participants, as both dietary
817 approaches are acceptable as current patient management as recommended in international critical
818 care nutrition guidelines. Neither dietary approach in this study expose participants to protein doses
819 outside of international guideline recommendations. Moreover, we have previously shown in our
820 multi-centre feasibility trial that we can safely deliver protein doses within international
821 recommendations using the interventional formulae and protein doses consistent with current
822 Australian practice using the usual care formulae (intervention: 1.52 (0.52) vs. usual care: 0.99 (0.27)
823g of protein/kg ideal body weight (IBW)/day; group difference 0.53 (95% CI 0.38 to 0.69)
824 Furthermore, the difference in mortality in this feasibility trial favoured augmented protein delivery
825 (difference between groups using generalised estimating equation -1.3% (95% CI -17.7 to 15.0%).
826 We submit that allowing withdrawal of enrolled participants (such as with a delayed or an ‘opt-out’
827 approach) may introduce bias into mortality estimates, which could affect the accuracy of mortality
828 estimates, potentially harming future participants.

830 2.3.10 (b) the benefits from the research justify any risks of harm associated with not seeking
831 consent; The Investigators have exercised beneficence designing the TARGET Protein protocol with
832 maximum benefit and harm minimisation to participants at the forefront. For example, the
833 interventional therapy falls within current guidelines, the data management plan protects participant
834 privacy and identity, safety event reporting guidelines will be followed, and a study monitoring plan
835 will be in place.
836
837 The potential benefit of participation for the individual is that preliminary evidence supports the
838 concept that the intervention dietary approach (increasing protein) may improve outcomes when
839 compared to current usual care. TARGET Protein will lead to an increased understanding and an
840 improvement in care of critically ill patients and inform international guidelines on enteral nutrition in
841 this patient group. This may lead to improved outcomes of critically ill patients and reduced
842 _hospital/health care costs.
