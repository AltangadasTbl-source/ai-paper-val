709 10.2 Ethical implications of a cross over cluster randomised control design
710 ~The rationale for the use of a cluster randomised crossover (CRXO) trial is that this is the most
711 — appropriate method to answer the research question. The effect size of the intervention, augmenting
712 dietary protein, is relatively small and a large number of participants would be required using a
713 traditional two arm randomised control trial (RCT) making recruitment prohibitive, even across
714 multiple intensive care units, Employing a CRXO design enables recruitment at the institutional level
715 with reduced burden on participating sites in regard to study logistics and data collection and allowing
716 for a larger number of participants recruited at a feasible cost from a funding perspective. Perhaps
717 most importantly, by reducing logistics it reduces risk of incorrect formula being administered —
718 — which has implications for individual patient safety and trial integrity. Finally, as feeding protocols
719 vary between ICUs the cross-over component of a cluster randomised cross sectional trial ensures
720 variability and intraclass coefficient is reduced. Accordingly, our design has similar power as a
721 parallel group trial at markedly reduced cost with greater safety.
722
723 As the intervention will be given to all eligible patients in participating ICUs as part of ‘usual practice’
724 — for that ICU, large numbers of patients will participate and the investigators propose that the most
725 — suitable and feasible method of enrolment in the study will be without individual patient consent.
726 Additionally, it is not possible to identify and consent potential cluster participants prior to unit
727 randomisation due to the very nature of ICU patients’ emergency admission status.
