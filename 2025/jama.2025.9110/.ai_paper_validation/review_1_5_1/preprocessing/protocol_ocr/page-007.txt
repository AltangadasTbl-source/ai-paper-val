211 3. LAY SUMMARY

212 An intensive care unit (ICU) stay is associated with significant muscle wasting in up to 80% of

213 critically ill patients. This muscle wasting results in ‘ICU-acquired weakness’ that is associated with

214 slower weaning from ventilator support, longer time to discharge alive from ICU and hospital, higher

215 in-hospital costs which persist well after discharge from the acute care setting. Nutrition therapy,

216 delivered as liquid nutrition via a tube into the stomach, forms usual care for critically ill patients. The

217 provision of additional protein has the potential to attenuate muscle atrophy, and hence improve

218 — outcomes following critical illness. Current international guidelines recommend delivery of protein

219 prescription of 1.2 - 2.0 g/kg/day or higher, but this is based on very low quality of evidence, and

220 there is observational evidence to support both benefit and harm of augmented protein prescription.

221 Therefore, there is a need for a high-quality randomised controlled trial of differing protein

222 prescriptions. We propose a cluster randomised clinical trial with the aim of evaluating the effect of

223 higher dietary protein delivery on outcomes of critically ill adult patients when compared to usual care.

224 In this study each ICU will be randomised to one of the two study formulae for 3 months and then

225 crosses over to the other study formulae for 3 months, which is then repeated, with each site

226 participating for 12 months. Each eligible patient admitted to the ICU during these 3-month periods

227 will receive the same study formula.
