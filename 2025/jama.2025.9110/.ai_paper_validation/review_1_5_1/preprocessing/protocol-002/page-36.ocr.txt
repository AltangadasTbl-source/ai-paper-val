1192 with associated increased mortality “° *’. Poor muscle quality, in addition to muscle mass, is an important
1193 determinant of functional outcomes following critical illness “*, with increased intramuscular lipid content
1194 (myosteatosis) and fibrous tissue a major cause of poor muscle quality in the critically ill *. The pathogenesis of
1195 muscle wasting in critical illness is multi-factorial with suboptimal nutritional delivery, particularly dietary
1196 protein, a potential contributor “*. Increased protein administration has been reported to attenuate muscle
1197 wasting in a number of small randomised controlled trials in critically ill patients °°. It is therefore plausible
1198 that augmented protein administration, to that recommenced in international nutrition guidelines, has the
1199 potential to reduce muscle wasting in critical illness.

1202 logistically feasible for widespread use in the ICU setting “©. Computed tomography (CT) scans can be used to

1204 mass, which has been shown to correlate with whole body muscle mass in a number of clinical conditions *°**.

1206 muscle density as a measure of muscle quality *”“*. These techniques have been recently used to assess skeletal
1207 muscle mass and quality in the critically ill *°. Critically ill patients frequently have CT scans performed for
1208 clinical purposes, and for some patients these images are obtained repeatedly throughout their ICU admission.
1209 Therefore, these images can be repurposed and analysed for research purposes to advance our understanding
1210 without additional risk or burden to patients ©” >’.
