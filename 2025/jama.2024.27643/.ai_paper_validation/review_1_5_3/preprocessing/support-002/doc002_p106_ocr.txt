Nonclinical Summary of In Vitro and In Vivo ALS Neuroprotection Studies
1.2.2.1 In vitro ALS Neuroprotection Models
CNM-Au8 exhibits neuroprotective effects in a broad range of neural cell types in in vitro and in
vivo preclinical models of ALS.
1.2.2.1.1 CNM-Au8 Protects Primary Rodent Motor Neurons from Excitotoxicity
Excitotoxicity is the pathological process by which neuronal death occurs as a result of excessive
stimulation of receptors at excitatory synapses such as the NMDA receptor. Excitotoxicity has
been implicated in acute neurological damage from ischemia and traumatic brain injury and in
the chronic neurodegeneration in Huntington's disease. Glutamate excitoxicity is also believed to
be a significant contributor to motor neuron death in ALS. Excitotoxic neuronal death via over-
activation of NMDA receptors contributes to excessive flux of calcium (Ca2+) into the cell. This
triggers a range of responses resulting in cell death, including increased oxidative stress,
inappropriate activation of proteases such as calpain, dysregulation of Ca2+-related pathways,
mitochondrial damage, and the apoptotic cascade.
