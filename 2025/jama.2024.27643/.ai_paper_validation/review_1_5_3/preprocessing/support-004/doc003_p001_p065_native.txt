1. Governing Documents
This Master Statistical Analysis Plan (M-SAP) defines the default outcome measures, analysis
samples, and planned analyses for the placebo-controlled portion of regimens tested in the
HEALEY ALS Platform Trial for Regimen-specific Appendices (RSAs) initiated after
implementation of version 5.0 of the HEALEY ALS Platform Trial Master Protocol in February
2023. RSAs initiated on earlier versions of the HEALEY ALS Platform Trial Master Protocol
will refer to version 2.0 of the M-SAP dated 06 Mar 2020. Each RSA will have a regimenspecific SAP (R-SAP) that specifies any deviations or further details that supersede the
specifications given here. The M-SAP and each R-SAP supplement the Master Protocol, the
"ALS Master Protocol Recommended Statistical Analysis, Design and Simulation Report"
(MPRDR, Appendix 1 to this M-SAP), and the applicable RSA. Please refer to the Master
Protocol and applicable RSA for details on the rationale for the study design, eligibility criteria,
conduct of the trial, clinical assessments and schedule of assessments, definitions and reporting
of adverse events, data management conventions, and regulatory oversight and compliance
procedures. The MPRDR and any regimen-specific deviations described in the applicable RSA
and R-SAP are authoritative in defining the primary and interim analyses. In case of
discrepancies between either SAP and the Master Protocol and applicable RSA concerning other
matters of analysis, the M-SAP and applicable R-SAP are authoritative. In case of discrepancies
between the M-SAP and R-SAP, the R-SAP is authoritative. On all other matters, the Master
Protocol and applicable RSA are authoritative.
2. Study Design
2.1

Overview

The HEALEY ALS Platform Trial is a perpetual multi-center, multi-regimen clinical trial
evaluating the safety and efficacy of investigational products for the treatment of ALS.
Investigational products will be evaluated according to details specified in distinct RSAs. Each
RSA will describe the nature of the intervention and its mechanism of action, including the mode
and frequency of administration, number of doses, the specific target population (to be selected
within the pre-defined subsets of the Master Protocol), additional enrollment criteria (if any),
sample size, and other specific intervention-related information and assessments (safety or other
assessments that may be in addition to those outlined in the Master Protocol).
2.2

Study Objectives

Primary Efficacy Objective:
x

To evaluate the efficacy of multiple investigational products as compared to placebo on
ALS disease progression.

Secondary Efficacy Objectives:
x

To evaluate the effect of multiple investigational products on selected secondary
measures of disease progression.

Safety Objectives:
x

To evaluate the safety of multiple investigational products for people with ALS.

Exploratory Efficacy Objectives:

Each regimen will test the efficacy of a distinct treatment.
2.3

Study Population

Individuals eligible for trial participation are men or women at least 18 years old who meet the
El Escorial criteria of possible, laboratory-supported probable, probable, or definite for a
diagnosis of sporadic or familial ALS, report onset of weakness within 36 months of screening,
possess a slow vital capacity (SVC) at least 50% of that expected based on age, sex, and height,
are treatment naïve or on a stable dose of riluzole, edaravone, or both, are naïve to or have
started treatment with sodium phenylbutyrate/taurursodiol at least 30 days prior to Master
Protocol Screening, lack unstable clinical or psychiatric conditions, and were not recently
exposed to other investigational treatments or prior gene therapy. Detailed inclusion and
exclusion criteria are specified in the Master Protocol and applicable RSA.
Participants will be recruited from approximately 60 centers located throughout the US that are
part of the Northeast ALS (NEALS) Consortium.
2.4

Participant Flow

Participants that consent to the Master Protocol will be screened for Master Protocol-level
inclusion and exclusion criteria. Those determined eligible will be randomly assigned to an
applicable regimen. Participants that consent to their assigned regimen will be screened for any
regimen-specific inclusion and exclusion criteria. Those determined eligible will be randomized
to either active or placebo treatment for their assigned regimen.
This perpetual platform trial will continue enrolling research participants for as long as there are
regimens that are enrolling. Enrollment into a specific regimen will continue until the target
number of randomized participants in a regimen has been reached unless stopped earlier due to a
determination of regimen success or futility.
Each regimen will compare double-blind active and placebo treatment groups for a maximum of
up to 28 weeks or as otherwise specified in the RSA. Participants will complete a baseline visit
prior to initiating study drug. After the baseline visit, participants will be seen in clinic at 4, 8,
16, and 24 weeks and will complete telephone visits at 2, 12, and 20 weeks and 28 days after last
dose of study drug or as otherwise specified in the RSA.
An active treatment extension (ATE) will be available to participants who complete the doubleblind phase as described in their respective RSAs. Participants may also opt to re-screen for the
Master Platform and enroll in a second regimen, if eligible.
Detailed descriptions of study procedures and timing are specified in the Master Protocol and
applicable RSA.
2.5

Regimen Allocation

Participants determined eligible for Master Protocol-level inclusion and exclusion criteria will be
randomized with equal probability to all applicable regimens. Applicable regimens are those for

2.6

Treatment Allocation

Prior to the baseline visit, eligible participants for a given regimen will be randomly allocated in
a 3:1 ratio to active or placebo treatment. Active participants may be further divided at random to
more than one dose group, if applicable. Randomizations follow a permuted-block schedule,
stratified for all combinations of use vs. non-use of riluzole, edaravone, and sodium
phenylbutyrate/taurursodiol at the time of screening for the Master Protocol. The randomization
schedule will be prepared by computer program by an unblinded study statistician who will not
contribute to decisions concerning the course, conduct, or analysis of the HEALEY ALS
Platform Trial.
2.7

Treatment Administration

Details of treatment administration are described in the applicable RSA.
2.8

Allocation Concealment

Allocation to regimen is not concealed. Allocation to treatment groups within a regimen is
concealed to participants and investigators. The regimen randomization schedule is known only
to the unblinded study statistician who generated the schedule, any unblinded staff responsible
for managing the interactive response technology (IRT) used for randomization, all staff at the
central pharmacy responsible for distribution of study drug, and the unblinded Independent
Statistical Analysis Committee (ISAC) responsible for interim analyses. An independent Data
and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will receive unblinded data as described in the DSMB
Charter. Concealment of the true regimen treatment assignment of specific participants is
achieved by using anonymous study drug kit identifiers linked at time of dispensation to
participants. Member of the HEALEY ALS Platform Trial Executive Committee, members of
the Regimen Steering Committee, site investigators and other site staff including research
pharmacists, clinical coordination and data management staff, the medical monitor, and all
participants are blinded to regimen treatment assignments of participants. Additional details
concerning blinding are described in Data Sharing Plan and the Clintrex/CMSU Firewall Plan.

3. General Considerations for Data Analysis
3.1

Statistical Software

Statistical analyses will be performed using SAS (SAS Institute, NC, USA), R (R Foundation for
Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), or JAGS (Plummer, SourceForge).
3.2

Summary Statistics

Data will be summarized with respect to disposition, demographics, pre-treatment
characteristics, efficacy endpoints, safety endpoints, and tolerability. Summary statistics for
continuous variables will include the number of observations, the mean, median, standard
deviation, inter-quartile range, and range. Summaries of categorical data will include counts,
denominators, and percentages.
3.3

Precision

Results will generally be reported to 3 significant figures. Percentages will generally be reported
to 0.1 percentage points. P-values will generally be reported to two digits when greater than or
equal to 0.095, to three digits when greater than or equal to 0.00095 and less than 0.095, and as
<0.001 for all smaller values.
3.4

Transformations

Data that are strictly positive, continuous, and strongly right skewed will typically be logtransformed prior to any inferential testing. Skewness greater than 3 will be used as a guide in
determining which variables to transform. Which variables warrant transformation and how they
will be transformed will be determined prior to regimen data lock. Original, untransformed
values will be used for all summaries. Any data transformations used in the primary and interim
analyses are described in the MPRDR (Appendix 1 to this M-SAP).
3.5

Multiplicity Adjustments

A single primary analysis is planned. The default criterion for significance at the final analysis is
adjusted to ensure an overall one-sided type 1 error rate less than 0.025 in the absence of early
stopping for futility. Secondary efficacy endpoints will be tested in a pre-specified order using a
closed testing procedure to maintain an overall two-tailed family-wise error rate less than 0.05
under the condition that the primary and all secondary analyses are null. Nominal comparisonwise p-values will also be reported for all analyses unless otherwise specified.
3.6

Missing Data

If baseline values of a given measure are missing, the last observed pre-treatment value will be
used. Missing baseline covariates will be imputed using the mean of the respective covariate. For
any baseline covariate that is transformed for analysis, means will be imputed after
transformation. Off-schedule results, e.g., those collected as part of an Early Termination Visit,
will be used in place of the closest missing scheduled visit (while preserving visit sequence) for
analyses that depend on visit-specific data. Other results will not be carried forward.

The planned mixed model analyses, where applied, yield estimates that are unbiased conditional
on the observed values under a missing at random assumption. Secondary analyses of efficacy

4. Study Endpoints
4.1

Efficacy Endpoints

The primary efficacy endpoint is change from baseline through Week 24 in disease severity as
measured by the ALS Functional Rating Scale-Revised (ALSFRS-R) total score and survival.
Secondary efficacy endpoints include change from baseline through Week 24 in muscle strength
as measured isometrically using hand-held dynamometry (HHD) and grip strength, respiratory
function as assessed by slow vital capacity (SVC), and survival.
4.2

Exploratory Efficacy Endpoints

The following categories of exploratory endpoints will be evaluated:
x

Biofluid biomarkers

x

Patient-reported outcomes

4.3

Safety Endpoints

The following safety endpoints will be evaluated:
x

Treatment-emergent adverse and serious adverse events.

x

Changes in laboratory values and treatment-emergent and clinically significant laboratory
abnormalities.

x

Changes in ECG parameters and treatment-emergent and clinically significant ECG
abnormalities.

x

Treatment-emergent suicidal ideation and suicidal behavior.

Reported proportions will use as their denominator all participants in the applicable Safety
sample (see Section 6.1 below).
5. Measurement Definitions
5.1

ALSFRS-R

The ALSFRS-R (Cedarbaum et al. 1999) is a 12-item instrument completed by clinician
interview for assessing participants’ function in four domains: bulbar, fine motor, gross motor,
and respiratory. Each item is scored from 0 to 4 with higher scores indicating greater function.
The ALSFRS-R total score is the sum of all items (range 0 to 48). Domain scores are the sum of
their respective items (range 0 to 12): bulbar function = Q1-Q3, fine motor function = Q4-Q6,
gross motor function = Q7-Q9, and respiratory function = QR1-QR3 (Q10-Q12).
The average rate of disease progression prior to the Baseline Visit as measured by ALSFRS-R
(pre-baseline slope of ALSFRS-R or delta-FRS) will be calculated as 48 minus the ALSFRS-R

5.2

SVC

Slow vital capacity (SVC) is the maximum volume of air that can be slowly exhaled after slow,
maximal inhalation. Trained technicians coach participants through 3 to 5 maneuvers using a
spirometer. A minimum of 2 completed SVC maneuvers are required to calculate an estimate of
maximum SVC for a given participant visit. A single maximum vital capacity reported by a
pulmonary function lab and obtained by either a slow or forced maneuver is also acceptable. The
maximum volume expired is converted to percent of predicted normal using normal values for
forced vital capacity (FVC). FVC normal values are calculated based on sex, age, height, and
ethnicity using equations published by the Global Lung Function Initiative (GLI, Quanjer et al.
2012). Higher values indicate greater respiratory function.

HHD and Grip Strength

Manual muscle testing by hand-held dynamometry (HHD) assesses isometric muscle strength by
recording force generated against a hand-held dynamometer placed against a given limb by a
trained evaluator. Each muscle is tested with a standard beginning position, usually midway
between maximum flexion or extension of the muscle being studied, and with the dynamometer
placed at a defined position. Trained evaluators coach participants through 3 to 5 maximal
exertions for each muscle and record the maximum force observed in pounds. Evaluators note if
participants are not able to overcome the force exerted for a given muscle. Conversely, a value of
0 is recorded for a given muscle if the participant cannot assume the testing position due to
weakness or can attain the position but cannot exert measurable force.
The following 11 muscles are tested bilaterally by HHD: shoulder flexion, elbow flexion, elbow
extension, wrist extension, abductor pollicis brevis contraction, abductor digiti minimi
contraction, first dorsal interosseous contraction, hip flexion, knee flexion, knee extension, and
ankle dorsiflexion. Grip strength is tested bilaterally by Jamar hand dynamometer. Between
HHD and hand grip, 24 muscles or muscle groups are tested. The force recorded for each muscle

is converted to a percentage change from baseline among muscles with non-zero strength at
baseline. Individual percentages are averaged together to yield upper extremity, lower extremity,
and global average percentages. Higher values indicate greater strength retention.
HHD data will also be summarized as time to loss of strength. HHD0 and HHD0[2] will be
defined as the times from the Baseline Visit to the first and second post-baseline occurrence of a
muscle with a strength recording of 0, respectively, among those muscles that were non-zero at
baseline. All 11 muscles assessed by HHD as well as grip strength will be considered. Both
HHD0 and HHD0[2] are composite endpoints with death or death-equivalent, censored at the
Week 24 Visit if completed or at the earlier of the end of the Week 24 Visit window or date last
known alive if a Week 24 visit is not completed. Larger HHD0 and HHD0[2] times indicate
longer preservation of strength.
5.4

Biofluid Biomarkers

Blood and urine biomarkers will be assayed. Biomarkers will include serum creatinine and serum
and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) neurofilament light chain (NfL). Serum creatinine will be assayed
by the kinetic Jaffe method (test 001370, Labcorp, Burlington, NC). NfL will be assayed by
single-molecule array (Simoa; Quanterix, Billerica, MA). Levels of serum and CSF NfL that are
reported to be below the limit of quantitation will be imputed at the limit of quantitation. Levels
of serum and CSF NfL will be log-transformed in all analyses. Other biomarkers may be
analyzed.

5.6

Survival

The primary survival endpoint will be the composite of death or death equivalent. A secondary
survival endpoint of death alone independent of any death equivalent (including a death
equivalent that occurs prior to baseline) is also defined. Death equivalent will be defined as use
of permanent assisted ventilation (PAV), where PAV is defined as more than 22 hours per day of
noninvasive or invasive mechanical ventilation for more than seven consecutive days. The date
of PAV initiation is the first day of the consecutive days. The date of PAV initiation, where
applicable, will be imputed as the fifteenth day of a month if not specified more precisely. Any
participant on PAV at baseline will be censored at baseline for the composite survival endpoint.
Time at risk for both the composite survival endpoint of death or death equivalent and the
survival endpoint of death alone will be measured from each participant's Baseline Visit and will
be censored at the Week 24 Visit if completed or at the earlier of the end of the Week 24 Visit
window or date last known alive if a Week 24 visit is not completed.
Additional secondary analyses of survival may consider different durations of follow-up.
5.7

King's ALS Clinical Staging System

The King's ALS Clinical Staging System (Roche et al. 2012) is a 4-level ordinal scale with the
first three levels indicating the number (1, 2, or 3) of distinct central nervous system regions

(bulbar, upper limb, and lower limb) with neuromuscular dysfunction and levels 4a and 4b
indicating nutritional or respiratory failure secondary to ALS, respectively.
Participants will be classified to King's stage 1, 2, 3, 4a, or 4b based on scores from ALSFRS-R
assessments according to a published derivation (Balendra et al. 2014). Bulbar involvement is
defined as a score less than 4 on any of the ALSFRS-R questions in the bulbar domain (questions
1, 2, and 3). Upper limb involvement is defined as a score less than 4 on either of the ALSFRS-R
questions related to hand function (questions 4 and 5A). Lower limb involvement is defined as a
score less than 4 on the ALSFRS-R question about walking (question 8). Nutritional failure is
defined as responding that the participant uses gastrostomy for greater than 50% of their
nutrition. Respiratory failure is defined as a score of 0 on the ALSFRS-R question addressing
dyspnea (question 10 or R-1) or a score less than 4 on the ALSFRS-R question about use of
mechanical ventilation (question 12 or R-3). Participants without evidence by ALSFRS-R scores
of involvement of any of the three central nervous system regions will be scored as King's stage
1 due to their confirmed diagnosis with ALS. Participant may meet criteria for both King's stage
4a and 4b.

6. Statistical Methodology
6.1

Analysis Sets

The following analysis sets will be used for testing efficacy, safety, and tolerability endpoints of
a specific focal regimen:
x

Efficacy Full Analysis Set (FAS): Participants who were randomized within the focal
regimen plus placebo participants from specified regimens who had an opportunity to
complete placebo-controlled follow-up, classified according to their randomized treatment
assignment. Observations made after premature permanent discontinuation of study drug are
included in this sample, should such participants remain on study. Observations completed
after regimen data lock will be excluded. Participants determined to not meet ALS diagnostic
criteria are excluded.

x

Efficacy Concurrent-controls (ECC) Set: The subset of participants in the FAS analysis set
who were randomized within 180 days prior to the first randomization for the applicable
regimen and within 180 days after the last randomization for the applicable regimen,
exclusive.

x

Efficacy Regimen-only (ERO) Set: The subset of participants in the FAS analysis set who
were randomized within a given regimen.

x

Efficacy Common Mode of Administration (ECM) Set: The subset of participants in the FAS
analysis set who are in regimens in which study drug is administered by the same route as the
focal regimen (including oral, subcutaneous injection, intravenous infusion).

x

Efficacy Per-protocol (EPP) Set: The subset of participants in the FAS analysis set who
initiated study treatment and who meet other requirements for protocol compliance as
specified in the R-SAP, classified according to the treatment they actually received. This
sample may include placebo participants from no other, some other, or all other regimens and
may be restricted to those who would have been eligible for the applicable regimen as
specified in the R-SAP. If a participant's data are truncated for inclusion in the EPP analysis
set due to a time-dependent event (e.g., non-adherence to protocol-specified dosing, initiation
of a prohibited medication, initiation of a medication approved for treatment of ALS),
clinical events observed after the censoring event may be included in the EPP analysis set as
specified in the R-SAP. Data from placebo participants shared from other regimens will not
be truncated due to non-adherence to protocol-specified dosing.

x

Safety Full (STF) Set: Participants who initiated treatment within the focal regimen plus
placebo participants from specified regimens who are not known to be ineligible for the focal
regimen and who initiated treatment in their respective regimen, classified according to the
treatment they actually received. Observations made after premature permanent
discontinuation of study drug are included in this sample, should such participants remain on
study. Observations completed after regimen data lock are excluded.

Safety Narrow (STN) Set: The subset of participants in the STF analysis set who were

6.3

Primary Efficacy Analysis and Supportive Analyses

Unless otherwise specified in the RSA, the primary analysis for each regimen is a Bayesian
shared-parameter model of function and survival. Details of the model, including documentation
of operating characteristics under a range of scenarios, are provided in the MPRDR (Appendix 1
to this M-SAP). Any regimen-specific deviations are described in the applicable RSA and RSAP.
The estimand of the primary analysis is the relative rate of disease progression (the "disease rate
ratio" or DRR) of active treatment relative to placebo in the FAS population under an assumption
that active treatment slows mean time to death or death equivalent by the same proportion as
treatment slows the mean rate of functional progression as measured by change in ALSFRS-R
total score over time. The estimand is defined by the following attributes:
x

Treatment: active treatment vs. placebo.

x

Population: FAS population as defined in Section 6.1.

x

Variables: time to death or death equivalent and rate of change in ALSFRS-R total score
from baseline to the Week 24 Visit.

x

Intercurrent event 1: treatment discontinuation due to death or death equivalent: no ALSFRSR data from participants who reach the death or death equivalent endpoint are included in the
analysis, handled via mortality component in model, composite variable strategy approach.

x

Intercurrent event 2: treatment discontinuation not due to death or death equivalent: handled
via treatment policy approach, all data will be used including data collected during the
placebo-controlled period after treatment discontinuation regardless of concomitant
medication, for those participants who have not been censored due to mortality. Missing data
post-treatment will not be imputed, handled via missing at random assumption.

x

Population-level summary: mean ratio of hazard or progression rate of active treatment
relative to placebo.

6.5

Secondary Efficacy Analyses

6.5.1

Hierarchical Testing

Primary inference for secondary efficacy endpoints will be based on analysis of the FAS analysis
set using a repeated-measures linear mixed model for functional endpoints (see Section 6.5.2
below) and by Kaplan-Meier product-limit estimates and log-rank test for survival (see Section
6.5.5 below). The default sequence for testing is the following:
1. Change from baseline to Week 24 in upper limb muscle strength as assessed
isometrically using HHD and grip strength and measured as average percent change from
baseline among maneuvers with non-zero strength at baseline in the FAS analysis set,
2. Change from baseline to Week 24 in respiratory function as assessed by slow vital
capacity (SVC) and measured as percent predicted using GLI normal values in the FAS
analysis set,
3. Change from baseline to Week 24 in lower limb muscle strength as assessed
isometrically using HHD and measured as average percent change from baseline among
maneuvers with non-zero strength at baseline in the FAS analysis set, and
4. Survival defined as freedom from death and permanent assisted ventilation (PAV) from
baseline to Week 24 in the FAS analysis set.
RSAs may specify a different sequence of secondary endpoints to evaluate using a closed testing
procedure or another method for controlling family-wise type 1 error rate.
If the primary analysis indicates a significant slowing in disease progression from the Bayesian
shared-parameter model of function and survival, then each secondary efficacy endpoint would
be declared significant in the specified sequence using a criterion of two-tailed p < 0.05. After
the first failure to declare significance, no endpoints lower in the hierarchy can be significant.
Nominal comparison-wise p-values for secondary efficacy endpoints will also be reported.

6.5.4

Survival

The survival endpoints will be analyzed in the FAS, ECC, ERO, ECM, EPP, STF, STN, and
SRO analysis sets. The proportion of participants who reach each survival endpoint and the time
from baseline to that endpoint will be summarized by treatment group in all analysis samples. If
more than 10% of a given sample reaches a survival endpoint, then the estimated time to a 10%
event rate will be estimated by Kaplan-Meier product-limit estimates with complimentary loglog confidence bounds by treatment group. Time to each survival endpoint will be compared
between treatment groups by log-rank test. If at least one occurrence of a given survival endpoint
is observed in each treatment group, then the treatment-dependent hazard ratio with profile
likelihood confidence bounds will be estimated by unadjusted Cox proportional hazards
regression and by Cox proportional hazards regression adjusting for age at baseline, time since
ALS symptom onset, delta-FRS, baseline log-transformed serum NfL level, use of riluzole at
baseline, use of edaravone at baseline, and use of sodium phenylbutyrate/taurursodiol at baseline
with a random gamma-distributed regimen-specific frailty term. If convergence is not obtained
or the analysis sample includes 3 or fewer regimens, then the frailty term will be dropped.
6.5.5

CAFS

To account for potentially informative loss to follow-up due to death, treatment groups of a given
regimen will be compared by the Combined Assessment of Function and Survival (CAFS, Berry
et al. 2013) in the FAS, ECC, ERO, ECM, and EPP samples. CAFS is a joint-rank test
(Finkelstein and Schoenfeld 1999) of a composite endpoint combining function and survival.
The test consists of calculating a rank-sum score for each individual relative to pair-wise
comparisons with all other participants. Participants are ranked according to time to the survival
endpoint when that is observed for both members of a pair or when one is censored after the
observed event time for the other. Pairs that cannot be ranked by time to the survival endpoint
are ranked by absolute change from baseline in the functional endpoint at the maximum followup time at which both participants have an observation. Pair-wise rankings are scored -1 for the
member of the pair with shorter time to the survival endpoint or greater decline in function and 1
for the other member or 0 for both members when time to the survival endpoint is equal or not
evaluable and change in function is equal, e.g., when one participant is lost to follow-up after
baseline. Pair-wise ranking scores are summed by participant to calculate a rank-sum. Inference
of treatment-dependent difference in the hierarchical composite of death and disease progression
is based on a Wilcoxon rank-sum test of the participant-specific rank-sum scores or on a
regression of the rank-sum scores adjusting for covariates.

Treatment-emergent Adverse Events

TEAEs are defined as those adverse events with onset dates in the interval from treatment
initiation to either the Final Safety Visit if completed, the date of death if the participant dies, the
date of last study contact if the participant early terminates or is lost to follow-up, or the date of
first dose of study drug during participation in the ATE, if so exposed. Adverse events with onset
on the day of placebo-controlled treatment initiation and adverse events with incompletely
specified onset date where the ambiguous date spans the day of double-blind treatment initiation
will be assumed to be treatment emergent except for those known to precede first exposure to
study drug.
The incidence of TEAEs will be summarized by the number of events of a given classification
experienced by participants in each treatment group and by the number and proportion of
participants experiencing such an event in each treatment group in all safety samples. The
number of events and proportion of participants experiencing TEAEs will be summarized
without regard to MedDRA term for any TEAE, any serious TEAE, any severe TEAE, any fatal
TEAE, any related TEAE, and any TEAE leading to discontinuation of study drug. TEAEs will
be summarized by MedDRA system organ class, high level term, and preferred term for all
TEAEs. TEAEs, serious TEAEs, and non-serious TEAEs observed in at least 5% of participants
in a given treatment group will be summarized by MedDRA system organ class and preferred
term and by MedDRA preferred term alone. TEAEs will be summarized by MedDRA system
organ class and preferred term for serious TEAEs, severe TEAEs, fatal TEAEs, related TEAEs,
and TEAEs leading to discontinuation of study drug. TEAEs and serious TEAEs will be
stratified by the following subgroups: age, sex, race, ethnicity, baseline riluzole use, baseline
edaravone use, baseline sodium phenylbutyrate/taurursodiol use, weight, BMI, and CKD stage.
Subgroup classifications will be the same as described in Section 6.5.9 Subgroup Analyses
except that the "other" group will be retained regardless of prevalence.
For overall summaries of TEAE not classified by MedDRA term and for summaries of TEAEs,
serious TEAEs, and non-serious TEAEs observed in at least 5% of participants in a given
treatment group, treatment-specific TEAE incidence rates in units of number per 100 participant
years and treatment-dependent differences will be estimated by Poisson regression with log link

5.0 Introduction
The ALS Master Protocol is a perpetual, multicenter, multi-regimen, randomized, placebocontrolled, adaptive platform trial. The primary objective of the trial is to evaluate the
efficacy of multiple investigational products, as compared to placebo, on ALS disease
progression. The master protocol will include multiple regimens where a regimen is
defined as both an active treatment and the corresponding randomized placebo.
Participants in the master protocol will be randomized in two stages; first to one of the
actively enrolling regimens; and then 3:1 to active treatment or placebo within the
regimen.
This document describes the master protocol recommended primary endpoint, primary
analysis, and interim analyses. This will be the default statistical design and primary
analysis for all regimens investigated in this master protocol. The recommended design is
intended to support inference of substantial evidence of effectiveness when the data so
warrant.
Each regimen will have a regimen-specific appendix (RSA). Deviations from the master
protocol recommended statistical design are permitted. The exact statistical design along
with complete operating characteristics for each regimen will be specified in the RSA.
The master protocol recommended primary efficacy endpoint is change from baseline
through week 24 in disease severity as measured by the ALS Functional Rating ScaleRevised (ALSFRS-R) total score and survival. The primary analysis is a Bayesian shared
parameter analysis of ALSFRS-R and survival. The primary analysis compares each
regimen-specific active treatment to a shared control group.
Page 33 of 62

The sample size enrolled in each regimen will be determined based on the specifics of the
intervention, anticipated effect size, and number of treatment arms evaluated within that
regimen. There are frequent interim analyses in order to potentially stop a regimen early
for success or for futility. The default design for a regimen will include stopping early only
for futility. Interim analyses will be conducted at the same time for all regimens within the
master protocol, every 12 weeks of trial time. A regimen is eligible for these interim

2.0 Primary Efficacy Analysis
The following describes the Bayesian shared parameter primary analysis method of
function and survival for each regimen, the primary analysis population, prior distributions
for all model parameters, and specific posterior summaries of interest that will be used in
the design. We denote the regimen being analyzed as the “analysis regimen” throughout.

2.1 Primary Analysis Population and Shared Control
The primary analysis population for each analysis regimen is guided by the intent to treat
(ITT) principle and includes all participants randomized to the active treatment arm within
the analysis regimen, all participants concurrently randomized to the control arm within
the analysis regimen, and control participants from other regimens from a shared control,
excluding any participant determined to not meet ALS diagnostic criteria.
The primary analysis dataset uses longitudinal data on disease progression (ALSFRS-R
measurements at 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 and 24 weeks, at early termination visits, and
assessments during double-blind follow-up that substitute for missed scheduled visits) for
those who have not been censored due to reaching the survival endpoint (a composite of
death or death-equivalent) as well as occurrence of the survival endpoint during planned
double-blind follow-up on participants within the primary analysis population. Follow-up
for primary analysis of survival among participants who do not die or reach a death
equivalent prior to the Week 24 Visit will be censored at the Week 24 Visit date, if
completed, or at the earlier of the end of the Week 24 Visit window or date last known alive
if a Week 24 visit is not completed. Off-schedule results, e.g., those collected as part of an
Early Termination Visit, will be used in place of the closest missing scheduled visit (while
preserving visit sequence) for analyses that depend on visit-specific data. Off-schedule
results that do not substitute for a missed schedule visit will not be used.
The shared control group will include participants randomized to the control arm across
other actively enrolling regimens (concurrent shared controls) and previously completed
regimens (non-concurrent shared controls) within the master protocol as specified in the
RSA. Concurrent shared controls are defined as participants on a control arm who were
randomized within a 6-month window of the start and stop of randomization of the
analysis regimen. For the purposes of analysis, 6 months is defined as 180 days. For
example, if the analysis regimen enrolls from Month 12 – Month 24 in the platform, the

For interim analyses, all available data at the time of the analysis will be used including

potential to lead to significant differences in the patient population in terms of the rate of
disease progression within the primary analysis population and will be prespecified within
each RSA. The first three regimens enrolling within the platform did not include any major
additional inclusion/exclusion criteria.

2.2.1 Treatment Effect ALSFRS-R and Mortality
The disease rate ratio (DRR) under treatment T is the parameter exp(ߠ் ). For a placebo
patient, the DRR is set equal to 1 (ߠ଴ = 0). Thus, the DRR parameter for active treatment
represents the relative change to the rate of decline of ALSFRS-R and the rate of mortality
of treated participant relative to a placebo participant. The estimated DRR can also be
interpreted as the average rate of decline in function and mortality, with the weights across
function and mortality depending on the proportion of patients with observed mortality.
Appropriately, more weight is given to mortality relative to function as the proportion of
observed mortality increases. If the DRR is less than 1 then the rate of disease progression
on function and mortality for actively treated participants is slower than that for placebo.
The DRR value represents the proportional slowing in time (or increase) of the disease. A
DRR = 0.75 corresponds to a 25% slowing in the rate of functional progression and time to
mortality and a value of 0.25 represents a 75% slowing in the rate of functional
progression and time to mortality.
The prior distribution for the DRR for the regimen-specific active treatment, T=1, is
uniform between 0 and 2; placing equal weight on a DRR<1 and DRR>1:
exp(ߠଵ ) ~ܷ݂݊݅(0,2).

5.0 Clinical Trial Simulations
Clinical trial simulation is used to provided simulated example trials and to quantify
operating characteristics for each regimen. In particular, virtual patient outcomes are
created under different assumptions for key design parameters including placebo rates of
progression for ALSFRS-R, measurement error for ALSFRS-R, mortality rates, treatment
effects on ALSFRS-R, treatment effects on mortality, dropout rates and accrual rates. For
each set of simulation assumptions (i.e., a scenario), many trials are simulated and virtually
executed, including all interim analyses for early success and early futility. Trial operating
characteristics are summarized across all simulated trials for each scenario.

Additionally, to demonstrate that our model properly accounts for mortality and to show
the sensitivity of the model to treatment-dependent differences in mortality, two simulated
trials have been explored (Tables 6.2.2 and 6.2.3 and Figures 6.2.1-6.2.2). Within each
simulated trial, we fix the ALSFRS-R data for each participant as well as the mortality data
of participants in the placebo arm. We then let the mortality data on the active treatment
arm vary to explore how the key supportive joint rank analysis (CAFS) and the proposed
Bayesian shared-parameter primary analysis respond to an increasing number of deaths
on the active treatment arm. The point at which inference about benefit from treatment
switches from significant to non-significant, the tipping point, reveals how sensitive a given
analysis is to an excess of deaths in the active arm. An analysis with a smaller tipping point
is more sensitive and would be less likely to support a claim of benefit from the active
treatment when the active treatment slows functional progression does not also reduce
mortality rates.
The base simulations assume a substantial slowing in rate of functional decline of 30%.
Within these example trials, as more participants on the active treatment arm experience
mortality, the estimated disease rate ratio (DRR), the common treatment effect estimate
between mortality and function, increases towards 1 (no effect of treatment) and the
posterior probability that the treatment slows the rate of progression (DRR < 1) decreases.
This is similar to the change seen in the CAFS joint rank analysis: as mortality in the active
treatment arm increases, its p-value becomes less significative (see details below). The two
example trials differ in the overall mortality rate (Example 1 has a higher rate than
Example 2). Comparison of the two example trials shows that the sensitivity of the results
to the mortality hazard ratio depends on the overall mortality rate (the mortality hazard
ratio is smaller at the tipping point for Example 1 than for Example 2). These examples
demonstrate that the proposed Bayesian shared-parameter model is more sensitive to
discrepancies in the effect of treatment on mortality vs. the effect of treatment on function
relative to the CAFS joint rank analysis.

6.4 Operating Characteristics Additional Sensitivity Scenarios
This section provides additional sensitivity results of the overall operating characteristics

characteristics in this section assume no futility stopping.
Table 6.4 shows the overall power (under a DRR of .70 for Mortality and Function) and

Under all Null scenarios, Type I error is controlled at 2.5% or less without futility stopping.

and regimens enrolling further apart).
Under all scenarios where the treatment effect is common between ALSFRS-R slowing and

when the regimens start six months apart since the first regimen will have less shared
