5. Verified Scientific Findings

C01 — Primary composite outcome analysis-unit labels conflict

  • Evidence status: Verified in evidence-verification round 1; accepted by the critic.
  • Category: Presentation inconsistency.
  • Severity: Major.
  • Exact source locations: jama_kumar_2025_oi_250034_1750956984.08518.pdf, PDF p. 1/JAMA p. 149, Abstract Results; jama_kumar_2025_oi_250034_1750956984.08518.pdf, PDF p. 4/JAMA p. 152, Results and Figure 1; jama_kumar_2025_oi_250034_1750956984.08518.pdf, PDF p. 6/JAMA p. 154, “Primary and Secondary Outcomes”; jama_kumar_2025_oi_250034_1750956984.08518.pdf, PDF p. 7/JAMA p. 155, Table 3 and “Site-Specific Differences”; jama_kumar_2025_oi_250034_1750956984.08518.pdf, PDF p. 8/JAMA p. 156, Figure 2; and joi250034supp3_prod_1750956984.12018.pdf, PDF p. 3, Supplementary Table 2, primary-outcome rows.
  • Reported values/statements: The Abstract and main Results call 83/1625 and 84/1625 counts of women. Figure 1 calls the two 1625 denominators infants. Figure 2 places 83/1625 and 84/1625 under “total No. of patients.” The site-specific prose again calls the component counts women, while Supplementary Table 2 reproduces those site components in unit-neutral N and n (%) columns.

Reasoning procedure

  1. Establish the distinct participant and infant totals. The Abstract and Figure 1 report 1626 randomized women and 1634 infants in the sildenafil group, and 1631 randomized women and 1641 infants in the placebo group. The arm differences correspond to the displayed 8 and 10 twin pregnancies:

    • Sildenafil: 1626 women + 8 additional infants from twin pregnancies = 1634 infants.
    • Placebo: 1631 women + 10 additional infants from twin pregnancies = 1641 infants.
    • Overall randomized: 1626 + 1631 = 3257 women; 1634 + 1641 = 3275 infants.

    These are direct source observations plus the shown sums. They establish that “women” and “infants” are not interchangeable counts in this trial.

  2. Reproduce the arm-specific Figure 1 pathways to the primary-outcome denominator. Figure 1 displays mutually distinct flow losses and terminal boxes:

    • Sildenafil infant follow-up: 1634 starting infants - 4 complete consent withdrawals - 4 lost to follow-up - 1 future-data/follow-up consent withdrawal = 1625 infants followed up. The terminal box is “1625 Infants with primary outcome.”
    • Placebo infant follow-up: 1641 starting infants - 2 complete consent withdrawals - 10 lost to follow-up - 2 future-data/follow-up consent withdrawals = 1627 infants followed up; 1627 - 2 infants with incomplete primary-end-point component data = 1625 infants with primary outcome.
    • Overall primary-outcome availability: 1625 + 1625 = 3250 infants, matching the adjacent Results statement, “The primary end point was available for 3250 infants.”

    The same figure also supports the article's reported consent accounting: 4 + 2 complete withdrawals and 1 + 2 future-data/follow-up withdrawals total 9 women; 3257 - 9 = 3248 women with outcome data, as stated in the Results. This check confirms that the article intentionally distinguishes women from infants in the flow presentation.

  3. Match the overall primary-outcome values across text, table, and figure. The following are direct source observations:

    Presentation Sildenafil citrate Placebo Visible analysis-unit wording
    Abstract Results, PDF p. 1/JAMA p. 149 83/1625 (5.1%) 84/1625 (5.2%) “women” for the sildenafil count; the sentence describes the placebo arm in the same women-based construction
    Results, PDF p. 6/JAMA p. 154 83/1625 (5.1%) 84/1625 (5.2%) “women” in both arms
    Table 3, PDF p. 7/JAMA p. 155 83/1625 (5.1%) 84/1625 (5.2%) No./total No. (%); no unit stated in the primary row
    Figure 2, PDF p. 8/JAMA p. 156 83/1625 (5.1%) 84/1625 (5.2%) “No. of events/total No. of patients (%)”

    The displayed percentages reproduce as 83 / 1625 × 100 = 5.1077%, shown as 5.1%, and 84 / 1625 × 100 = 5.1692%, shown as 5.2%. The counts and percentages agree; the locked finding concerns the conflicting unit labels.

  4. Reconcile the site-specific rows to the same overall records. Supplementary Table 2, PDF p. 3, partitions the primary outcome by Mater Mother's Hospital and other sites:

    • Sildenafil denominator: 883 + 742 = 1625; events: 16 + 67 = 83.
    • Placebo denominator: 887 + 738 = 1625; events: 24 + 60 = 84.

    These totals exactly reproduce Table 3 and Figure 2. The main-article site-specific prose on PDF p. 7/JAMA p. 155 describes 16/883, 24/887, 67/742, and 60/738 as counts of women. Thus, the site rows are not a separate outcome set that could explain away the labels; they aggregate to the same overall denominators and events.

  5. Check the Figure 2 partitions. Each displayed yes/no subgroup pair independently returns the same 1625-per-arm denominator and 83/84 event totals:

    Figure 2 subgroup Sildenafil denominators; events Placebo denominators; events
    Small for gestational age 106 + 1519 = 1625; 10 + 73 = 83 105 + 1520 = 1625; 13 + 71 = 84
    Multiple pregnancy 16 + 1609 = 1625; 2 + 81 = 83 20 + 1605 = 1625; 7 + 77 = 84
    Preeclampsia 41 + 1584 = 1625; 4 + 79 = 83 38 + 1587 = 1625; 5 + 79 = 84
    Cesarean delivery in preceding pregnancy 57 + 1568 = 1625; 4 + 79 = 83 71 + 1554 = 1625; 1 + 83 = 84
    Noncephalic presentation 4 + 1621 = 1625; 1 + 82 = 83 2 + 1623 = 1625; 1 + 83 = 84
    Postnatal diagnosis of fetal anomaly 19 + 1606 = 1625; 7 + 76 = 83 22 + 1603 = 1625; 4 + 80 = 84

    This is a within-figure consistency check. It confirms that Figure 2 repeatedly uses the same analysis set while its header calls the units patients.

  6. Separate the verified label conflict from nonissues. Table 3 states that relative risks were calculated using generalized estimating equations adjusted for multiple births, and Figure 2 describes its overall generalized-estimating-equation model. A crude calculation, (83/1625) / (84/1625) = 0.988, therefore need not reproduce the adjusted RR of 1.02. The completed workflow did not challenge the counts, percentages, RR, confidence interval, P value, participant flow, or subgroup arithmetic. Those internally consistent elements strengthen the conclusion that the retained problem is presentation of the analysis unit, not numerical computation.

Existing supported conclusion: The critic retained a Major presentation inconsistency because identical primary neonatal-composite records are labeled infants, women, and patients. Since the article separately enumerates randomized women and infants and accounts for multiple births, the terminology could obscure whether the primary analysis concerns randomized women or infants.

Limit on interpretation: The supplied package does not establish which of the competing labels should be used as the correction, and this report does not infer a production mechanism. It also does not question the reported model estimates or the substantive trial conclusion. Those matters are outside this locked finding.

Verification instruction: On the cited original pages, identify the intended primary-outcome analysis unit from the authors' analysis specification and data structure. Then harmonize the Abstract, Results prose, Figure 1 terminal boxes, Table 3/site-specific wording, Figure 2 header, and any corresponding supplement label without changing the verified numerical records unless source data independently require it.

C02 — Placebo ethnicity percentages use different denominator conventions

  • Evidence status: Verified in evidence-verification round 1; accepted by the critic.
  • Category: Presentation inconsistency.
  • Severity: Minor.
  • Exact source locations: jama_kumar_2025_oi_250034_1750956984.08518.pdf, PDF p. 4/JAMA p. 152, Results, “Participants and Adherence,” ethnicity paragraph; and jama_kumar_2025_oi_250034_1750956984.08518.pdf, PDF p. 5/JAMA p. 153, Table 1, placebo Ethnicity heading and rows.
  • Reported values/statements: The prose describes the full placebo cohort of 1631 participants, includes 2 (0.1%) with missing ethnicity, and gives Australia/New Zealand as 874 (53.6%) and Pacific Islander as 53 (3.2%). Table 1 gives the placebo column total as n = 1631 but labels the Ethnicity subsection n = 1629 and gives the same counts as 874 (53.7%) and 53 (3.3%).

Reasoning procedure

  1. Identify the two visible denominator rules. Table 1 explicitly changes the placebo denominator from the column's randomized total of 1631 to an Ethnicity subsection denominator of 1629. The prose explicitly reports 2 missing ethnicity observations within the 1631-person placebo cohort. Therefore:

    • Nonmissing ethnicity denominator = 1631 - 2 = 1629.
    • Full randomized placebo denominator, with missing retained in the percentage base = 1631.

  2. Reconcile the Table 1 category counts. The eight nonmissing placebo ethnicity counts in Table 1 sum to the subsection denominator:

    42 + 18 + 874 + 311 + 14 + 73 + 53 + 244 = 1629.

    Adding the 2 missing observations reported in the nearby prose gives 1629 + 2 = 1631, the full placebo cohort. These are derived sums of direct source counts.

  3. Reproduce the table percentages using nonmissing observations. Using 1629 as Table 1 directs:

    • Australia/New Zealand: 874 / 1629 × 100 = 53.6525%, displayed as 53.7%.
    • Pacific Islander: 53 / 1629 × 100 = 3.2535%, displayed as 3.3%.

  4. Reproduce the prose percentages using all randomized placebo participants. Using 1631, including the 2 missing observations:

    • Australia/New Zealand: 874 / 1631 × 100 = 53.5868%, displayed as 53.6%.
    • Pacific Islander: 53 / 1631 × 100 = 3.2495%, displayed as 3.2%.

  5. Compare like with like. The category, group, and counts are identical across prose and table. Only the denominator convention changes. Both displayed percentage pairs are arithmetically reproducible at one decimal place; there is no count discrepancy. The candidate is therefore the unexplained adjacent use of different denominator rules, not an assertion that either calculation is arithmetically wrong.

  6. Check the scope of the retained finding. The table also uses a nonmissing sildenafil ethnicity denominator of 1624 instead of the full 1626-person sildenafil cohort. For the highlighted sildenafil counts, the one-decimal displays do not create the same visible table/prose percentage differences. The authoritative candidate specifically concerns the two placebo percentages above; this report does not broaden it into a new candidate.

Existing supported conclusion: The critic retained a Minor presentation inconsistency because adjacent reporting uses nonmissing observations in Table 1 and the full randomized placebo cohort in the prose without explaining the switch, producing different percentages for identical counts.

Limit on interpretation: Both denominator conventions are mathematically supportable. The supplied pages do not establish which convention the authors intended to standardize on, so this report does not designate either the table or prose percentages as the corrected values.

Verification instruction: Confirm with the authors or the analysis/reporting specification whether ethnicity percentages should use nonmissing observations or all randomized participants. State the selected denominator rule and apply it consistently to Table 1 and the nearby prose, while retaining an explicit missing-data count.

C03 — eTable 2 RR 0.91 (0.82-1.00), P = .050

  • Evidence status: Rejected in evidence-verification round 1; not sent to the critic.
  • Category considered: Statistical reporting inconsistency.
  • Severity: Not assigned because the candidate was rejected before critic review.
  • Exact source locations: joi250034supp3_prod_1750956984.12018.pdf, PDF p. 5, Supplementary Table 2, “Spontaneous vaginal birth,” Other Sites row and table legend; and jama_kumar_2025_oi_250034_1750956984.08518.pdf, PDF p. 4/JAMA p. 152, Statistical Analysis, significance rule.
  • Reported values/statements: The supplement row gives sildenafil 370/746 (49.6%) and placebo 410/750 (54.7%), RR 0.91 (95% CI, 0.82-1.00), P = .050. The table legend identifies a log-binomial regression model. The main article states, “Two-tailed P < .05 was considered statistically significant.”

Reasoning procedure

  1. Reproduce the displayed event rates. These diagnostic calculations agree with the row:

    • 370 / 746 × 100 = 49.5979%, displayed as 49.6%.
    • 410 / 750 × 100 = 54.6667%, displayed as 54.7%.

  2. Check the point-estimate direction and magnitude. The diagnostic crude risk ratio is:

    (370 / 746) / (410 / 750) = 0.9074, displayed as 0.91.

    The event-rate direction and printed RR agree. This calculation is not substituted for the identified log-binomial model.

  3. Perform the printed confidence-interval checks. The endpoints are ordered, and the point estimate is contained within them:

    0.82 ≤ 0.91 ≤ 1.00.

    At the printed precision, the upper endpoint is the null RR of 1.00; the displayed interval therefore does not exclude the null.

  4. Apply the article's printed P-value rule exactly. The stated rule is P < .05, not P ≤ .05. The displayed P value .050 is not less than .05. At printed precision, the P value and confidence interval therefore do not create opposing significance decisions.

  5. Check for a conflicting narrative claim. The supplied main article and supplement do not call this other-sites row statistically significant. Consequently, no text/table contradiction is present for the verifier to confirm.

  6. Preserve the rounding and inferential limitations. The supplied files do not provide the unrounded RR, confidence limits, P value, model coefficient, standard error, or enough model output to determine on which side of a threshold the unrounded quantities fall. Nor do they establish that the P value and confidence interval use an identical test construction. The completed workflow therefore did not infer a correction from the boundary-rounded displays.

Existing supported conclusion: The evidence-verifier rejected the proposed statistical reporting inconsistency. At reported precision, the interval includes 1.00, P = .050 does not satisfy P < .05, and no contradictory significance claim is made. No correction is supported by the supplied documents.

Limit on interpretation: Rejection does not prove what the unrounded model output was. It means only that the proposed contradiction cannot be established from the provided printed values and statements. This report does not convert the rejected candidate into a different scientific finding.

Verification instruction: Confirm the row, table legend, and P < .05 rule in the cited PDFs. If an editorial check beyond the supplied evidence is desired, inspect the original unrounded log-binomial output and the definitions used for the confidence interval and P value; do not infer a correction from the rounded row alone.
