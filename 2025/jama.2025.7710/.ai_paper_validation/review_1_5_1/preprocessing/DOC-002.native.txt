SYNOPSIS AND SCHEMA
Background

In Australia, almost 1 in 4 caesarean sections are performed for suspected
fetal distress. A fetus can become distressed when the placenta is unable to
adequately provide it with sufficient oxygen and nutrients during labour. The
only therapeutic options when fetal distress develops are emergency operative
birth by caesarean section (CS) or instrumental vaginal birth (IVB) – both of
which can have serious consequences. Sildenafil Citrate (SC) preferentially
dilates pelvic blood vessels and increases utero-placental blood flow. We
propose that oral SC improves uterine perfusion in labour thus reducing
adverse perinatal outcomes due to fetal distress.

Aim

The iSEARCH Trial will determine if, in women in labour at term receiving oral
50 mg SC vs placebo given in ≤3 doses, reduces a primary composite
endpoint, determined up to 28 days after birth, of ten perinatal outcomes
related to intrapartum hypoxia.

Primary
objective

Compared with placebo, SC achieves a 35% reduction from 7% to 4.55% in
the relative risk of the composite perinatal outcome, determined 28 days after
birth: (i) Intrapartum stillbirth or (ii) 28-day neonatal death, or (iii) Apgar score
<4 at 5 minutes, or (iv) cord artery pH <7.0, or (v) neonatal encephalopathy,
or (vi) neonatal seizures, or (vii) neonatal respiratory support for >4h, or (viii)
neonatal unit (Special Care or Intensive Care) admission lasting >48 h, or (ix)
persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn or (x) meconium aspiration

Secondary
objectives

1. Compared with placebo, SC improves the rate of each of the ten items of
the primary composite perinatal outcome.
2. Compared with placebo, SC reduces the risk of CS or IVB for fetal distress
by 25% (from 20% to 15%).
3. Compared with placebo, SC is cost-effective.
Tertiary and
1. Compared with placebo, SC improves the rate of the following Maternal
correlative
and Obstetric Outcomes: CS for fetal distress, IVB for fetal distress, CS
objectives
for other indications, IVB for other indications, spontaneous vaginal birth,
length of 1st and 2nd stage labour, post-partum haemorrhage (blood loss
>1000ml), post-partum hysterectomy, uterine rupture, Maternal Intensive
Care Unit admission, Maternal mortality.
2. Compared with placebo, SC reduces the relative risks of cerebral palsy,
cognitive delay and neurosensory disability at 2 years of age.
Design
A randomised, multicentre, two-arm parallel, phase III, double-blind, placebocontrolled trial.
Population
Women with singleton or twin pregnancies, planning vaginal birth at ≥37
weeks gestation, screened for eligibility and approached by research midwives
from 34+0 weeks for consent to participant in the trial.
Study
The treatment group will receive 50 mg of SC orally every 8 hours (no more
treatments
than 3 doses) when admitted in spontaneous labour or for induction of labour.
The control group will receive matching placebo at the same timepoints (no
more than 3 doses).
Assessments
Clinical assessments at baseline, after labour, birth, pre-discharge and Ages
and Stages Questionnaire1,2 for neurodevelopmental outcomes at 2 years of
age.
Statistical
A sample size of 3,200 is needed to detect a 35% reduction from 7% to 4.55%
considerations for an alpha of 0.05 and >80% power with 10% drop-out in each arm. This
sample size would also yield >90% power to detect a 25% reduction for the
secondary outcome CS or IVB for fetal distress.

1 BACKGROUND
Hypoxia during labour causes fetal distress, a key factor in 23% of emergency caesarean births in
Australia.3 Hypoxic injury can lead to intrapartum stillbirth, severe neonatal morbidity, neonatal
encephalopathy (NE) and cerebral palsy. 4 5 6 NE is a major risk factor for cerebral palsy. Eighty
percent of infants with moderate or severe NE suffer hypoxic brain injury near the time of birth - in
>50% of cases this injury occurs during labour.7 Other than emergency operative birth by Caesarean
Section (CS) or by Instrumental Vaginal Birth (IVB), we lack therapeutic options to achieve safe
outcomes for the infant.
Sildenafil Citrate preferentially dilates pelvic vessels, increasing utero-placental perfusion.8 It was
originally used in hypertension and ischaemic heart disease and is effective in adult and newborn
pulmonary hypertension.9 10 In our systematic review of 16 maternal studies, side effects of SC were
mild with no long-term adverse outcomes.11
Pilot data from the RidStress Phase 2 RCT
We showed in a world-first Phase 2 RCT (RidStress) of 300 women at term, in a Report of Major
Impact in the American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynaecology in January 2020,12 that up to 3 oral
doses of SC vs placebo: (a) halved CS or IVB for fetal distress [RR 0.49, 95% Confidence Interval
(CI) 0.33 - 0.73), p=0.0004] (b) halved the duration of the 2nd stage of labour (36 vs. 66 min, p=0.02)
and (c) halved fetal heart rate (FHR) abnormalities [15% vs. 32%; RR 0.48, 95% CI 0.31 - 0.75,
p=0.0009]. The trial however lacked power to evaluate neonatal outcomes.
In partnership with the Mater Research Institute-University of Queensland, NHMRC Clinical Trials
Centre (CTC), Stillbirth Centre for Research Excellence and the Australian Cerebral Palsy Alliance,
this MRFF funded, 4-year, Phase 3 RCT will test if SC reduces the relative risk of a primary
composite endpoint of ten perinatal outcomes attributable to intrapartum hypoxia by 35%, from 7%
to 4.55%, in 3200 women in spontaneous or induced labour at term in 16 Australian hospitals.
iSEARCH is the world’s first Phase 3 trial to re-purpose SC - a widely available, well characterised,
affordable, off-patent vasodilator drug - by evaluating whether, compared to Placebo, it improves
perinatal and maternal outcomes by reducing operative birth for fetal distress. These are major areas
of unmet need, identified as critical priorities by The Lancet.13 14 iSEARCH will also contribute to a
separately funded, international Individual Participant Data Prospective Meta-Analysis (IPD PMA)
powered to show a 10% to 20% reduction in intrapartum stillbirth, 28 day neonatal mortality and
other severe neonatal morbidity attributable to intrapartum hypoxia. The IPD PMA will include
multiple concurrent international trials in high, low- and middle income countries following a shared
protocol, funded by relevant national and international bodies and guided by an international Data
and Safety Monitoring Committee with an agreed Charter.12
The STRIDER Trials: The international STRIDER (Sildenafil Therapy In Dismal prognosis Earlyonset severe intrauterine growth Restriction) RCTs used daily SC doses of 75 mg SC for up to 10
weeks (~5,250 mg in total).15,16 In 2019, The Dutch STRIDER trial was stopped due to a nonsignificant increase in neonatal deaths and an increase in persistent pulmonary hypertension of the
newborn in the SC group.17 Although a subsequent meta-analysis of data from 329 infants from the
Australia-New Zealand, United Kingdom and Netherlands trials showed no difference in the risk of
neonatal death or persistent pulmonary hypertension,18 the investigators recommend that SC is not
prescribed outside of clinical trials.19 The indication and dosage of SC in the iSEARCH trial are
however very different from the STRIDER trials as we will only use up to 150mg in women >37 weeks
gestation.

1.1.2 Known potential risks
SC was initially used to treat hypertension and ischaemic heart disease and then widely used for
erectile dysfunction. It is available on prescription in Australia and over the counter in other countries.
It is also used in adult and newborn pulmonary hypertension.9 10 Our systematic review of 16 obstetric
studies, including 165 patients from nine countries including UK, Iran, Egypt, India, Taiwan,
Singapore, Ireland and Chile, found no long-term adverse maternal or perinatal outcomes.11 Our
recent Phase 2 RCT also did not demonstrate any differences in adverse outcomes between the
placebo and SC cohorts.

1.1.3 Known potential benefits
If the study hypotheses are confirmed, wider implementation in other healthcare settings, particularly
in low- and middle-income countries (LMIC) would also be likely, thus benefiting regions with the
highest rates of perinatal morbidity and mortality. Intrapartum stillbirths in particular, account for 1 in
2 of all stillbirths, of which almost 98% occur in LMIC.21 Maternal deaths following CS are also
disproportionately high – 1 in 4 deaths in LMICs occur in women who have undergone CS.22 If SC
is shown to be of benefit, it will reduce the global health burden attributable to birth asphyxia which
is a major cause of disability and comparable to congenital malformations, Type II diabetes or
HIV/AIDS,23 and may reduce maternal mortality rates.
Rationale for Sildenafil Citrate to reduce intrapartum hypoxia and fetal distress
Most cases of fetal distress in labour reflect some degree of pre-existing placental impairment24
which reduces the capacity of the fetus to cope with the stress of uterine contractions. 25 In many
term pregnancies, uterine blood flow can fall by 60% during contractions26 provoking unexpected
fetal distress. Fetal decompensation occurs27 if there is insufficient time for placental reperfusion
between contractions.24 Fetal distress is a major factor in 1 in 4 emergency CS in Australia3 and is
associated with significant perinatal morbidity and mortality (hypoxic ischaemic encephalopathy,
cerebral palsy, intrapartum stillbirth and neonatal death).4 5 6 The only therapeutic options in
response to fetal distress are emergency operative birth by CS or IVB – both of which have serious
consequences including complications in future pregnancies.28,29 There is an urgent unmet need for
effective interventions to reduce perinatal mortality due to intrapartum stillbirth 30 and improve
maternal and neonatal outcomes by reducing fetal distress and emergency operative births in labour.
We31 have shown that a low fetal cerebro-placental ratio (CPR) on ultrasound identifies babies at
increased risk of intrapartum fetal distress. We also showed that women with low pre-labour levels
of placental growth factor (PlGF) are at similar risk.32 However, neither the CPR, PlGF nor
computerised analysis of fetal heart rate patterns33 or fetal ECG,34 have been shown to improve
important perinatal outcomes, further illustrating why this is a critical area of unmet need.

SC is a phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor which enhances Nitric Oxide (NO) mediated effects by
inhibiting cyclic guanosine monophosphate degradation. Increased bioavailability of NO improves
vasodilatation and reduces vascular dysfunction secondary to vasoconstrictors and antiangiogenic
factors.35,36 SC preferentially dilates pelvic blood vessels, increasing utero-placental blood flow.8 We
postulate that SC improves uterine perfusion in labour, preventing fetal decompensation reducing
adverse perinatal outcome due to fetal distress.
We completed a Phase 2 RCT12 which tested if SC reduced the primary outcome of operative birth
(CS or IVB) for fetal distress in term labour. SC reduced the relative risk of operative birth for fetal
distress by 51% [(RR 0.49, 95% CI 0.33 - 0.73), p=0.0004; NNT = 5 (3-11)] and it also reduced
pathological FHR patterns (15% vs. 32%; RR 0.48, 95% CI 0.31 - 0.75, p=0.0009) but it lacked power
to show a significant effect on the composite neonatal outcome which was inconclusive with wide
confidence intervals (20.7% vs. 21.3%; RR 0.97 (95% CI 0.62-1.50), p=0.89) and thus did not rule
out major benefit or harm of this key endpoint.
In Summary
Given our promising Phase 2 results, iSEARCH will evaluate if intrapartum oral SC versus placebo
reduces the relative risk of a composite of severe perinatal outcomes (Primary Outcome) related to
intrapartum hypoxia by 35%, from 7% to 4.55%. Our overarching research questions are: Does
maternal oral Sildenafil Citrate in labour reduce the primary composite endpoint of ten severe
neonatal outcomes and secondary outcomes which include emergency operative birth for fetal
distress, and is it more cost effective compared to placebo?

3 DESIGN
This study is a randomised, multicentre, two-arm parallel, phase III, double-blind, placebo-controlled

4 STUDY POPULATION
Participants must meet all of the inclusion criteria and none of the exclusion criteria to be eligible for
this trial. No exceptions will be made to these eligibility requirements at the time of registration. All
enquiries about eligibility should be addressed by contacting the NHMRC CTC prior to registration.

4.1 Target Population
Pregnant women from 34+0 weeks planning a vaginal birth at term (>37 weeks gestation). The
research will be conducted in a manner sensitive to the cultural principles of Aboriginal and Torres
Strait Islander communities.

4.2

Inclusion Criteria

1.

Women with singleton or dichorionic twin pregnancies planning in-hospital vaginal birth at
term (>37 weeks gestation).
2. Age 18 years and older.
3. Willing and able to comply with all study requirements.
4. Signed, written informed consent. For Non-English speaking participants, a Health Care
Interpreter must be engaged to obtain informed consent.

4.3 Exclusion Criteria
1. A woman should not be entered if the responsible clinician or the woman are, for any medical
or non-medical reasons, reasonably certain that SC would be inappropriate for her in
comparison with no treatment or some other treatment that could be offered in or outside the
trial.56
2. Triplets or higher order multiple births, which are generally delivered electively before term.
3. Contraindications to the investigational product SC (see 5.4).57
4. Participants who are taking any type of nitrate drug therapy or who utilize short-acting nitratecontaining medications during labour [such as sodium nitroprusside, bosentan,
fosamprenavir and ritonavir combination, hepatic enzyme inhibitors CYP3A4 (including
itraconazole, ketoconazole, ritonavir, cimetidine, erythromycin, saquinavir, darunavir), or
hepatic enzyme substrates (CYP3A4), medications used to treat pulmonary arterial
hypertension, and other phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors like riociguat], due to the risk of
potentially life-threatening hypotension.57
5. Severe hepatic or renal impairment.57

4.4 Study Enrolment
4.4.1 Screening
Written informed consent must be signed and dated by the participant, and signed and dated by the
Investigator, prior to any study-specific screening investigations being performed.
All women will be screened for eligibility and approached by research midwives from 34+0 for consent
to participate in the trial. Where appropriate, a researcher from the trial team will speak with the
potential participant about the trial via a virtual/Telehealth platform, where e-signatures are to be
integrated by participant and staff into the Participant Information Sheet and Consent Form. After

4.4.2 Registration and Randomisation
Women will be centrally randomised through the NHMRC CTC. Treatment allocation will be balanced
using minimisation for the study site and women will be allocated to the treatment groups in a ratio
of 1:1.
Registration, including randomisation, must be done according to the instructions in the Study
Manual.
Randomisation will be performed after registration and consent to participate has been obtained
when the participant is admitted to the hospital either in spontaneous labour or for induction of labour.
It will be done according to the instructions in the Study Manual and should be undertaken after all
screening assessments have been performed and the responsible investigator has verified the
participant’s eligibility. Women may only be randomised once in this trial. Once the randomisation
process has been completed, the participant will be assigned a treatment arm.

4.5 Withdrawal of consent
Participants may withdraw consent at any time during the study without the need to justify their
decision. Withdrawal of consent will not jeopardise future medical care.
Participants who do not receive study treatment for any reason will be encouraged to continue in the
study. Please refer to section 6 regarding study procedures to be performed.
Participants who do not receive allocated treatment or withdraw from the study follow-up visits after
randomisation will be included in the trial database and will be reported.

After registration but before the onset of labour, women at select participating Queensland hospitals,

6.2.2

Management of Labour and Puerperium

All women routinely have their blood pressure taken when admitted to the labour ward. Blood
pressure monitoring should follow local protocols and guidelines. Intrapartum FHR monitoring should
follow local protocols and guidelines. Umbilical cord blood gases will be performed in all women.
Classification of FHR abnormalities is based on the Royal Australian and New Zealand College of
Obstetricians and Gynaecologists guidelines.46 Management after birth will be in accordance with
local hospital guidelines.

6.2.3 Management of the Infant after birth
All infants will also receive routine oxygen saturation screening as is current standard practice in
Australia. This test complements the newborn examination and is used to detect hypoxemia in
infants that otherwise appear well. It will be performed 24-48 hours after birth, although if necessary,
it can also be done as early as 4 hours after birth. Infants with oxygen saturations of >95% are very
unlikely to have major congenital heart or significant pulmonary disease, including persistent
pulmonary hypertension of the newborn. Infants with oxygen saturations <95% will receive further
assessment by the paediatric team and additional investigations including echocardiography may be
required.

Women and their newborns will be followed up for at least 28 days after end of treatment as
described in Section 8.2 if adverse events are ongoing at the end-of-treatment visit.
Participants will be followed up by phone or text or email every 6 months until the study endpoint is
reached. This is to ensure that their contact details remain up-to-date for the longer-term childhood
neurodevelopment outcome study at 24-36 months (subject to further funding).

6.4 Follow-up after participant withdrawal
If a participant wishes to stop the 6-monthly contact, they will be asked for consent to allow
information about their ongoing health status to be periodically collected via phone/email contact via

and Welfare (AIHW) National Death Index (NDI).
For participants who have been lost to follow-up, Medicare may be used to provide updated contact

7.1.1 Intrapartum Stillbirth or 28-day Neonatal Death
Intrapartum stillbirth is the death in labour of an infant that was alive at the commencement of labour.

7.1.2 28-day neonatal mortality
Neonatal mortality is the death of an infant within 28 days of birth

7.1.3 Apgar score <4 at 5 minutes
Numerical score58 to evaluate the infant’s condition at 5 minutes after birth based on five
characteristics – heart rate, respiratory condition, muscle tone, reflexes and colour. Assessed
routinely and recorded in the medical record.

7.1.4 Cord artery pH <7.0
Significant metabolic acidosis defined as cord arterial blood pH <7.0.59

7.1.5 Neonatal encephalopathy
A clinically defined syndrome of disturbed neurologic function in the earliest post-natal days of life in
an infant born at or beyond 35 weeks of gestation, manifested by a subnormal level of consciousness
or seizures, and often accompanied by difficulty with initiating and maintaining respiration and
depression of tone and reflexes.60 Measured in infants >35 weeks using a modified Sarnat criteria60
to assess severity of encephalopathy and graded from 0-3. Grade 0, no hypoxic ischaemic
encephalopathy to grade 3, severe hypoxic ischaemic encephalopathy.

7.1.6 Neonatal seizures
Defined as the paroxysmal alteration in neurological function of behavioural, motor or neurological
function. May be clinical or diagnosed on electroencephalogram.60

7.1.7 Respiratory support lasting >4 hours
Supplementary respiratory support includes intubation and intermittent positive pressure ventilation,
high flow oxygen via nasal cannula or CPAP.

7.1.8 Neonatal unit admission lasting >48 hours
Neonatal unit admission is defined as the number of hours from admission to discharge.

Upon registration to the study, consent will be sought for participation in the infant neurodevelopment

Follow-up assessment for neurodevelopmental outcomes at 2-3 years corrected age will consist of

8.2 Economic Evaluation
A modelled cost-utility analysis will be undertaken using trial data. The analysis will be from a payer’s
perspective (covering government and individuals), and cover a one-year time horizon. A Markov
microsimulation model will be constructed comparing treatment with SC with placebo (standard of
care). Quality adjusted life years for will be assigned to each health state, and will be obtained from
the literature61 . Costs will include direct costs of the intervention (drug costs), as well as background
health service use – hospital inpatient, emergency department, outpatient, community based care,
and pharmaceutical use. All costs will be presented in 2023/24 dollars, with no discounting applied.
We will seek consent to extract inpatient, outpatient, and emergency department data and Medicare
Benefits Schedule and Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme data for all participants from study entry to
six months of age. Disutilities and costs associated with adverse events will be included. Parametric
bootstrapping will quantify the uncertainty associated with the model parameters and 95%
uncertainty ranges presented. A budget impact analysis will also be undertaken using a five-year
time horizon.

13 STATISTICAL CONSIDERATIONS
13.1 Sample Size
The incidence of the composite adverse outcome is estimated at 7% based on data in a recent large
RCT in term pregnancies54 and the Australian and New Zealand Neonatal Network. To detect a 35%
reduction from 7% to 4.55% for an alpha of 0.05 and >80% power with a 10% drop-out in each arm,
needs 3,200 women, also yielding >90% power to detect a 25% reduction for the secondary outcome
(CS or IVB for fetal distress).

13.2 Statistical Analysis
Analyses of the primary and secondary outcomes will adhere to an intent-to-treat basis using
generalised linear models (binary or normal). Intervention effect will be presented as relative risk
(RR) or mean difference (MD), as appropriate, with 95% confidence intervals. Two-tailed P values
<0.05 will be considered statistically significant. Primary analyses will be unadjusted. Numbers
needed to treat to prevent one adverse outcome will be calculated. Where there are differences in
the baseline characteristics between the two treatment groups that might be associated with
outcomes, secondary analyses of the primary outcome will be carried out using multiple (logbinominal) regression. Reporting will follow CONSORT and TRIPOD guidelines.
The key secondary outcomes for iSEARCH comprise the individual components of the primary
composite outcome (Section 2.2.1 above). P values adjusted for the ten comparisons performed for
this set of endpoints will be derived using the Benjamin-Hochberg procedure, with a family-wise error
rate of 5%.63,64 Results of other endpoint, subgroup and sensitivity analyses will be interpreted in

iSEARCH Protocol V2.1 [31st January 2022]

Page 20 of 26

proper context and with due consideration of the risk of Type I error. The clinical evidence of the
statistical evidence from iSEARCH will take into account the recommendations of Pocock et al. 65,66

13.3 Interim analyses
The Independent Data and Safety Monitoring Committee (IDSMC) will review interim data on the
primary outcome, specified adverse events and other evidence after 50% of recruitment or whenever
they deem appropriate informed by recommendations of Peto, Pocock and others.67-69 There will be
no adjustment to alpha for interim analyses using a Haybittle-Peto boundary.
Interim analyses of the primary composite outcome: The IDSMC will advise the TMC if in their view
there is proof beyond reasonable doubt of net benefit or harm for the primary composite outcome,
employing a commonly used formal threshold of P<0.001 for nominal significance, as recommended
by Geller and Pocock.69
Interim analyses of mortality: The IDSMC will advise the TMC if in their view there is a difference in
mortality due to intrapartum stillbirth and/or 28-day neonatal mortality identified as a deviation from
the null indicated by a Haybittle-Peto boundary which is equivalent to P <0.0027,67,68 which would
be needed to justify recommending early stopping.

generated in this study will remain confidential. All information will be stored securely at the NHMRC
CTC, University of Sydney and will only be available to people directly involved with the study.

15.3 Protocol deviations and amendments
A protocol deviation is any noncompliance with the clinical trial protocol, GCP, or other ethical or
regulatory requirement. The noncompliance may be either on the part of the participant, the
investigator, or the study staff. As a result of deviations, corrective actions are to be developed by
the sponsor and implemented promptly. All deviations must be addressed in study source
documents, reported to the CTC. Protocol deviations should be sent to the Lead HREC and RGO
as per their local guidelines.
Changes and amendments to the protocol can only be made by the TMC. Approval of amendments
by the Institutional HREC is required prior to their implementation. In some instances, an
amendment may require a change to a consent form. The Investigator must receive approval/advice
of the revised consent form prior to implementation of the change. In addition, changes to the data
collected, if required, will be incorporated in the amendment.
The investigator should not implement any changes to, or deviations from, the protocol except where
necessary to eliminate immediate hazard(s) to trial participant(s).
In response to unforeseen circumstances (e.g. natural disasters, pandemics), the study investigators
may direct an immediate change to the protocol with the primary aim of continuing health care and
ensuring the participant’s best interests at all times. Changes will be communicated with the relevant
parties e.g. the Lead HREC/RGOs, and regulatory authorities if appropriate, and participants in due
course.

15.4 Data Handling and Record Keeping
All trial data required for the monitoring and analysis of the study will be recorded on the eCRFs
provided. All required data entry fields must be completed. The eCRF serves as part of the
investigator’s record of participants’ study-related data. Data corrections will be done according to
the instructions provided. The investigator will be asked to confirm the accuracy of completed CRFs
by signing key CRFs as indicated.
Source documents pertaining to the trial must be maintained by investigational sites. Source
documents may include a participant's medical records, hospital charts, clinic charts, the
investigator's participant study files, as well as the results of diagnostic tests such as X-rays,
laboratory tests, and electrocardiograms. The investigator's copy of the case report forms serves as
part of the investigator's record of a participant's study-related data.

15.5 Study Monitoring
Data from this study will be monitored by Clinical Trials Program staff from the NHMRC CTC or their
delegates in accordance with a separate study monitoring plan. This will include centralised review
of CRFs and other study documents for protocol compliance, data accuracy and completeness.
Monitoring may include monitoring visits to investigational sites during the study for source data
verification, review of the investigator’s site file and drug handling records. The NHMRC CTC will
be given direct access to source documents, CRFs and other study-related documents. By signing
the informed consent form, the participant gives authorised NHMRC CTC staff direct access to their
medical records and the study data.

15.6 Audit and Inspection
This study may be participant to audit or inspection by representatives of the CTC or representatives
of local e.g. the TGA.

15.7 Clinical Study Report
A Clinical Study Report which summarises and interprets all the pertinent study data collected will
be issued which may form the basis of a manuscript intended for publication. The Clinical Study
Report or summary thereof will be provided to the Human research Ethics Committees (HRECs) that
approved the study and the funding agency (MRFF).

15.8 Publication and Data Sharing Policy
The Trial Management Committee will appoint a Writing Committee to draft manuscript(s) based on
the trial data. Manuscript(s) will be submitted to peer-reviewed journal(s). The Writing Committee
will develop a publication plan, including authorship, target journals and expected dates of
publication. All publications must receive written approval from the TMC prior to submission.
The CTC will ensure that a summary of the clinical trial results is submitted to an appropriate register
no later than 1 year after the completion date (referred to as the “primary completion date”) of the
clinical trial, which is defined as the date of final data collection for the primary outcome measure.

15.9 Conflict of Interest and Financial Disclosure
Any actual conflict of interest of persons who have a role in the design, conduct, analysis, publication,
or any aspect of this trial will be disclosed and managed. Furthermore, persons who have a perceived
conflict of interest will be required to have such conflicts managed in a way that is appropriate to
their participation in the trial. The study leadership in conjunction with the CTC and collaborative
group has established policies and procedures for all TMC members and site investigators to
disclose all conflicts of interest and will establish a mechanism for the management of all reported
dualities of interest. Investigators are responsible for providing information on financial interests
during the course of the study.
