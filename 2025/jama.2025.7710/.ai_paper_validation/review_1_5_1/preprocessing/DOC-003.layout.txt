154   2.2 Aim and Objectives
155   The aim of this study is to test the hypotheses that ≤3 doses of oral 50 mg SC vs. placebo will
156   improve perinatal and/or maternal outcomes related to intrapartum hypoxia.
157   The primary objective is to demonstrate or refute a reduction of 35% in the relative risk of the
158   composite perinatal outcome (from 7% to 4.55%) comprising ten components, shown below and in
159   Table 1. Composite neonatal outcomes, appropriately constructed, are often employed in multicentre
160   trials.
161   Composite primary outcome:
162      I.   Intrapartum stillbirth, or
163     II.   28-day neonatal death, or
164    III.   Apgar score <4 at 5 minutes, or
165    IV.    Cord artery pH <7.0, or
166     V.    Neonatal encephalopathy, or
167    VI.    Neonatal seizures, or
168   VII.    Neonatal respiratory support lasting over 4 h, or
169   VIII.   Neonatal unit (Special Care or Intensive care) admission lasting over 48 h, or
170    IX.    Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn, or
171     X.    Meconium aspiration

203   2.3 Design
204   This study is a randomised, multicentre, two-arm parallel, phase III, double-blind, placebo-controlled
205   trial.

206   2.4 Study population
207   Participants must meet all of the inclusion criteria and none of the exclusion criteria to be eligible for
208   this trial. No exceptions will be made to these eligibility requirements at the time of registration. All
209   enquiries about eligibility should be addressed by contacting the NHMRC CTC prior to registration.

210   2.4.1 Target Population
211   Pregnant women from 34+0 weeks planning a vaginal birth at term (>37 weeks gestation). The
212   research will be conducted in a manner sensitive to the cultural principles of Aboriginal and Torres
213   Strait Islander communities.

214   2.4.2 Inclusion Criteria
215      1.   Women with singleton or dichorionic twin pregnancies planning in-hospital vaginal birth at
216           term (>37 weeks gestation).
217      2.   Age 18 years and older.
218      3.   Willing and able to comply with all study requirements.
219      4.   Signed, written informed consent. For Non-English speaking participants, a Health Care
220           Interpreter must be engaged to obtain informed consent.

221   2.4.3 Exclusion Criteria
222       1. A woman should not be entered if the responsible clinician or the woman are, for any medical
223          or non-medical reasons, reasonably certain that SC would be inappropriate for her in
224          comparison with no treatment or some other treatment that could be offered in or outside the
225          trial.
226       2. Triplets or higher order multiple births, which are generally delivered electively before term.
227       3. Contraindications to the investigational product SC (see 5.4).
228       4. Participants who are taking any type of nitrate drug therapy or who utilize short-acting nitrate-
229          containing medications during labour [such as sodium nitroprusside, bosentan, fosamprenavir
230          and ritonavir combination, hepatic enzyme inhibitors CYP3A4 (including itraconazole,
231          ketoconazole, ritonavir, cimetidine, erythromycin, saquinavir, darunavir), or hepatic enzyme
232          substrates (CYP3A4), medications used to treat pulmonary arterial hypertension, and other
233          phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors like riociguat], due to the risk of potentially life-
234          threatening hypotension.
235       5. Severe hepatic or renal impairment.

236   2.5 Treatment Plan
237   Sildenafil Citrate is the study intervention in this trial. Placebo is the control intervention. Treatment
238   will commence after transfer to the labour ward once in labour or for induction of labour. The dose of
239   SC is 50mg orally or equivalent placebo every 8 hours to a maximum of three doses.

240   2.5.1 Study Treatment
      Participants will be randomised to one of the following two arms:
      Arm                Agent(s)             Dose          Route    Duration
      Active             Sildenafil Citrate        50 mg         Oral        Every 8 hours from the start of labour
                                                                             or induction of labour to a maximum
      Control            Placebo                   Placebo       Oral        of 3 doses.

241   2.5.2 Required Background Treatment
242   All participants are expected to receive background treatment consistent with routine obstetric care.

243   2.6 Randomisation and Blinding
244   Women will be centrally randomised through the NHMRC CTC. Treatment allocation will be
245   balanced using minimisation for the study site and women will be allocated to the treatment groups in
246   a ratio of 1:1.
247   Registration, including randomisation, must be done according to the instructions in the Study
248   Manual.
249   Randomisation will be performed after registration and consent to participate has been obtained when
250   the participant is admitted to the hospital either in spontaneous labour or for induction of labour. It
251   will be done according to the instructions in the Study Manual and should be undertaken after all
252   screening assessments have been performed and the responsible investigator has verified the
253   participant’s eligibility. Women may only be randomised once in this trial. Once the randomisation
254   process has been completed, the participant will be assigned a treatment arm.
255   The study is double-blinded. Unblinding is not generally necessary for the management of a
256   participant with an adverse event and, as it has an impact on the study’s validity, is strongly
257   discouraged. The need for unblinding should be very uncommon as the study intervention is rarely
258   associated with severe side effects and it will not delay or prevent standard management of the
259   participant. However, if required, it can be done after discussion with the Study Chair and NHMRC
260   CTC Clinical Lead using the emergency unblinding number in the study manual.

261   2.7 Outcome Definitions
262   In-hospital perinatal and maternal outcome events occurring from screening to discharge home will be
263   collected from medical records. With the exception of 28-day neonatal mortality, all outcome data
264   will be routinely available before discharge. There are no formal study assessments visits. Research
265   midwives will contact participants 28 days after discharge to ascertain if there any issues relating to
266   the study that require additional follow up.

267   2.7.1 Primary Composite Outcome
268   A composite of the following adverse perinatal outcomes:
269      •       Intrapartum stillbirth or
270      •       28-day neonatal mortality or
271      •       Apgar score <4 at 5 minutes or
272      •       Cord artery pH <7.0 or
273      •       Neonatal encephalopathy (Sarnat score) up to 28 days or
274      •       Neonatal seizures up to 28 days or

275       •        Neonatal respiratory support >4h or
276       •        Neonatal unit (Special Care or Intensive Care) admission lasting >48h or
277       •        Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn or
278       •        Meconium aspiration
279   2.7.1.1    Intrapartum Stillbirth or 28-day Neonatal Death
280   Intrapartum stillbirth is the death in labour of an infant that was alive at the commencement of labour.
281   2.7.1.2    28-day neonatal mortality
282   Neonatal mortality is the death of an infant within 28 days of birth
283   2.7.1.3    Apgar score <4 at 5 minutes
284   Numerical score to evaluate the infant’s condition at 5 minutes after birth based on five characteristics
285   – heart rate, respiratory condition, muscle tone, reflexes and colour. Assessed routinely and recorded
286   in the medical record.
287   2.7.1.4    Cord artery pH <7.0
288   Significant metabolic acidosis defined as cord arterial blood pH <7.0
289   2.7.1.5    Neonatal encephalopathy
290   A clinically defined syndrome of disturbed neurologic function in the earliest post-natal days of life in
291   an infant born at or beyond 35 weeks of gestation, manifested by a subnormal level of consciousness
292   or seizures, and often accompanied by difficulty with initiating and maintaining respiration and
293   depression of tone and reflexes. Measured in infants >35 weeks using a modified Sarnat criteria to
294   assess severity of encephalopathy and graded from 0-3. Grade 0, no hypoxic ischaemic
295   encephalopathy to grade 3, severe hypoxic ischaemic encephalopathy.
296   2.7.1.6    Neonatal seizures
297   Defined as the paroxysmal alteration in neurological function of behavioural, motor or neurological
298   function. May be clinical or diagnosed on electroencephalogram.
299   2.7.1.7    Respiratory support lasting >4 hours
300   Supplementary respiratory support includes intubation and intermittent positive pressure ventilation,
301   high flow oxygen via nasal cannula or CPAP.
302   2.7.1.8    Neonatal unit admission lasting >48 hours
303   Neonatal unit admission is defined as the number of hours from admission to discharge.
304   2.7.1.9    Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn
305   Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn is defined as a syndrome of failed circulatory
306   adaptation at birth due to delay or impairment in the normal fall in pulmonary vascular resistance that
307   occurs following birth. Infants with oxygen saturations of >95% are very unlikely to have persistent
308   pulmonary hypertension of the newborn.
309   2.7.1.10   Meconium Aspiration
310   Presence of meconium in the trachea and symptoms/signs fulfilling criteria for persistent pulmonary
311   hypertension of the newborn

312   2.7.2 Secondary Outcomes
313   2.7.2.1    Individual components of primary composite endpoints
314   These include individual components of the primary composite endpoint (Intrapartum stillbirth, 28-
315   day neonatal mortality, Apgar score <4 at 5 minutes, Cord artery pH <7.0, Neonatal encephalopathy,
316   Neonatal seizures, Neonatal respiratory support >4h, Neonatal unit (Special Care or Intensive Care)
317   admission lasting >48h, Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn or Meconium aspiration)
318   of the primary composite outcome.

319   2.7.2.2    CS or IVB for fetal distress
320   Defined as reduction in the relative risk of CS or IVB for fetal distress by 25% (from 20% to 15%).

321   2.7.3 Tertiary Outcomes
322   2.7.3.1    CS for fetal distress
323   Defined as an emergency CS for fetal distress during labour as detected by fetal heart rate
324   abnormalities based on the Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Obstetricians and
325   Gynaecologists guidelines and/or abnormal fetal scalp pH or lactate levels.
326   2.7.3.2    Instrumental vaginal birth for fetal distress
327   Defined as a vaginal birth using forceps or vacuum extraction for fetal distress.
328   2.7.3.3    CS for other indications
329   Other indications include CS for failure to progress, failed induction of labour, malpresentation, failed
330   instrumental birth, cord prolapse/presentation, uterine rupture etc.
331   2.7.3.4    Instrumental vaginal birth for other indications
332   Defined as a vaginal birth using forceps and vacuum extraction for non-fetal distress indications.
333   2.7.3.5    Spontaneous vaginal birth
334   Defined as the uncomplicated vaginal birth of an infant.
335   2.7.3.6    Length of 1st and 2nd stage of labour
            st
336   The 1 stage of labour is defined as commencing when labour is diagnosed by the attending obstetric
337   team to full dilatation (i.e. 10cm). The 2nd stage of labour is defined as the period of time from full
338   cervical dilatation to birth of the infant.
339   2.7.3.7    Post-partum haemorrhage
340   Defined as excessive bleeding (>1000ml) following the birth of a baby.
341   2.7.3.8    Post-partum hysterectomy
342   Defined as the removal of the uterus (+/- cervix) after birth.
343   2.7.3.9    Uterine rupture
344   Defined as a full thickness tear through the uterine myometrium and serosa.
345   2.7.3.10   Maternal Intensive Care Unit admission
346   Intensive care unit admission is defined as the admission of the participant to an intensive care unit.
347   2.7.3.11   Maternal Mortality
348   Maternal mortality is defined as death of mother before discharge

349   2.8 Sample Size
350   The incidence of the composite adverse outcome is estimated at 7% based on data in a recent large
351   RCT in term pregnancies and the Australian and New Zealand Neonatal Network. To detect a 35%
352   reduction from 7% to 4.55% for an alpha of 0.05 and >80% power with a 10% drop-out in each arm,
353   needs 3,200 women, also yielding >90% power to detect a 25% reduction for the secondary outcome
354   (CS or IVB for fetal distress).

355   2.9 Interim Analysis
356   The Independent Data and Safety Monitoring Committee (IDSMC) will review interim data on the
357   primary outcome, specified adverse events and other evidence after 50% of recruitment or whenever
358   they deem appropriate informed by recommendations of Peto, Pocock and others. There will be no
359   adjustment to alpha for interim analyses using a Haybittle-Peto boundary.

370   3 Statistical Analysis
371   3.1 Analysis datasets
372   3.1.1 Modified Intention To Treat population
373   The principal analysis of study endpoints will be according to the modified intention-to-treat (mITT)
374   principle including all subjects with available outcome data. All randomised babies who received no
375   study treatment and were later found to be ineligible (before randomisation) will be referred to
376   independent adjudicators for a blinded assessment. If the reason is judged not related to the
377   randomised treatment allocation or any outcome events post randomisation the baby will be excluded
378   from the mITT dataset in a sensitivity analysis.

379   3.1.2 Safety population
380   All randomised patients who receive at least 1 dose of study treatment will constitute the safety
381   population. Patients will be counted in the group according to the treatment they received.

382   3.2 General Principles
383   Summary statistics (means and standard deviations, medians and inter-quartile range) will be
384   presented for continuous baseline variables. Number (%) will be presented for categorical variables.
385   Categorical variables will be compared with chi-squared tests, with a test for trend over ordered
386   categories (Mantel-Haenszel Chi-square). Continuous variables will be compared using t-tests or the
387   Wilcoxon rank-sum test if non-normally distributed.
388   Analyses of the primary and secondary outcomes will adhere to the intention-to-treat principal using
389   Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE) to accommodate correlation of data between siblings from
390   multiple births (log-binomial). An exchangeable correlation structure will be assumed. As the data is
391   sparsely clustered due to a small proportion of mother’s having twins (i.e., ~0.6%), methods which
392   treat all babies as independent observations will also be performed. Results from the two methods will
393   be compared. Failure to take the non-independence of twins into account can lead to under-estimated
394   standard errors and biased parameter estimates. Therefore, the GEE method is intended to be the
395   primary analysis provided that the models are numerically stable and converge well. Maternal
396   secondary/tertiary endpoints will be analysed using Generalised Linear Models (log-binomial).
397   Intervention effect will be presented as relative risk (RR) or mean difference (MD), as appropriate,
398   with 95% confidence intervals. Two-tailed P values <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.
399   Primary analyses will be unadjusted. Numbers needed to treat to prevent one adverse outcome will be
400   calculated. Secondary analyses of the primary outcome will be carried out including adjustment for
401   relevant baseline prognostic factors using multiple (log-binominal or Robust Poisson) regression. If
402   appropriate, the conditional binomial exact test will be used to compare groups when expected cell
403   counts are small (i.e., <5).
404   The key secondary outcomes for iSEARCH comprise the individual components of the primary
405   composite outcome (Section 2.7.1 above). P values adjusted for the ten comparisons performed for
406   this set of endpoints will be derived using the Benjamin-Hochberg procedure, with a family-wise error
407   rate of 5%. Results of other endpoints, subgroup and sensitivity analyses will be interpreted in proper
408   context and with due consideration of the risk of Type I error. In general, complete-case analysis will
409   be performed with no imputation done for missing data.

410   3.3 Subject Disposition
411   A patient disposition flow-chart will be presented showing the number of mothers screened, eligible,
412   consented and randomised to each treatment arm. Reasons for withdrawal, losses to follow-up and
413   numbers available for mITT and safety analyses will be detailed.
414   A table will also be prepared showing the number of randomised mothers found to be ineligible with
415   reasons detailed.

416   3.4 Treatment Adherence
417   The proportions of mothers that received treatment will be calculated. The numbers receiving 1, 2
418   and all 3 doses will be described along with the time from randomisation to each dose and the dose
419   received. Reasons for not giving treatment will also be detailed.

420   3.5 Baseline Analyses
421   A table will be prepared showing the stratification factors by treatment arm (i.e., site). Descriptive
422   statistics will be prepared to summarise baseline characteristics (i.e., maternal demographics and
423   clinical details) by treatment allocation.

424   3.6 Analysis of the Primary Outcome
425   The primary analysis of the primary outcome a composite of ten perinatal outcomes will be based on a
426   GEE log-binomial model. A relative risk, with a 95% confidence interval (CI) will be presented to
427   indicate the relative reduction (or increase) in risk associated with Sildenafil therapy along with
428   numbers and proportions meeting the outcome in each treatment arm. Numbers needed to treat to
429   prevent one adverse outcome will be calculated.
430
431   In 27% of babies a cord artery test for pH level was not performed. As this is a component of the
432   primary outcome ‘not done’ for this component will be assumed to be no event.

433   3.7 Analysis of Secondary and Tertiary Outcomes
434   The analysis of neonatal secondary outcomes will be evaluated with the use of GEE log-binomial
435   models. Maternal outcomes will be analysed using a log-binomial generalized linear model for binary
436   outcomes, and a t-test for continuous outcomes.

437   3.8 Safety Outcomes
438   An analysis of neonatal and maternal mortality will also be performed by treatment received.
439   The frequency of maternal adverse events of interest will be analysed by treatment received. The
440   comparison between treatment arms will employ a chi-squared test or the conditional binomial exact
441   test if expected cell counts are small (i.e., <5).

442   3.9 Adjusted Analyses
443   Secondary analysis of the primary endpoint to adjustment for prognostic variables will be carried out
444   using multivariable regression. Adjustment variables will include: (1) Small for gestational age (Birth
445   weight <10th centile for gestation); (2) Pre-eclampsia; (3) Method of delivery for previous pregnancy
446   (no prior delivery, spontaneous vaginal delivery, instrument assisted vaginal delivery, and caesarean
447   section); and (4) post-natal diagnosis of fetal anomaly. If the log-binomial model fails to converge a
448   Robust (modified) Poisson regression model or other computational method will be applied and/or the
449   clustering amongst twins will not be accounted for. If it is not possible to fit a GEE model, multiple
450   birth will be included as a covariate in the model.

451   3.10 Sensitivity Analysis and missing data
452   The following sensitivity analyses will be performed for the primary outcome:
453      1) Imputation of missing data will be performed for Cord artery pH level using maximum
454           likelihood estimation based on the observed pooled event rates within twins (cT) and non-
455           twins (cNT). The imputation will be stratified by twin status due to the much higher Cord
456           Artery pH <7 event rate among twins (11% vs 0.6% among babies where cord artery pH was
457           tested) and higher missing data rate. This calculation will take into account the occurrence of
458           the other 9 components of the composite primary outcome. The imputation will be performed
459           according to the following steps:



                                                                                                               13
      iSEARCH – Statistical Analysis Plan                               Protocol version: V2.1 31st January 2022


460               a. The Cord Artery pH <7 event rates (cT and cNT) will be calculated among babies
461                  where Cord Artery pH testing was performed,
462               b. Of those babies having an event the fractions (fT and fNT) that suffered none of the
463                  other 9 components will be calculated,
464               c. The additional number of events will be calculated by multiplying the number of
465                  babies with Cord Artery Not Done who had not already met the primary endpoint by
466                  p and f above within each treatment arm (ST, SNT, PT, PNT) e.g., for non-twins in
467                  Placebo arm the calculation is PNTcNTfNT.
468       2) An analysis of the primary outcome based on 9 components only will be performed with Cord
469          artery pH <7 excluded from the definition.
470       3) A generalised linear model (log-binomial) will be fitted ignoring the correlation between
471          twins.
472       4) Models will be fitted with an extra covariate included to indicate whether or not Cord Artery
473          testing was done in order to adjust for any imputation. A GLM model will be used if there are
474          numerical estimation problems with the GEE model.
475

476   3.11 Subgroup Analyses
477   Consistency of the treatment effect on the primary end point will be evaluated across the following
478   pre-specified subgroups: (1) Small for gestational age (Birth weight <10th centile for gestation); (2)
479   multiple pregnancy; (3) pre-eclampsia; (4) Method of delivery for previous pregnancy CS; (5) non-
480   cephalic presentation; (6) post-natal diagnosis of fetal anomaly.
481
482   This will be performed by fitting an interaction term between the subgroup of interest and treatment in
483   a log-binomial GLM model (along with the relevant main effect terms). The two-sided level of
484   significance is 0.05, however due to the number of analyses results should be interpreted with caution.
485   Treatment effects will be determined from a model fitted to each of the levels of the subgroup, e.g.,
486   for pre-eclampsia separate models will be fitted for yes and no. Models will not include adjustment
487   for baseline covariates. A GEE model will not be applied because the number of twins in each
488   subgroup maybe too small for models to converge.
