7 OUTCOMES, ENDPOINTS AND OTHER MEASUREMENTS  
In-hospital perinatal and maternal outcome events occurring from screening to discharge home will 
be collected from medical records. With the exception of 28-day neonatal mortality, all outcome data 
will be routinely available before discharge. There are no formal study assessments visits. Research 
midwives will contact participants 28 days after discharge to ascertain if there any issues relating to 
the study that require additional follow up.   
7.1 Primary Composite Outcome 
A composite of the following adverse perinatal outcomes: 
• 
Intrapartum stillbirth or  
• 
28-day neonatal mortality or 
• 
Apgar score <4 at 5 minutes or  
• 
Cord artery pH <7.0 or 
• 
Neonatal encephalopathy (Sarnat score) up to 28 days or 
• 
Neonatal seizures up to 28 days or 
• 
Neonatal respiratory support >4h or 
• 
Neonatal unit (Special Care or Intensive Care) admission lasting >48h or 
• 
Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn or 
• 
Meconium aspiration 
7.1.1 Intrapartum Stillbirth or 28-day Neonatal Death 
Intrapartum stillbirth is the death in labour of an infant that was alive at the commencement of labour.  
7.1.2 28-day neonatal mortality 
Neonatal mortality is the death of an infant within 28 days of birth 
7.1.3 Apgar score <4 at 5 minutes  
Numerical score58 to evaluate the infant’s condition at 5 minutes after birth based on five 
characteristics – heart rate, respiratory condition, muscle tone, reflexes and colour. Assessed 
routinely and recorded in the medical record.   
7.1.4 Cord artery pH <7.0 
Significant metabolic acidosis defined as cord arterial blood pH <7.0.59 
7.1.5 Neonatal encephalopathy 
A clinically defined syndrome of disturbed neurologic function in the earliest post-natal days of life in 
an infant born at or beyond 35 weeks of gestation, manifested by a subnormal level of consciousness 
or seizures, and often accompanied by difficulty with initiating and maintaining respiration and 
depression of tone and reflexes.60 Measured in infants >35 weeks using a modified Sarnat criteria60 
to assess severity of encephalopathy and graded from 0-3. Grade 0, no hypoxic ischaemic 
encephalopathy to grade 3, severe hypoxic ischaemic encephalopathy.  
7.1.6 Neonatal seizures 
Defined as the paroxysmal alteration in neurological function of behavioural, motor or neurological 
function. May be clinical or diagnosed on electroencephalogram.60  
7.1.7 Respiratory support lasting >4 hours 
Supplementary respiratory support includes intubation and intermittent positive pressure ventilation, 
high flow oxygen via nasal cannula or CPAP.  
7.1.8 Neonatal unit admission lasting >48 hours 
Neonatal unit admission is defined as the number of hours from admission to discharge.

7.1.9 Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn  
Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn is defined as a syndrome of failed circulatory 
adaptation at birth due to delay or impairment in the normal fall in pulmonary vascular resistance 
that occurs following birth. Infants with oxygen saturations of >95% are very unlikely to have 
persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn.  
7.1.10 Meconium Aspiration 
Presence of meconium in the trachea and symptoms/signs fulfilling criteria for persistent 
pulmonary hypertension of the newborn 
7.2 Secondary Outcomes 
7.2.1 Individual components of primary composite endpoints 
These include individual components of the primary composite endpoint (Intrapartum stillbirth, 28-
day neonatal mortality, Apgar score <4 at 5 minutes, Cord artery pH <7.0, Neonatal encephalopathy, 
Neonatal seizures, Neonatal respiratory support >4h, Neonatal unit (Special Care or Intensive Care) 
admission lasting >48h, Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn or Meconium aspiration) 
of the primary composite outcome. There are ten items because outcomes (i) and (viii) each contain 
two individual components  
7.2.2 CS or IVB for fetal distress  
Defined as reduction in the relative risk of CS or IVB for fetal distress by 25% (from 20% to 15%). 
7.2.3 Health Outcomes Relative to Costs  
Cost effectiveness and budget impact of CS vs standard care in eligible populations. A cost-utility 
analysis will be undertaken from a payer’s perspective with a one-year time horizon. A budget impact 
analysis will also be undertaken using a five-year time horizon. All costs will be presented in 2024/25 
dollars, with no discounting applied. Further details are described in Section 8.2 below. 
7.3  Tertiary Outcomes 
7.3.1 CS for fetal distress 
Defined as an emergency CS for fetal distress during labour as detected by fetal heart rate 
abnormalities based on the Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Obstetricians and 
Gynaecologists guidelines and/or abnormal fetal scalp pH or lactate levels.  
7.3.2 Instrumental vaginal birth for fetal distress 
Defined as a vaginal birth using forceps or vacuum extraction for fetal distress.  
7.3.3 CS for other indications 
Other indications include CS for failure to progress, failed induction of labour, malpresentation, failed 
instrumental birth, cord prolapse/presentation, uterine rupture etc. 
7.3.4 Instrumental vaginal birth for other indications 
Defined as a vaginal birth using forceps and vacuum extraction for non-fetal distress indications.  
7.3.5 Spontaneous vaginal birth 
Defined as the uncomplicated vaginal birth of an infant.  
7.3.6 Length of 1st and 2nd stage of labour 
The 1st stage of labour is defined as commencing when labour is diagnosed by the attending obstetric 
team to full dilatation (i.e. 10cm).  The 2nd stage of labour is defined as the period of time from full 
cervical dilatation to birth of the infant.  
7.3.7 Post-partum haemorrhage  
Defined as excessive bleeding (>1000ml) following the birth of a baby.

7.3.8 Post-partum hysterectomy 
Defined as the removal of the uterus (+/- cervix) after birth.  
7.3.9 Uterine rupture 
Defined as a full thickness tear through the uterine myometrium and serosa.   
7.3.10  Maternal Intensive Care Unit admission 
Intensive care unit admission is defined as the admission of the participant to an intensive care unit.  
7.3.11 Maternal Mortality

8 SUBGROUP ANALYSES 
Consistency of the treatment effect on the primary end point will be evaluated across six subgroups: 
(1) Small for gestational age (Birth weight <10th centile for gestation); (2) multiple pregnancy; (3) pre-
eclampsia; (4) one or more previous CS; (5) non-cephalic presentation; (6) major fetal anomaly in 
this pregnancy. 
8.1 Health Related Quality of Life 
Upon registration to the study, consent will be sought for participation in the infant neurodevelopment 
follow-up portion of the study. If consent is given, contact details will be recorded and follow-up 
assessment will be performed as described below. If consent is not given or withdrawn before follow-
up takes place (2-3 years corrected age), no further contact will take place.  
Follow-up assessment for neurodevelopmental outcomes at 2-3 years corrected age will consist of 
the Ages and Stages Questionnaire (ASQ). To facilitate collection of 2-year study outcome data, 6 
monthly contact by phone, email and/or post will be maintained with participants who have consented 
to regular contact and update of contact details. For women who have consented but subsequently 
lost to follow-up, Medicare information may be used to provide updated contact information. 
Research midwives will mail or email the ASQ to women that have consented to infant 
neurodevelopment follow up. Separate funding will be sought for this aspect of the study. 
8.2 Economic Evaluation  
A modelled cost-utility analysis will be undertaken using trial data. The analysis will be from a payer’s 
perspective (covering government and individuals), and cover a one-year time horizon. A Markov 
microsimulation model will be constructed comparing treatment with SC with placebo (standard of 
care). Quality adjusted life years for will be assigned to each health state, and will be obtained from 
the literature61 . Costs will include direct costs of the intervention (drug costs), as well as background 
health service use – hospital inpatient, emergency department, outpatient, community based care, 
and pharmaceutical use. All costs will be presented in 2023/24 dollars, with no discounting applied. 
We will seek consent to extract inpatient, outpatient, and emergency department data and Medicare 
Benefits Schedule and Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme data for all participants from study entry to 
six months of age. Disutilities and costs associated with adverse events will be included. Parametric 
bootstrapping will quantify the uncertainty associated with the model parameters and 95% 
uncertainty ranges presented. A budget impact analysis will also be undertaken using a five-year 
time horizon.

9.1 Definitions 
An ADVERSE EVENT (AE) is any untoward medical occurrence in a patient or clinical investigational 
participant administered a pharmaceutical product and which does not necessarily have a causal 
relationship with this treatment.  An AE can therefore be any unfavourable or unintended sign 
(including an abnormal laboratory finding), symptom, or disease temporally associated with the use

of a medicinal investigational product, whether or not considered related to the medicinal product 
(see below). 
AEs include the following: 
- 
All suspected adverse drug or device reactions 
- 
All reactions from drug or device – overdose, abuse, withdrawal, sensitivity, toxicity or failure 
of expected pharmacological action (if appropriate) 
- 
Apparently unrelated illnesses 
- 
Worsening (severity, frequency) of pre-existing illnesses or symptoms 
- 
Injury or accidents 
- 
Abnormalities in physiological testing or physical examination that require clinical intervention 
or further investigation (beyond ordering a repeat examination) 
- 
Laboratory abnormalities that require clinical intervention or further investigation (beyond 
ordering a laboratory test). Any untoward event that occurs after the protocol-specified 
reporting period which the Investigator believes may be related to the drug or device. 
A SERIOUS ADVERSE EVENT (SAE) is any untoward medical occurrence that at any dose: 
- 
results in death, 
- 
is life-threatening (i.e. the participant is at risk of death at the time of the event), 
- 
requires inpatient hospitalisation or prolongation of existing hospitalisation, 
- 
results in persistent or significant disability or incapacity, 
- 
results in a congenital anomaly/birth defect, 
- 
other important medical events which, in the opinion of the investigator, are likely to become 
serious if untreated, or as defined in the protocol 
NOTES: 
(i) The term “life-threatening” in the definition of “serious” refers to an event in which the participant 
was at risk of death at the time of the event; it does not refer to an event which hypothetically 
might have caused death if it were more severe. 
(ii) Important medical events which may not be immediately life-threatening or result in death or 
hospitalization but which may jeopardize the participant or may require intervention to prevent 
one of the listed outcomes in the definition above should also be considered serious.

A SUSPECTED UNEXPECTED SERIOUS ADVERSE REACTION (SUSAR) is an SAE that is 
related to the drug or device and is unexpected, i.e. not listed in the investigator brochure or approved

possibility that the drug [intervention] caused the AE, i.e. there is evidence to suggest a causal 
relationship between the drug and the event.

The trial investigators are responsible for reporting all SAEs (including SUSARs), SSIs and USMs

The NHMRC CTC will provide SUSAR, SSI and USM reports and SAE line listings to Investigators

regulatory time windows (usually 7 days for fatal/life threatening events with an 8 day follow-up 
report, and 15 days for other events).62

Blood samples will be collected from consenting participants at Queensland sites and shipped to 
Mater Pathology laboratory in South Brisbane for testing and storage. Samples will be sent using 
routine daily Mater Pathology courier services. Samples will be stored indefinitely or until used up

retain the right to have their samples destroyed at any time by contacting their study doctor. If 
samples are destroyed, any data or analyses that were done before the request cannot be removed.

13 STATISTICAL CONSIDERATIONS 
13.1 Sample Size 
The incidence of the composite adverse outcome is estimated at 7% based on data in a recent large 
RCT in term pregnancies54 and the Australian and New Zealand Neonatal Network. To detect a 35% 
reduction from 7% to 4.55% for an alpha of 0.05 and >80% power with a 10% drop-out in each arm, 
needs 3,200 women, also yielding >90% power to detect a 25% reduction for the secondary outcome 
(CS or IVB for fetal distress).  
13.2 Statistical Analysis 
Analyses of the primary and secondary outcomes will adhere to an intent-to-treat basis using 
generalised linear models (binary or normal). Intervention effect will be presented as relative risk 
(RR) or mean difference (MD), as appropriate, with 95% confidence intervals. Two-tailed P values 
<0.05 will be considered statistically significant. Primary analyses will be unadjusted. Numbers 
needed to treat to prevent one adverse outcome will be calculated. Where there are differences in 
the baseline characteristics between the two treatment groups that might be associated with 
outcomes, secondary analyses of the primary outcome will be carried out using multiple (log-
binominal) regression. Reporting will follow CONSORT and TRIPOD guidelines.  
The key secondary outcomes for iSEARCH comprise the individual components of the primary 
composite outcome (Section 2.2.1 above). P values adjusted for the ten comparisons performed for 
this set of endpoints will be derived using the Benjamin-Hochberg procedure, with a family-wise error 
rate of 5%.63,64 Results of other endpoint, subgroup and sensitivity analyses will be interpreted in

proper context and with due consideration of the risk of Type I error. The clinical evidence of the 
statistical evidence from iSEARCH will take into account the recommendations of Pocock et al.65,66 
13.3 Interim analyses 
The Independent Data and Safety Monitoring Committee (IDSMC) will review interim data on the 
primary outcome, specified adverse events and other evidence after 50% of recruitment or whenever 
they deem appropriate informed by recommendations of Peto, Pocock and others.67-69 There will be 
no adjustment to alpha for interim analyses using a Haybittle-Peto boundary. 
Interim analyses of the primary composite outcome: The IDSMC will advise the TMC if in their view 
there is proof beyond reasonable doubt of net benefit or harm for the primary composite outcome, 
employing a commonly used formal threshold of P<0.001 for nominal significance, as recommended 
by Geller and Pocock.69  
Interim analyses of mortality: The IDSMC will advise the TMC if in their view there is a difference in 
mortality due to intrapartum stillbirth and/or 28-day neonatal mortality identified as a deviation from 
the null indicated by a Haybittle-Peto boundary which is equivalent to P <0.0027,67,68 which would 
be needed to justify recommending early stopping. 
14 STUDY ORGANISATION and COMMITTEES 
14.1 Study coordination 
The study is a locally developed and led investigator initiated collaborative group study.  
Coordination, monitoring, data acquisition and management and statistical analysis will be 
performed by the NHMRC CTC.  
14.2 Trial Management Committee 
The Trial Management Committee (TMC) will oversee study planning, monitoring, progress, review 
of information from related research, and implementation of recommendations from other study 
committees and external bodies (e.g. ethics committees).  
The TMC will consider recommendations from the IDSMC about whether to continue the study as 
planned, modify, or stop it, based on interim analyses or other information. The TMC will consist of 
all Chief Investigators and chaired by Prof Sailesh Kumar.  
14.3 Independent Safety and Data Monitoring Committee  
The IDSMC will be established by the TMC. Agreed terms of reference70 for the IDSMC will be 
developed with the TMC. Guided by its Charter, the IDSMC will provide independent assessment of 
participant safety and trial progress, making recommendations to the TMC about the continuation of 
the trial based on data made available by the trial statistician. The IDSMC will comprise an 
interdisciplinary group with relevant expertise. 
The IDSMC will be supplied with: 
• 
Cumulative list of SAEs on a 2-monthly basis (or sooner if requested) 
• 
Interim analysis report(s) 
• 
Relevant information about the study’s progress including accrual rates, compliance, etc. 
• 
Medical, ethical or other information that might influence decisions to continue the study
