2.6 Randomisation and Blinding 
243 
Women will be centrally randomised through the NHMRC CTC. Treatment allocation will be 
244 
balanced using minimisation for the study site and women will be allocated to the treatment groups in 
245 
a ratio of 1:1. 
246 
Registration, including randomisation, must be done according to the instructions in the Study 
247 
Manual. 
248 
Randomisation will be performed after registration and consent to participate has been obtained when 
249 
the participant is admitted to the hospital either in spontaneous labour or for induction of labour. It 
250 
will be done according to the instructions in the Study Manual and should be undertaken after all 
251 
screening assessments have been performed and the responsible investigator has verified the 
252 
participant’s eligibility. Women may only be randomised once in this trial. Once the randomisation 
253 
process has been completed, the participant will be assigned a treatment arm. 
254 
The study is double-blinded. Unblinding is not generally necessary for the management of a 
255 
participant with an adverse event and, as it has an impact on the study’s validity, is strongly 
256 
discouraged. The need for unblinding should be very uncommon as the study intervention is rarely 
257 
associated with severe side effects and it will not delay or prevent standard management of the 
258 
participant. However, if required, it can be done after discussion with the Study Chair and NHMRC 
259 
CTC Clinical Lead using the emergency unblinding number in the study manual.

289

291

293

2.8 Sample Size 
349 
The incidence of the composite adverse outcome is estimated at 7% based on data in a recent large 
350 
RCT in term pregnancies and the Australian and New Zealand Neonatal Network. To detect a 35% 
351 
reduction from 7% to 4.55% for an alpha of 0.05 and >80% power with a 10% drop-out in each arm, 
352 
needs 3,200 women, also yielding >90% power to detect a 25% reduction for the secondary outcome 
353 
(CS or IVB for fetal distress).

3 Statistical Analysis 
370 
3.1 Analysis datasets 
371 
3.1.1 Modified Intention To Treat population 
372 
The principal analysis of study endpoints will be according to the modified intention-to-treat (mITT) 
373 
principle including all subjects with available outcome data. All randomised babies who received no 
374 
study treatment and were later found to be ineligible (before randomisation) will be referred to 
375 
independent adjudicators for a blinded assessment. If the reason is judged not related to the 
376 
randomised treatment allocation or any outcome events post randomisation the baby will be excluded 
377 
from the mITT dataset in a sensitivity analysis.   
378 
3.1.2 Safety population 
379 
All randomised patients who receive at least 1 dose of study treatment will constitute the safety 
380 
population. Patients will be counted in the group according to the treatment they received. 
381 
3.2 General Principles 
382 
Summary statistics (means and standard deviations, medians and inter-quartile range) will be 
383 
presented for continuous baseline variables. Number (%) will be presented for categorical variables. 
384 
Categorical variables will be compared with chi-squared tests, with a test for trend over ordered 
385 
categories (Mantel-Haenszel Chi-square). Continuous variables will be compared using t-tests or the 
386 
Wilcoxon rank-sum test if non-normally distributed.  
387 
Analyses of the primary and secondary outcomes will adhere to the intention-to-treat principal using 
388 
Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE) to accommodate correlation of data between siblings from 
389 
multiple births (log-binomial). An exchangeable correlation structure will be assumed. As the data is 
390 
sparsely clustered due to a small proportion of mother’s having twins (i.e., ~0.6%), methods which 
391 
treat all babies as independent observations will also be performed. Results from the two methods will 
392 
be compared. Failure to take the non-independence of twins into account can lead to under-estimated 
393 
standard errors and biased parameter estimates. Therefore, the GEE method is intended to be the 
394 
primary analysis provided that the models are numerically stable and converge well. Maternal 
395 
secondary/tertiary endpoints will be analysed using Generalised Linear Models (log-binomial). 
396 
Intervention effect will be presented as relative risk (RR) or mean difference (MD), as appropriate, 
397 
with 95% confidence intervals. Two-tailed P values <0.05 will be considered statistically significant. 
398 
Primary analyses will be unadjusted. Numbers needed to treat to prevent one adverse outcome will be 
399 
calculated. Secondary analyses of the primary outcome will be carried out including adjustment for 
400 
relevant baseline prognostic factors using multiple (log-binominal or Robust Poisson) regression. If 
401 
appropriate, the conditional binomial exact test will be used to compare groups when expected cell 
402 
counts are small (i.e., <5). 
403 
The key secondary outcomes for iSEARCH comprise the individual components of the primary 
404 
composite outcome (Section 2.7.1 above). P values adjusted for the ten comparisons performed for 
405 
this set of endpoints will be derived using the Benjamin-Hochberg procedure, with a family-wise error 
406 
rate of 5%. Results of other endpoints, subgroup and sensitivity analyses will be interpreted in proper 
407 
context and with due consideration of the risk of Type I error. In general, complete-case analysis will 
408 
be performed with no imputation done for missing data. 
409

437

3.9 Adjusted Analyses 
442 
Secondary analysis of the primary endpoint to adjustment for prognostic variables will be carried out 
443 
using multivariable regression. Adjustment variables will include: (1) Small for gestational age (Birth 
444 
weight <10th centile for gestation); (2) Pre-eclampsia; (3) Method of delivery for previous pregnancy 
445 
(no prior delivery, spontaneous vaginal delivery, instrument assisted vaginal delivery, and caesarean 
446 
section); and (4) post-natal diagnosis of fetal anomaly. If the log-binomial model fails to converge a 
447 
Robust (modified) Poisson regression model or other computational method will be applied and/or the 
448 
clustering amongst twins will not be accounted for. If it is not possible to fit a GEE model, multiple 
449 
birth will be included as a covariate in the model. 
450 
3.10 Sensitivity Analysis and missing data 
451 
The following sensitivity analyses will be performed for the primary outcome:  
452 
1) Imputation of missing data will be performed for Cord artery pH level using maximum 
453 
likelihood estimation based on the observed pooled event rates within twins (cT) and non-
454 
twins (cNT). The imputation will be stratified by twin status due to the much higher Cord 
455 
Artery pH <7 event rate among twins (11% vs 0.6% among babies where cord artery pH was 
456 
tested) and higher missing data rate. This calculation will take into account the occurrence of 
457 
the other 9 components of the composite primary outcome. The imputation will be performed 
458 
according to the following steps:

where Cord Artery pH testing was performed,

b. Of those babies having an event the fractions (fT and fNT) that suffered none of the

3.11 Subgroup Analyses 
476 
Consistency of the treatment effect on the primary end point will be evaluated across the following 
477 
pre-specified subgroups: (1) Small for gestational age (Birth weight <10th centile for gestation); (2) 
478 
multiple pregnancy; (3) pre-eclampsia; (4) Method of delivery for previous pregnancy CS; (5) non-
479 
cephalic presentation; (6) post-natal diagnosis of fetal anomaly. 
480 
 
481 
This will be performed by fitting an interaction term between the subgroup of interest and treatment in 
482 
a log-binomial GLM model (along with the relevant main effect terms). The two-sided level of 
483 
significance is 0.05, however due to the number of analyses results should be interpreted with caution. 
484 
Treatment effects will be determined from a model fitted to each of the levels of the subgroup, e.g., 
485 
for pre-eclampsia separate models will be fitted for yes and no. Models will not include adjustment 
486 
for baseline covariates. A GEE model will not be applied because the number of twins in each 
487 
subgroup maybe too small for models to converge.
