370  3 Statistical Analysis

371  3.1 Analysis datasets

372   3.1.1 Modified Intention To Treat population
373   The principal analysis of study endpoints will be according to the modified intention-to-treat (mITT)
374    principle including all subjects with available outcome data. All randomised babies who received no
375    study treatment and were later found to be ineligible (before randomisation) will be referred to
376    independent adjudicators for a blinded assessment. If the reason is judged not related to the
377    randomised treatment allocation or any outcome events post randomisation the baby will be excluded
378   from the mITT dataset in a sensitivity analysis.

379   3.1.2 Safety population
380    All randomised patients who receive at least 1 dose of study treatment will constitute the safety
381    population. Patients will be counted in the group according to the treatment they received.
382  3.2 General Principles
383   Summary statistics (means and standard deviations, medians and inter-quartile range) will be
384    presented for continuous baseline variables. Number (%) will be presented for categorical variables.
385    Categorical variables will be compared with chi-squared tests, with a test for trend over ordered
386    categories (Mantel-Haenszel Chi-square). Continuous variables will be compared using t-tests or the
387   Wilcoxon rank-sum test if non-normally distributed.

388    Analyses of the primary and secondary outcomes will adhere to the intention-to-treat principal using
389    Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE) to accommodate correlation of data between siblings from
390    multiple births (log-binomial). An exchangeable correlation structure will be assumed. As the data is
391    sparsely clustered due to a small proportion of mother’s having twins (i.e., ~0.6%), methods which
392     treat all babies as independent observations will also be performed. Results from the two methods will
393   be compared. Failure to take the non-independence of twins into account can lead to under-estimated
394    standard errors and biased parameter estimates. Therefore, the GEE method is intended to be the
395    primary analysis provided that the models are numerically stable and converge well. Maternal
396    secondary/tertiary endpoints will be analysed using Generalised Linear Models (log-binomial).

397    Intervention effect will be presented as relative risk (RR) or mean difference (MD), as appropriate,
398    with 95% confidence intervals. Two-tailed P values <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.
399    Primary analyses will be unadjusted. Numbers needed to treat to prevent one adverse outcome will be
400    calculated. Secondary analyses of the primary outcome will be carried out including adjustment for
401    relevant baseline prognostic factors using multiple (log-binominal or Robust Poisson) regression. If
402    appropriate, the conditional binomial exact test will be used to compare groups when expected cell
403    counts are small (i.e., <5).

404   The key secondary outcomes for iSEARCH comprise the individual components of the primary
405    composite outcome (Section 2.7.1 above). P values adjusted for the ten comparisons performed for
406     this set of endpoints will be derived using the Benjamin-Hochberg procedure, with a family-wise error
407    rate of 5%. Results of other endpoints, subgroup and sensitivity analyses will be interpreted in proper
408    context and with due consideration of the risk of Type I error. In general, complete-case analysis will
409   be performed with no imputation done for missing data.
410  3.3 Subject Disposition
411  A patient disposition flow-chart will be presented showing the number of mothers screened, eligible,
412    consented and randomised to each treatment arm. Reasons for withdrawal, losses to follow-up and
413   numbers available for mITT and safety analyses will be detailed.
414  A table will also be prepared showing the number of randomised mothers found to be ineligible with
415    reasons detailed.

416  3.4 Treatment Adherence
417   The proportions of mothers that received treatment will be calculated. The numbers receiving 1, 2
418   and all 3 doses will be described along with the time from randomisation to each dose and the dose
419    received. Reasons for not giving treatment will also be detailed.
420  3.5 Baseline Analyses
421  A table will be prepared showing the stratification factors by treatment arm (i.e., site). Descriptive
422     statistics will be prepared to summarise baseline characteristics (i.e., maternal demographics and
423    clinical details) by treatment allocation.
424  3.6 Analysis of the Primary Outcome
425   The primary analysis of the primary outcome a composite of ten perinatal outcomes will be based on a
426   GEE log-binomial model. A relative risk, with a 95% confidence interval (CI) will be presented to
427    indicate the relative reduction (or increase) in risk associated with Sildenafil therapy along with
428   numbers and proportions meeting the outcome in each treatment arm. Numbers needed to treat to
429    prevent one adverse outcome will be calculated.
430
431    In 27% of babies a cord artery test for pH level was not performed. As this is a component of the
432    primary outcome ‘not done’ for this component will be assumed to be no event.
433  3.7 Analysis of Secondary and Tertiary Outcomes
434   The analysis of neonatal secondary outcomes will be evaluated with the use of GEE log-binomial
435    models. Maternal outcomes will be analysed using a log-binomial generalized linear model for binary
436    outcomes, and a t-test for continuous outcomes.
437  3.8 Safety Outcomes
438   An analysis of neonatal and maternal mortality will also be performed by treatment received.
439   The frequency of maternal adverse events of interest will be analysed by treatment received. The
440    comparison between treatment arms will employ a chi-squared test or the conditional binomial exact
441     test if expected cell counts are small (i.e., <5).
442  3.9 Adjusted Analyses
443   Secondary analysis of the primary endpoint to adjustment for prognostic variables will be carried out
444    using multivariable regression. Adjustment variables will include: (1) Small for gestational age (Birth
445    weight <10th centile for gestation); (2) Pre-eclampsia; (3) Method of delivery for previous pregnancy
446    (no prior delivery, spontaneous vaginal delivery, instrument assisted vaginal delivery, and caesarean
447    section); and (4) post-natal diagnosis of fetal anomaly. If the log-binomial model fails to converge a
448   Robust (modified) Poisson regression model or other computational method will be applied and/or the
449    clustering amongst twins will not be accounted for. If it is not possible to fit a GEE model, multiple
450    birth will be included as a covariate in the model.
451  3.10 Sensitivity Analysis and missing data
452   The following sensitivity analyses will be performed for the primary outcome:
453        1)  Imputation of missing data will be performed for Cord artery pH level using maximum
454            likelihood estimation based on the observed pooled event rates within twins (cT) and non-
455           twins (cNT). The imputation will be stratified by twin status due to the much higher Cord
456           Artery pH <7 event rate among twins (11% vs 0.6% among babies where cord artery pH was
457             tested) and higher missing data rate. This calculation will take into account the occurrence of
458            the other 9 components of the composite primary outcome. The imputation will be performed
459           according to the following steps:




                                                                                              13     iSEARCH – Statistical Analysis Plan                                Protocol version: V2.1 31st January 2022


460                   a.  The Cord Artery pH <7 event rates (cT and cNT) will be calculated among babies
461                 where Cord Artery pH testing was performed,
462                 b.  Of those babies having an event the fractions (fT and fNT) that suffered none of the
463                    other 9 components will be calculated,
464                   c.  The additional number of events will be calculated by multiplying the number of
465                   babies with Cord Artery Not Done who had not already met the primary endpoint by
466                p and f above within each treatment arm (ST, SNT, PT, PNT) e.g., for non-twins in
467                  Placebo arm the calculation is PNTcNTfNT.
468        2) An analysis of the primary outcome based on 9 components only will be performed with Cord
469             artery pH <7 excluded from the definition.
470        3) A generalised linear model (log-binomial) will be fitted ignoring the correlation between
471            twins.
472        4)  Models will be fitted with an extra covariate included to indicate whether or not Cord Artery
473             testing was done in order to adjust for any imputation. A GLM model will be used if there are
474           numerical estimation problems with the GEE model.
475
476  3.11 Subgroup Analyses
477    Consistency of the treatment effect on the primary end point will be evaluated across the following
478    pre-specified subgroups: (1) Small for gestational age (Birth weight <10th centile for gestation); (2)
479    multiple pregnancy; (3) pre-eclampsia; (4) Method of delivery for previous pregnancy CS; (5) non-
480    cephalic presentation; (6) post-natal diagnosis of fetal anomaly.
481
482    This will be performed by fitting an interaction term between the subgroup of interest and treatment in
483    a log-binomial GLM model (along with the relevant main effect terms). The two-sided level of
484    significance is 0.05, however due to the number of analyses results should be interpreted with caution.
485    Treatment effects will be determined from a model fitted to each of the levels of the subgroup, e.g.,
486    for pre-eclampsia separate models will be fitted for yes and no. Models will not include adjustment
487    for baseline covariates. A GEE model will not be applied because the number of twins in each
488    subgroup maybe too small for models to converge.
