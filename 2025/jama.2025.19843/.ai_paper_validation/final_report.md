## Verified Scientific Findings

### V-02 — Day-30 MACE definition and placebo count cannot be reconciled

**Issue statement.** The main article defines dialysis as a day-30 major adverse cardiovascular event component, but the results supplement reports fewer placebo participants with day-30 MACE than with dialysis and repeats the MACE count in a figure whose definition omits dialysis; the definition, count, and display therefore cannot be jointly verified.

- **Evidence status:** Accepted by the evidence verifier; retained by the critic.
- **Category:** Cross-document inconsistency.
- **Secondary descriptor:** Statistical reporting inconsistency.
- **Severity:** Major, as retained by the critic. The verifier's earlier screening severity was Moderate; the critic's Major rating controls the final finding.
- **Affected outcome:** Prespecified secondary day-30 MACE composite in the placebo group.
- **Unaffected scope:** No error in the primary outcome or effect on the overall trial conclusion is established.

#### Exact source locations

1. **Main-article composite definition:** [jama_combes_2025_oi_250087_1766516490.94011.pdf — PDF p. 3](../jama_combes_2025_oi_250087_1766516490.94011.pdf#page=3), journal p. 62, Methods, “Outcomes,” right column, paragraph beginning “Predefined secondary outcomes included.”
2. **Supplementary composite count:** [joi250087supp3_prod_1766516490.97011.pdf — PDF p. 5](../joi250087supp3_prod_1766516490.97011.pdf#page=5), eTable 3, “Secondary End Points,” row “Major adverse cardiovascular events during first 30 days,” Placebo column, denominator in the column header.
3. **Supplementary component count:** [joi250087supp3_prod_1766516490.97011.pdf — PDF p. 5](../joi250087supp3_prod_1766516490.97011.pdf#page=5), eTable 3, row “Dialysis by day 30,” Placebo column.
4. **Supplementary figure definition and event count:** [joi250087supp3_prod_1766516490.97011.pdf — PDF p. 13](../joi250087supp3_prod_1766516490.97011.pdf#page=13), eFigure 4, title and D30 panel, cumulative number of events at day 30.

#### Source evidence

**Evidence A — main-article definition, verbatim.** The Methods define “major adverse cardiovascular events (death, heart transplant, escalation to need for left ventricular assist device, stroke, dialysis, or heart failure rehospitalization) at days 30 and 60”. The observed source statement explicitly includes **dialysis**.

**Evidence B — eTable 3 values.** The placebo header states **N = 104**. The displayed day-30 values are:

- Major adverse cardiovascular events during first 30 days: **36 (34.6%)**.
- Dialysis by day 30: **38 (36.5%)**.

**Evidence C — eFigure 4 definition and count.** The figure title defines MACE as “Death, Cardiac Transplant, Escalation to Permanent Left Ventricular Assist Device, Stroke, or Re-Hospitalization for Heart Failure.” **Dialysis is not listed.** The D30 placebo curve shows **36** cumulative events, matching the eTable 3 MACE numerator.

#### Reported-versus-comparator comparison

| Element | Reported item | Comparator | Direct result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Placebo day-30 count | MACE: 36 participants | Dialysis: 38 participants | MACE is 2 participants smaller than a component named in the main-article definition. |
| Placebo day-30 percentage | MACE: 34.6% | Dialysis: 36.5% | MACE is 1.9 displayed percentage points lower. |
| MACE definition | Main article includes dialysis | eFigure 4 omits dialysis | The textual definitions differ. |
| MACE event count | eTable 3: 36 | eFigure 4: 36 | The count agrees across the supplement despite the figure's definition differing from the main article. |

#### Reasoning procedure

1. **Match population, outcome, and time point.** Both eTable 3 rows are day-30 outcomes in the placebo column with the same randomized denominator, 104. The comparison is not between different arms, time points, or denominators.
2. **Identify the required relationship.** Under the main-article definition, dialysis is one component of the MACE union. If a participant satisfies a named component, that participant belongs to the composite, regardless of overlap with other components. Consequently, the count of the union cannot be less than the count of dialysis alone:

   \[
   n(\mathrm{MACE}_{30}) \geq n(\mathrm{dialysis}_{30}).
   \]

3. **Apply the rule to the printed integer numerators.** The supplement reports:

   \[
   n(\mathrm{MACE}_{30}) - n(\mathrm{dialysis}_{30}) = 36 - 38 = -2\ \text{participants}.
   \]

   The observed difference is negative, so the reported composite is two participants smaller than a stated component and therefore cannot follow the main-article definition.
4. **Reproduce the printed percentages from the common denominator.** Direct source observations are the numerators 36 and 38 and denominator 104. Derived checks are:

   \[
   \frac{36}{104}\times100 = 34.6154\ldots\% \rightarrow 34.6\%,
   \]

   \[
   \frac{38}{104}\times100 = 36.5385\ldots\% \rightarrow 36.5\%.
   \]

   Both displayed percentages correctly round to one decimal place. Their displayed difference is:

   \[
   34.6\%-36.5\%=-1.9\ \text{percentage points}.
   \]

   From the unrounded counts, the exact difference is:

   \[
   \frac{36-38}{104}\times100=-1.9231\ldots\ \text{percentage points}.
   \]

5. **Assess rounding tolerance.** A one-decimal percentage may vary slightly because of rounding, but the numerators are integer participant counts. No percentage-rounding tolerance can transform 36 into a value greater than or equal to 38. The nesting failure therefore remains after checking the denominator and rounding.
6. **Cross-check the supplementary figure.** eFigure 4 repeats **36** D30 placebo MACE events. Its D30 endpoint display also reconciles internally as **68 at risk + 36 events = 104** for placebo and **66 at risk + 35 events = 101** for levosimendan. This confirms that the table's 36 was carried into the figure; it does not reconcile that count with a main-article definition that includes dialysis.
7. **Separate source observations from derived logic.** The MACE definitions, denominator, counts, percentages, and plotted event count are direct observations. The nesting inequality, arithmetic differences, exact percentage calculations, and conclusion that the items cannot all follow the same definition are derived checks.
8. **Preserve the two unresolved explanations.** The supplied package supports at least two alternatives already bounded by the critic: dialysis may have been mistakenly included in the main article's written definition, or a definition that includes dialysis may not have been followed in the composite derivation/reporting. The supplied documents do not identify which item needs correction. Participant-level overlap or non-overlap among components cannot make a union smaller than one of its included components, so raw participant overlap is not needed to establish the reporting inconsistency; the intended specification is needed to determine the correction.

#### Existing supported conclusion

The reported day-30 placebo MACE count is smaller than a component expressly included in the main-article definition, while the supplement displays the same MACE count under a definition that omits that component. The day-30 MACE definition, count, associated effect estimates, and eFigure 4 display cannot be jointly verified.

#### Bounded impact and limit on interpretation

The finding is bounded to the reported secondary MACE composite and its related table/figure presentation. It does **not** establish that two dialysis cases were necessarily omitted, that the raw data are invalid, or that the composite numerator must take any particular corrected value. It also does not establish which definition the investigators intended, quantify a corrected risk difference or relative risk, demonstrate a primary-outcome error, or establish an effect on the article's overall conclusion.

#### Human verification instruction

1. Open the main article at PDF p. 3, journal p. 62, Methods—Outcomes. Confirm that **dialysis** appears in the list defining MACE at days 30 and 60. If dialysis is absent in a controlling source version, record the version difference; if present, proceed with the nesting check.
2. Open the results supplement at PDF p. 5, eTable 3. Confirm that the placebo header is **N = 104**, day-30 MACE is **36 (34.6%)**, and dialysis by day 30 is **38 (36.5%)**.
3. Recalculate `36 / 104 × 100` and `38 / 104 × 100`. Results rounding to 34.6% and 36.5% confirm that the displayed percentages use the stated denominator and that the discrepancy is in the relationship between the integer counts, not percentage transcription.
4. Apply the composite-component rule. If dialysis is an included component, a MACE numerator below 38 cannot satisfy that definition; confirmation of 36 and 38 therefore confirms the locked reporting inconsistency.
5. Open the results supplement at PDF p. 13, eFigure 4. Confirm that the title omits dialysis and that the D30 placebo event count is **36**. This confirms the figure/table count agreement and main-article/figure definition disagreement.
6. Review the authors' controlling outcome specification and prespecified participant-level derivation. A controlling definition that excludes dialysis would resolve the count nesting by identifying a written-definition discrepancy in the main article. A controlling definition that includes dialysis would require a reconciled composite derivation and, if applicable, corrected table effect estimates and figure. Either result identifies the correction without changing the already established reporting inconsistency.

### V-01 — Figure 2 risk/event display

**Issue statement under review.** Figure 2 displays “at risk” and “with event” values whose simple sums exceed the randomized group totals at days 5, 10, and 15, but the supplied figure does not define the event row or risk-set convention well enough to establish that the two displayed rows are mutually exclusive participant counts.

- **Evidence status:** Uncertain; not forwarded as a final issue.
- **Category:** Not assigned by the evidence verifier. For traceability only, the originating arithmetic checker labelled the candidate “Presentation inconsistency (internal count relationship).” This checker label is not a new final category assignment.
- **Potential severity:** Not assigned in the authoritative record; no severity is inferred in this detailing report.
- **Critic status:** Not reviewed because the verifier did not forward the candidate.
- **Missing evidence:** The figure-generation definition or data dictionary for “No. of patients with event” and the displayed competing-risk risk set, or author clarification providing the same information.

#### Exact source location

[jama_combes_2025_oi_250087_1766516490.94011.pdf — PDF p. 6](../jama_combes_2025_oi_250087_1766516490.94011.pdf#page=6), journal p. 65:

- Figure 2, “Cumulative Incidence of Successful ECMO Weaning, Weaning Failure, and Death Before Weaning by Treatment Group and Competing Events Shown in Survival Curves,” rows “No. of patients at risk” and “No. of patients with event,” days 0, 5, 10, and 15.
- Figure 2 caption, which describes the cumulative incidence and competing events but does not define the two numerical rows' joint counting convention.
- Adjacent Table 2, primary outcome and competing-event rows, including footnote d on censoring.

#### Reported values

| Treatment group and row | Day 0 | Day 5 | Day 10 | Day 15 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Levosimendan, patients at risk | 101 | 88 | 20 | 10 |
| Levosimendan, patients with event | 0 | 36 | 84 | 93 |
| Placebo, patients at risk | 104 | 76 | 37 | 19 |
| Placebo, patients with event | 0 | 41 | 70 | 89 |

The randomized group sizes are **101 levosimendan** and **104 placebo**, as shown by the day-0 risk row and the adjacent Table 2 headers.

#### Reasoning procedure

1. **Identify the displayed quantities.** The direct source observations are the group totals and the two labelled rows at days 5, 10, and 15. The figure states that the plotted favorable event is successful ECMO weaning and that death before weaning and unsuccessful weaning are competing risks. It does not state exactly what its “with event” row counts.
2. **Perform the diagnostic additions.** If, and only if, “at risk” and “with event” are disjoint participant states at the same time, their sum should not exceed the randomized total before accounting for censoring and other mutually exclusive competing-event states.

   | Time | Levosimendan diagnostic sum | Difference from N=101 | Placebo diagnostic sum | Difference from N=104 |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | Day 5 | 88 + 36 = **124** | **+23** | 76 + 41 = **117** | **+13** |
   | Day 10 | 20 + 84 = **104** | **+3** | 37 + 70 = **107** | **+3** |
   | Day 15 | 10 + 93 = **103** | **+2** | 19 + 89 = **108** | **+4** |

   These are integer additions, so rounding tolerance does not apply to the diagnostic sums.
3. **State why the diagnostic is not dispositive.** The necessary premise—mutual exclusivity of the two displayed rows—is not established by the figure, caption, or nearby methods. Competing-risk and subdistribution displays can use risk-set conventions that are not recoverable from these labels alone. The phrase “No. of patients with event” is also not defined as cumulative unique favorable events, all competing events, or another plotting quantity. Therefore the simple additions cannot, by themselves, prove that any printed value is wrong.
4. **Cross-check the adjacent endpoint partition.** Table 2 provides a separately labelled primary-outcome partition that does reconcile:

   - Levosimendan: 69 successful weaning + 15 weaning failures + 15 deaths before weaning + 2 censored = **101**.
   - Placebo: 71 successful weaning + 21 weaning failures + 12 deaths before weaning = **104**.

   For levosimendan, footnote d identifies the two censored observations as one participant still receiving ECMO at day 30 and one participant lost before a competing event. This reconciliation does not define the Figure 2 rows, but it limits the observed ambiguity to the supporting display rather than demonstrating an inconsistency in the final endpoint partition.
5. **Separate direct and derived evidence.** The labels and values are direct source observations. The sums and excesses are diagnostic derived calculations. Treating the two rows as disjoint would be an unverified assumption; the locked Uncertain disposition follows from the missing definition, not from a decision that the displayed sums are valid or invalid.
6. **Consider the recorded alternative interpretations.** If the rows are conventional disjoint counts of participants still at risk and participants with cumulative unique events, the excesses would support a presentation inconsistency. If “with event” uses a non-disjoint or otherwise specialized plotting definition, the additions are not a valid reconciliation rule and the candidate would resolve without correcting the printed values. The supplied package cannot choose between those alternatives.

#### Existing supported conclusion

The Figure 2 values and diagnostic excesses are reproducible, but the source does not establish the counting rule required to interpret them as an inconsistency. The candidate therefore remains Uncertain and is not a final scientific finding.

#### Limit on interpretation

This candidate does not establish an erroneous risk set, event count, curve, subdistribution hazard ratio, primary-outcome numerator, or participant-flow total. It should not be cited as proof that participants were double counted. The reconciled Table 2 partitions and the absence of a figure-row definition prevent a stronger conclusion from the supplied materials.

#### Verification instruction

1. Open Figure 2 on main-article PDF p. 6, journal p. 65. Transcribe the two rows at days 0, 5, 10, and 15 and confirm the values shown above.
2. Repeat each displayed diagnostic addition and compare it with the randomized N for the same arm. Confirmation reproduces the numerical pattern but does not, alone, confirm an error.
3. Inspect the original figure-generation code, exported plotting data, caption source, or data dictionary. Determine precisely whether “with event” is cumulative unique successful-weaning events and whether “at risk” excludes every participant already counted there.
4. If the two rows are confirmed to be mutually exclusive under the source plotting convention, recompute each risk set after prior events, competing events, and censoring; values exceeding N would then support the originating presentation candidate. If the rows are confirmed to be non-disjoint or to use another defined convention, document that convention and regard it as resolving the diagnostic excess.
5. Independently confirm the adjacent Table 2 partition. If 69 + 15 + 15 + 2 = 101 and 71 + 21 + 12 = 104, retain the limitation that the final endpoint categories reconcile even while the figure-row meaning is investigated.
