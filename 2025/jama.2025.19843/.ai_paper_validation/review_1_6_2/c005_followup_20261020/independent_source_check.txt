Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events (MACE): MACE is defined as the occurrence of any of the following events on days 30 and 60: death, cardiac transplant, escalation to a permanent left ventricular assist device, stroke, or re-hospitalization for heart failure. The incidence of MACE will be described using survival analysis with Kaplan-Meier estimation and 95% confidence interval. Only survival analysis up to 60 days will be performed. In this analysis, time will be defined as the time from the date of randomization to the date of MACE occurrence, the date of the last follow-up update, or the end of follow-up, whichever comes first. Differences between groups will be assessed using the log-rank test. To adjust for the primary etiology of cardiogenic shock, a Cox proportional hazards model will be employed.

Contextual subset comparison and review-policy boundary
If D30 dialysis is a component of MACE using the same population, time window and component coding, the number of patients with MACE must be at least the number with dialysis. The exact printed placebo counts 36 and38 do not satisfy that conditional inclusion relation. Overlapping components cannot reduce a union below a member.
The historical review did not register this as a separate numeric candidate: its required half-unit display-precision calculation uses [37.5,38.5] versus[35.5,36.5], gap1, relative discrepancy1/36=0.0277778<=0.05. Bounded downstream implications
A systematic review or meta-analysis could attach a six-component MACE definition to a result computed with five components, or the reverse, unless the actual implementation is clarified. The current sources do not establish which occurred.
At a fixed time and with identical population/coding, let A be patients with at least one of the five shared components and D patients with dialysis. Then N_six=|A union D|=|A|+|D minus A|. Aggregate component counts do not supply the overlap, so adding/subtracting29 or38 cannot reconstruct a changed MACE count or RR.
For a time-to-first-event analysis, adding dialysis could bring forward event time even among patients already counted through a different later component. Thus agreement of final counts would not by itself establish identical event-time analyses. No numerical direction, magnitude, P value or conclusion change is inferred.
No supplied participant-level flags, overlap records, event dates, implementation code or explicit amendment establish the analyzed definition. Both alternatives remain possible: five-component implementation with dialysis retained in protocol/main wording, or six-component implementation with dialysis omitted from SAP/figure lists. Current evidence confirms the printed definition discrepancy only.
