12  STATISTICAL ASPECTS

12.1  Planned statistical methods, including the timetable for any planned interim analyses

The statistical analysis will be performed by a biostatistician of the Clinical Research Unit of Pitié
Salpêtrière Hospital, under the responsibility of Pr Florence Tubach.

All comparisons will be done with a two-sided type I error of 5%.

Descriptive analysis will always be overall and by treatment group.

Comparative analysis between groups will be systematically performed:
          - Without adjustment;
          - With adjustment on baseline prognostic factors whose distribution could be, despite the
randomization, unbalanced between treatment groups. These adjustments will potentially require
using the appropriate models, whose relevance will be discussed according to the distribution and to
the type of variables.


12.1.1  Descriptive methods

Qualitative variables will be described by numbers and percentages.
Quantitative variables will be described by numbers, mean, standard deviation, 95% confidence
interval of the mean, median, range, and interquartile range.
Censored data will be described by Kaplan-Meier estimation or cumulative incidence function, with
rate at 15, 30 and 60 days if appropriate with 95% confidence interval.


12.1.2 Software used

Statistical analyses will be performed with the SAS® or R software with the most recent version at the
time of the analysis.

12.1.7 Main outcome analysis

The main outcome is the time to successful ECMO weaning within the 30 days following randomization
in the presence of competing risks.

5 types of follow-up termination can appear within the 30 days following randomization:
-   Subject not weaned from ECMO at 30 days: censorship
-   Death before D30 after randomization, without ECMO weaning: competitive event
-   Subject with successful ECMO weaning: event of interest
-   Subjects with unsuccessful ECMO weaning (i.e. death, need for repeat ECMO, need for another
    mechanical circulatory device or heart transplantation within 30 days after ECMO removal):
    competitive event
-   Lost to follow-up within the 30 days following randomization: censorship.

Time to successful ECMO weaning will be defined as the difference between the date of randomization
and the weaning date.

Time to unsuccessful ECMO weaning with will be defined as the difference between the date of
randomization and the ECMO removal date, in patients for whom repeat ECMO, need for another
mechanical circulatory device or heart transplantation or death will occur within 30 days after ECMO
removal.

Time to death without ECMO weaning will be defined as the difference between the date of
randomization and the date of death.

Time to lost to follow-up will be defined as the difference between the date of randomization and the
date of the latest news.

12.1.8 Secondary outcomes analysis

Qualitative outcomes:
-    will be described by numbers and percentages;
-    will be tested with χ² test, corrected χ² test or Fisher test, as appropriate.
-   To account for prognostic factors or for exploratory analyses we will use a logistic model.

Quantitative outcomes:
-    will be described by numbers, mean, standard deviation, 95% confidence interval of the mean,
   median, range, and interquartile range;
-    will be tested with the Student t-test, the Student t-test or the Mann-Whitney-Wilcoxon test, as
    appropriate
-   To adjust on prognostic factors, a linear regression model will be used.

Censored outcomes:
- Mortality will be described by survival analysis with Kaplan-Meier estimation and 95% confidence
interval. Only survival analysis within 60 days will be performed. In this analysis, time to death will be
defined as the difference between the date of randomization and the date of death (whatever ECMO
weaning had occurred before or not) or the date of the last news or the end of the follow-up (60 days).
Difference between groups will be tested with the logrank test. To adjust for prognostic factors, a cox
model will be used.


12.2  Sample size calculation


We estimated the cumulative incidence of successful ECMO weaning in the control arm at 50% in the
presence of competing causes (death or weaning failure) from a 30-day cohort study of similar
patients. According to Latouche et al. [38], with HR=1.75 (subdistribution hazard ratio), an alpha risk
of 5% and a power of 80%, we need 101 events (= successful ECMO weaning) and 206 subjects in total
(103 per group).

      proportion.

No replacement is foreseen.

All comparisons will be done with a two-sided type I error of 5%.
